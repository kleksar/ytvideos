## Independent findings

### Alta Banka card failure

The core event in the host's explanation is confirmed: the National Bank of Serbia (NBS) revoked ALTA banka a.d. Beograd's operating licence. NBS's English release is dated 31 October 2026 and says the decision was made at that day's meeting; it cites deterioration in the bank's financial condition and depositor risk. The Serbian NBS notice says the licence was revoked on 30 October. The Deposit Insurance Agency's English notice dates the decision to 31 October. Because these official pages conflict, describe the event as confirmed by 31 October and verify the signed administrative decision before using a single exact decision date. The bank itself subsequently announced that bankruptcy proceedings had begun.

- [NBS English notice, 31 October 2026](https://nbs.rs/sr_RS/scripts/showcontent/index.html?id=21850)
- [NBS Serbian notice specifying 30 October](https://www.nbs.rs/sr/scripts/showcontent/index.html?id=21851)
- [Deposit Insurance Agency notice and coverage terms](https://www.aod.rs/clan/vest/vest_forma/138)
- [ALTA Banka statement on bankruptcy proceedings](https://altabanka.rs/saopstenje-za-javnost/)

The Deposit Insurance Agency states that eligible depositors have coverage up to €50,000 per depositor and reimbursement begins no later than seven business days after the Commercial Court's bankruptcy ruling. The official releases do not verify the host's specific declined payment at checkout, his balance, or what caused that transaction to fail. Preserve that part as his first-person account; do not imply that particular political allegations or transfers were established.

### Halloween in Serbia

U.S. National Retail Federation's 2026 survey found 74% planned to celebrate Halloween, 61% to hand out candy, and 50% to wear a costume. A YouGov survey of 5,132 GB adults on 30 October 2026 found 26% planned to give treats to children, 11% to take a child trick-or-treating, and 52% planned no Halloween activity. The practice is familiar, especially in the U.S., but less universal in Britain. Familiarity may make the tiger costume less surprising to viewers; the distinctiveness is the Serbian setting and the host's personal comparison.

Two English-language YouTube videos specifically about Halloween in Serbia surfaced in search: one published in 2018 with 2,138 views and one in 2020 with 3,615 views. These modest examples show a small existing niche, not evidence of mass demand.

- [NRF, 2026 Halloween survey](https://nrf.com/media-center/press-releases/nrf-halloween-survey-shows-consumer-spending-expected-to-reach-13-5-billion)
- [YouGov GB, 30 October 2026 daily survey](https://yougov.com/en-gb/daily-results/20261030-b6e67-1)
- [Halloween in Serbia and How Serbs Celebrate (2018)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Orm1Rzb0aQ8)
- [Halloween in Serbia (2020)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wqJ6A64f6MM)

**Editorial assessment:** a seasonally useful, easy-to-understand reserve. Do not portray the tiger costume as strange to Americans; the contrast needs to come from the host's experience of Halloween coexisting with Orthodox customs. Avoid turning his broad characterization of Serbian religiosity into an objective claim about every resident.
