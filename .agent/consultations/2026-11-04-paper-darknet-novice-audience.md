## Extra High recommendation

Keep an English Short at about 90 seconds. Rebuild the first 20–23 seconds to establish the minimum mental model before the operation: ordinary sites versus hidden sites accessed through Tor; Tor as a privacy tool, not a synonym for crime; AlphaBay and Hansa as two separate illicit marketplaces, not two parts of the darknet. Avoid a technical lecture. Main question: “What if the website you trusted was secretly run by the police?” Viewer promise: “How police secretly took over one dark web marketplace — then watched its traffic surge when another was shut down.”

## Timed structure (8 beats)

1. **0–12s — Two kinds of sites.** Ordinary websites and hidden websites reached through Tor. Explain Tor only as a technology that can help protect privacy; the darknet is not a separate internet and is not all criminal.
2. **12–23s — Two marketplaces.** In 2017, AlphaBay and Hansa were separate online marketplaces selling illegal goods. Hook: what if police quietly became the operator of one?
3. **23–34s — Police take Hansa.** After Hansa administrators were arrested in Germany, Dutch police took control on June 20, 2017, and kept the site running.
4. **34–46s — The turn.** Police copied and moved Hansa to servers in the Netherlands. The site still looked familiar; its operator had changed.
5. **46–60s — AlphaBay closes.** AlphaBay shut on July 4. Hansa's daily visitors rose from roughly 1,000 to 8,000.
6. **60–72s — Evidence gathered.** More than 50,000 transactions were recorded. The Dutch release refers to about 10,000 addresses of foreign buyers/deliveries shared with Europol; use one spoken figure and put the other onscreen if needed.
7. **72–81s — Operation revealed.** Hansa closed July 20 and visitors saw a police notice.
8. **81–90s — Answer the question.** Tor can help hide a network address; it does not guarantee that a site is operated by who it claims to be. End on the distinction between hiding a visitor's location and knowing who controls a platform.

## Accuracy and audience constraints

- The story is about a police operation against two criminal marketplaces, not about all darknet use or Tor as a whole.
- Do not imply police broke Tor; they took administrative control of one marketplace.
- Confirm the exact nature of the ~10,000 records against the Dutch OM source before narration; do not call them IP addresses, identified people, arrests, or convictions.
- Do not invent ordinary users' emotions, dialogue, motives, or individual outcomes.
- The Extra High view: 90 seconds is enough if only the basic terms and one causal sequence are explained; estimated narration 175–195 English words. A long film needs additional documented human or legal material.
