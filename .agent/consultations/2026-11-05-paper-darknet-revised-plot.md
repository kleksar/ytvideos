## Extra High recommendation and plot

Concept: **The Market That Was Already Under Police Control**.

Dramatic entry: one online market disappears; visitors head to another; police already control the second one. Begin with the event and hidden relationship, then rewind to reveal how the police came to control Hansa before AlphaBay closed. This is a story about the sequence of two operations, not a general explanation of darknet technology.

0–9 sec — **One market goes dark; another keeps running.** In Paper diorama, AlphaBay facade goes dark, a stream moves toward Hansa; briefly show that police are already behind Hansa’s facade. Mystery: how did police get there before the new visitors?

9–18 sec — **Name the places only once they matter.** Identify AlphaBay and Hansa as two separate online markets in the darknet, a part of the internet reached through special access rather than an ordinary browser. No Tor lecture or access instructions. Question: why would shutting one market send traffic to another?

18–30 sec — **Rewind: police take Hansa.** After Hansa’s administrators are arrested, Dutch police gain control. This establishes that the second market is already under police control before the AlphaBay closure. Question: how can visitors fail to see the change?

30–41 sec — **Same facade, different operator.** Show the exact copy moved to Dutch servers; Hansa continues to look and function as before. Visitors do not notice the transfer of control. The unchanged surface carries the reveal.

41–53 sec — **July 4: AlphaBay closes.** Put the date and event on the timeline. The earlier two events now connect: one market is shut while the other, already controlled by police, remains open. Question: how large will the traffic shift be?

53–65 sec — **The traffic surges.** Show the Dutch prosecutor’s daily visitor figures rising from about 1,000 to 8,000. Do not claim every new visitor came from AlphaBay; the reported figure is total Hansa traffic.

65–78 sec — **The operation produces records.** More than 50,000 transactions were recorded and about 10,000 foreign addresses were passed to Europol. Use the numbers to show the operation’s documented scale; never turn addresses into identified people or arrests. Prefer voiceover on one number and a readable on-screen card for the other if pacing requires.

78–90 sec — **July 20: Hansa closes and the operation is revealed.** End on the timeline joining takeover, AlphaBay closure, traffic surge, and public disclosure/closure. Payoff: when AlphaBay vanished, Hansa was already being run by police. Avoid a generic moral about darknet safety.

## Main question and promise

Main question: “How did police end up running the market people turned to after AlphaBay disappeared?”

Viewer promise: “AlphaBay vanished. Thousands moved to Hansa. What they couldn’t see was that police already controlled it.”

## Format assessment and limits

The outline can support an English Short; 90 seconds is the upper edge. Extra High estimated 165–185 words including visual pauses. If narration needs a Tor explainer, broad darknet context, or general discussion of anonymity, cut to 70–80 seconds rather than pad. The 90-second length is an editorial estimate, not performance evidence.

Avoid: implying Tor was hacked; treating all darknet users as criminals; invented visitor feelings/dialogue; asserting all 8,000 visitors migrated from AlphaBay; interpreting addresses as identities or arrests; overexplaining technical access or ending with a general safety moral. The initial visual can reveal the premise early, but the following beats must explain the chronology rather than repeat the same events.

> On July 4, 2017, one online market shut down. Another kept running. The market that stayed open was already under police control.
>
> Rewind two weeks. AlphaBay and Hansa were two separate markets in a hidden part of the internet—sites you couldn’t open like an ordinary webpage.
>
> On June 20, after Hansa’s administrators were arrested, Dutch police took over the market. They copied it to servers in the Netherlands and kept it running. To visitors, nothing seemed to change.
>
> Then authorities shut down AlphaBay in an internationally coordinated operation. Hansa’s daily visitors surged—from about one thousand to eight thousand.
>
> The police had kept Hansa open long enough to record more than fifty thousand transactions.
>
> On July 20, Hansa closed. Only then did visitors see the truth: the market that had stayed open after AlphaBay disappeared had been under police control all along.
