## Coordinator's independent source check

Allen defines the welfare ratio as average annual earnings divided by the cost of a poverty-line consumption bundle. He explicitly uses 250 workdays, a notional man/woman/two-child family, and a 5% minimal rent allowance; he also states that household size, earners, and workdays are assumptions that could vary. In the reported trend, London laborers were below the modeled poverty line before the Black Death, while fifteenth-century real earnings were generally above it. London's modeled laborer ratio was 1.42 in 1500–1549, fell to 1.16 in 1600–1649, and stood at 1.41 in 1800–1849 before rising later. These are model estimates, not observed household accounts.
