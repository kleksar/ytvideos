001 The Great Emu War (1932)
002 November fourth, 1932.
003 In Western Australia,
004 soldiers wait beside a dam
005 with machine guns.
006 Ahead of them,
007 around a thousand emus
008 are approaching the water.
009 The birds have finally
010 gathered in one place.
011 The soldiers let them come
012 closer, then open fire.
013 A few emus fall.
014 Then a machine gun jams.
015 The rest scatter
016 across the open land.
017 It was one of their best
018 chances to stop a whole flock.
019 Almost every bird escaped.
020 How did Australia
021 end up sending machine
022 guns against emus?
023 To understand that,
024 go back two days.
025 On November second,
026 a small army unit
027 arrives in the wheat-growing
028 district near Campion.
029 The men have two machine
030 guns and orders to help local
031 farmers get rid of the birds.
032 Those farmers
033 have been fighting
034 to keep their farms alive.
035 After the First World
036 War, many former
037 soldiers had settled on land
038 in Western Australia.
039 Some of that land was hard
040 to farm even in good years.
041 In parts of the district,
042 settlers had already abandoned
043 their farms.
044 Then the Great Depression
045 struck.
046 Wheat prices collapsed.
047 Drought made
048 everything worse.
049 For the families who stayed,
050 the harvest
051 was their livelihood.
052 A lost field of wheat
053 could mean a year of work
054 with nothing to show for it.
055 And now
056 the emus were arriving.
057 They were huge,
058 flightless birds, nearly as tall
059 as a person, with powerful legs
060 built for running.
061 Thousands were moving
062 through the farming districts
063 looking for food and water.
064 To the birds, the wheat
065 fields were an easy meal.
066 To the farmers,
067 they were crops
068 that had taken
069 months of work to grow.
070 The emus ate
071 the grain and trampled what
072 they didn't eat.
073 They damaged fences
074 as they moved through, leaving
075 openings for rabbits to enter
076 and do even more harm.
077 The farmers couldn't
078 keep them out.
079 So they turned
080 to the government for help.
081 The Defence Minister
082 agreed to send
083 soldiers with machine guns.
084 A cameraman came
085 along to record the operation.
086 On paper,
087 the plan seemed simple.
088 Find a large flock.
089 Bring the guns within range.
090 Open fire.
091 But the soldiers
092 discovered the problem
093 almost as soon as they arrived.
094 On their first day,
095 they spotted a group of emus.
096 Farmers tried to herd the birds
097 toward the waiting guns.
098 Instead, the flock broke apart.
099 Some birds ran one way.
100 Others ran another.
101 By the time
102 the soldiers could shoot,
103 there were only small groups
104 moving across the fields.
105 Only a few were killed.
106 The soldiers had weapons made
107 to deliver sustained fire.
108 But that meant
109 little when the targets
110 kept scattering.
111 And then came
112 the opportunity at the dam.
113 Around a thousand emus
114 were gathered near the water.
115 This time the soldiers waited
116 until they were close
117 before firing.
118 But after roughly a dozen birds
119 were killed, a gun jammed.
120 The flock escaped.
121 The men had found the large
122 target they needed.
123 And lost it.
124 So they tried
125 something different.
126 They put a machine
127 gun on the back of a truck.
128 If they couldn't get the birds
129 to come to the guns,
130 they could take the gun
131 after the birds.
132 There was just one problem.
133 Emus can run fast.
134 The truck couldn't
135 keep up over the rough ground.
136 It jolted
137 so badly that the gunner
138 struggled to aim.
139 A machine
140 gun was now bouncing
141 across the countryside, chasing
142 birds it could barely reach.
143 The soldiers kept trying.
144 But each encounter
145 ended much the same way:
146 a burst of gunfire, birds
147 scattering, and more emus
148 disappearing over the fields.
149 Their commander, Major
150 Meredith, compared the birds'
151 ability to withstand gunfire
152 to that of tanks.
153 Back in Canberra,
154 the operation was attracting
155 a different kind of attention.
156 Politicians questioned
157 the campaign.
158 One joked that if anyone
159 deserved medals,
160 it was the emus.
161 A few days after the failed
162 ambush, the government
163 recalled the soldiers.
164 In Canberra,
165 the shooting was over.
166 In the farming
167 district, the birds
168 were still eating wheat.
169 The soldiers had gone,
170 but the damage hadn't stopped.
171 Fences were still being broken.
172 Crops were still at risk.
173 The birds were still moving
174 through the district.
175 The farmers
176 asked for help again.
177 And the government agreed
178 to another attempt.
179 So the soldiers
180 returned with their guns.
181 This time,
182 they killed more emus.
183 But the work
184 dragged on for weeks.
185 The birds were spread
186 across too much country,
187 and the soldiers had to find
188 them before they could fire.
189 Even when the guns
190 were successful,
191 another group of emus
192 could turn up somewhere else.
193 By December, hundreds
194 of birds had been killed.
195 Even so, thousands
196 remained in the district.
197 The shooting hadn't
198 stopped the damage.
199 The army ended its campaign.
200 For the farmers,
201 there was no dramatic finish.
202 They still needed ways
203 to protect their fields.
204 In the years that followed,
205 people continued killing
206 emus for bounty payments,
207 while fences remained
208 important for protecting crops.
209 For the birds,
210 the campaign had been deadly.
211 Many had been shot.
212 But thousands remained
213 across the region.
214 Australia had sent soldiers
215 into farmland with machine
216 guns to stop the birds
217 from destroying the wheat.
218 The soldiers had fired, chased,
219 withdrawn, and returned.
220 The farmers
221 still needed protection.
222 The emus had suffered
223 heavy losses.
224 Yet across the wheat-growing
225 district, the birds continued
226 searching for food and water.
227 The soldiers were gone.
228 And the emus kept coming.
