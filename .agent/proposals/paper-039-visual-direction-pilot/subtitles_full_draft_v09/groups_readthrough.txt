001 The Great Emu War (1932)
002 November fourth, 1932.
003 In Western Australia,
004 soldiers wait beside a dam
005 with machine guns.
006 Ahead of them,
007 around a thousand emus
008 are approaching the water.
009 The birds have finally
010 gathered in one place.
011 The soldiers let them come
012 closer, then open fire.
013 A few emus fall.
014 Then a machine gun jams.
015 The rest scatter
016 across the open land.
017 It was one of their best
018 chances to stop a whole flock.
019 Almost every bird escaped.
020 How did Australia
021 end up sending
022 machine guns against emus?
023 To understand that,
024 go back two days.
025 On November second,
026 a small army unit
027 arrives
028 in the wheat-growing district
029 near Campion.
030 The men have two machine guns
031 and orders to help local
032 farmers get rid of the birds.
033 Those farmers
034 have been fighting
035 to keep their farms alive.
036 After the First World War,
037 many former soldiers
038 had settled on land
039 in Western Australia.
040 Some of that land was hard
041 to farm even in good years.
042 In parts of the district,
043 settlers had already abandoned
044 their farms.
045 Then the Great Depression
046 struck.
047 Wheat prices collapsed.
048 Drought made
049 everything worse.
050 For the families who stayed,
051 the harvest
052 was their livelihood.
053 A lost field of wheat
054 could mean a year of work
055 with nothing to show for it.
056 And now
057 the emus were arriving.
058 They were huge,
059 flightless birds, nearly as tall
060 as a person, with powerful legs
061 built for running.
062 Thousands were moving
063 through the farming districts
064 looking for food and water.
065 To the birds,
066 the wheat fields
067 were an easy meal.
068 To the farmers,
069 they were crops
070 that had taken
071 months of work to grow.
072 The emus ate
073 the grain and trampled what
074 they didn't eat.
075 They damaged fences
076 as they moved through, leaving
077 openings for rabbits to enter
078 and do even more harm.
079 The farmers couldn't
080 keep them out.
081 So they turned
082 to the government for help.
083 The Defence Minister
084 agreed to send
085 soldiers with machine guns.
086 A cameraman came
087 along to record the operation.
088 On paper,
089 the plan seemed simple.
090 Find a large flock.
091 Bring the guns within range.
092 Open fire.
093 But the soldiers
094 discovered the problem
095 almost as soon as they arrived.
096 On their first day,
097 they spotted a group of emus.
098 Farmers tried to herd the birds
099 toward the waiting guns.
100 Instead, the flock broke apart.
101 Some birds ran one way.
102 Others ran another.
103 By the time
104 the soldiers could shoot,
105 there were only small groups
106 moving across the fields.
107 Only a few were killed.
108 The soldiers had weapons made
109 to deliver sustained fire.
110 But that meant
111 little when the targets
112 kept scattering.
113 And then came
114 the opportunity at the dam.
115 Around a thousand emus
116 were gathered near the water.
117 This time the soldiers waited
118 until they were close
119 before firing.
120 But after roughly a dozen birds
121 were killed, a gun jammed.
122 The flock escaped.
123 The men had found the large
124 target they needed.
125 And lost it.
126 So they tried
127 something different.
128 They put a machine gun
129 on the back of a truck.
130 If they couldn't get the birds
131 to come to the guns,
132 they could take the gun
133 after the birds.
134 There was just one problem.
135 Emus can run fast.
136 The truck couldn't
137 keep up over the rough ground.
138 It jolted
139 so badly that the gunner
140 struggled to aim.
141 A machine gun
142 was now bouncing
143 across the countryside, chasing
144 birds it could barely reach.
145 The soldiers kept trying.
146 But each encounter
147 ended much the same way:
148 a burst of gunfire, birds
149 scattering, and more emus
150 disappearing over the fields.
151 Their commander,
152 Major Meredith, compared
153 the birds' ability to withstand
154 gunfire to that of tanks.
155 Back in Canberra,
156 the operation was attracting
157 a different kind of attention.
158 Politicians questioned
159 the campaign.
160 One joked that if anyone
161 deserved medals,
162 it was the emus.
163 A few days after the failed
164 ambush, the government
165 recalled the soldiers.
166 In Canberra,
167 the shooting was over.
168 In the farming
169 district, the birds
170 were still eating wheat.
171 The soldiers had gone,
172 but the damage hadn't stopped.
173 Fences were still being broken.
174 Crops were still at risk.
175 The birds were still moving
176 through the district.
177 The farmers
178 asked for help again.
179 And the government agreed
180 to another attempt.
181 So the soldiers
182 returned with their guns.
183 This time,
184 they killed more emus.
185 But the work
186 dragged on for weeks.
187 The birds were spread
188 across too much country,
189 and the soldiers had to find
190 them before they could fire.
191 Even when the guns
192 were successful,
193 another group of emus
194 could turn up somewhere else.
195 By December, hundreds
196 of birds had been killed.
197 Even so, thousands
198 remained in the district.
199 The shooting hadn't
200 stopped the damage.
201 The army ended its campaign.
202 For the farmers,
203 there was no dramatic finish.
204 They still needed ways
205 to protect their fields.
206 In the years that followed,
207 people continued killing
208 emus for bounty payments,
209 while fences remained
210 important for protecting crops.
211 For the birds,
212 the campaign had been deadly.
213 Many had been shot.
214 But thousands remained
215 across the region.
216 Australia had sent
217 soldiers into farmland
218 with machine guns
219 to stop the birds
220 from destroying the wheat.
221 The soldiers had fired, chased,
222 withdrawn, and returned.
223 The farmers
224 still needed protection.
225 The emus had suffered
226 heavy losses.
227 Yet across the wheat-growing
228 district, the birds continued
229 searching for food and water.
230 The soldiers were gone.
231 And the emus kept coming.
