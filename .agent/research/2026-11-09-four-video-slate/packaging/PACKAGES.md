## Paper · Neptune

### A · The Planet Found With Math #Neptune #SpaceHistory

Uranus was not quite where astronomers expected it to be. Could another planet be pulling it off course?

In 1846, Urbain Le Verrier calculated where to look. At the Berlin Observatory, Johann Galle and Heinrich d’Arrest compared the sky with a star chart—and found a point of light missing from the chart. The next night it had moved: Neptune had been found.

## Nuviko · Harrison

### A · How a Clock Helped Sailors Find Their Way #JohnHarrison #Longitude

How could a clock help a ship find its position in the middle of the ocean? Sailors could compare local noon with the time back home to work out how far east or west they had travelled—but only if their clock stayed accurate at sea.

English clockmaker John Harrison spent years tackling the problem. In 1761, his son took Harrison’s H4 timekeeper on a voyage to Jamaica, putting the watch to the test.
