## Three competing viewer-facing applications

### Finalist A: British Airways Flight 5390 (selected)
- Audience: general English-speaking history/survival Short viewers; no knowledge of flight models required.
- Stop scrolling: physically impossible-looking captain outside a flying airliner; first exact line: "The captain was hanging outside a passenger jet." / first shot S001.
- Stay: attendant grabs him → hatch/door chaos → copilot controls descent → rescuers cannot pull him back → steward weakens → handoff → landing.
- Payoff: all aboard survive; official investigation finds 84 undersized and 6 too-short bolts installed 27 h before flight.
- Objection: spectacular image risks fake-documentary visual or retelling the same fact without progress; visuals must clearly be reconstruction and script avoids speculative emotions.

### Finalist B: Vincent Coleman at Halifax (1917)
- Audience: English-speaking viewers drawn to personal choices amid historical disasters.
- First exact possible line: "A dispatcher was warned to run from a ship full of explosives. He went back to his telegraph."
- Stop: choice to return to a station when others flee.
- Stay: collision → warning from sailor → danger to arriving passenger train → brief final transmission → explosion.
- Payoff: trains approaching Halifax were halted; Coleman died, and the museum preserves his damaged watch/key.
- Objection: 1917 disaster needs care; assertions that he alone saved exactly 300 people overstate what primary source proves.

### Finalist C: Alcatraz escape (1962)
- Audience: English-speaking escape/mystery viewers.
- First exact possible line: "Three men vanished from America's most famous prison, leaving heads in their beds."
- Stop: dummy heads instead of sleeping inmates.
- Stay: homemade tools → unseen shaft → rooftop exit → raincoat raft → river/tide search.
- Payoff: the FBI closed its case in 1979 without confirming the men's fate; escape does not equal survival.
- Objection: central survival question remains unanswered; famous film/documentary competition is high and a fabricated answer would be dishonest.

## Choice vs strongest alternative

The Halifax story may offer a more emotional ending. Flight 5390 offers the more immediate and comprehensible cold frame, multiple truly distinct turns (first grip, failed attempt, handoff, landing), a human payoff, then a mechanical explanation without claiming motives. Its biggest remaining weakness is familiarity from documentaries and an extreme image that AI might misrender. We choose it *despite* those limitations, not because aircraft are easy to depict. Strong competitor B remains viable for a later independent review; do not publish from this note.

One-sentence value: **After an aircraft windscreen failed, a crew kept its captain from falling out until landing, and the inquiry found incorrectly sized bolts installed only 27 hours earlier.** The full script must add actual changed stakes beyond that sentence, not drag one disclosed fact.
