The captain was hanging outside a passenger jet.
The copilot was still flying.
On June tenth, nineteen ninety, British Airways Flight fifty-three ninety was climbing over England.
At around seventeen thousand feet, the left cockpit windscreen blew out.
Air pressure pulled Captain Tim Lancaster through the opening, leaving his head and torso outside.
A flight attendant rushed in and caught him around the waist.
The cockpit door had blown onto the radio console, and roaring air filled the flight deck.
The copilot regained control and started an emergency descent.
The crew tried to pull the captain inside. The airflow made that impossible.
The first steward's arms were weakening from cold and bruises.
Another took over, strapped himself into a jump seat and gripped both ankles.
Meanwhile, the copilot headed toward Southampton Airport.
He landed. Only then could rescuers pull Lancaster back inside.
The captain had broken bones and frostbite. But he was alive, and nobody aboard had died.
Investigators discovered that the windscreen had been replaced twenty-seven hours earlier.
Eighty-four bolts were too narrow. The other six were too short.
