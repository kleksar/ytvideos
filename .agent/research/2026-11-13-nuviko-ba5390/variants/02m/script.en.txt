The captain was hanging outside a passenger jet. His first officer was still flying it.

On June tenth, nineteen ninety, British Airways Flight fifty-three ninety was climbing out of Birmingham. At about seventeen thousand feet, the left cockpit windscreen came out of its frame. Both pilots had released their shoulder harnesses after takeoff. Captain Tim Lancaster was forced through the opening, with his upper body outside.

Cabin attendant Nigel Ogden rushed into the cockpit and grabbed Lancaster around the waist. The cockpit door had fallen onto the radio console. In the roaring airflow, first officer Alastair Atchison regained control and began an emergency descent.

Ogden and another attendant tried to pull Lancaster inside. They couldn't overcome the wind. Ogden was bruised, cold and losing strength. A colleague took over, secured himself in a jump seat and gripped both of Lancaster's ankles. The aircraft still had to reach a runway.

Atchison diverted to Southampton. Twenty-two minutes after the window failed, he landed. Airport rescuers finally brought the captain back inside. Lancaster had fractures and frostbite, but he survived. So did everyone else aboard.

Investigators found that the windscreen had been replaced only twenty-seven hours earlier. Eighty-four fastening bolts were too narrow; the other six were too short. The window was fitted from outside, where cabin pressure pushed against those bolts. The mechanic had matched replacements to an old bolt that was already the wrong size. There had been no independent final check.

The mistake had been made on the ground, before the aircraft's first flight after the repair. Then, in the climb, the windscreen came out.
