In 1629, two boats raced for the same rescue ship.
One carried men who meant to seize it.
Three months earlier, the Dutch trading ship Batavia had struck a reef off Western Australia.
Its commander sailed away to find help, leaving hundreds of survivors on tiny islands.
A merchant named Jeronimus Cornelisz took control of the supplies and weapons.
His followers began murdering other survivors.
He sent twenty-two soldiers to a distant island, took their weapons, and left them there.
But the soldiers found fresh water and food.
When escapees told them about the killings, they built defenses from coral and wreckage.
Led by Wiebbe Hayes, they fought off repeated attacks and finally captured Cornelisz.
The remaining mutineers attacked again, this time carrying muskets.
Then a sail appeared on the horizon. The rescue ship was coming back.
Both sides launched boats. The mutineers wanted to tell their version first.
Hayes reached the commander before them and warned him about the plan to seize his ship.
The mutineers were arrested, and the surviving castaways were brought aboard.
