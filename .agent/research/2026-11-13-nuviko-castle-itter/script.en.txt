In May 1945, a German officer asked American tankers to fight alongside him.

The war in Europe was nearly over. But SS troops threatened a castle in Austria.

Inside were French political prisoners, including former prime ministers. They feared being killed before rescue arrived.

The German officer, Josef Gangl, was helping local resistance fighters. He refused to abandon the prisoners.

An American lieutenant, Jack Lee, agreed to help. His rescue column headed for the castle.

But weak bridges and roadblocks kept most of his vehicles from reaching it.

By night, one Sherman tank and a small group of Americans and Germans guarded the gate.

That morning, SS troops emerged from the woods and surrounded the castle.

The Sherman fired back. Heavy guns then struck the tank repeatedly, knocking it out of action.

Even the French prisoners picked up rifles. The defenders fought men wearing Germany's own uniforms.

A former French tennis champion slipped through the fighting to help bring reinforcements.

The defenders were running low on ammunition. Gangl was killed trying to protect a prisoner.

Then American reinforcements arrived, and the SS attack collapsed. The French prisoners were free.

Gangl never saw the end of the battle.
