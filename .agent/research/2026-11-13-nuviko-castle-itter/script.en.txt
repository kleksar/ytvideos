A German officer asked American tank crews to help him fight the SS.

It was May 1945. The war in Europe was almost over, but French prisoners were still held in a castle in Austria.

Some had been prime ministers. SS troops were threatening the castle.

The officer, Josef Gangl, was working with local resistance fighters.

An American lieutenant, Jack Lee, agreed to help and took a rescue column toward the castle.

Weak bridges and roadblocks stopped most of the vehicles from getting through.

By night, one Sherman tank and a handful of Americans and Germans were at the gate.

The next morning, SS troops came out of the woods and surrounded them.

The Sherman fired back until heavy guns knocked it out.

The French prisoners picked up rifles too.

A former French tennis champion made his way out through the fighting to help bring reinforcements.

Ammunition was running low. Gangl was killed while trying to protect a prisoner.

Then American reinforcements reached the castle. The SS attack ended, and the French prisoners were free.

What had brought American and German soldiers to the same gate?
