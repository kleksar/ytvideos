A German major asked American tank crews to fight beside him against the SS.

It was May 1945. Germany was losing the war, but French political prisoners were still held at Castle Itter in Austria. Two had served as prime minister. The prisoners feared SS troops would reach them before the Americans.

The German officer was Josef Gangl. He had joined forces with local Austrian resistance fighters and went to the Americans for help.

Lieutenant Jack Lee agreed. He went to the castle, then gathered a rescue force. The roads and bridges could not carry his whole column. Only one Sherman tank and a small group of American and German soldiers reached the castle.

Lee found German guards preparing to leave. He put them back on the defenses, with Americans beside them and the tank blocking the gate.

The next morning SS troops moved through the trees. Their guns struck the tank, then the castle. Some French prisoners took up weapons too.

With ammunition running low, tennis champion Jean Borotra made his way through the fighting to help bring reinforcements. Gangl was killed while trying to protect a prisoner.

American troops finally reached the castle. The attack ended. The French prisoners survived, but the officer who had sought help did not.

Before these soldiers could defend the same gate, one of them had to make the first move.
