In April 1862, a train conductor tried to catch a stolen steam locomotive on foot.

William Fuller was working on a Confederate train in Georgia. When it stopped for breakfast, Union raiders took the locomotive, the General, and left the passenger cars behind.

The station had no telegraph office. Fuller and other railroad men ran along the tracks. They found a handcar, then got hold of a small locomotive.

The raiders were trying to disrupt the Confederate railway. But stopping to damage the line cost them time. They also had to wait for ordinary trains coming the other way.

When damaged track blocked Fuller's engine, the men went on foot again and reached another one. The Texas was facing the wrong way, so they drove it backwards.

The raiders tried to set fire to a bridge, but rain had soaked the wood. The bridge survived. The General needed wood and water, and by now Fuller was so close that the raiders had little time to stop.

After roughly seven hours, the General ran out of steam. The raiders abandoned it and scattered. All were captured; eight were executed. The railway was soon repaired.

Six of the survivors became the first soldiers to receive the Medal of Honor in 1863.

The General itself survived the war. You can see it in a museum in Kennesaw, near the breakfast stop. Together with its tender, it weighs about fifty thousand pounds. Standing beside that enormous machine, the hardest part of the story to believe is how the chase began.
