A train conductor stepped out from breakfast and saw his steam engine leaving without him. He ran after it.

Georgia, April 1862. A group of Union raiders had stolen a Confederate locomotive called the General.

Their plan was to head north, cut telegraph wires, pull up tracks, and burn bridges behind them.

Conductor William Fuller started on foot. He found a handcar, then managed to get another locomotive.

Ahead of him, the raiders damaged track and dropped empty freight cars behind them to slow the chase.

At a river, they tried setting a wooden bridge on fire. The wood was soaked from rain and wouldn't burn.

Fuller kept closing in. The raiders could barely stop for the wood and water their engine needed.

Then Fuller took over another locomotive, the Texas. It was facing the wrong way, so he chased them backward, with its tender out in front.

The pursuit went on for seven hours, over nearly ninety miles of track.

Near Ringgold, the General ran out of steam. The raiders jumped off and scattered.

They were all caught. Eight were later executed as spies.

The railroad was quickly repaired. The raid had failed to cause lasting damage.

The General, though, survived the war. You can still see it in a museum in Kennesaw, Georgia.

It's only a few hundred yards from the railway stop where the crew had gone to eat breakfast.
