## Three competing short pitches, at comparable decision depth

### A — Ghost Army / Operation Viersen (selected)
- **Audience:** English-speaking casual historical-puzzle, deception and “how did that work?” Short viewers, without assumed military knowledge.
- **Exact opening:** “Rows of American tanks stood near the Rhine. Up close, they were made of rubber.”
- **Reason to stop:** an army tank turns out to be a fabric illusion in a real WWII operation, not a fiction trope.
- **Reason to keep watching:** two divisions secretly leave → a 1,100-person unit impersonates over 30,000 → fake vehicles by day → loudspeakers and radio by ear → real engineer movements → troops actually cross elsewhere → captured German map indicates wrong division positions.
- **Payoff:** March 23 crossing encountered little resistance; the 79th was marked approximately at the false position while the 30th was absent from the captured German order of battle. The 23rd's contribution was **one part of Ninth Army's wider deception**, not the sole proven cause of resistance levels.
- **Strongest objection:** a fake-tank premise can be exhausted immediately; 60–90 seconds of trick inventory would be weak. The independent documentary event *after* the fake tanks—the captured map—must function as the closing change, not another adjective about cleverness.
- **One-sentence reduction:** a small deception unit made two removed divisions appear to remain by the Rhine, and the later captured German map placed one where the decoys stood.

### B — Demon Core / Louis Slotin
- **Audience:** English-speaking science history and risky-lab-procedure viewers.
- **Possible exact opening:** “A scientist died after an experiment with a plutonium core. Months later, a second scientist used the same core.”
- **Stop:** a real laboratory object involved in two fatal incidents.
- **Development:** first experiment and exposure → later separate demonstration with reflector shells → screwdriver slip and lethal radiation → safety implications.
- **Payoff:** the two scientists died following separate accidents involving the core; the technical mechanism matters, not a supernatural curse.
- **Strongest objection:** avoid implying a single repeated mistake or speculative exact exposure timeline. The physicist's danger is strong, but responsible explanation demands more time and verification than a short hook suggests.
- **Opened evidence:** American Physical Society historical article, 2014-05-21: https://www.aps.org/apsnews/2014/05/slotin (accessed 2026-11-13).

### C — Vincent Coleman / Halifax
- **Audience:** English-speaking historic human-choice and disaster viewers.
- **Possible exact opening:** “A railway dispatcher was warned to run from a burning ammunition ship. He went back to his telegraph.”
- **Stop:** a particular choice under immediate threat, rather than only a disaster statistic.
- **Development:** collision and warning → inbound passenger traffic → telegram to railway stations → blast → consequences for trains.
- **Payoff:** his signal warned and halted multiple approaching trains; Coleman died in the explosion. Whether he specifically stopped Passenger Train No. 10 remains disputed.
- **Strongest objection:** false “he saved 300 people on Train No. 10” promise would be a material error; it also appeared as the strongest alternative in existing #34 research.
- **Opened evidence:** Maritime Museum of the Atlantic: https://maritimemuseum.novascotia.ca/stories/vincent-coleman-and-halifax-explosion (accessed 2026-11-13; paragraphs “Results”).

**Winner versus closest competitor C:** Coleman has a stronger personal loss but the expected simple headline (“one telegram saved exactly 300”) cannot be stated as established. Ghost Army has a surprising practical mechanism, multiple new actions, and a later captured-map evidence payoff. Its disadvantage is a less emotionally intense ending and more familiar “rubber tanks” subject. Demon Core is also strong but less accessible without explaining nuclear criticality. None has measured audience-retention evidence. Choosing A is an editorial hypothesis, NOT independent reviewer acceptance.
