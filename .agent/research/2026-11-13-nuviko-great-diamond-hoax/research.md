## Three verified finalist pitches

### A. Diamond Hoax, 1872 — **selected**

- **Audience:** English-speaking curious history/true-fraud/clever-detection Shorts viewers, no need to know American mining history.
- **Exact opening:** "Two prospectors carried a bag of real diamonds into a San Francisco office. The mine they were selling didn't exist."
- **Why stop:** immediate real-evidence / non-existent-mine contradiction, a more surprising problem than an ordinary fake-gem trick.
- **What changes after hook:** secret location → Tiffany's expensive sample appraisal → planted real gems at the actual site → Janin endorses it → geologist King notices abnormal gem patterns and disturbed anthills → a deep trench finds no native deposit.
- **Payoff:** the gem authenticity was never evidence of their geological origin; investor company dissolved after King's investigation.
- **Strongest objection:** unfamiliar 1870s names and no sympathetic protagonist; must keep Arnold/Janin/King roles clear, concise and visually distinct. Intro already states mine fake, so middle must teach the *physical trick and its detection*, not hide the verdict.
- **Primary evidence:** retrospective participant memoir and Emmons field notes as reproduced by Smithsonian; further independent EARTH account.

### B. Leonid Rogozov, 1961 — strongest alternative

- **Audience:** science/survival/remote-expedition curiosity viewers.
- **Exact opening proposal:** "The only doctor at an Antarctic station picked up a scalpel and began operating on his own abdomen."
- **Why stop:** strong instantly understandable physical situation and no other doctor.
- **What changes:** symptoms worsen, no evacuation, team prepares improvised surgical support, he performs operation with local anesthetic, survives and recovers.
- **Payoff:** the operation succeeded under confinement and with non-medical assistants.
- **Strongest objection:** the central answer is essentially contained in the opening, graphic procedure risks a narrow audience, and near-certain viewer expectations leave less of a new detective/reversal pay-off; also Nuviko queue already includes several survival rescues.
- **Evidence opened:** Vladislav Rogozov & Neil Bermel (2009), https://www.bmj.com/content/339/bmj.b4965 ; Vladislav Rogozov (2004), https://vesmir.cz/cz/casopis/archiv-casopisu/2004/cislo-1/operace-vlastniho-apendixu-antarktide.html . Contemporary re-telling https://www.medicaldaily.com/leonid-rogozov-self-appendectomy-antarctica-481062 published 2026-11-10, but details/duration conflict with other retrospectives: NOT used in a candidate script.

### C. Great Moon Hoax, 1835

- **Audience:** newspaper/media-history and science-mystery viewers.
- **Exact opening proposal:** "In 1835, a New York newspaper told its readers that astronomers had found bat-winged people on the Moon."
- **Why stop:** bizarre historical visual claim and recognizable media failure.
- **What changes:** six staged articles cite a fictitious correspondent/defunct journal; increasingly fantastic moon animals; even researchers are sent in circles looking for the source.
- **Payoff:** a fabricated newspaper sensation posing as scientific news, not an actual lunar discovery.
- **Strongest objection:** for a modern viewer the fraud may be obvious immediately; six articles and creature descriptions can become a list rather than a consequential mini-story.
- **Evidence opened:** HISTORY https://www.history.com/this-day-in-history/august-25/the-great-moon-hoax (published 2009-11-24, updated 2025-05-27). Associated public video: https://www.history.com/videos/this-day-in-history-08251835-the-great-moon-hoax.

### Why A beats B

Rogozov has the stronger single-frame bodily shock. Diamond Hoax has the stronger **sequence after the first sentence**: expert appraisal makes the trap more plausible; an on-site inspection should defeat the trick but instead reinforces it; the geologist then finds the ground itself contradicts the jewels, with observable ant-mound evidence and the deep trench. Unlike a rescue or technical mishap already waiting in the Nuviko backlog, it is an evidence-versus-provenance detective scam. Risk: confusing three named men. Remove lesser figures from narration.
