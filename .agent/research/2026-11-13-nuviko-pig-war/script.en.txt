In 1859, an American farmer shot a pig in his potato patch. Weeks later, British warships were facing American soldiers. This wasn't really about the pig. The garden stood on San Juan Island, which both countries claimed. Their border treaty mentioned "the channel." But two different channels could fit that description. The pig belonged to a British trading company. When British authorities threatened to arrest the farmer, the American settlers asked their army for protection. Sixty-four US soldiers landed on the island. Britain sent a warship. Then two more. Neither side backed down. American reinforcements brought cannons. British sailors practiced firing their guns at the shoreline. What began in a potato patch now threatened to become a war. Washington sent a senior general to stop the escalation. Instead of fighting, the two countries agreed to share the island under a temporary military occupation. Temporary lasted twelve years. Finally, in 1872, a ruling issued by the German emperor awarded the island to the United States. No one on either side died in the standoff. The only casualty was the pig.
