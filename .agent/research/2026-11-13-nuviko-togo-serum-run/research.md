## Audience/value decision before script

Audience: broad English-speaking viewer who likes animals, real teamwork and survival, not a specialist on mushing or Alaskan geography. Film's one-sentence value: **the relay that saved Nome depended on Togo's long, dangerous round trip and an almost-missed exchange, although Balto became famous for the final stage.** This sentence alone does not explain why the changing relay almost caused the meeting to fail, how medicine got back over sea ice, and why public credit settled elsewhere.

- **Stop:** realistic image of one lead dog and its team on precarious sea ice, and a plain human stakes opener (medicine for sick children).
- **Stay:** icebound town → 20-team relay → Seppala departs in opposite direction to intercept → planners add unannounced teams → near-missed exchange → reversal with life-saving cargo → ice crossing repeated → different crew reaches finish → recognition mismatch.
- **Receive:** antitoxin actually reached Nome within six days; both dog teams deserve recognition; Togo's especially difficult distance was largely eclipsed by fame given to Balto.
- **Hardest objection:** Togo vs Balto is a known story and a 2019 Disney film exists; "the real hero" clickbait risks doing injustice to other dog teams, especially Native Alaskan mushers. Solution: straightforward shared-rescue narrative, not a fabricated rivalry, with independently checked action/ending.

## Three comparable fully sourced pitches before narration

**A — Togo / Nome serum relay (chosen).**
- **Audience:** animal lovers and broad history viewers, especially someone familiar with Balto but not the relay.
- **Stop / exact opener:** `An experienced sled dog crossed dangerous sea ice to help deliver medicine to sick children.`
- **After hook:** port/flying unavailable → 20-team relay → Seppala goes east to meet westbound cargo → extra teams inserted without notice → almost misses Ivanoff → reverses with serum across Norton Sound → different final team makes Nome → why the statue honours Balto.
- **Payoff:** aid is delivered; the public credits the last relay team, but Togo led a much longer/harder portion. Not a claim Balto stole anything or was unworthy.
- **Main objection:** presence of a 2019 feature film; sentimental and known reveal; opening must emphasize an actual first-event hazard and middle must deliver the changed route, not just name comparison.

**B — Operation Paul Bunyan (strongest structural alternative).**
- **Audience:** English history viewers attracted to extreme close-call geopolitical escalation.
- **Stop / exact opener:** `Two officers were killed over a tree. Three days later, American bombers were in the sky while soldiers cut it down.`
- **After hook:** routine pruning and killings in August 1976 → Washington considers military response and goes to DEFCON 3 → large US show of force including B-52 exercises and Navy presence → armed engineers fell the tree on Aug 21 without fighting.
- **Payoff:** the tree was removed during overwhelming military preparation, but a wider clash did not follow during the operation. Explicitly avoid claiming the B-52s carried nuclear arms or that a war was definitely one move away.
- **Main objection:** violent beginning and routine military deterrence may be less emotionally distinct from several pending Nuviko WWII/naval episodes; whether policy decisions were motivated by *one tree* is an oversimplification, since officers were killed.

**C — 1906 San Francisco firebreak demolition.**
- **Audience:** viewers interested in difficult rescue choices and infrastructure failure.
- **Stop / exact opener:** `San Francisco's firefighters couldn't get water. The Army began blowing up the buildings they were trying to save.`
- **After hook:** ruptured water/gas pipes fuel three days of fires → mayor's committee debates destroying buildings → army conducts dangerous hand-carried explosive demolitions around Van Ness Avenue → fire finally dies and enormous parts of the city are lost.
- **Payoff:** a hard moral dilemma, not a neat miracle: the historical value of the blasts remains contested.
- **Main objection:** NPS specifically says initial demolitions were ineffective, and historians dispute whether dynamiting ultimately prevented or added damage; any Shorts ending claiming explosives saved the city would exceed evidence. Serious risk of disappointing the huge rescue promise.

**A vs B:** B begins more explosively and includes a concrete dramatic state change, but it risks using deaths as thumbnail bait and duplicates the geopolitical/military texture of pending #17/#20/#22; A offers a different species, emotion, environment, and linked chain of logistics instead of naval/WWII stakes. A's weakness is fame of the Balto/Togo premise. **No measured retention or audience preference is available**, so this is an authorial editorial decision not an empirical ranking. B remains a strong different-format story candidate, not an interchangeable Togo backup.
