A U.S. destroyer fired a live torpedo at the battleship carrying President Roosevelt.

It was supposed to be protecting him.

In November 1943, Roosevelt was crossing the Atlantic aboard USS Iowa.

His escort, USS William D. Porter, was rehearsing a torpedo attack.

The launch should have been simulated. Instead, a real torpedo shot out.

Now they had to warn Iowa. But the ships were under radio silence.

Porter tried flashing a warning by signal lamp. The first messages were wrong.

As the torpedo approached, Porter's captain broke silence and radioed Iowa.

Iowa turned sharply. The torpedo exploded behind the battleship.

How did a drill turn real? A firing primer had been left in the tube.

The president was safe. The weapon had come from the ship assigned to defend him.

And all of it began with a practice launch.
