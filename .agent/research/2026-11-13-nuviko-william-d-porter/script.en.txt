A U.S. destroyer fired a live torpedo at the battleship carrying President Roosevelt.

It was one of the ships sent to protect him.

November 1943. Roosevelt was crossing the Atlantic on USS Iowa.

Nearby, the crew of USS William D. Porter was practising a torpedo attack.

The torpedo was supposed to stay in its tube. Instead, it shot into the water.

They needed to warn Iowa. The problem was, the ships were keeping radio silence.

Porter tried the signal lamp. The first warnings came out wrong.

So the captain broke radio silence and called Iowa.

The battleship turned sharply.

The torpedo exploded behind it. Roosevelt was safe.

A firing primer had been left in the tube. That was why the practice launch had fired a real weapon.
