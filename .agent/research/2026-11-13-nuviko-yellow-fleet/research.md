## Three competing pitches, assessed before drafting full narration

### A — Yellow Fleet (chosen)
- **Audience:** English-language history-curious people, interested in absurd consequences of geopolitical events and ingenious ordinary life.
- **Exact opening:** “Sailors were playing football on a cargo ship in the middle of a war zone.”
- **Why stop:** scene seems physically and socially contradictory, immediately visible without knowing the event or leaders.
- **Why continue:** a six-day war becomes years at anchor; crews leave/rotate; vessels from opposing blocs cooperate, organize races, an Olympics and stamps.
- **Payoff:** most vessels cannot sail away under their own power after the blockade ends, while Münsterland and Nordwind do — human improvisation contrasted with ruined ships.
- **Biggest objection:** a collection of amusing activities might become a list. Keep time escalation and damaged-vessel outcome central, and never imply the 1968 game itself was an active battle.

### B — Titan II Damascus
- **Audience:** aviation/engineering/disaster Shorts viewers, with a strong nuclear-weapon anxiety hook.
- **Exact opening proposal:** “A mechanic dropped a metal socket. Hours later, the missile underneath him exploded.”
- **Why stop:** tiny action with huge and immediate consequence.
- **Why continue:** leaking fuel, evacuation, accident response, blast, intact non-detonated nuclear warhead; complex consequential escalation.
- **Payoff:** an exploding missile is not the same as a nuclear detonation, but one airman died; careful accuracy needed.
- **Biggest objection:** fatal accident plus existing technical-survival and aircraft queue; hard to cover responsibly without misleading nuclear scare.
- **Opened source:** https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/titan-ii-missile-explosion-2543/ ; historical report https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/politics/1980/09/20/blast-kills-1-injures-21-at-missile-silo/c3470a6a-dfe0-4618-9702-4a133141011b/ ; date of historical event September 1980.

### C — Skylab in Esperance
- **Audience:** science and history viewers who enjoy bureaucratic absurdity and hard punchlines.
- **Exact opening proposal:** “An Australian town fined NASA for littering — from space.”
- **Why stop:** startling pairing of spacecraft and mundane ticket.
- **Why continue:** 1979 Skylab breakup, debris in Western Australia, $400 joke fine; radio listeners paid on NASA's behalf in 2009.
- **Payoff:** officially recorded comic fine outlives Skylab; NASA did not pay it.
- **Biggest objection:** first sentence almost answers the episode; remaining material risks delaying an obvious punchline.
- **Opened sources:** https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/114725-first-fine-for-littering-from-space ; https://www.abc.net.au/news/2020-05-31/nasa-skylab-fell-to-earth-esperance-retrofocus/12282468 .

### Selection versus strongest alternative
Titan II arguably has stronger immediate danger and action, but Yellow Fleet gives a different tonal lane from current Nuviko queue: not another near-fatal technical failure or combat rescue, but a documented improv community growing out of years of paralysis and a sharply bittersweet material finish. Yellow Fleet's risk is turning into an activity list; remove any paragraph that fails to create a new fact, constraint or consequence. Choosing Yellow Fleet is an editorial hypothesis **not** guaranteed interest.
