Sailors were playing football on a cargo ship in the middle of a war zone.

In June 1967, a convoy of merchant ships entered the Suez Canal. Fighting broke out between Egypt and Israel.

Egypt closed the canal, blocking it with sunken ships and mines. Fourteen freighters anchored in the Great Bitter Lake.

The war lasted six days. The blockade lasted much longer. Weeks of waiting turned into months.

Sand blew across the decks. The ships became known as the Yellow Fleet.

Most sailors went home. Shipping companies rotated small crews aboard to maintain the vessels.

Their countries stood on different sides of the Cold War, but here the crews shared supplies.

They formed the Great Bitter Lake Association, organizing boat races, football matches, and a sailing club.

During the 1968 Olympics, they held their own games and gave out handmade medals.

Some even made unofficial postage stamps. Meanwhile, the ships kept aging at anchor.

In 1975, the canal was cleared. Only two of the fourteen ships could leave under their own power.

Münsterland and Nordwind reached Hamburg. The others had to be towed out.
