## Three competitive short pitches

### A — 1904 marathon: WINNER
- Audience: English-speaking viewers interested in a real sporting puzzle and historic everyday absurdity, no sports expertise required.
- Exact hook: “At the 1904 Olympic marathon, Fred Lorz crossed the finish line first. Trouble was, he'd covered part of the race in a car.”
- Stop-scroll: first finish by a runner who partly used a car.
- Continue: heat and the single water stop, Fred drops out, car breaks down, he runs in, receives cheers, is disqualified; then exhausted Hicks reaches the finish with attendants and a stimulant.
- Payoff: Hicks gets gold under rules then in place, in 1908 the marathon gets a drug ban; Lorz wins Boston legitimately the following year.
- Strongest objection: event widely recounted; viewers may know the car twist. The short therefore uses the second finisher and consequential different rules rather than appending random race trivia.

### B — Crash at Crush (closest alternative)
- Audience: viewers curious about spectacular public stunts and consequences.
- Exact hook: “A railroad built a temporary town so people could watch two locomotives smash into each other.”
- Stop-scroll: a staged real head-on railway collision, an audience of tens of thousands.
- Continue: paid train excursions, deliberate collision, unexpected boiler explosion and shrapnel hitting the crowd.
- Payoff: two deaths and multiple injuries, according to the Texas historic marker.
- Strongest objection: tragic material and train content overlaps thematically with Nuviko's current train-chase PR #43. Strong visual opening but ethically much darker.

### C — Mona Lisa stolen and offered for sale
- Audience: international general-interest museum and mystery viewers.
- Exact hook: “For two years, the Louvre couldn't find the Mona Lisa. Then its thief tried to sell it.”
- Stop-scroll: well-known painting in a baffling ordinary commercial transaction.
- Continue: former museum worker Peruggia removes panel, conceals it, later approaches Florence dealer, authenticity inspection follows.
- Payoff: painting recovered, suspect arrested.
- Strongest objection: highly recognizable answer and widespread existing tellings; many viewers already know the culprit.

**Selection:** A is stronger than B for Paper because it combines several verified changes of expectation with absurdity but without using real fatalities as amusement; B's giant collision might be more visual but less suited to the current tonal variety. C's recovery is compelling but easier to know already. All three still require a fresh independent viewer verdict. It is valid to reject A after review.
