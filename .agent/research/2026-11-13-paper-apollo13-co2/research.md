## Sources examined (2026-11-13)

1. NASA, **“Houston, We've Had a Problem”** — https://www.nasa.gov/history/houston-weve-had-a-problem/
   - An oxygen-tank explosion led to using lunar module Aquarius as lifeboat.
   - Square Command Module LiOH canisters were incompatible with the Lunar Module's round canisters.
   - The team led by Ed Smylie made a workable adapter using on-board-representative materials, tested the setup; Mission Control radioed procedure.
   - Swigert and Lovell built two adapters; CO₂ dropped significantly **within minutes** after installing the first.
2. NASA, **Apollo 13: Mission Details** — https://www.nasa.gov/missions/apollo/apollo-13-mission-details/
   - Lunar module had life support designed for two for roughly two days but supported three for approximately four days.
   - After about a day and a half, dangerously high CO₂ triggered a warning; ground developed a solution with bags, cardboard and tape.
3. NASA, **Apollo Expeditions to the Moon, chapter 13.4** (Lovell's recollection) — https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/history/SP-350/ch-13-4.html
   - Confirmation of the shape mismatch and use of a hose, bags, cardboard and tape.
   - Do **not** repurpose original wording as character dialogue.
4. Space Center Houston, **How did they make that CO₂ scrubber?** — https://spacecenter.org/apollo-13-infographic-how-did-they-make-that-co2-scrubber/
   - The movie includes an analogue of the real incident. Houston could not send the astronauts pictures, so the team explained the assembly verbally.
5. NASA, **OCHMO-TB-004 Carbon Dioxide** — https://www.nasa.gov/ochmo-tb-004-carbon-dioxide-2/
   - Measured CO₂ change from 14.9 to 1 mmHg following the workaround (not narrated because the units distract).

**Scope of the facts:** The adapter solved the CO₂-removal crisis, **not** all of Apollo 13's survival problems. “Nearly suffocated” denotes the documented life-threatening exposure; nobody died or suffocated.
