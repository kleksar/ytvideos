A square filter wouldn't fit a round socket. That nearly suffocated three astronauts aboard Apollo 13.

In April 1970, an oxygen tank exploded on the way to the Moon. The command module was crippled. All three men squeezed into the tiny lunar lander to stay alive.

But the lander was built for two, not three. Carbon dioxide from their own breath kept building up.

They had spare filters in the command module. Those were square. The lander's air system used round cartridges.

Back in Houston, engineers gathered what the crew already had: cardboard, plastic bags, duct tape, and a spacesuit hose. They built a rough adapter and tested it.

It worked. But they couldn't send the astronauts a picture.

So Mission Control explained the whole thing over the radio, one step at a time. The crew cut, folded, taped, and connected the makeshift box to the air system.

Within minutes, the carbon dioxide reading began to fall.

They still had a dangerous journey home. But at least now the air was being cleaned.
