# Research — 1859 telegraph that worked without batteries

**Channel:** Paper Detour · **Format:** English Short, 9:16 · **Research date:** 2026-11-13
**Story angle:** A broken long-distance telegraph link became usable after both stations removed their ordinary power supply. A solar storm had induced current in the wire. Keep the narrative centered on the Boston–Portland exchange; do not turn it into a generic modern doomsday forecast.

## Uncertainty and excluded claims
- No first-ever claim, no exact wording for operators beyond the five-word source excerpt, no fake speech from Carrington.
- The exact architecture, hand gestures, facial expressions, clock view and background are illustrative paper reconstructions. Do not present them as recovered footage.
- Schematic auroral/magnetic ribbons explain induction; **do not** animate a literal bolt running from the Sun to the instrument.
- The opening scene uses a quote from a historical *published account*, not an authenticated verbatim recording.
- A few online summaries say the 2 Sep exchange took place at night; the near-contemporary account instead places Boston–Portland activity in the morning. Use daylight in the storyboard.
- The historic source says more than two hours. At this stage, timing of narrated scenes is only an editorial estimate, not measured TTS.
