## Audience and one-sentence summary

English-speaking Shorts viewers who respond to real incidents, human error and deadpan absurdity. The story is **not** about whale hunting or an animal being killed; the whale was dead before the authorities arrived. One-sentence compression: *An Oregon highway crew blew apart a stranded dead whale, but instead of cleaning the beach they rained blubber onto spectators, crushed a car, and ended up burying the remains anyway.* It has three distinct developments after the unusual hook: an understandable disposal dilemma, a well-intentioned plan that backfires on bystanders, and the failure to solve the original cleanup problem.

Reasons **start:** visually nonsensical but truthful “whale rain”, readable without knowing any names. Reasons **continue:** why was a whale in the air, and why did a seemingly safe quarter-mile viewpoint fail? Reasons **finish:** car struck, no injuries, crew forced to bury the very remains it tried to avoid, later change in practice. **Main weakness:** original TV footage has circulated online for decades, so novelty among older internet users is limited. Avoid overexplaining after the smashed car; the buried-remains payoff justifies the final third.

This is an editorial comparison, not evidence of guaranteed views/retention.

## Equal comparison of the three finalists

### A. Whale / chosen
- **Viewer:** English-speaking general viewer who likes strange true mishaps and ironic consequences.
- **Exact first line / scroll-stop:** “In 1970, chunks of whale rained from the sky on an Oregon beach.” Contradiction that demands a cause; immediate weather-image promise, not historical name recognition.
- **After opening:** giant rotting carcass; ordinary burial feared to fail; half-ton dynamite to make bird-sized pieces; quarter-mile audience; blast; big pieces fall toward people; sedan crushed; seagulls absent; crews bury remains. Successive expectations change.
- **Payoff:** nobody hurt, cleanup still required, later dead-whale burial normal; original decision was self-defeating.
- **Objection:** publicly famous footage; some viewers may know outcome or dislike the gross-out element. Keep gore nonexplicit, visualize tactile paper without sensational injury.
- **Compression challenge:** if viewers know the car, does “they had to bury it anyway” pay for the additional 10–15s? Author considers yes; independent viewer judgment is **UNVERIFIED**.

### B. Borja Ecce Homo / strongest alternative
- **Viewer:** meme/art curiosity viewers who also enjoy restorative human endings.
- **Exact proposed first line:** “A woman tried to fix an old church painting. Her unfinished work ended up supporting a care home.”
- **After opening:** water damage → incomplete brushwork during 2012 → local outrage and global meme → tourists unexpectedly visit → admission fees and charity → owner becomes more accepted; 2025 death is not necessary to this story.
- **Payoff:** her supposed failure ultimately benefited her community. Interview supports her perspective; not a mean-spirited mockery.
- **Objection:** core visual meme is familiar, and first line gives away benefit; requires careful, rights-respecting exact art reference. Distinct virtues: empathy and human stakes; might outperform whale for a different audience, no supporting comparable retention data.

### C. Cape Hatteras lighthouse / third finalist
- **Viewer:** engineering and “how did they do that?” viewers.
- **Exact proposed first line:** “They moved a 4,830-ton lighthouse almost three thousand feet—without taking it apart.”
- **After opening:** erosion threatens a fragile foundation → public worries moving will destroy it → beams inserted underneath, hydraulics lift it → track system pushes five feet at a time → continuous sensors → new foundation and station restored in 23 days.
- **Payoff:** intact tower preserved; useful explanation of a counterintuitive engineering solution rather than only a dramatic image.
- **Objection:** opening nearly tells full answer; remaining events risk reading as a list of machine parts rather than genuine dramatic re-evaluation. NPS remains an excellent primary reference for a future slower-format story.

**Winner vs B:** B has greater compassion and a surprising economic afterlife; A has a stronger immediate visual impossibility and a three-step physical reversal that a first-time cold viewer can follow without needing art history or the original image. **Winner's remaining failure risk:** recognition/saturation of widely shared original video. No viewer review yet, so decision is a creative hypothesis rather than a validated quality result.
