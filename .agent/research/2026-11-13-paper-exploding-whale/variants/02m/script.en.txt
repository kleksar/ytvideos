A piece of whale fell out of the sky and crushed the roof of a parked car. How did it get there?

In 1970, a dead sperm whale washed ashore near Florence, Oregon. It lay on the beach for days. As it rotted, the smell got worse. The highway crew had to get it off the beach.

They ruled out burial because they worried the carcass would soon be exposed again. So they tried something else. They'd blow it apart and let the gulls clean up the little pieces.

Spectators watched from the dunes, about a quarter of a mile away. First came the blast. Then the pieces started falling. They were big enough that people ran for cover. One piece came down even farther from the blast, right on that parked car. No injuries were reported.

Back on the beach, the gulls were gone. A big piece of the whale was still where it had been. By evening, the crew was back on the sand, burying the remains after all.

That should have been the end of the cleanup. Except for the car.
