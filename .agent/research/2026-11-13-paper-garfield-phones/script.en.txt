An orange Garfield telephone washed up on a beach in France.
Then another. And another. It kept happening for more than thirty years.
Sometimes collectors found only the cat's eyes, or a broken keypad.
They cleared the plastic. But the sea kept delivering more.
Volunteers suspected a lost shipping container, but nobody knew where it was.
Then, in 2019, a local farmer came forward.
After a storm in the nineteen eighties, he and his brother had found a container full of these phones.
He knew where it was: a sea cave hidden in the cliffs.
The cave was dangerous, and the entrance could only be reached at low tide.
The volunteers waited for the water to fall, then climbed over slippery rocks.
Orange phone parts appeared near the entrance. Deeper inside were the container's remains.
And beneath the rocks, they found twenty-three complete handsets.
But most of the cargo was already gone. Thirty years of tides had carried it out to sea.
