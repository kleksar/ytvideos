An orange Garfield telephone washed up on a beach in France.
It was a real landline phone, shaped like the cartoon cat. Lift the receiver and its eyes would open.
Then another phone appeared. Then pieces of one: an eye, a keypad, a curly cord.
For more than thirty years, people on Brittany's coast kept finding them. Beach-cleaning volunteers picked up the plastic, but the next tide could bring more.
They suspected a shipping container had been lost at sea. They couldn't find it.
In 2019, their unusual cleanup campaign reached a local farmer, René Morvan. He remembered a storm in the nineteen eighties. As a young man, he and his brother had seen a container stuffed with those phones.
It wasn't on the open seabed. He said it was lodged inside a cave in the cliffs.
The volunteers had to wait for a low tide. The rocks were slippery, and the cave was difficult to reach.
On March twenty-second, they made it inside. They found rusting metal, Garfield fragments and twenty-three complete handsets among the boulders.
There was their answer. But the container was largely empty. The sea had already taken most of what was inside.
The exact ship was still unknown. Even the possibility of another container remained open.
They had located a source, not collected the plastic it had released. After the discovery, the volunteers were still finding pieces on the shore.
