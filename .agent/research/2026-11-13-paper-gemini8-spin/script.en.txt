Neil Armstrong's spacecraft was spinning so fast he could barely read the controls.

Three years before the Moon landing, this was Armstrong's first flight into space.

He and David Scott had just made history: the first docking of two spacecraft in orbit.

Then the linked vehicles started to roll. Armstrong fought the rotation, but it kept coming back.

They were out of radio contact with Earth. They suspected the unmanned spacecraft they'd docked with.

So they undocked. Immediately, the spinning got even worse.

Without the heavier spacecraft attached, their small capsule was now turning almost once every second.

The instruments blurred. They were in danger of blacking out.

Armstrong shut down the normal steering thrusters. There was one more system he could try.

He fired the thrusters reserved for their return to Earth. At last, the spin stopped.

Only afterward did they discover the culprit: one of Gemini's own thrusters had been stuck firing.

Stopping the spin had consumed almost three-quarters of their emergency fuel. They could no longer continue the mission.

The planned spacewalk was cancelled. Both astronauts survived an early splashdown in the Pacific.
