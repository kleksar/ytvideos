## Three short competing pitches, same readiness standard

**A · Henry Box Brown (winner).** Audience: English-speaking people who enjoy ingenuity under extreme human stakes, not specialists in US slavery. Stop: a shipping warning on a crate containing a person. **Exact first line:** “THIS SIDE UP WITH CARE.” That was the warning on a wooden crate in Virginia in 1849. Continue: Brown's family was sold, he devises travel as freight, cramped air holes, transfers, dangerous upside-down leg, handlers mistake box for mail. Payoff: arrival in Philadelphia and public stage reenactments in freedom. Main objection: an attempt to turn slavery into a quirky delivery trick or to overdramatize undocumented motivations. Mitigation: Brown's family and his own testimony anchor events; language stays respectful; 1849 stage activity verified separately for later life.

**B · Balloon Boy (strongest alternative).** Audience: English viewers of media mysteries. Stop: believed six-year-old aboard a giant empty balloon. **Exact first line:** “An enormous silver balloon crossed Colorado with a six-year-old boy supposedly inside.” Continue: rescue scramble, empty balloon lands, boy found hidden in garage, sheriff revises interpretation and family disputes hoax narrative. Payoff: unexpected safe child and later contested interpretation, not false certainty. Main objection: end becomes a disputed claim about the family; additional 2025 Netflix documentary makes saturation/format comparability unclear.

**C · Pepsi 349.** Audience: general viewers of corporate mistakes and mass deception. Stop: a small soda cap appears to promise a life-changing prize. **Exact first line:** “One number made thousands of soda caps look like jackpots.” Continue: many caps had the announced number but security codes did not match; complaint and court proceedings; legal decision has narrower scope than broad promises. Payoff: understanding the disastrous gap between public number and internal authentication. Main objection: incomplete legal/participant context in 60–90 seconds risks unjustified claims and manufactured tragedy.

**Winner vs B:** B's flying image is initially stranger, but the payoff gets bogged in mutually disputed intent and already has a widely distributed 2025 documentary. A's inverted-crate episode changes the danger in the middle; the final stage-performance reversal is distinct from mere survival and is grounded in institutional sources. A is not a guaranteed high-CTR choice; a fresh audience review could still reject it. **One-sentence compression:** A man escaped slavery inside a shipped crate, nearly succumbed when it was carried upside down, and later turned that exact experience into a public stage story.
