## Editorial signal transferred, not an AI detector

Source: [Wikipedia Russian essay on patterns of generated text](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Википедия:Признаки_сгенерированности_текста). The essay addresses Wiki prose and explicitly says the signs are observations rather than sufficient proof of AI authorship. We adapt the *writing critique* only.

- Baseline opens with a three-part inventory (`No engine. No wings. Just forty-two...`) and later has several explanatory chunks. B moves quickly into the actual launch mechanics: tied to a car, one tether cut, second snapped, sudden climb.
- Remove dry list details with no later payoff (radio) and redundant high-altitude statements. Preserve act of shooting balloons, losing pellet gun, dumping ballast, catching electrical wires and reaching ground alive.
- Add a brief **real remark** quoted by Smithsonian Air & Space in 2019, not fabricated dialogue: Larry reportedly said `A man can't just sit around` when asked about the stunt. This is a SECONDARY retrospective attribution, not an authenticated on-camera interview clip. Russian conveys its double meaning naturally; avoid claiming a literal equivalent word for word.
- Keep Smithsonian ownership/donation as a physical consequence without a moral summary. End on an object-based return to the same first source image and same observed event, not two different images.
- Word count: A 203, B 212 approximate EN words. No measured speed/retention or CTR.
