Sixteen thousand feet above California, a man was floating through the sky in a lawn chair.

No engine. No wings. Just forty-two giant weather balloons filled with helium.

On July second, 1982, truck driver Larry Walters launched from a backyard near Los Angeles.

He wanted to fly, but expected his chair to stay much closer to the ground.

He brought a radio, water jugs for ballast, and a pellet gun to puncture balloons on the way down.

But the balloons carried him far higher than planned, into airspace used by passenger planes.

Airline pilots spotted him and alerted air traffic control. The air up there was freezing and thin.

Larry started shooting balloons. The chair finally began descending.

Then he dropped the gun. He couldn't pop any more.

As his descent grew too fast, he poured out some of the water ballast to slow it.

The dangling ropes snagged power lines in Long Beach, cutting electricity to nearby homes.

Larry made it down alive. The aviation authorities fined him.

But he gave the lawn chair to a neighborhood kid. Years later, that kid donated it to the Smithsonian.
