Sixteen thousand feet above California, a man was drifting in a lawn chair.

Forty-two helium balloons held him up. There wasn't a motor anywhere.

July 1982. Truck driver Larry Walters had tied the chair to a car in a backyard near Los Angeles.

One tether was cut. The other snapped, and Larry shot into the sky.

To get down, he'd brought a pellet gun to pop balloons. Water jugs hung below the chair as ballast.

Instead of staying low, he had climbed into controlled airspace near Los Angeles airport.

Pilots on passing airliners spotted him and called air traffic control.

Larry aimed the gun upward and popped some balloons. The chair finally began to descend.

Then he dropped the gun. Balloons still hung overhead, but he couldn't puncture any more.

Now the chair was dropping too fast. Larry dumped water from the jugs to slow it.

In Long Beach, his ropes caught power lines. Nearby homes lost electricity.

Larry got down alive. Aviation authorities later fined him for the flight.

When asked why he'd done it, Larry said, “A man can't just sit around.” He gave the chair to a neighborhood kid, who later donated it to the Smithsonian.
