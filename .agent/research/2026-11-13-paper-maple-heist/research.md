## Three competing finalists

### A — Maple syrup (selected)
- **Audience:** broad English viewers of surprising true crime; no specialist background required.
- **Opening:** An accountant nearly falls from a stack of barrels; a minor physical clue leads to an $18m theft.
- **Reason to stay:** empty drum -> several empties -> apparently heavy drums full of water -> replacement scheme -> trucking through buyers -> 9,571 stolen barrels -> arrests and major penalty.
- **Payoff:** how ordinary stocktaking exposed a long-running criminal operation, plus a verified penalty exceeding one main participant's admitted personal profit.
- **Objection:** title reveals the theft early, so the middle must convey distinct actions rather than repeat the amount. The legal tail risks slowing pace and may be cut on first-reader feedback.

### B — WWII bat bombs
- **Audience:** fans of strange military history and unintended consequences.
- **Opening:** A weapon meant to be delivered by bats started fires at the airbase where it was tested.
- **Reason to stay:** capture animals -> cool them -> attach small incendiaries -> early awakening -> own installations burn.
- **Payoff:** an attempt at control becomes a vivid failure; project never deployed.
- **Objection:** the best dramatic twist may be known almost immediately; remaining technical details can become repetitive. Historical targeting of Japanese civilian buildings makes a light comic style uncomfortable.

### C — Alcatraz 1962
- **Audience:** suspense and escape-plan viewers.
- **Opening:** Guards find sleeping-looking heads instead of prisoners.
- **Reason to stay:** improvised drill -> hidden passage -> heads and raft from raincoats -> shoreline launch.
- **Payoff:** the escape method, but not whether the men survived.
- **Objection:** unsolved fate can leave a false promise if survival is the hook. A third-party [Oct 30, 2026 Short listing](https://youthvid.com/no/video/how-a-spoon-becomes-an-escape-from-alcatraz-j9a2R48Y_v4/) claimed millions of views by Nov 11; not independently checked against YouTube primary data, and not evidence of our channel's likely performance.

**Comparative decision:** A has a fact-supported discovery, midpoint reversal, criminal mechanism, traceable consequence and answer. B may have the strongest single absurd visual, but less room after its reveal. C has many action beats and public interest, but high familiarity and no confirmed survival outcome. The winner is an editorial hypothesis, not a measured CTR/retention prediction. Different readers can disagree.
