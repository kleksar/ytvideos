One barrel shifted under an accountant's hand. He almost fell off the stack.

That was how a maple syrup theft worth eighteen million Canadian dollars came to light.

He was checking a warehouse in Quebec in 2012. It held part of the maple producers' reserve.

A full barrel weighed over six hundred pounds. This one was empty.

Then they found more. Other barrels still felt heavy, so inspectors opened one.

Water. The thieves had drained out the syrup and filled the barrels again to hide the theft.

For months, trucks had been carrying the syrup away. Buyers resold it outside Quebec.

When police counted the losses, 9,571 barrels were missing.

Sixteen people were arrested. One buyer had made about a million dollars in profit.

He got eight years in prison. The Supreme Court later upheld a fine of more than nine million dollars.

They had hidden the missing syrup for months. What finally gave them away?
