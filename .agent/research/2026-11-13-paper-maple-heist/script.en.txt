An accountant nearly fell off the top of a stack of maple syrup barrels.

That near-fall exposed a theft worth eighteen million dollars.

In 2012, he was counting stock at a huge warehouse holding Quebec's strategic maple syrup reserve.

A full barrel weighed over six hundred pounds. But the one he grabbed suddenly moved.

It was empty. And soon he found more just like it.

Some barrels still felt heavy. Inspectors opened one — and found water instead of syrup.

For months, thieves had been draining the syrup and replacing it with water to hide the theft.

The stolen syrup went out by truck, passed through middlemen, and was resold outside Quebec.

When police pieced it together, 9,571 barrels had disappeared.

Sixteen people were arrested. One of the key buyers personally made about a million dollars from the scheme.

He received eight years in prison. Later, Canada's Supreme Court upheld a fine of more than nine million dollars.

An enormous theft, hidden for months. And it was exposed by just one empty barrel.
