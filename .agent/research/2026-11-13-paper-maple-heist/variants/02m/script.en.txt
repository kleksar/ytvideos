An accountant climbed a stack of maple syrup barrels in Quebec. One rocked beneath his weight. He almost fell.

It was July 2012. Michel Gauvreau was checking a warehouse belonging to Quebec's maple syrup producers. A full barrel weighed roughly six hundred pounds. But the one that nearly sent him falling was empty.

He tested another. Empty again.

Then the inspectors opened a barrel that still seemed full. The liquid inside was clear water.

Someone had not simply broken into a warehouse and taken a few bottles. For months, a group had been moving barrels of syrup out, taking the contents, and sometimes returning containers filled with water to keep the stockpile looking intact.

Why was there so much syrup in one building? Quebec's producers kept a reserve to help manage supplies between harvests. It was valuable enough to be worth stealing by the truckload.

The investigation eventually uncovered 9,571 stolen barrels worth more than eighteen million Canadian dollars. Police arrested sixteen people.

One central participant, Richard Vallières, said he personally made about a million dollars. He received an eight-year prison sentence. In 2022, Canada's Supreme Court upheld a fine exceeding nine million dollars, tied to the value of syrup he had sold, not just his profit.

The barrels had disguised the crime for months. Then an accountant stepped on one that should have been heavy.
