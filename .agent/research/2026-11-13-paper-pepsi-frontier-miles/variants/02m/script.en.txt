A Harrier lands beside a high school. The Pepsi commercial puts a price on it: seven million points.

Back in 1995, Pepsi points bought real prizes, like shirts and jackets. But a fighter jet?

John Leonard wanted to find out whether he could claim one.

He read the rules: additional points cost ten cents each. So he didn't need to drink millions of Pepsis.

But every order needed at least fifteen original points from the packaging.

Leonard found backers. In March 1996, he mailed Pepsi those fifteen points and a check for $700,008.50.

On the order form he wrote “1 Harrier Jet.” The official catalog had no jet listed.

Pepsi sent his check back. The plane in the ad, the company said, was a joke.

Leonard disputed that. Soon they were fighting it out in court.

The judge ruled that a reasonable viewer wouldn't believe a military jet landing at school was a serious offer.

He appealed and lost again in 2000. No jet.

Then, in February 2026, another airline brought the old story back.

Frontier said it was giving Leonard seven million airline miles. That was the flight-related prize he finally got.
