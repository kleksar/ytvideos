Seven million Pepsi Points for a Harrier fighter jet. That was the price on the screen.

In a 1995 Pepsi commercial, a student arrives at school in a military aircraft. The ad displays its point price just as it did for shirts and jackets.

John Leonard, from Seattle, decided to order the plane. At first he tried to collect points by buying Pepsi. Then he discovered a rule in the catalog: extra points cost ten cents each, and every order needed at least fifteen original points from the packaging.

A plane worth about twenty-three million dollars suddenly appeared within reach for around seven hundred thousand.

Leonard raised the money through acquaintances. In March 1996, he sent fifteen original points, a check for $700,008.50 and an order form on which he had added the aircraft himself.

The check came back with coupons for free drinks. Pepsi explained that the plane was a joke and was not in the catalog.

Leonard's lawyers demanded the plane. Pepsi went to court first. In 1999, a federal judge ruled that the ad was not a binding offer. Leonard appealed and lost again in 2000.

Then, in February 2026, Frontier Airlines announced seven million airline miles for Leonard in a new promotion. Miles could help him travel with his family, unlike a fighter jet he couldn't fly himself.

He never got the plane, and the court ruling never changed. The airline took its number from the old commercial. What price had Pepsi put on the screen?
