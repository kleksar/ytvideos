# Editorial research — Stone of Destiny (Christmas 1950)
Date researched: 2026-11-13. Channel: Paper Detour, English Short. Scope: isolated candidate, not an official ytv run. Full screen paper art is only a future plan.

## One-sentence answer
Four Scottish students removed the 152-kilogram coronation stone from Westminster Abbey in 1950, broke it during the escape, hid and repaired it in Scotland, then left it for police—who later fooled reporters on the return trip—before an official return to Scotland in 1996.

## Three competing pitches — same decision fields
**A. Stone of Destiny — English-language history/curious-objects viewers across UK/US.** Stop: an apparently worthless but 152kg royal stone, removed by students on Christmas. First exact line: “On Christmas Day 1950, four Scottish students came to steal the stone beneath Britain's coronation chair.” After hook: the stone breaks, the open Abbey door attracts a policeman, a pretend couple lets them escape, a stonemason repairs it, and reporters chase a decoy. Payoff: Scotland got the stone officially in 1996; present-day Perth museum and 2023 coronation. Main objection: known movie/Scottish political controversy; exact break extent contested.
**B. Dreadnought hoax — naval trivia and social engineering viewers.** Stop: Royal Navy gave a flagship tour to fake visiting dignitaries. Exact proposed start: “The Royal Navy welcomed a royal family aboard its newest battleship. None of them were royal.” After hook: fake telegram, special train, fake foreign-language conversation, ceremonious tour, exposure in newspaper. Payoff: Navy embarrassment and question how easy entry was. Main objection: critical disguise involved blackface; presenting it as harmless good fun flattens the context, while the ending mainly repeats the prank.
**C. Star of India heist — crime and museum-mystery viewers.** Stop: priceless gems vanished through a fourth-floor open window with weak alarms. Exact proposed start: “The museum had priceless gemstones—and windows its thieves didn't even need to break.” After hook: exterior climb, cut showcases, police trail to suspects, Miami leads to recovery of key jewels. Payoff: major stones found, security weaknesses exposed; not every gem recovered. Main objection: other queued heist #19 and many actors/exceptions need careful 60–90s explanation.

**Decision:** A beats C on emotional object (whose symbol is it?), more independent reversals (crack/policeman/repair/press wrong car), and ending spanning decades, not just loot recovered. A beats B on better sustained consequences and no need to aestheticize racial impersonation. A's weakness is pre-existing audience knowledge of a 2008 feature film, and a 2025 British Academy research video; real incremental novelty UNVERIFIED. If independent reader says 'understandable but not worth watching', reject, do not move to production.
