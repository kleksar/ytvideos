On Christmas Day 1950, four Scottish students came to steal the stone beneath Britain's coronation chair.
Not gold. Not a jewel. A 152-kilogram block of sandstone.
Edward I had taken that stone from Scotland in 1296.
Three hid inside Westminster Abbey. Kay Matheson waited in a borrowed car.
They pried the stone from under the chair. It broke as they lifted it.
They carried out the pieces. Then they noticed the Abbey door was still open.
Two ran back to close it. Right then, a policeman came by.
Their friends in the car pretended to be a couple out late. He walked on.
By morning, detectives were searching roads north. The stone was gone.
It lay hidden for weeks, and a Glasgow stonemason repaired the break.
In April, the students left it at Arbroath Abbey. Police took it back.
Nobody was prosecuted. But getting it back to London caused one more chase.
Reporters followed a police limousine. The stone was inside a different car—a Jaguar.
In 1996, the British government finally returned it to Scotland.
It came south for King Charles's coronation in 2023. Today it rests in Perth Museum.
Now it travels with specialists and a police escort. One journey began with a borrowed car.
