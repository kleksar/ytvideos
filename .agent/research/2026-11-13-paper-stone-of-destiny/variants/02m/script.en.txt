On Christmas morning in 1950, a policeman spoke to two young people outside Westminster Abbey. According to their later recollections, a piece of Britain's coronation stone was in their car.
The Stone of Destiny was a 152-kilogram block of sandstone. Edward I had taken it from Scotland in 1296.
Four Scottish students had come to take it back. They reached the Coronation Chair, but the stone broke while they were removing it.
Ian Hamilton and Kay Matheson recalled posing as a couple looking for somewhere to stay. The policeman let them go.
The rest of the stone was still inside the Abbey. The students eventually got both pieces out, hid them separately and took them north. A Glasgow stonemason repaired the break.
In April 1951, the stone was left at Arbroath Abbey. Police recovered it. The authorities decided against prosecution.
Now it had to go back to London. Reporters were waiting to follow the police convoy.
They watched a Jaguar leave and stayed put. When a limousine appeared with an escort, they followed it.
The stone was already on its way in the Jaguar.
In 1996, the government returned the stone to Scotland. It came south again for Charles III's coronation in 2023 and is now displayed at Perth Museum.
Today, moving it involves specialists and police escorts. Its repaired fracture recalls a very different journey, when the people responsible for protecting the stone came remarkably close to finding it.
