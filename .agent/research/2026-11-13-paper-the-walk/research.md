## 3. Three competing proposals

### A. The Walk — selected

Opening question: how can police arrest a performer who is out of reach between two skyscrapers? Development: covert overnight rigging, an unexpected bow-and-arrow detail, a return crossing, kneeling and lying on the wire while police keep quiet. Payoff: he reaches the roof alive and is arrested; charges are dropped in exchange for a free children's show. The final question returns directly to the opening answer.

Why stop: a person on a wire above a city is legible without knowing his name. Why stay: his choices keep escalating after a simple crossing would have been enough. What the viewer gets: a completed episode and an unexpected resolution, not only a height statistic.

Strongest objection: the original film's movement, photography and star performance may explain much of an edit's popularity. A static paper treatment may not reproduce that attraction. The 126.9M observation is not a forecast for our reconstruction. Native Shorts status of that seed is unverified, and there are fewer than three independently confirmed successful creators; do not call it a validated repeatable pattern.

### B. Hacksaw Ridge — nearest emotional alternative

Proposed opening: a medic goes back for the wounded after the other soldiers withdraw. Development: repeated return trips and lowering men from the escarpment. Payoff: men reach safety because he remains behind. Suitable for Nuviko History without movie dialogue, fabricated thoughts or graphic imagery.

Its two strong indexed examples provide better replication than one giant The Walk seed. It loses this editorial choice on immediate visual simplicity and the opportunity to vary the slate away from another military-rescue story, not because 23M is a poor result. Full-read attempts to the Army citation/article were unavailable or rate-limited; no finished Doss script is claimed. Source leads: https://www.army.mil/MEDALOFHONOR/citations20.html and https://www.army.mil/article/176953/how_do_you_thank_a_hero_tell_his_story_again_and_again .

### C. 21 / Monty Hall — nearest interactive alternative

Proposed opening in first person: I am offered a second choice after an empty door is opened. Development: compare staying and switching under identical, explicitly stated host rules. Payoff: switching wins in two of the three equally likely prize placements; staying wins in one. This is a model, not an observed casino experiment. Source opened: https://sites.me.ucsb.edu/~moehlis/21.html , section The Monty Hall Problem.

The audience can participate, and the conditional probability is a useful reveal. The objection is explanatory load: hiding what the host knows or when the offer is made changes the problem. The Walk needs less setup for a cold viewer. One older 1.76M example does not establish a low ceiling for 21, and it says nothing about the separate card-counting scene.

## 4. What is and is not better than Apollo 13

Editorial hypothesis: The Walk offers a simpler first-frame promise, a voluntary risk rather than a technical failure, physical escalation and an ironic resolution. Apollo has a stronger practical engineering explanation and a clear life-support mechanism. Neither strength cancels the other.

The sampled The Walk counter is the largest observed number in this scan. It is NOT evidence that total demand is larger than Apollo's, that native Shorts performance is better, that a paper version will outperform the existing script, or that revenue will be higher. The comparison mixes ages, channels and incompletely verified formats; historical metric timestamps and channel baselines are unavailable. We have not measured our own Apollo episode. No totals, confidence percentages or view ratios between films are reported.

Decision: produce this as the next editorial candidate, not replace Apollo. A later permitted publication can test transfer using own-channel stayed-to-watch/engaged views and retention at matched publication ages. That is a proposed evaluation, not a scheduled job or a new channel rule.

## 6. Editorial design and author review

Promise: an arrest that cannot happen until a dangerous performance ends. First-image concept only: the wire and the two buildings make the reason for waiting visible. Tone: suspense followed by an unexpected administrative resolution, not a tragedy teaser. Working internal label: The police had to watch the show. This is not an approved public title or thumbnail package.

Opening/ending alternatives considered:

1. Height first / a generic statement about courage: rejected because the ending merely revisits the subject and adds a moral.
2. Arrest followed by a children's-show bargain at the opening: rejected because it gives away the most useful later reversal and makes the middle an explanation of an already-told ending.
3. Waiting police first / explicit arrest question at the end: selected. The middle explains why they wait; the return and legal outcome are paid off before repetition.

Exact EN seam: `But how do you arrest someone walking between skyscrapers? To arrest the tightrope walker, the police had to wait for his show to end.`

Exact RU seam: `Но как вообще арестовать человека, который гуляет между небоскрёбами? Чтобы арестовать канатоходца, полицейским пришлось дождаться конца представления.`

The question is retrospective; the story already answered it before the boundary. It must not be performed as a new teaser or an instruction to withhold the ending. Both edge sentences are complete. Cold entry names the police, performer and delay without requiring the film. Author read-through covered the complete texts and repeated seam; it is not independent review or listened-to audio. The rhetorical final question could still sound forced in performance and remains a specific reviewer check.

Revision notes: removed the redundant sentence explaining that the solution to one performance was another performance; the Central Park fact already supplies that irony. Removed exact crossing count and the disputed origin/preparation story. Kept only one rigging detail rather than turning the Short into a setup tutorial. Do not put a plane flying toward the towers or create a 9/11 association. The cameo is a brand device, not a claim about the historical event. Detailed storyboard and prompts remain unstarted.

### Editorial causal chain and viewer benefit

Secret plan → guards force hiding (need to avoid discovery) → thin line is essential to pulling heavier cable (otherwise nothing to walk on) → sunrise crossing (performs public spectacle) → seeing officers changes the act into a prolonged standoff → return to roof enables arrest → public response helps change charges to a free children's performance. For the ten-minute account, earlier public criticism and later myth corrections add separately evidenced historical context, but their placement can slow the walk; the detailed weakness is preserved rather than concealed in the review.
