To arrest the tightrope walker, the police had to wait for his show to end.

He was more than four hundred metres above New York, on a wire between the Twin Towers.

It was 1974. Philippe Petit and his friends had secretly rigged the wire overnight. To get the first line across, they had used a bow and arrow.

That morning, Petit stepped out without a safety net.

He crossed to the other tower. Then turned around and walked back.

When police appeared on the roof, he smiled and knelt on the wire. One sergeant stepped back and told everyone to keep quiet.

Then Petit lay down.

The performance lasted about forty-five minutes. When he finally returned to the roof, the officers took him into custody.

But the charges were dropped in exchange for a free performance for children in Central Park.

But how do you arrest someone walking between skyscrapers?
