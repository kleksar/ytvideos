## Three finalists at comparable proposal depth

### A. Berlin Candy Bomber (selected)
- **Audience:** English-speaking viewer interested in unusual real history and a human choice with material consequences, no prior Berlin knowledge.
- **Exact opener:** "Chocolate floated down over West Berlin on tiny parachutes made from handkerchiefs."
- **Why stop scrolling:** obvious contradiction between an airlift to feed a blockaded city and miniature chocolate parachutes.
- **After hook:** children share two sticks of gum → how can they identify one of many planes? → crew builds parachutes → unauthorized repetition and public news → commanding officer reprimands → airlift commander authorizes and expands it → donated candy/handkerchief work across America.
- **Payoff:** approx. 23 tons dropped during the whole operation; the drops continued when the pilot had left, not an isolated one-off stunt.
- **Strongest objection:** story is already a familiar inspirational WWII/Cold War anecdote, and could turn into an easy moral or chronology recap. Response: keep verifiable procedural actions and handoff, no invented child's dialogue or emotive conclusion.

### B. 1966 Palomares lost nuclear bomb
- **Audience:** technical/military Cold War viewers.
- **Exact alternative opener:** "Three nuclear bombs were found after the crash. The fourth was somewhere under the Mediterranean."
- **Why stop scrolling:** clear unresolved underwater object and high stakes.
- **After hook:** air collision in January 1966 → witness information narrows location → Alvin spots bomb in March → first lift fails → second operation uses CURV and recovers it in April.
- **Payoff:** fourth weapon recovered, with important caveat that on-land plutonium contamination and cleanup cannot be treated as nonexistent.
- **Strongest objection:** full truth becomes a rushed and misleading account of nuclear risk; another high-stakes military rescue beside a substantial backlog.
- **Decision vs A:** Palomares has harder immediate peril, but Candy has more tonal diversity and a complete, humane, independently sourceable final consequence with less compressed collateral complexity.

### C. 1917 Halifax dispatcher Vincent Coleman
- **Audience:** broad historical viewers who like a concrete high-cost decision.
- **Exact alternative opener:** "A ship was burning in Halifax harbor. A railway dispatcher stayed behind to send a warning."
- **Why stop scrolling:** direct action against a visible deadline.
- **After hook:** station evacuation and telegraph warning, dispatcher killed in blast, relief trains were organized after message.
- **Payoff:** warning contributed to railway response, **not** a confidently documented passenger train No. 10 stop.
- **Strongest objection:** disputed familiar ending undermines simple heroic payoff; treating train rescue as certain would invent causal closure.
- **Decision vs A:** stronger physical jeopardy but larger factual uncertainty on the most narratively valuable beat.
