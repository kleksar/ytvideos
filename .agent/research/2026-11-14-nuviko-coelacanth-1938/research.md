## Three independently distinct finalists
### A — coelacanth (selected)
Audience: history-of-science/general curiosity. Exact cold opening: **“A museum curator spotted a blue fin sticking out of a trawler's catch in South Africa.”** Why stop: a vivid object that turns an ordinary fishing catch into a biological identification mystery; the strange fleshy fins appear immediately after. After hook: she tries to preserve it, cold storage fails, organs are lost before an expert sees it, then species identification changes the meaning. Payoff: the fossil-only group was still alive; Smith and his wife sought an intact fish and succeeded in 1952. Strongest objection: "fish thought extinct" is widely retold and the middle could turn into a static educational fact. Response: focus on verifiable escalating preservation obstacles and fourteen-year second-fish search rather than repeat the headline. Source-backed mechanism, not manufactured peril. Novelty is relative to local Draft queue, not internet originality.

### B — Alcatraz (strongest immediate hook)
Audience: true crime and historical mystery. Cold opening: **“The guard reached into a prison cell and knocked the sleeping man's head off.”** Source supports the dummy head fell after a guard prodded it; opening is an editorial reconstruction of the FBI account, not claimed primary quotation. Why stop: physical surprise and high recognizability. After hook: concealed vents, decoy heads, improvised raincoat raft, actual escape. Payoff: three prisoners escaped the island; their ultimate fate remains unknown. Strongest objection: culturally familiar story and no verifiable human-outcome payoff, risking made-up survival. Beats A on instant tension, loses on verifiable full resolution and differentiation from an already rescue/escape-heavy Nuviko queue. Source FBI [S5].

### C — Florence's Mud Angels
Audience: culture, restoration and ordinary people helping after disaster. Cold opening: **“Young volunteers carried soaked books out of a flooded library, passing them from hand to hand.”** This is a staging condensation of documented collective work, not an exact eyewitness scene. Why stop: unusual salvage target, human effort. After hook: flood damage to almost one million books, volunteer rescue, creation of conservation/restoration operation. Payoff: collections saved and restoration methods changed; long-term work continued. Strongest objection: very large human tragedy and diffuse years-long ending hard to represent honestly in a single 60–90s story without falsely equating one volunteer with the whole outcome. Source BNCF [S6].

**Selection A over B:** lower familiar-action intensity, but clearer development toward a documented *complete* second specimen and a different science-first mood versus Nuviko's present wartime, sinking, rescue and escape queue (#72 Coleman, #70 Koepcke, #68 Candy Bomber, #66 Paul Bunyan, #64 Coldfeet, #62 St Nazaire, #43 locomotive, #34 Flight 5390, #20 S-5).
