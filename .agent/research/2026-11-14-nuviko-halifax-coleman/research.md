## Three comparable applications (audience → exact hook → change → payoff → objection)

**(1) Coleman — chosen.** Audience: English history Short audience without technical railway knowledge. Exact cold first line: `“Hold up the train.” Those words were sent minutes before an explosion killed the dispatcher who typed them.` (key on desk). Stop: he knowingly sent a warning moments before he died. Stay: fire, sailor warning, decision to return, message, blast, actual train restrictions, honest ambiguity and relief dispatch. Payoff: other inbound trains stopped; rail system alerted; more detailed call followed and **six relief trains left that day**; museum preserves his key. Strongest objection: legend that Coleman's action **saved that particular 300-person passenger train is contested**; any unqualified depiction is a disqualifying factual error. This exact draft avoids it in S011.

**(2) St Francis Dam — strongest alternative.** Audience: historical engineering-disaster viewers. Exact hook: `The dam keeper showed the engineer muddy water on the hillside. Hours later, the dam collapsed.` Stop: visually concrete warning before collapse. Change: warning → inspection and declaration safe → nighttime breach → destructive flood downstream. Payoff: despite inspection, water swept across a 54-mile disaster zone; estimated 400–600 killed. Objection: CSU explicitly lists several possible causes; can't equate muddy water/one engineer's decision to proven singular cause; high danger of sensational simplification and similarities to other disasters.

**(3) John Snow pump — scientifically interesting alternative.** Audience: intellectually curious general English viewers. Hook: `The water pump handle came off. But the cholera outbreak was already fading.` Stop: conflicts with familiar simple medical legend. Change: investigation → parish board 7 Sep → handle removed 8 Sep → later examination found early decline, possible prevention of renewed spread. Payoff: a useful public-health action does not imply it caused all observed recovery. Objection: takes more careful explanation of disease trend than a Short usually handles; weaker self-evident physical action than Coleman.

**Winner vs alternative and queue:** St Francis supplies a stronger immediate image but post-collapse development risks unverified speculation. Coleman has two documented, distinct real consequences after the blast. Its audience risk is that Historica Canada dramatizes an overly confident saved-passenger story; this version states the limit plainly, and first payoff (other trains) is independent of that debate. Queued Nuviko S-5, Coldfeet, Togo, Castle Itter, Koepcke, Candy Bomber and St Nazaire are rescue/military/arctic stories; Halifax is a Canadian civilian rail-dispatch action and network relief. In #34/#38/#68/#70, Coleman was considered but **not selected** (each explicitly flags the disputed passenger train). This is **not a duplicate PR** and is tested under a different, constrained claim: real other inbound trains + six relief trains, without the unproven 300-person rescue. A reviewer may still reject the angle. Closed #13 is the *solar-storm battery-free telegraph* story explicitly rejected as boring; no revival of that topic or its text.

## Sources / date / supported claims / uncertainty

**F1 — Maritime Museum of the Atlantic:** https://maritimemuseum.novascotia.ca/stories/vincent-coleman-and-halifax-explosion — publication date not stated, read 2026-11-14. “Vincent Coleman and the Halifax Explosion”, sections “Explosion”, “Results”, preceding biography. Sailor delivered warning to wooden railway station; Coleman left with boss then turned back; began message `Hold up the train`; 9:05 blast and death; telegraph to Halifax–Truro; **other** trains halted. Museum states **Train No. 10 disputed** between an interview-based account (already passed Rockingham) and contemporary 7 Dec 1917 Moncton Transcript (held 15 minutes). Do NOT assert exactly 300 saved or exact train outcome. Museum: another detailed request for help **about an hour later**; **six** trains from different towns with doctors/nurses/firefighters/supplies that day. Cause of six-train response is **initial warning AND subsequent request**, not a magical solo dispatch by Coleman.

**F2 — Nova Scotia Government, release 2004-12-02:** https://news.novascotia.ca/en/2004/12/02/ns-artifacts-hero-halifax-explosion — read 2026-11-14, museum exhibit of Coleman's real telegraph key, pocket watch and family artifacts. Confirms alert to line Halifax–Truro and six help trains. The precise appearance/layout of physical key as an image was **not** obtained; visual prompt is not a facsimile.

**F3 — Parks Canada, page modified 2022-12-01:** https://parks.canada.ca/culture/designation/evenement-event/halifax-explosion — read 2026-11-14. Collision Mont-Blanc/Imo on Dec 6 1917, explosives aboard French vessel, fire/drift to Pier 6, crowded spectators only few officers knew hazard, explosion at **9:05**, around **2000** dead. `Around` preserves approximation. Do not portray photoreal generated frames as actual archived photo.

**F4 — CSU Channel Islands Library:** https://library.csuci.edu/collections/special-collections/st-francis-dam.htm — undated webpage read 2026-11-14. Warning and inspection; March 12, 1928 collapse and 400–600 deaths; unresolved causes listed, so not a simple single-culprit story.

**F5 — UCLA Epidemiology History:** https://epi-snow.ph.ucla.edu/Stream2_BSPoutbreak_e.html — undated webpage read 2026-11-14. Quotes Snow's 1855 book on removal of handle after Sept 7 meeting, and Henry Whitehead's later account noting decline before 8 Sept removal. Claims about preventing new cases are historical interpretation, not proof that removal stopped prior outbreak.
