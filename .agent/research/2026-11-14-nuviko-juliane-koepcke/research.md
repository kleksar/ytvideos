## Three verified finalist pitches

**Winner — Juliane Koepcke.** Audience: new English-speaking viewers attracted by human survival and practical knowledge rather than a named historical figure; age/country are hypotheses, NOT measured cohorts. **Exact start:** “Seventeen-year-old Juliane Koepcke heard running water after falling from an airplane into the Amazon.” **Why stop:** airplane breakup and audible clue in one intelligible line. **After hook:** separated seat row → damaged vision → parents' water lesson → flights overhead unable to see → careful one-shoe movement, dwindling sweets, river current → empty shelter → human rescue. **Payoff:** eleven days to safety, sole survivor of 92; later she helped protect the forest. **Strongest objection:** widely retold, and 240 EN words may lose less patient viewers; the reason the fall itself was survivable is uncertain. Do not turn that uncertainty into an invented skydiving mechanism.

**Strongest alternative — Palomares.** Audience: Cold War, engineering and underwater search. **Exact proposed opening:** “A fisherman saw what looked like a parachuting airman drop into the sea. Every airman had already been accounted for.” **Why stop:** eyewitness contradiction and unexplained fourth bomb. **After hook:** B-52/tanker collision → three bombs on land and one underwater → tiny Alvin sub finds it → hook bends and bomb sinks again → different CURV system finally raises it. **Payoff:** 80 days underwater before successful recovery. **Strongest objection:** queue already contains Coldfeet, St Nazaire and Paul Bunyan; a repeat of military danger/tech might crowd out variety and omit persistent contamination effects. Sources read: https://www.whoi.edu/oceanus/feature/little-alvin-and-the-lost-h-bomb/ (19 Nov 2021; eyewitness and Alvin operator accounts), https://www.brookings.edu/the-palomares-broken-arrow-january-1966/ (historical archival project).

**Third — Vincent Coleman.** Audience: disaster response, small decision with broad consequences. **Exact proposed opening:** “A dispatcher was warned that the burning ship carried ammunition. He went back to his telegraph key.” **Why stop:** a clear irreversible action in immediate danger. **After hook:** collision, telegram, explosion, regional railway response. **Payoff:** Coleman died; the warning reached other stations and helped activate urgent relief. **Strongest objection:** whether passenger train No. 10 was specifically stopped by his message is contested. Without pretending he 'saved 300' the story is sad and less surprising. Source read: https://museum.novascotia.ca/stories/vincent-coleman-and-halifax-explosion (Museum historical page, n.d., Results section including contradictory records). PR #13 telegraph was rejected as boring; this is not the same story or a general ban on technology, but reduces preference for unsupported hype.

**Editorial choice:** Juliane, due to a civilian woman's decision-making, Peru, visual river mechanism, and later conservation; different epoch/geography/tone versus pending military-heavy Nuviko queue. Palomares is arguably stronger for repeated reversals; no made-up virality number resolves the comparison.

## Story boundaries and exclusions
- Only survivor **eventually**, out of 92. Other reports suggest a few may have briefly survived the impact; not asserted otherwise.
- Physical mechanism of falling from ~3,000 m isn't proven. Do not depict rotating seat as proven parachute or claim storm updraft explanation.
- Boat/shelter day differs (ABC vs 2024 first-person); script only says “Eventually.” Rescue **after eleven days** supported in later interview.
- No unsupported quotes or words attributed to Juliane/parents/locals; no speculation about what she felt or how she should medically treat injuries.
- Research cannot establish YouTube audience demand from comparable video public views. Age 18+, English-speaking target, audience geography and film performance are **hypotheses/UNVERIFIED**; no CTR, retention estimates or guarantees.
- Primary first-person testimony differs from independent crash investigation; visual shots are only proposed reenactments, not preserved documentary frames.
