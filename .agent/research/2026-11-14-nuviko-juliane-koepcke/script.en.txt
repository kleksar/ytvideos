Seventeen-year-old Juliane Koepcke heard running water after falling from an airplane into the Amazon.

On Christmas Eve, 1971, her plane broke apart during a storm over Peru. Her mother was aboard.

Juliane fell about three thousand metres with a row of seats. She woke alone under the trees with a broken collarbone.

Her glasses were gone, and one eye was nearly swollen shut. Her mother was missing.

Her parents studied the rainforest. Her father had taught her that running water could lead to people.

She found a tiny stream coming out of the ground and followed it.

Search planes flew overhead, but the dense canopy hid her.

She had just one shoe, so she tested the ground with that foot first.

She found a bag of sweets from the wreck and left unfamiliar plants alone.

The stream grew into a river. Weakened, she sometimes let the current carry her.

The sweets ran out. She kept moving through the rain.

Eventually she spotted a boat beside a shelter. No one was there.

When local men appeared, she told them she'd been on the missing flight. They took her to safety.

Eleven days after the crash, Juliane was safe. Of the ninety-two people aboard, she was the only survivor. Her mother had died.

She later became a biologist and helped protect part of that same rainforest.

Back under the canopy, with her eyesight so poor, the clue that led her to people had been a sound.
