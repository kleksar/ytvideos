## Three concrete finalist pitches

### A — Nellie Bly, recommended
- **Audience:** English-language short-form viewers interested in human agency, journeys, logistics and historical surprises; gender/age profile of this channel unverified.
- **Exact opening:** “A clerk at a Hong Kong shipping office told Nellie Bly another woman had sailed from there three days earlier—to race her around the world.”
- **Why stop:** a shipping clerk reveals the reporter is accidentally in a global race; who wins, and how?
- **What changes after hook:** editor gives two days → one satchel → narrow train connection with Jules Verne → parallel opposite-way journalist → an enforced wait → Pacific storms → snow blocks US route → one-car chartered train.
- **Full payoff:** arrival Jersey City Jan25 1890 after 72d 6h 11m; Bisland returned later; original Verne benchmark beaten by both.
- **Strongest objection:** well-known famous-person story; a one-line summary “she circled Earth in 72 days” would be flat. Rival discovery and the multi-transport final leg are needed, not extra geography/travelogue.

### B — Palomares missing bomb, strongest alternative
- **Audience:** English-history/technology viewers, military archaeology interest.
- **Exact proposed opening:** “A fisherman saw something drift into the sea under a parachute. The air crews were accounted for—but one hydrogen bomb wasn't.”
- **Why stop:** spectacular mismatch between witness interpretation and missing weapon.
- **What changes:** three weapons on land → fourth submerged → imprecise location → Alvin undersea search → white parachute finally found and recovered.
- **Payoff:** weapon located and retrieved, not nuclear detonation; HOW the search resolved.
- **Strongest objection:** already high density of military/war and trapped-at-sea scripts in queue (#20/#62/#64/#66). Scope of nuclear contamination and exact recovery operations require fact-sensitive background beyond short.

### C — Alcock and Brown flight
- **Audience:** English aviation/adventure/early-technology viewers.
- **Exact proposed opening:** “The first nonstop plane across the Atlantic ended nose-down in an Irish bog.”
- **Why stop:** apparent crash versus achievement.
- **What changes:** Atlantic weather/instruments → pilots keep the plane going → approach Ireland → bog landing → both survived.
- **Payoff:** the flight did cross nonstop despite crash-like ending.
- **Strongest objection:** overlaps existing aircraft/space crises (#23/#34), and direct Cambridge source returned 403; not enough verification for a final storyboard in this run.

**Selection decision:** A over B: real documentable twist discovered by the subject mid-journey, fresh tone/era/protagonist for Nuviko queue and later second obstacle, not simply a military search. C less differentiated.
