## Three finalist pitches before writing

**A. ANOM — selected for Paper Long.** English-language viewers interested in secrecy, trust and real investigations without cryptographic vocabulary. Exact opening: “ANOM phones were not displayed in ordinary shops. Buyers got them from dealers they already trusted.” Cold interest: trusted sellers accidentally expand police surveillance. Question S003: how did investigators make criminal networks distribute the trap themselves? New turns: Phantom Secure closes and customers shift; a seller's product becomes police-controlled; hidden copies; distribution through trusted referrals and Hakan Ayik; rival services close and demand grows; 2021 coordinated takedown; legal challenge through 2025. Payoff: the police-run service turned its own customers into distributors, then revealed itself. Strongest objection: fern, Cipher and Crumb covered the same major event; the script needs a concrete referral mechanism rather than a chronological replica. Scoring before full text (authorial): value 9, hook 9, development 9, payoff 9, freshness 7, visual 8, speech UNVERIFIED, demand 7.

**B. Auschwitz escape, four prisoners, 1942 — strongest different-genre alternative.** Exact opening: “Four prisoners drove out of Auschwitz in SS uniforms. The guard at the barrier still had not raised it.” Audience: historical survival, high-stakes problem solving. Why stop: genuine life-and-death deception; what changes: prepared work detail and hatch → stolen SS uniforms and a Steyr 220 from garage → blocked gate opened on Piechowski's command → car broke down, escape continued on foot. Payoff: four successfully escaped, with serious consequences and wartime afterlife. Strongest objection: concentration-camp narrative demands great care; a heroic heist format risks trivializing the setting and reprisals. Primary source read in this run: Auschwitz Memorial educational record https://lekcja.auschwitz.org/en_15_ucieczki/sco_17.html (undated, accessed Nov 14). **Do not claim the car belonged to commandant Höss**: testimony says Paul Kreutzmann's vehicle. Concept screening value9, hook9, development9, payoff8, freshness7, visuals8, voice UNVERIFIED, demand UNVERIFIED (not developed to full video baseline).

**C. Chowchilla kidnapping, 1976 — powerful different-format alternative.** Proposed opening: “Twenty-six children and their driver were trapped in a buried van. They had to get themselves out.” Audience: rescue/true crime. Why stop: stakes; development: abducted school bus → transfer to underground van → adult and older children improvise an exit → all survive. Payoff: group rescue. Objection: can easily exploit trauma; the strongest image could exhaust premise while later consequences need care. In previous shortlist the 2026 Scary Interesting Long had ~1.13M indexed public views vs neighbor videos roughly 0.73–3.51M; not an exceptional outlier and not age-normalized. Primary source was previously read: https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/3d/111/671.html ; this run has **not reread its entirety**, so not new fully verified finalist.
