How did the FBI persuade criminal networks to sell surveillance phones to one another? Why did buyers trust those phones? And what happened when the police finally revealed who controlled them?

In 2018, American investigators shut down Phantom Secure, a supplier of modified encrypted phones. Its customers still wanted private messages. An encrypted-device seller had another product in development: ANOM. He offered it to the FBI. Working with Australian federal police, investigators turned ANOM into a service they could secretly operate.

The handsets were designed mainly for messaging. Buyers thought their conversations were hidden from police. But each message generated a concealed second copy that could reach investigators. This wasn't a break into every encrypted app. It was a specific platform under police control.

How would they find users without exposing the trap? Through dealers the buyers already trusted. Australian police later said the fugitive Hakan Ayik unwittingly recommended ANOM to associates. A customer trusted a dealer, bought a device, and could recommend it to another customer. The network grew through its own referrals.

When authorities disrupted rival encrypted-phone services, prosecutors said demand for ANOM rose. The messages accumulated. According to the US Justice Department, investigators collected more than twenty-seven million messages from a platform that sold over twelve thousand devices and services.

On June 7, 2021, ANOM was shut down. A coordinated international operation followed. Authorities reported about eight hundred arrests by the June announcement, across the investigation, not eight hundred convictions. By February 2025, the Justice Department reported roughly twelve hundred arrests.

And the story didn't finish with the raids. In 2025 Australia's High Court upheld legislation affecting ANOM evidence, without settling every question about the original interception.

The police did not need every buyer to believe the FBI. They needed buyers to believe the people selling them the phones.
