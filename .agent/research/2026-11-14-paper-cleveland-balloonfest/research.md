## Three competing story pitches (before writing)
**A — Balloonfest, 1986.** English broad-knowledge/curiosity audience; exact opening: “Nearly one and a half million balloons filled the sky above Cleveland. Then the weather sent them back down.” A first-time viewer sees a spectacular mass of balloons and a reversal. After the hook: all-morning preparation, approaching weather and early launch, brief successful spectacle, downdrafts, roadway/lake litter, obstructed ongoing search, lawsuits, distant shore. Payoff: a REAL Guinness record at the same time as an unexpectedly large cleanup problem. Strongest objection: viral retellings falsely blame the event for two fishermen's deaths and sometimes exaggerate a universal disaster; documentary copy must explicitly distinguish search interference from causes of deaths and show that witnesses remember the event positively.
**B — Dublin whiskey fire, 1875.** English curiosity/history audience. Opening: “A burning river of whiskey ran down a Dublin street. People came with cups.” Viewers ask what happened to them. Casks burst in a warehouse fire; spectators scoop spirits; testimony traces one man's drinking and fatal alcohol poisoning; 13 deaths reported from drink, not fire. Payoff: the lethal effect came from consumption rather than the flames. Strongest objection: incredibly strong one-line reveal, but without dwelling on suffering or extraneous fire-brigade detail, little fresh meaningful development for a 60–90 second Short; sober tone necessary. Source: The Liberties / Irish Times reprint and inquest, link below.
**C — Mary Toft, 1726.** English history/medical-myth audience. Opening: “A surgeon told the king's court a woman was giving birth to rabbits.” Surprising witnessed claim, rival physicians and pamphlets, doubt and confession. Payoff: a medical scandal, not biological miracle. Strongest objection: accounts disagree on who arranged and coerced the hoax, and the original evidence was obtained under threat; staging this as an amusing individual con risks victim-blaming. RCS historical summary + Wellcome original satirical artefact below.

Decision: A is stronger than B for this **specific** Paper Detour Short because the opening spectacle is followed by several distinct fact-supported reversals and a nuanced finish, rather than elongating one punchline. C has unusual imagery but important unresolvable agency/coercion questions. A still risks overclaiming fishermen causality: if script review finds the qualifier too cumbersome or middle loses attention, reject this candidate rather than inventing casualties or cut the evidence.
