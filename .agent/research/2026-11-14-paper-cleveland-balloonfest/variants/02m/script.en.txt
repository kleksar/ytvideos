A Cleveland charity released almost one and a half million helium balloons just ahead of a storm.

It was September 1986. United Way wanted to bring people downtown and set a world record. George Fraser had seen a photograph of a million balloons rising over Disneyland. He brought the man who had organized that launch to Cleveland.

Volunteers spent hours filling balloons beneath a net as wide as a city block. By the time they were ready, rain was approaching.

The organizers moved the release forward. Thousands of people watched the balloons rise around the Terminal Tower. For a moment, it was exactly the sight the charity had promised.

Then the rain arrived. Water added weight to the balloons, and the wind and sinking air drove many back down.

A family driving home saw people stopping on the highway to gather balloons. A collision involving several cars was reported.

Out on Lake Erie, Coast Guard crews were already looking for two fishermen who had disappeared the previous evening.

Now the rescuers had to search through floating balloons. Both men were later found dead, but there is no evidence that the balloon release caused their deaths.

Some of the balloons even crossed the lake. Weeks later, they were still washing up in Canada.

Cleveland made it into the record book with 1,429,643 balloons. The count tells us how many went up. It doesn't tell us where they landed.

So how did all those balloons end up on Lake Erie in the first place?
