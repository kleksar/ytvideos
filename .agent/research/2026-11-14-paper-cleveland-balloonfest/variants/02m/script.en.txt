Nearly one and a half million balloons filled the sky above Cleveland. Then the weather sent them back down.

In 1986, the local United Way charity wanted to break a world record and bring the city together.

Volunteers spent the morning tying balloons beneath a net stretched over an entire city block.

A cold front was approaching. Organizers moved the release earlier, hoping to beat the rain.

The net lifted, and a huge cloud of color curled around Cleveland's Terminal Tower.

For a few minutes, the crowd had the spectacle it came to see.

Then rain weighed down the balloons. Wind carried many toward Lake Erie instead of letting them keep rising.

They scattered across roads and waterfront. A multi-car pileup was reported on a busy lakeside highway.

Some covered Lake Erie, where the Coast Guard was already searching for two fishermen missing since the previous night.

Rescuers said the balloons made that search harder. The men were later found dead; they had disappeared before the launch.

On a nearby farm, a horse's owner said falling balloons frightened the animal. A lawsuit followed.

Others drifted across the lake and washed up on Canadian beaches weeks later.

Guinness counted 1,429,643 balloons. Cleveland had its world record, along with a lot of unwanted litter.
