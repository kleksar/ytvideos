## Finalist A — Dagen H, chosen
**Audience:** English-speaking paper-curiosity Short viewers who don't know Swedish political history.
**Exact hook:** At 4:50 a.m., the vehicles still allowed on Sweden's roads stopped on the left, moved carefully across to the right, and waited.
**Why stop:** a national rule implemented by a simple ten-minute physical act; strong legibility in handmade paper.
**After hook:** 1955 voters say NO → 1963 parliamentary YES → re-engineered junctions/buses/signs → signs unwrapped → most traffic closed → permitted restart → real Stockholm jam.
**Payoff:** the change succeeded without the feared national accident catastrophe, although a local traffic jam and some minor accidents occurred.
**Strongest objection:** early hook already gives away direction change. Remaining interest must be in preparations and surprising popular-vote reversal, not fabricated danger.

## Finalist B — Kiruna church, strongest alternative
**Audience:** people interested in unusual construction, urban change, landmark preservation.
**Exact proposed hook:** `In 2025, a church weighing more than six hundred tons rolled through the streets of northern Sweden.`
**Why stop:** huge structure moving intact on a road, a visually immediate anomaly.
**After hook:** mine-driven town-center relocation → route prep → intact church move over two days.
**Payoff:** actual 5-kilometer move completed Aug 19–20 2025.
**Strongest objection:** one spectacular image gives away most of the resolution; cause involves mining and displaced communities not easily simplified. Operator LKAB has a material interest. Still a strong valid future prospect.

## Finalist C — Cardiff Giant
**Audience:** history fans interested in public frauds and comic escalation.
**Exact proposed hook:** `Two men dug a well in New York and unearthed a ten-foot stone man. Then the farmer began charging people to see it.`
**Why stop:** a physical impossibility and instantly understandable human opportunism.
**After hook:** planted gypsum sculpture → crowds → Barnum rebuffed → Barnum makes his own copy → original exposed as a fraud.
**Payoff:** an object once advertised as petrified human remains was manufactured, and a competing fake claimed to be the real one.
**Strongest objection:** another nineteenth-century media/spectacle hoax immediately after pending #71, plus a related social-misbelief pattern in #73. No independent preference test.

**Winner versus closest B:** Dagen H has more consequential changes after its opening and a timely payoff from controlled process to messy but successful real outcome. Kiruna likely has the stronger single spectacle image, but fewer stages that add new understanding in a short narrative. This is editorial judgment, not measured popularity.

## 2026-11-17 source additions for longer versions (verified public source reading)

Verified at [the Swedish National Museums of Transport History, Högertrafik](https://www.statensmuseermtf.se/vara-museer-och-samlingar/vara-samlingar/transporthistoria/vaghistoriska-samlingarna/trafikhistoria/hogertrafik) and [The Guardian's Sept 4, 1967 report republished in 2022](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2022/sep/07/dagen-h-sweden-switches-to-driving-on-the-right-1967). Original selection and sources retained above.

- Museum sections on early proposals and 1955 referendum: multiple deferred plans, 1955 advisory result 82.9% NO among ballots, 53% turnout. Do not imply 82.9% of all Swedish residents. Parliament first retained left-hand traffic; vote for change in May 1963 was 294–50.
- Museum “Genomförandet”: dedicated commission and 24 county representatives; roughly 8,000 buses rebuilt or replaced, changes to curbs, one-way systems and junctions; about 360,000 signs replaced plus 130,000 reminder signs; new signs preinstalled under covers.
- Museum “Beslut i riksdagen” on cost: about 400 million kronor initial estimate, later above 600; special tax on registered motor vehicles. Do not quote an undisputed final cost or assign undocumented personal opinions to individual drivers.
- Museum “Massivt informationsarbete”: broad media plus contact with groups not easily reached and translated/accessibility materials; *how* to drive right was taught in the final days to reduce premature new habits. Twelve hours of TV, forty hours of radio on three channels and ten languages at switch weekend.
- Museum “Intensivt arbete”: about 20,000 workers on final road preparations. “Dagen H” times: ordinary traffic banned 01:00–06:00 except authorized vehicles, some city centers longer; 04:50 stop left then carefully move right and wait, 05:00 permitted restart, 06:00 general return.
- Guardian 1967 contemporaneous Kungsgatan reporting: cheering spectators, cyclists and filming crews caused congestion; police later restored movement; some minor accidents. Do not infer absence of all national accidents, or assert later declines causally due to the rule change.
