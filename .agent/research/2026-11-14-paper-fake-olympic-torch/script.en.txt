The real Olympic torchbearer reached Sydney Town Hall. The mayor had already welcomed a flame.

It was 1956, and the relay was carrying the Olympic flame across Australia toward the Melbourne Games.

Mayor Pat Hills was waiting in Sydney for runner Harry Dillon to bring the torch to Town Hall.

Some university students had a different idea. They painted a chair leg silver and nailed a pudding tin to it.

They stuffed the tin with kerosene-soaked underwear and lit it. Their homemade Olympic torch was ready.

Their first runner wore sports clothes, but swung his arms so wildly that the burning underwear flew out.

He dropped the torch and ran off. Barry Larkin, who hadn't planned to run, picked it up instead.

Barry wore trousers and a necktie. Once the crowd closed around him, people took him for the official athlete.

Even the police joined in, clearing a path and escorting him straight to the Town Hall.

The mayor hurried outside, took the fake torch, and started his prepared speech without noticing what it was.

While he spoke, Barry walked away, disappeared into the crowd, and took a tram back to college.

An organiser told the mayor that the torch was a fake.

Hills had to tell the waiting crowd they'd been fooled. Barry had already escaped.

About ten minutes later, the genuine runner, Harry Dillon, approached the same Town Hall steps.
