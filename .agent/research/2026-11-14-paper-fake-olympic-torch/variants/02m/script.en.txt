A student in a necktie arrived at Sydney Town Hall carrying an Olympic torch. The police had escorted him there. He wasn't an Olympic runner.

In 1956, Australia was hosting the Games for the first time. Sydney's mayor, Pat Hills, was waiting for the real flame on its journey to Melbourne.

A group of students had brought a ridiculous imitation: a silver-painted chair leg topped by an old pudding tin, with burning underwear inside.

Their first runner was dressed for sport. People laughed, and so did some police officers, according to Barry Larkin, one of the students.

Then the burning material fell out, and the runner abandoned the torch. Barry had to take his place.

He was wearing trousers and a tie. Yet as the crowd gathered around him, spectators started treating him like the expected athlete. Real police officers came to escort him.

At Town Hall, the mayor hurried outside and began his prepared welcome. He had accepted the students' imitation.

Barry left while the speech was still going on. He made his way back to college.

A relay organiser alerted Hills. The mayor had to explain the mistake to the crowd. The actual runner, Harry Dillon, arrived shortly afterward, and the real relay continued.

Barry later recalled that his college rector congratulated him the next morning. When he turned up for an exam, the students applauded.

He failed the exam. But what a story they had to tell about him. What had he done the day before?
