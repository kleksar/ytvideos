A thirteen-year-old boy in Cornwall spent two years searching beaches for a tiny black LEGO octopus.

He wasn't looking for a toy someone had dropped yesterday. This one belonged to a cargo lost before he was born.

In February 1997, a storm hit the container ship Tokio Express off southwest England.

Sixty-two containers fell overboard. One was carrying nearly five million LEGO pieces, many made for ocean-themed sets.

Among the flippers, diving tanks and swords were just 4,200 octopuses.

Nobody knows exactly how the container opened, but pieces soon began washing up on Cornish beaches.

Tracey Williams started collecting them and recording the finds. She found an octopus in 1997.

It took her eighteen years to find another.

Then in 2020, a storm exposed part of a sand dune at Perran Sands. Hundreds of pieces from the spill were discovered there.

The beach could hide old toys for years. Others remained offshore; the route of any one piece is still unknown.

In April 2024, the boy found his octopus at Marazion. He had already collected 789 LEGO pieces.

Two days later, another octopus turned up at Porthleven.

That August, fisherman Richard West hauled a LEGO shark from his net about twenty miles south of Penzance. Its dorsal fin was missing.

The boy's search was over. But the shark was evidence that beach finds alone couldn't account for the lost cargo.

Most of the shipment remains unaccounted for. So who would spend two years looking for one little piece of it?
