## Three competing fully articulated pitches

### A. London Bridge was built before its river (selected)

- **Audience:** English-language curious-history viewers who respond to practical engineering and things happening in the wrong order; no prerequisite knowledge of London.
- **Exact opening:** “In Arizona, they built London Bridge on dry ground. There was no river underneath.”
- **Why stop:** a familiar name placed on dry desert with an immediate physical contradiction.
- **What changes after hook:** old bridge replaced in London → original outer stones numbered and shipped → new five-span concrete supporting body → dressed with familiar granite → mound removed and water channel dug underneath.
- **Full payoff:** the bridge became a working crossing only after crews made its waterway, splitting the peninsula into an island; still in use.
- **Strongest objection:** moving a monument is well known; viewers may think they relocated the entire structural bridge, so script must explicitly separate original facing from new concrete core.
- **Why this one:** causal progression continues after the famous purchase; each construction step changes the site, and the answer is visible rather than only a quip.

### B. Niagara Falls was turned off in 1969 (strongest alternative)

- **Audience:** broad science/history viewers with a strong mental picture of Niagara.
- **Exact opening:** “In 1969, workers made the American side of Niagara Falls stop flowing.”
- **Why stop:** enormous familiar waterfall suddenly dry, verified photographs.
- **What follows:** temporary cofferdam diverted water to Horseshoe Falls; engineers studied rockfall; the fallen stones were helping support the rock face.
- **Payoff:** the major proposal to clear away fallen rock was rejected. The diversion was temporary, not a permanent shutdown of all Niagara Falls.
- **Strongest objection:** the spectacular first image may convey almost the entire premise; historical aftermath needs careful technical explanation and could read like an anticlimax.
- **Head-to-head:** Niagara's instant image may beat London Bridge's; London has more distinct, verifiable physical transformations after the opening and a surprising construction-order resolution. No claim of better CTR/retention.

### C. Australia fined NASA for a space station's litter (Skylab, 1979)

- **Audience:** English short-form viewers who like documented bureaucracy and dry humor.
- **Exact opening:** “When NASA's space station fell in Australia, the local council sent NASA a littering fine.”
- **Why stop:** a real municipal fine addressed to the space agency.
- **What follows:** Skylab's uncontrolled reentry left fragments around Esperance; $400 fine went unpaid by NASA; 2009 listeners paid it on NASA's behalf.
- **Payoff:** the town received the money three decades later, not from the original addressee.
- **Strongest objection:** one witty fact plus coda risks stretching a straightforward story to 60–90 seconds.
- **Head-to-head:** stronger punchline but less sustained mechanism and scene progression than London Bridge.
