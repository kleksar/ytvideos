Four empty iron hooks at the Louvre. Visitors lined up to stare at them. The Mona Lisa was gone.

August 1911. Vincenzo Peruggia walked in while the museum was closed to visitors.

He'd worked making glass cases for the Louvre. A white worker's smock helped him blend in.

He took down the portrait, removed its frame and glass case, and walked out carrying the wooden panel.

Nobody noticed until the next day. At first, staff thought it had been taken for photography.

Then its empty frame turned up on a staircase. The police questioned current and former workers.

They questioned Peruggia too, in his Paris room. The painting was hidden there in a trunk.

He said he'd only read about the theft in the newspapers. They accepted that and left.

For over two years, the portrait stayed hidden. The police had no idea where it was.

In late 1913, Peruggia brought it to Florence and contacted an art dealer.

He claimed he wanted it back in Italy. But Leonardo himself had brought it to France centuries before.

The dealer asked the Uffizi's director to inspect the painting. He recognised the original.

They alerted the police, who arrested Peruggia. The Mona Lisa was back in the Louvre by January 1914.

The theft made the painting far more famous. While it was missing, crowds had come just to see...
