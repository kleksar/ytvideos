## Decision and viewer value
One-sentence answer: U.S. marshals lured wanted people to a fictional free-football-ticket party in Washington, staffed it with undercover officers and a giant chicken mascot, then arrested guests upstairs in small groups while reception continued downstairs.

Audience: English-language Short viewers interested in true unusual social mechanisms, disguises, and historically documented absurdity; familiarity with Washington's 1985 football franchise not required. Why stop: armed chicken mascot at a party. Why stay: more than 3,000 invitations, a fake company's revealing FIST initials, staged brunch, separate small groups and emcee's cue each change understanding. Payoff: contemporary reports of 101 arrests and a detainee who still asked about football after release. Expected tone: wry documentary account, with no invented crime, personal motive or action.

## Three competitive proposals
### A — Operation Flagship, selected
Audience: viewers of bizarre but grounded historical stories and undercover schemes, including non-sports followers.
Exact opening: “At a football party in Washington, a giant chicken was hiding a pistol beneath one wing.”
Stops: absurd mascot with concealed gun; core fact grounded in UPI.
Changes after hook: baited invites → FIST acronym → realistic brunch → small-group routing → surprise/arrests → courthouse buses.
Full payoff: arrest operation, contemporaneous 101 count, mistakenly detained people freed, one participant asking whether the game was still on for him.
Main objection: 1985 sting already appears in long documentaries and a 2024 fictional-film inspiration; cold viewers might find the procedural setup predictable after the first reveal. Concealed firearm may be exaggerated if imaged as drawn/threatening.

### B — Disco Demolition Night
Audience: sports, music and pop-culture-history audiences.
Exact opening proposal: “They sold baseball tickets for 98 cents if you brought a disco record to blow up.”
Stops: upside-down promotion that rewards destruction of a record.
Changes: unexpectedly large attendance, thrown records, planned detonation, spectators on field.
Payoff: White Sox forfeited game two; 39 arrests reported.
Main objection: disco backlash has Black, Latino and queer cultural context that a purely comedic short could distort. One dominant catastrophe and sensitive editorial frame.

### C — Crash at Crush
Audience: spectacle, strange old technology and railway history viewers.
Exact opening proposal: “A railroad invited a crowd to watch two locomotives deliberately crash. Then the boilers exploded.”
Stops: paid-for deliberate collision.
Changes: temporary crash site, speeding locomotives, blast throwing metal into spectators.
Payoff: Texas historical marker records two deaths and injuries to spectators.
Main objection: limited development beyond one shocking accident; previous Paper PR #47 had already considered this alternative. Fatality count differs in some later retellings, so broader claims need care.

**Comparison:** A wins on development after a verified visual hook: a series of concrete new revelations and an ironic human ending. B is equally strong as an attention-grabber but requires delicate historical/cultural context for an honest short; C is one tragic spectacle. A could still fail an independent first reader; no invented viral score.

## Expanded 02m/05m/10m evidence audit — 2026-11-17

This addendum does not replace the common research or its nine-story screening. It verifies **new material used in the two Long versions**, distinguishes observed accounts from scripted paraphrase, and preserves 02m's unchanged source.

- **Why tickets; individual at center:** Tobias Roche, deputy marshal, noticed local scarcity of Washington football tickets, adapted a prior Hartford Boy George concert-ticket sting, proposed the new idea in September, and worked with Herbert Rutherford and Robert Leschorn. Contemporary interview with Roche, Carla Hall, Washington Post, **1985-12-17**, https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/lifestyle/1985/12/17/hook-line-38/60d7fcbb-ec63-465f-89a9-34d6d12bc66f/ . His attributed explanations are documentary statements; scripts do not invent thoughts or conversations.
- **Challenge of locating wanted people:** reported problem of changed home addresses, with forwarded mail sometimes reaching them, and avoidance of unpredictable home arrests. Washington Post, John Ward Anderson, **1985-12-16**, https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/politics/1985/12/16/football-party-proves-a-bust/6b356b0b-babb-454c-8aa4-8ad59753dc34/ . These are organizers' reasons, not an independent causal proof that every warrant could not be served in a conventional way. The 3,000+ figure is **mailed invitations**, not attendees.
- **Realistic fictional company:** Flagship International Sports Television / FIST name, season-ticket and Super Bowl drawing, more than 3,000 last-known-address mailings: same 1985-12-16 article and U.S. Marshals historical reading room https://dev.usmarshals.gov/who-we-are/history/historical-reading-room/fugitive-investigative-strike-teams-no-such-thing-free-lunch . Telephone bank, hold music, fictitious Mr. Cran (NARC backward), I. Michael Detnaw (WANTED backward), rehearsals, early arrivals before planned opening: Hall's **1985-12-17** Roche interview. Names and backward spellings are reported, not narrator-invented puzzles.
- **Personnel and staging:** 166 law-enforcement personnel overall, out-of-town officers to reduce recognition, roles as caterers/ushers/maintenance workers and mascot; official USMS and two contemporary Washington Post articles above. Small arrivals groups **12–18** and upstairs separate arrest room: Anderson. About **28** officers in the arrest detail: Anderson, not the full 166. Literal simultaneously pictured upstairs/downstairs moments would remain illustrative, not archival footage.
- **Chicken's concealed pistol:** an indexed account by Vincent Del Giudice, UPI, **1985-12-15**, https://www.upi.com/amp/Archives/1985/12/15/Fugitives-arrested-in-Redskins-sting/5929503470800/ reports a pistol under a wing; U.S. Marshals and later accounts confirm the chicken disguise and armed officers, but exact physical placement lacks independently inspected photographic evidence. **MEDIUM confidence on under-wing position; treat as attributed press account.** Do not depict the weapon drawn or used.
- **Result and distinction:** Washington Post **1985-12-16** contemporaneously reports **101 arrests**, five additional people detained and released when not wanted, two recovered guns, no reported injuries and roughly **$22,100** operation cost. UPI early dispatch says **100**; later broader accountings vary. All versions attribute the 101 report rather than asserting a universally settled total. Arrest is not conviction; mistakenly detained people were not necessarily accused of any offense.
- **Human aftermath:** One detainee was reported to ask about going to the game after release (Anderson, 1985-12-16, reported speech paraphrased); Roche reported two more wanted people called to surrender after the operation (Hall, 1985-12-17). Neither story is presented as independently recorded audio. No interview dialogue is invented.
