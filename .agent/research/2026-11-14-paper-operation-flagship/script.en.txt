At a football party in Washington, a giant chicken was hiding a pistol beneath one wing.

Inside the costume was a deputy U.S. marshal. The people handing out coffee were undercover officers too.

It was December 1985. Marshals had mailed more than three thousand invitations to people with outstanding arrest warrants.

A new sports TV company told them they'd won two tickets to a Washington football game.

To collect the prize, they had to attend a free pregame brunch. There was even a chance to win a trip to the Super Bowl.

The company was called Flagship International Sports Television. Its initials, FIST, matched the marshals' own fugitive task force.

At the convention center, visitors found sports banners, name tags, pastries, and smiling staff. The party looked real.

Officers checked the arrivals, then guided them upstairs in small groups, supposedly to collect their tickets.

A man in a tuxedo and top hat welcomed them and talked up the football game.

Then he announced a surprise. That was the signal.

Armed officers entered the room and arrested the people they'd lured there.

Outside, buses carried those under arrest to court instead of the stadium.

Contemporary reporting put the arrests at 101. Five other people were released after checks showed they weren't wanted.

One man in handcuffs even asked if they were still going to the game after he got out.
