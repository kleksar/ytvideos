A giant chicken was working undercover at a football party in Washington.

Inside the costume was a deputy U.S. marshal. Even the people serving food were playing roles.

In December 1985, the marshals sent more than three thousand invitations to people wanted on arrest warrants. Some of those people had moved, and the police hoped the letters would be forwarded.

The invitations said a new sports television company was giving away two tickets to a Washington football game. Guests could collect them at a free brunch and enter a draw for a trip to the Super Bowl.

The company's initials were FIST. Those were also the initials of the marshals' fugitive task force.

At the convention center, visitors found football banners, pastries and cheerful staff. They were expecting a game, not a police interview.

But when guests went to collect their tickets, they found themselves listening to a presenter in a tuxedo and top hat. He spoke about the game, then announced a surprise.

The police arrested them. The buses that were supposed to carry the winners to the stadium took them to court.

Contemporary reports put the number of wanted people arrested at 101. Five other visitors were mistakenly detained and later released after their identities were checked.

One man reportedly still wanted to know if he could go to the game after getting out.

The free tickets had never been real. Of all the people welcoming the guests, who would you least expect to be a federal officer?
