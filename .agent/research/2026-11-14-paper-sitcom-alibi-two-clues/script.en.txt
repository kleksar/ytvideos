A sitcom camera accidentally filmed a man who would soon be accused of murder.

In 2003, Juan Catalan took his six-year-old daughter to a Los Angeles Dodgers baseball game.

Three months later, police arrested him for the killing of a teenage girl.

Juan said he'd been at the game. He had a ticket, and family members backed him up.

Investigators weren't convinced.

His lawyer searched footage from the baseball broadcast, but Juan's face was impossible to make out.

Then a strange lead surfaced. A comedy crew had also been filming in the stands that night.

They were shooting Curb Your Enthusiasm, a show starring Larry David.

The lawyer obtained unused clips. There was Juan with his daughter, right in the corner of the frame.

It looked like the proof he needed. But the scene had been filmed well before the killing.

So the lawyer checked phone records, too.

A call connected through a tower near the stadium shortly before the murder.

The lawyer also checked other calls from that night to piece together the timeline.

Together, the footage and the phone records helped undermine the accusation.

The case was dismissed. Juan walked out after five and a half months in jail.
