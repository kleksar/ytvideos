# Packaging brief — Nuviko History / Flight 571

**Audience promise:** How did a herdman learn fourteen people were alive in a crash site that official searches had abandoned? Narrative mood: human survival, care toward the dead, practical last-mile communication, not a horror spectacle.

## Honest English title options
1. **The Note That Saved 14 People in the Andes**
2. **They Stopped Searching. Two Survivors Had to Find Help.**
3. **72 Days in the Andes — How a Stone Carried the Rescue Message**

All are hypotheses to test in proper packaging; none claims measured CTR. #1 most specific on payoff, #2 risks saturation with other survival titles, #3 should not imply note alone rescued without Catalán and helicopters.

## Thumbnail promise concepts (briefs, no image generation)
- **River gap:** two small exhausted hikers on one bank, herdsman far opposite; a small note/stone foreground in meaningful proportion. Strong question: how do they get the message across? Avoid fake mountains/river.
- **No one is coming:** tiny broken fuselage in a massive valley, a traversing trail leading away. This may resemble Shackleton imagery and needs differentiation.
- **Note and rescue:** folded note in near foreground, stranded airplane barely visible among snowy peaks; no fabricated readable note words, no misleading archival label.

**First proof:** S001—S002 show a separated herdsman, two people asking about fourteen, and official abandonment of search. **Full payoff:** S020—S024 actual note crosses river and triggers human relay/evacuation; S025—S027 close consequences and return to physical message. No result held back behind an artificial second video.
