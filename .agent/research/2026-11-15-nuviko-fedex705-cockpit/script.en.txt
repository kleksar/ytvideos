A wounded pilot banked a FedEx cargo jet until it was almost upside down. He did it on purpose.

April 1994. Flight 705 took off from Memphis with three crew members and a fourth FedEx employee riding along.

That passenger was flight engineer Auburn Calloway. The next day, he faced a company hearing over irregularities in his reported flight hours.

The crew thought he was simply catching a ride. They didn't know he had brought hammers and a speargun.

After takeoff, Calloway entered the cockpit and attacked the three men with a hammer.

Captain David Sanders, copilot Jim Tucker and flight engineer Andy Peterson were badly injured. Tucker could barely move his right arm or leg.

Calloway went back for the speargun. Peterson grabbed at it, and Sanders joined the struggle.

Tucker was still at the controls. While the other two fought, he pulled the big DC-10 into a steep bank.

The plane tilted to roughly 135 degrees. Everyone struggling behind him was thrown off balance.

It gave the crew a chance, but it didn't end the attack. Calloway fought on even after they tried to pin him down.

Sanders took over the controls, while Tucker went to help Peterson restrain Calloway.

Now Sanders had to land a heavily loaded cargo plane while injured, with the fight still going on behind him.

He requested a longer runway at Memphis and brought the aircraft safely down. Emergency responders boarded and handcuffed Calloway.

All three crew members survived. Their injuries, however, ended their commercial flying careers.

A federal appeals court later upheld Calloway's life sentence for attempted aircraft piracy.
