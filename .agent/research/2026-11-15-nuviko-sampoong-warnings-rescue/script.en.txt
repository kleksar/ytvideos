On June 29, 1995, a luxury department store in Seoul stayed open even as cracks spread across its upper floor. The managers had been warned the building could collapse. Some executives left. The shoppers weren't evacuated. How could a store that was only a few years old become so unsafe? And could anyone survive what followed?

The trouble began long before the first crack appeared. During construction, the original plans were altered to turn the building into a department store. The open shopping floors required wide spaces. Some support columns ended up smaller than the design called for.

On the fifth floor, an area once planned for a skating rink became restaurants instead. An engineering study later found that this change increased the floor's permanent load by about thirty-five percent. Heavy air-conditioning units on the roof added more weight.

Concrete floors were supposed to carry their loads into the columns underneath. But the slabs, columns and reinforcing steel no longer had enough margin for the weight they carried. Cracks were already visible upstairs before the day of the collapse.

On June twenty-ninth, the cracks worsened, and sections of the top floor were closed. The air-conditioning was turned off after complaints about vibration. Neither step removed the extra weight or repaired the weakened structure.

Engineers inspected the damage. Police later said executives knew the building was in danger. Still, the store kept serving customers. Senior staff left without announcing an evacuation. That evening, the upper floors gave way.

One wing folded down through the levels beneath it and into the basement. Customers and employees were trapped beneath slabs of broken concrete. The final toll was five hundred and two people killed and nine hundred and thirty-seven injured.

In the first days, rescue crews worked through openings under the fallen floors. After roughly two days, they reached a group of workers trapped together in a basement room. A pipe supplied air, water and food while rescuers cut a way through the debris.

Moving a heavy slab could shift other slabs onto people still alive beneath them. The search continued through the following days, as hopes of finding survivors faded. Then, more than two weeks after the collapse, a rescuer heard a faint sound under the rubble.

On July fifteenth, they reached nineteen-year-old shop assistant Park Seung-hyun. She had been working in the children's clothing department when the store fell. The rescuers opened a passage, and Park came out alive after sixteen days beneath the building.

One of the first things she asked was what day it was. Investigators later traced the disaster to excessive loads, undersized supports and poor construction. They described how the concrete floor failed around its supporting columns.

The company's chairman was convicted over the collapse, along with other people involved. But before the court hearings and engineering reports, a young worker had waited in the dark for rescue. The day Park was carried out, the answer to her question was July fifteenth.

She was the last person rescued alive from Sampoong.
