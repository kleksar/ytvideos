Why did a Seoul department store stay open after its managers saw dangerous cracks? Why did some executives leave while shoppers were still inside? And who was found alive more than two weeks after the building fell?

On June 29, 1995, Sampoong Department Store was still serving customers. Upstairs, the cracks had grown so serious that management closed part of the top floor. But no general evacuation was announced.

The danger had been built into the store years earlier. Its design had been changed to create wide shopping spaces, with some supporting columns made smaller. The top floor became restaurants instead of the planned skating rink, adding weight. Heavy air-conditioning equipment added more on the roof.

That afternoon, engineers inspected the damage. According to later court records, some managers proposed evacuating people, but the chairman refused. Closing a floor and switching off the air conditioning could not repair the concrete.

Around six that evening, one wing collapsed through the floors below. Five hundred and two people died, and nine hundred and thirty-seven were injured.

Rescuers searched the unstable wreckage. They brought people out during the first days, but the search stretched into a second week. Then, on July fifteenth, a young shop assistant was found alive beneath the broken slabs.

Her name was Park Seung-hyun. She was nineteen. After sixteen days trapped in the darkness, she asked for water and wanted to know the date. She had lost track of how long she had been there.

The company's chairman and others were later convicted. Investigators identified overloaded floors, inadequate supports and defective construction. But those findings came too late for the people who were never evacuated.

Park was the last person rescued alive from Sampoong. The answer to her question was July fifteenth.
