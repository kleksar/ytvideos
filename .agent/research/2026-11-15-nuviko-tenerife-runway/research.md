### Three finalists tested with the same questions

| Candidate | Why stop? (opening action / question) | Why remain? (verified causal development) | What is paid off? | Strongest objection |
|---|---|---|---|---|

| **Chile miners** | After 17 days rescuers discover 33 miners alive, but they're 700 metres below ground. How extract them? | Discovery → thin supply borehole → engineering/rescue capsule → individual ascents after 69 days | Rescue engineering and all 33 alive | Rescue payoff similar to existing Tham Luang #113; later drama can become repetitive shaft-drilling |

**Decision:** Tenerife wins among these three *as an editorial hypothesis*, not because raw views are comparable or because its tragedy is larger: the audience learns a surprising difference between two kinds of radio clearance, follows a documented sequence of changes in what each party believes, and reaches a concrete operational payoff. United 232 has a stronger human heroism arc but a more demanding aerodynamic explanation. Chile has the happier emotional finale but overlaps #113 structurally. Tenerife's greatest weak point is its saturated topic: packaging must promise the *mechanism of the missed warning*, not a generic “worst crash”.

## 3. Primary source and fact ledger

Central primary source: Spanish accident report as reproduced in ICAO Circular 153-AN/56, including Netherlands comments, hosted by FAA Safety: https://www.faasafety.gov/files/gslac/courses/content/232/1081/finaldutchreport.pdf . PBS NOVA's [annotated final-eight-minutes transcript](https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/nova/planecrash/minutes.html) says it reproduces the Spanish report recording transcript; PBS [program description](https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/nova/planecrash/about.html) and [safety changes overview](https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/nova/planecrash/safer.html) provide accessible narrative/impact context. Independent primary for finalist UA232: [NTSB investigation](https://www.ntsb.gov/investigations/pages/dca89ma063.aspx). Chile: [NASA history/oral histories](https://www.nasa.gov/history/history-publications-and-resources/oral-histories/chilean-miners-rescue/).

| Hard claim for proposed EN/RU script | Evidence | Caution |
|---|---|---|
| 27 Mar 1977, Las Palmas bomb and threat of another bomb caused diversion of both flights to Los Rodeos | Spanish report, history of flight; PBS about | These were earlier *conditions*, not the report's “fundamental cause” |
| Crowded parallel taxiway meant departing aircraft backtracked on active runway; KLM ahead, Pan Am behind | Spanish report and PBS recorded exchanges 16:58–17:03 | Do not label taxiing on the runway itself an illegal action |
| KLM turned 180 at runway far end; Pan Am instructed to exit at third left taxiway; remained on runway | Spanish report + NOVA transcript 17:02–17:04 | Pan Am's exit confusion was a contributing fact, **not** equivalent to permission for KLM takeoff |
| Fog reduced visibility; runway centerline lights out of service | Spanish report and NOVA 17:04:58 | Fog alone didn't cause the accident; don't use “zero visibility” as universal fact |
| KLM received **ATC route clearance** to Papa Beacon, not authorization to roll; captain began departure without takeoff clearance | Spanish report 17:05:44–17:06:21 and conclusions; PBS | Distinguish two kinds of clearance in ordinary language before introducing jargon |
| KLM first officer said ambiguous “we are now at take-off”; tower “O.K. ... Stand by for take-off, I will call you”; Pan Am said they were still taxiing; simultaneous transmissions caused squeal | Spanish report timestamps and Dutch notes; PBS NOVA audio transcript | Quoted snippets are from translated/transcribed recorder record, no fabricated movie dialogue |
| KLM engineer expressed doubt whether Pan Am had left; two pilots affirmed; aircraft continued | Spanish report and Dutch comments, 17:06 after squeal | Do not invent captain's inner motives, emotions or last thoughts |
| Collision killed 583 total; 61 survived on Pan Am, none on KLM | Spanish report injury tables and later fatalities, PBS | Some deaths occurred after initial impact; “killed instantly” would be false |
| Communication standardization and better crew coordination developed in wake of Tenerife | PBS safety overview | Don't imply a single event *created* all CRM concepts or eliminated runway risk |

**Material disputed explanation:** the Spanish report calls unauthorized KLM takeoff fundamental; the Netherlands analysis emphasizes mutual radio misunderstanding and coincidence. Do not tell the story as if the only cause was “one misunderstood word,” or as if the Spanish finding was universally rejected. The script uses observable sequence, and assigns the actionable cause — departure without takeoff clearance — without inventing hidden motives. The report mentions possible duty-time tension while Dutch comments dispute whether stress can be demonstrated; exclude confident psychological claims.

## 4. Promise → opening → payoff (Long)

**Target viewer:** English-speaking non-specialist who can immediately grasp two passenger jets on a single runway. **Visual anchor:** two 747 silhouettes separated by fog on one runway, tower with no usable line of sight. Historical images for source checking are not permission to reproduce them.

**Recommended working title:** **Two 747s, One Runway: How Tenerife Happened**. Do not describe the collision as happening *before* takeoff: KLM was already rolling for takeoff and attempted to lift off just before impact. Thumbnail editorial concept: two huge jets facing the same runway in fog; concise optional text `NO CLEARANCE`. Avoid invented near-collision eyewitness imagery presented as photographs.

**Exact opening lines in EN script:** “Two Boeing 747s were on the same runway. One was still trying to find its exit. The other had begun its takeoff roll. Neither crew could see the other through the fog.” Follow with a single concrete viewer question: how an ATC route clearance became mistaken for permission to take off and why warnings were missed. The first promise is confirmed immediately: two aircraft, same runway, conflicting actions. **First substantive step:** bombing diversion and the blocked taxiway forced an unusual layout, not generic biographies.

**Full payoff:** the audience can explain ATC routing vs takeoff clearance, overlapping radio warnings, continuing takeoff despite runway occupancy, 61 survivors, and subsequent safer terminology/crew practices. Documentary stakes are not fabricated. Proposed duration “6–8 minutes” is an **unmeasured editorial goal only**; no audio, voice analysis, SRT or MP4 performed.
