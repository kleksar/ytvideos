How could two Boeing 747s collide on one runway when both crews were talking to air traffic control? And why didn't a question inside one cockpit stop the takeoff?

In March 1977, a bomb at Gran Canaria diverted KLM and Pan Am flights to Tenerife. Parked aircraft blocked the parallel taxiway, so both 747s had to taxi on the runway. Fog kept the tower from seeing them clearly.

KLM turned around at one end to depart. Pan Am followed, told to leave at the third exit. Its crew discussed the turns and asked for confirmation. The controller repeated: the third. Pan Am passed it. The exact reason remains uncertain, but the aircraft was still on the runway.

KLM's first officer pointed out that they lacked ATC clearance. The tower then gave a route to fly after departure. That was not permission to take off. While the first officer repeated the route, KLM released its brakes.

The controller told KLM to wait for takeoff clearance just as Pan Am transmitted that it was still taxiing. The calls overlapped into radio interference. KLM kept accelerating.

But a later exchange was audible. The tower asked Pan Am to report when it had cleared the runway. Pan Am said it would. KLM's flight engineer asked the captain whether Pan Am was clear. He repeated the question. The captain said yes.

Thirteen seconds later, the jets collided. The crash killed 583 people; 61 survived aboard Pan Am. Investigators named KLM's takeoff without clearance as the fundamental cause.

The final question was not lost in static. It was heard, answered incorrectly, and did not stop the aircraft.
