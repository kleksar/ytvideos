### Three finalists (pre-script editorial diagnostics, not measured demand)

**Tham Luang, 2018 — selected, author hypothesis.**
- Proposed exact opening: “How do you bring thirteen people out of a flooded cave when the children have never learned to dive?”
- Stop: an apparently impossible extraction; viewer question: *how can unconscious people breathe underwater and be brought through narrow passages?*
- Development: discovery alive creates a harder extraction problem; hazardous route and fatality defeat an ordinary escorted dive; oxygen/monsoon make waiting risky; doctors and divers solve the panic problem through anaesthesia while accepting a new breathing/mask risk; four return, then a pause, then nine more.
- Full payoff: all 13 return alive over July 8–10, and the audience understands the counter-intuitive method, not just the known headline.
- One-sentence heart: the rescue succeeded when divers transformed inexperienced, frightened children into anaesthetized patients and managed their breathing through a submerged cave.
- Strongest objection: famously covered story; the successful ending is familiar, and a 3–5-minute summary may offer less than existing documentaries. Author pre-draft scores: audience 9 medium, opening 9 medium, development 8 medium, payoff 8 medium, freshness 6 high, visual 8 medium, speech UNVERIFIED before drafting, demand 6 low.

**USS Forrestal flight-deck fire, 1967 — strong alternative.**
- Proposed exact opening: “One rocket fired by accident — and the aircraft carrier's own bombs began exploding.”
- Stop: terrifying chain reaction aboard a ship; viewer question: *how did one rocket set off a devastating catastrophe, and why could the crew not stop it?*
- Shift: older unstable ordnance detonated faster than the firefighting team expected; consequence: later Navy fire training and procedures (not just “there was a fire”).
- Payoff: damage control kept the carrier afloat but 134 crew died; fire-safety training changed.
- Objection: compressing the disaster risks an enumeration of detonations and deaths, without an equivalent surprising viewer-learning mechanism to the sedation choice. Source: U.S. Navy historical record https://www.history.navy.mil/content/history/nhhc/browse-by-topic/ships/aircraft-carriers/forrestal.html (accessed 2026-11-15); U.S. Navy 2024 retrospective https://www.navy.mil/Press-Office/News-Stories/display-news/Article/3927776/the-heritage-hour-forrestal-fire-and-nnsy/ (accessed 2026-11-15). **Operation Gunnerside, 1943 — strong alternative.**
- Proposed exact opening: “Could a handful of skiers disable a guarded factory without firing a shot?”
- Stop: small team and difficult target; question: *how did the saboteurs enter, and what did their raid really change?*

- Objection: viewers may have already seen covert-mission tricks; risk of exaggerated atomic-bomb causation and overlap with existing espionage/sabotage editorial slate (#103, #89).
