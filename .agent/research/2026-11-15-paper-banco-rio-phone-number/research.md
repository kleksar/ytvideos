## Strong finalist comparison before narrative

### 1. Banco Río (selected, editorial hypothesis)
Audience: true-crime/survival-of-a-standoff and investigative mystery viewers. Exact intended opening: "Police surrounded a bank near Buenos Aires. Twenty-three people were being held inside." Stop reason: human hostage risk, seemingly inescapable cordon. Viewer asks: How did they leave, and how could police later identify them? After hook: not only a prepared drain escape, but human testimony, a TV-room mobile number and a spouse's separate tip. Payoff: physical escape route beat the cordon; phone contacts and a witness lead turned an escape into arrests and convictions. One-sentence core: A gang used a hostage siege to cover a tunnel escape from a surrounded bank, but phone information and a spouse's tip helped investigators identify them. Strongest objection: already heavily filmed/fictionalized; audience demand for a NEW English paper telling is unmeasured, as is whether the ethics finish slows the pace. Initial 1/3/4 = 8/8/8 (medium/medium/medium), #7 UNVERIFIED until full text.

### 2. The Shed at Dulwich (strong alternative)
Audience: viewers who mistrust online rankings and enjoy absurd consumer comedy. Proposed opening: "London's top-ranked restaurant didn't have a kitchen. It didn't even take reservations." Stop reason: recognizable food-review trust failure. Question: Can the fake listing really survive when actual diners arrive? After hook: people phone constantly; creator holds a one-night dinner. Payoff: crowds dine in an improvised room, revealing ranking illusion. Main objection: the original VICE film itself has unusually strong authorial novelty; repeating its actual staged reveal risks being a diluted retelling, and success at #1 is known from opening. Core: A fake restaurant rises to No. 1, then its creator must host real diners. Author pre-script 1/3/4=8/8/8, freshness 5, demand UNVERIFIED. Factual sources: https://www.washingtonpost.com/news/food/wp/2017/12/08/it-was-londons-top-rated-restaurant-just-one-problem-it-didnt-exist/ (2017-12-08); https://twistedsifter.com/videos/guy-gets-fake-restaurant-to-top-spot-on-tripadvisor/ (2018-02-26), original video https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bqPARIKHbN8 (no reliable public views in research).

### 3. Antwerp diamond theft 2003 (strong alternative)
Audience: heist-mystery viewers. Proposed opening: "A diamond vault was emptied. A discarded bag would point police back to the gang." Stop reason: vault access and apparently minor mistake. Question: How did investigators turn waste into proof? After hook: gate/security vulnerabilities vs physical trash trail. Payoff: mundane discarded evidence despite sophisticated entry. Objection: familiar "mastermind makes one tiny mistake" framing already covered in many heist channels; need more exact court evidence before claiming a distinctive progression. Core: Sophisticated diamond theft leads to the perpetrators through discarded evidence. Author pre-script 1/3/4=8/7/8; fails the STRONG_CANDIDATE development floor pending new angle. Sources: https://www.wired.com/video/watch/trash-foils-diamond-heist (Wired historical clip); https://time.com/7308331/stolen-heist-of-the-century-netflix/ (2025 documentary discussion).

Secondary, less developed: Hatton Garden—available footage and older thieves, but climax is broadly familiar arrest; Bangladesh Bank—stolen transfers and misspelling offer dramatic reversal, but dense SWIFT explanation needs substantive skill and a significantly different angle from existing fraud drafts. They were considered, not fully fact-cleared or scored as finalists. A separate "niche research" exercise may find stronger competitors; this is the actual bounded sample.
