## Comparison of *the finished texts*

**2 → 5:** 02m compresses Daghlian, Slotin and prohibition into a causal line. 05m adds the **documented intermediate change in safety rules**, Slotin's training of a successor and the exposure of others, preventing the false inference that no one responded after 1945. This is worth the added time. Potential 02m dropout: abstract explanation before Daghlian is named.

**5 → 10:** 10m adds Daghlian's biography and lone late-night work, a second witness perspective, more nuance on varying medical dose estimates, the 21st/same-room coincidence and the actual multi-year shift to remote critical operations. These are supported and independently comprehensible. But after the phrase **“Slotin died on May 30”**, the narrative spends more time on coincidence and procedural history; this is the clearest likely **editorial** drop-off point, not observed retention. 05m's weakest spot is its early explanation of criticality before the screwdriver. The original 34-paragraph draft loses time similarly in early neutron exposition.

**Recommendation: 05m Long**, because it answers both why the first death was not enough and what materially changed, with a shorter route from person → mistake → consequence → redesigned work. 02m may outperform it empirically; this is not a three-production approval. A strong 10m alternative remains available without replacing the baseline.
