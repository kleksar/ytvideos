## Three finalists — viewer-first case and best objection

**Elmer McCurdy.** First line: “A TV crew tried to move a cowboy mannequin in a California funhouse. Its arm snapped off, exposing human bone.” Cold audience stops for the impossible-looking prop; after that first line the question becomes **who was he, and why was he still on display?** Subsequent changes in understanding: embalming/shot wound means not a recent murder → penny and ticket identify a museum → former owner's son supplies a name → alleged brothers turn out to be carnival promoters → repeated transfers explain loss of identity → documented burial closes the story. Exact payoff: April 1977 burial in Oklahoma, 65 years after death. Strong objection: recognizable and often-retold subject; horror/corpse imagery can repel Paper viewers and the Long-form analogues do **not** show exceptional same-author performance. Short thesis: *A movie crew broke what it thought was a mannequin and accidentally closed the 65-year commercial afterlife of an outlaw.*

**Hawaii false missile alert, 2018.** Opening candidate: “Everyone in Hawaii got a missile warning. The correction took thirty-eight minutes.” Audience self-identification and terror are stronger than McCurdy's for many people; the progression is false drill recording → real alert → delayed correction without template → inquiry and process fixes. Exact payoff: no missile, preventable technical and organizational failure. Strong objection: FCC could not independently verify the sending employee's self-account, so the easy “pressed the wrong button” explanation is misleading; compressing the distinctions into a Paper Short risks inaccurate blame. Also shares media-alert/panic adjacency with Nuviko #119 and Paper #114, though not the same event. Sources read: [FCC findings reproduced by Maui Now](https://mauinow.com/2018/01/30/fcc-releases-preliminary-report-on-hawaii-false-missile-alert/), [Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/information-technology/2018/01/fcc-says-hawaii-officer-who-sent-false-missile-alert-thought-it-was-not-a-drill/), [CNN comparable](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n4tLngM0i6A) and [N+ UNIVISION](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2CRpXUURbXg), different author/language, no matched author baseline. Opening 8, development 8, payoff 8, audience-interest evidence UNVERIFIED; fully written speech UNVERIFIED.

**Great Seal bug.** Opening candidate: “The American ambassador had a listening device on his wall. Investigators couldn't find a battery.” Question: how could a silent, unpowered gift transmit audio for years? Progression gift → years without detection → hidden cavity → radio signal beamed from outside → membrane modifies reflected radio signal. Payoff: outsiders powered the device, so no detectable transmission while idle. Strong objection: once the radio principle is said the story is essentially solved; in 60–90 seconds much of the remainder is engineering exposition, with weaker human payoff. Sources read: [International Spy Museum](https://www.spymuseum.org/exhibition-experiences/about-the-collection/collection-highlights/the-great-seal/), [NSA museum](https://www.nsa.gov/History/National-Cryptologic-Museum/Exhibits-Artifacts/Exhibit-View/Article/2718563/cold-war-great-seal/); YouTube [Today I Found Out](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bXL3xBHmCLI), published 2023-07-12, public index ~265,536 views; [La Bàn Khoa Học Short](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VOLPmGo40o0), 2026-01-10, ~107,523 views, Shorts, duration UNVERIFIED. Mixed format and no baselines: no proven demand. Opening 8, development 7, payoff 7, fully written speech UNVERIFIED.

**Selection:** McCurdy has a concrete and documented **second** mystery after the broken arm — the museum tickets, a false family claim and the question of returning him to a grave — unlike a tech fact exhausted by its solution. Hawaii is the **strongest alternative** on universal relevance and direct human stakes, and might outperform creatively; it loses *this particular factual and narrow Short attempt* on disputed motive and excessive context. Compared with pending [Paper #116 Goiânia](https://github.com/kleksar/ytvideos/pull/116), McCurdy has weaker danger but a wholly different forensic dark-comedy/mystery and an unusually definite human closure. Compared with [Paper #114 Boston Mooninite](https://github.com/kleksar/ytvideos/pull/114), it is not a misunderstanding that sparks a public panic, but actual object identification. **No claim that it beats #116 in measured viewer demand.**

Each bridge changes the question: “fake or real?” → “who?” → “why still hanging?” → “what happened afterward?” The scene of the coin delivers a specific answer instead of a geography montage.
