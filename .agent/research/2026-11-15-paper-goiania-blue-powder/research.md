# Goiânia 1987 — editorial research / claim boundaries

EDITORIAL_CANDIDATE_UNREGISTERED. Research date 2026-11-15. Channel: Paper Detour, English illustrated Long 16:9. Not a production receipt, no independent review, no channel analytics access.

## Audience promise and angle

**Promise:** A glowing object from a scrapyard caused an invisible contamination emergency. We follow the object and its fragments, not a dry chronology, until Maria Gabriela's bag gives investigators something testable. A viewer learns precisely why the illness went unrecognized, how contamination spread across homes, what stopped it and what the damage cost.

**Cold-open:** How did a scrap-metal find make over 100,000 people seek radiation screening, and why did the clue arrive in a plastic bag?
**First visible proof:** owner sees the blue capsule at night (narration block 2); the public-health bag is established in block 22 and resolved in blocks 40–42.
**End/payoff:** the unsafeguarded radiotherapy source was moved through homes; Maria Gabriela's action gave authorities a physical clue. Do not claim she single-handedly saved the city.

### Finalists and objections

1. **Goiânia — selected.** Why stop: bright, beautiful powder makes ordinary neighbours sick; familiar everyday discovery meets invisible danger. Why remain: clinic abandonment → scrap removal → opened source → distribution → misleading diagnoses → Maria's bus trip → detector → stadium → human consequences. Payoff: traceable cause and effective intervention at terrible cost. Objection: serious fatal event requires restraint; visually showing radiation is a conceptual reconstruction, not visible green fumes.
2. **McDonald's Monopoly / PR #100.** Why stop: FBI disguised as film crew interviews a $1m winner. Why remain: rigged tokens → recruiters → tip → undercover filming → prosecutions. Payoff: contractor and fraudulent prize circulation. Objection: already documentary HBO McMillions and very successful English videos; the proposed Long spends several paragraphs re-explaining mechanism. Better brand recognition and broader English proven reach than Goiânia; Goiânia offers less familiar visuals and a clearer scene-to-scene escalation.
3. **Pepsi 349 / PR #102.** Why stop: national TV number promised perceived fortune, but thousands were denied. Why remain: number versus validation code → compensation → protests and legal resolution. Payoff: court distinction and tragic consequences. Objection: code explanation is abstract to show in still dioramas, and court case is an emotionally difficult ending. Goiânia has more concrete causal physical actions; Pepsi has broader consumer context.
4. **Banco Río / PR #108 — close runner-up.** The tunnel/boat escape and the phone-number mistake provide a good two-twist heist. But the 2006 story is heavily adapted in Spanish film/TV; verifying dialogue, weapons and exact amounts adds contested narrative details. It is less distinct from conventional crime-heist channels.

This is an editorial decision, not a performance ranking. If the first independent listener does not want the ending after the opening, reject rather than force a 9/10.

## Fact → primary source → boundary

1. **1985 clinic relocation, abandoned unsecured teletherapy unit, attempted dismantling, highly dispersible caesium chloride:** IAEA, 1988, The Radiological Accident in Goiânia, executive summary pp. 1–2; https://www-pub.iaea.org/MTCD/Publications/PDF/Pub815_web.pdf . In narration, never say the clinic intentionally exposed people.
2. **Opening and blue glow, fragments the size of rice grains, distribution and illness confusion:** IAEA 1988, chronology pp. 23–26, especially table II; link above. The light is reported appearance of material in the dark; do NOT draw visible coloured radiation beams, assume the entire mass glowed or claim a specific unverified physical mechanism.
3. **13/18/28/29 September dates, Maria Gabriela and worker travelling by bus with bag, detector and first evacuation:** Fiocruz / Radis, 2026-06-19, https://radis.ensp.fiocruz.br/reportagem/cesio-137/como-aconteceu-o-acidente-do-cesio-137-em-goiania/ . IAEA chronology notes some discrepancies in exact earlier manipulations; avoid precise dialogue, personal thoughts and moment-by-moment reenactment.
4. **Six-year-old daughter contaminated at family meal; Maria Gabriela among deceased:** IAEA 1988, pp. 25 and executive summary; Fiocruz timeline 2026. No graphic detail or invented scenes.
5. **~112,000 screened, 249 contaminated, 20 required hospital treatment, four deaths, demolition of seven houses:** IAEA 1988 executive summary / Part II, Fiocruz 2026. Fiocruz gives 112,800 screened. Script uses 'more than 112,000' and separates contamination from illness.
6. **Never say a nuclear explosion or reactor meltdown occurred; this was a radiological source accident, not a power plant accident.** No evidence that everyone on the bus was contaminated or that the powder itself was blue under all lighting. No fabricated quotes, exact radiation doses for named individuals, crowds in specific positions, or claims about precise visual props.
