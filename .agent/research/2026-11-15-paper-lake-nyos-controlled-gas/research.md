## Three verified finalist pitches BEFORE detailed script

**A. Lake Nyos — winner.** Initial EN line: “In August 1986, a lake in Cameroon killed more than 1,700 people in a single night.” Why stop: large human loss with no burnt buildings, no erupting volcano. Exact viewer question: how could dissolved gas kill people away from water, and could it happen again? Change after hook: stratification → buoyant self-reinforcing release → deadly valley gas → hazard remains → pipe uses rising CO2 bubbles → first pipe insufficient → expanded managed degassing. Payoff: explain the controlled, self-powered gas fountain, not falsely claim permanent safety. Objection: several 2026 creators already told versions of Nyos, even the engineering response. Why spend time: a real visible mechanism and a second unexpected practical solution, not a claim of rarity. One-sentence story: “Gas accumulated unnoticed at the bottom of a lake and suffocated villages in 1986; engineers later used the same bubbling process to vent it under monitoring.” Sources: NASA, USGS 2005/2008, 2023 review, UNESCO 2020 below.

**B. Stella Liebeck (1994).** Potential EN start: “The woman mocked for suing over hot coffee had been hospitalized with severe burns.” Why stop: widespread belief in absurd lawsuit conflicts with physical injury. Viewer question: what facts did jurors see, and what did they actually award? Development: burns and prior warning complaints → comparative fault → substantial but reduced punitive award → settlement, countering reported headline. Exact payoff: the case was not “a woman got a huge payout for being clumsy”; the judgment was cut and case settled confidentially. Objection: reinterpretation already common, legal middle mostly explanation, overlaps current Paper #100 McDonald’s Monopoly in brand. One sentence: “A case mocked as frivolous involved severe injuries, corporate knowledge, a comparative-fault verdict and a later confidential settlement.” Primary legal explainer: https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/Liebeck_v_McDonalds_Restaurants_1994 (read 2026-11-15). Further cultural comparison: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Q9DXSCpcz9E (Dropout 2016, searchable public page). Audience baseline and full viewing not verified for this finalist.

**C. Vasa (1628/1961).** Potential EN start: “Sweden’s newest warship sailed for about twenty minutes and sank inside its own harbour.” Why stop: how could a flagship fail so quickly? Viewer question: which design/conditions tipped it; how recovered 333 years later? Development: tall/narrow, gunports open, water enters; then discovery and 1961 recovery. Payoff: a very well-preserved ship with a working museum. Objection: sinking reason quickly obvious; a long middle easily collapses into technical “divers dug → slung → raised” like rejected #88 angle. One sentence: “An unstable warship sank in twenty minutes and re-emerged 333 years later as a museum object.” Read museum factual record: https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/vasa-history/facts-about-vasa ; https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/visit/exhibitions/salvaging (2026-11-15). YouTube: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BD6sPrk7QL4 (2021, 62,249 views at public index; format/age baseline UNVERIFIED), https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1a0PIhMpfLU (2020, 329,224 indexed views; different author and exposure). No fair same-author baseline assembled.

**Comparison:** Liebeck has the strongest human injustice/twist; Nyos wins for this particular English paper Long because the first answer (“CO2”) unlocks a *second* genuine mechanism, then a managed fountain solving a specific continuing danger. Vasa loses because it is too easy for the answer and recovery to exhaust its promise early. Closest waiting PR #98 Demon Core has two avoidable accidents and remote handling, but physical nuclear accidents are already in queue; Nyos' dissolved pressure/rising gas model is a distinct useful mechanism. The conclusion does not assert Nyos will outperform them in views or retention. Saturation of direct Nyos analogues is a serious negative.

## Editor's hypothesis and counterargument

**One-line promised payoff:** “The same CO2 that made a lake deadly can power a controlled pipe that removes the gas before it builds up again.” But risk isn't eliminated and the cause of the initial trigger remains uncertain.

**Strongest objection:** This is a crowded theme with PBS Terra, SciShow, Kurzgesagt and Nadia Omara all publishing during 2026, including same broad arc. Why choose Paper's version? A tight 3–4 minute causality-first story, visualizing the invisible chain and controlled fountain as a *physical model* rather than piling up facts. This is a meaningful format/method difference, **not** evidence that viewers will choose it. If an independent first listener says it merely duplicates the existing video, reject it at the gate; do not rebrand as a success.
