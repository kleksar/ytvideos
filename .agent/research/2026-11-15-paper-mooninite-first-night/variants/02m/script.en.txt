Why did a glowing sign shut down a Boston highway? And how did the man arrested over it end up being hired to light up the same city?

On January thirty-first, two thousand seven, a commuter noticed a black panel beneath an overpass. Batteries and wires were exposed. Police didn't know who had attached it, so they stopped traffic and called a bomb squad.

The squad destroyed the panel. There was no explosive. Its colored lights formed a cartoon alien making an obscene gesture.

The sign advertised a film based on Aqua Teen Hunger Force. A marketing firm had given dozens of these signs to two artists, Peter Berdovsky and Sean Stevens. They'd been hanging around Boston for weeks. Similar displays in other cities had caused no alarm.

But Boston was investigating an unknown object near busy transport infrastructure. More reports brought officers to other streets and bridges. Berdovsky recognized the sign and even filmed the response. His agency asked him to remain quiet while it dealt with the situation.

Turner Broadcasting finally accepted responsibility late that afternoon. The two installers were arrested, but their charges were later dropped after community service. The companies behind the advertising agreed to pay two million dollars for the emergency response.

Six years later, Boston planned a New Year's light show projected across the Public Library. Its organizers needed an artist who knew how to work with illuminated images.

They chose Berdovsky.

The city that had arrested him over unapproved glowing panels was now inviting him to illuminate a public celebration. Asked why, Mayor Thomas Menino said, “We're a forgiving city.”
