### Three comparable finalist pitches

**Pepsi 349 — selected.** Audience: ordinary consumers who understand the expectation of winning. Cold open EN: “A television presenter read out three digits: three, four, nine. Across the Philippines, people believed they'd just won a fortune.” Why stop: a widely announced winning number apparently failed to win. Why stay: the security code explains why, but the dispute expands through a small compensation offer, protests, fatal attacks and contrasting court rulings. Payoff: the court treated the number/code combination differently from what many viewers understood on TV. Main objection: resembles existing Paper PR #100 McDonald's Monopoly as a promotion scandal; #100 is an insider rigging scheme, while 349 is a mass public/private-promise mismatch. An existing #51 uses the Pepsi brand in a wholly different jet-advertising suit. High topic familiarity is a risk, not proof of success.

**New Coke — nearest alternative.** Cold open proposal: “Coca-Cola tested a new drink on nearly 200,000 people. Then customers demanded the old one back.” Original company history and 1985 reportage verify taste tests, backlash, return of the original formula after 79 days. Viewer value: why a positive taste test can misread product identity. Next turn: customers hoard the old product and management relaunches Classic. Objection: core revelation is already in the initial question, and the outcome is very familiar. This angle could make a tighter Short, not necessarily a stronger full narrative than 349. Sources: https://www.coca-colacompany.com/about-us/history/new-coke-the-most-memorable-marketing-blunder-ever ; https://www.history.com/this-day-in-history/april-23/new-coke-debuts-one-of-the-biggest-product-flops-in-history ; contemporary https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/politics/1985/07/11/old-coke-is-back/cfa020fb-82ba-4216-a9fb-b9a5442a8f19/ .

**Chowchilla — strong survival alternative.** Cold open proposal: “Twenty-six children and their bus driver disappeared; their school bus was found empty.” Primary appellate court opinion confirms captivity and that driver and children escaped; Inside Edition survivor coverage confirms a buried vehicle and escape. Stakes and payoff strong, but this story has many already-produced survivor documentaries. Our creative angle would need to earn its place without invented child dialogue or exploitative visuals. Sources: https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/3d/111/671.html ; https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TuzlgRobBmw ; https://www.cbs.com/shows/video/ZHXxerQf44DyyCGpGk0bRxaTdfUkquqy/ .

**Choice:** compared to New Coke, the Pepsi crisis opens a second, meaningful conflict after the immediate technical answer: whether the advertised number or the security code determined a prize, and how that dispute affected real people. Chowchilla has more direct physical tension but is less suitable for a restrained, distinct 3-minute Paper explanatory story given existing survivor productions. The chosen format stays serious; not a comic misprint story.

## Documentary claims / limits

1. **1992 Number Fever, DTI-approved rules, separate number plus code, high prize, extension**: Supreme Court E-Library, G.R. 167866, judgment text dated October 2006: https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/42523 . The court describes *approved* terms, not a measurement of every viewer's comprehension.
2. **349 announced May 25, wrong codes, disputes, court result**: same court decision; Supreme Court 2006 G.R. 146007/146295 reproduced at https://lawyerly.ph/juris/view/ca210 . The two judgments have slightly different phrasing and dates, so narration says “in cases decided in 2006”; no assertion of one universal outcome for every lawsuit.
3. **Around 800,000 caps, goodwill offer of ₱500, half-million claimants, injuries/deaths, disputed advertising understanding**: Oliver Teves, Washington Post, 1993-07-29: https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/business/1993/07/29/a-pepsi-giveaway-gone-wrong/1a6af4b1-2b6f-4e5a-9b1d-5dc6799ac2af/ . Per contemporaneous source, a bomb aimed at a Pepsi truck killed a teacher and young child; **exact city differs among later retellings**, so script does not name location.
4. **Software failure:** contemporary report attributes the explanation to Pepsi; no independently conclusive mechanical diagnosis. Script says “company blamed”, not “investigators proved”.
5. **Deaths:** independent contemporary report verifies at least a teacher and a young child killed in one attack; later sources describe other fatal attacks. Script says “other people died” without asserting precise aggregate or who ordered attacks. Many protests were peaceful; false implication that all demonstrators were violent is forbidden.
6. **Claimant views:** contemporary reporting records disagreement about whether security code had been adequately advertised; court judgments describe approved warning notices. These are different vantage points and script preserves the disagreement.

## Narrative promise and limits

Viewer promise: why announcing a true winning number could leave many people legally unable to redeem the printed matching number; how the dispute escalated beyond the technical cause. First proof at S001-S003: exact three-digit result and question about differing outcomes. Mechanism at S013; court payoff at S025–S030. **Not claimed:** intentional fraud by Pepsi, proven software root cause, automatic entitlement to prize, every participant a rioter, invented historical conversations, precise counts of deaths beyond documented evidence, or reliable current viewer demand.
