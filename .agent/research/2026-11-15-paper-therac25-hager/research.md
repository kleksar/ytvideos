# Research — Therac-25: a failure others could reproduce

Observed 2026-11-15. Paper Detour, EN Long 16:9, current remote main 5d3222c8888c775d2195c8b2949949f2d992e98d. Editorial experiment, **not** officially registered. Audience: English-speaking general-history/technology viewer who wants a concrete answer to how an apparently safe medical machine injured people, and how a professional could verify the cause.

## Audience first — what is worth watching?

Viewer question: **The screen and manufacturer said a huge overdose could not happen. How did Fritz Hager prove that it did, and what did other clinics discover?** More than a startling one-line fact: a second patient is injured, a physicist finds a timing-sensitive demonstration after hours, colleagues reproduce it, a previously unexplained injury becomes legible, and later investigations show there was more than one dangerous defect.

After hook the expectation changes from unaccountable operator error to a mismatch between displayed mode and real machine configuration. Final payoff: the test became reproducible evidence, and the corrective action needed physical safeguards, not one cosmetic keyboard fix. For the Paper audience, an immediate human stake and a visible diagnostic mechanism carry the narrative; unfamiliar technical words are explained through physical action.
