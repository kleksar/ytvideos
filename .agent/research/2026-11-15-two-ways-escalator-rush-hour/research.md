## Top three proposals compared before writing

### A — escalator
- One-sentence logline: *A walking lane helps hurried individuals, but at a heavily congested long escalator, letting everyone stand in both lanes can move more people per minute, and the policy still proves hard to sustain.*
- Opening exact: “Which clears a packed escalator faster: one side for walkers, or standing on both?”
- First listener: familiar social rule, surprise of crowd efficiency. Viewer asks “How could standing still let more people board?”
- What changes: walkers personally move faster, **but** gaps on that lane reduce boarding; identical modeled queue yields 230 vs 302 boarders; real Holborn evidence applies chiefly under high congestion; behavior reverts when enforcement stops.

- Strongest objection: a 2016 London local context, not a new personally measured A/B or a universal law. Video competition exists and Shorts demand unverified.

## Counter-signals and editorial decision

- Some low-traffic stations and shorter escalators do not share the high-congestion advantage: https://www.cibsejournal.com/news/standing-only-escalators-scrapped/.
- A fresh paper titled `Assessing the impact of rider-only escalator etiquette using agent-based modelling` (2025, abstract accessible at https://gala.gre.ac.uk/id/eprint/51899/) further emphasizes demand levels and model validation rather than universal rules.

- Audience Shorts baselines, comments (only comment counts seen), proprietary watch-time/retention, growth velocity, own channel results: **UNVERIFIED**.

Result: **provisional author STRONG_CANDIDATE**, **experimental viewer interest**. Real story is that a quick individual may make the whole crowd slower under congestion, yet the throughput-maximizing rule is unpopular and inapplicable when the escalator is quiet.
