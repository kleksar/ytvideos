Which clears a packed escalator faster: one side for walkers, or standing on both?

I put four hundred commuters in a two-minute model, using boarding rates from a real London rush-hour trial.

First, I leave one side for walkers. They're faster individually,

but gaps open between them. Two hundred and thirty people board.

Now everyone stands two abreast. It looks slower,

yet three hundred and two board—seventy-two more.

London really saw roughly thirty percent more capacity on a long, crowded escalator.

But on a quiet escalator, walking can still get me up faster.

And after London's trial, commuters went back to the old habit.
