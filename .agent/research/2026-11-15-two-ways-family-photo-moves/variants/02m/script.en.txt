I have to take six family photos before everyone sits down. Five relatives, one camera, and a list that keeps sending the same people back and forth. Should I take the pictures in that order, or change it?

I set up a fictional counting model. Every photo must contain exactly the people requested. The posing area starts empty and ends empty. Each person stepping in or out counts as one move. I am counting crossings, not filming time.

I try the list as written. First my parents pose. They step aside for Grandma and the two kids, then return for the full-family picture. Four people leave for Grandma's solo shot. She steps out, the kids pose alone, and then I call Grandma and my parents back for the last picture. Finally everyone leaves.

I count twenty-four moves. Six photos are finished, but some people have crossed the edge of the frame again and again.

I reset the same six pictures. This time I put Grandma alone first. My parents join her; then Grandma steps out for their photo. For the full-family photo, I bring everyone in. My parents leave, and the children finish with Grandma, then by themselves.

After the last exit, I count twelve moves. Same six pictures. Same five people. Half as many crossings.

But Grandma still had to leave and come back. What if I let her finish every photo in one visit? I start with the parents, add Grandma, bring in the kids for the full family, let the parents go for Grandma with the kids, then photograph Grandma alone before the kids return for their own picture. Fourteen moves altogether, but just two for Grandma.

So I haven't discovered a universal fastest photo shoot. I've found a choice: twelve total moves, or fourteen with less back-and-forth for Grandma. At the next family gathering, someone hands me another photo list. Everyone is waiting beside the camera. I have to decide the order all over again.
