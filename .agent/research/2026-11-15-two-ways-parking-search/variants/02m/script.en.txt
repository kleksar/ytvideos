Can the parking space closer to the shop actually get me there later?

I pull into a simple model car park, two hundred metres from the entrance. I can see one empty space as I drive in. Should I take it, or keep going?

I will compare two methods from exactly the same starting point. A takes the first available space. B drives all the way to the entrance, turns around in a return lane, then takes the closest available space on the way back.

My clock stops when I reach the shop door. I am using made-up, fixed speeds for this model: five metres per second in the car and one point two five on foot. I leave out traffic, parking manoeuvres and queues.

First I try A. The first space is one hundred and fifty metres from the door. I drive fifty metres, park, then walk. Ten seconds in the car, a hundred and twenty on foot. Two minutes and ten seconds.

I reset the same car park for B. There is another empty space, thirty metres closer to the door. It is only a hundred and twenty metres away. That sounds better.

But I have to drive to the entrance and come back before I can reach it. Sixty-four seconds driving, ninety-six walking. Two minutes and forty seconds.

Closer parking. Thirty seconds later at the door.

Then I change just one thing: that second space is now forty metres from the door. A still takes two minutes ten. B takes only eighty seconds. Now B wins by fifty seconds.

The point is not that one strategy always wins. Saving walking only helps if it pays for the extra driving. Without knowing which spaces are free, I cannot promise a winner.

And the next time I turn into a crowded car park, I face that same question.
