## Audience and promise
English-speaking U.S./Canada history-curious viewers; evergreen history plus November–December seasonal search hypothesis (**not proven demand**). Specific question: Why does Nova Scotia send a Christmas tree to Boston? Full answer: 1917 blast → Boston's relief delayed by blizzard → 1918 thanks → tradition renewed 1971. Never promise a mysterious crime, a prevented explosion, a lone savior or a modern eyewitness.

## Title/thumbnail pairs, meaningful alternatives
A — `Why Canada Sends Boston a Christmas Tree Every Year` / one holiday tree beside a distant historical emergency train in snow; no fake archival photo; readable conceptual contrast.
B — `The Explosion Behind Boston's Christmas Tree` / 1917 harbour ship fire and a modest Boston gift inset; don't depict a nuclear blast or imply that tree existed that morning.
C — `A Rescue Train. A Blizzard. A Christmas Tree.` / rail car with medical boxes blocked by snow, tree as small final visual; no unverified train colour or precise design.

First 5–15 sec is the answered mystery in question form; the first observed historical action is two ships meeting in Halifax's narrow wartime passage; later hook is the hidden cargo aboard a ship on fire. Payoff is the first 1918 tree, the gap, the revived 1971 tradition, and the present symbolic gift.

Risk: tree-focused viewer can feel misled by prolonged explosion story. Counter: announce both ship disaster and why it relates to Boston in opening questions, show tree first and again at ending. Winter audience hypothesis does not prove increased CTR. No borrowed photo, original event footage, actual licence, thumbnail bytes, image prompts, TTS, MP4 or publishing requests.
