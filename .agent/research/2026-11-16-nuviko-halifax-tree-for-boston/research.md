### Three substantial pitches (before complete script)
**A / Halifax** — opening: `One Christmas tree travels from Canada to Boston every winter.` First expected answer: a holiday gift, then burning munitions vessel; after opening: spectators versus invisible cargo → explosion → overwhelmed city and local response → Boston train stopped by blizzard → recovery → annual gift resumed after hiatus. Payoff: tree on Boston Common is a recurring thank-you for material help. Why viewer stops: unusual international holiday custom. Why stays: disaster-to-rescue sequence with a time obstacle. Strong objection: 2025 WBUR video on exact tree received only 2,345 views, and disaster is already covered by major channels. Seasonal hypothesis, **not proven demand**.
**B / Damascus** — opening: a dropped socket punctures an intercontinental missile; after: leak → trapped hazard → emergency response → fatal chemical explosion → weapon ejected without nuclear detonation. Payoff: a non-nuclear accident with nuclear stakes. Strong objection: safety/motives and mechanism must be carefully checked; a major PBS `Command and Control` documentary already owns the strongest angle, and directly comparable YouTube baseline was not obtained. Sources: https://www.governmentattic.org/40docs/NNSApost1980BrokenArrow_2021.pdf (official accident summary), https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/politics/1980/09/23/titan-warhead-moved-safely-to-air-force-base/1d57a98f-2a62-47fd-895f-baa13afc911b/ (contemporaneous report).
**C / Gunnerside** — opening: Norwegian commandos reach the occupied heavy-water plant without detection; after: stealth entry → explosives in critical facility → escape → facility returns to operation; payoff: real sabotage with limited strategic impact, no false "they alone prevented a bomb." Strong objection: familiar WWII sabotage story and risk of exaggerating nuclear causation; 2025 comparator had modest public views. Source: https://ahf.nuclearmuseum.org/ahf/history/operation-gunnerside/.

**Choice:** Halifax beats Damascus on a distinctive annual visual payoff grounded in a current cultural question and breaks from the existing aviation/military-heavy slate. Damascus may beat Halifax on immediate shock; it is the nearest unresolved alternative, **not rejected forever**. Halifax beats Gunnerside on a clearer human-to-human present-day consequence. Halifax's own weakness is demand and a long-format risk: 10 minutes needs nontrivial relief/reconstruction detail, not a slower recitation of the explosion.

## Primary/near-primary claim ledger (actually opened/checked)
All URLs observed 2026-11-16. References are for internal fact-check, **not narration attribution**:
- Parks Canada government background https://www.canada.ca/en/parks-canada/news/2016/07/the-halifax-explosion.html : paragraphs "Backgrounder" 1–4, collision Imo/Mont-Blanc on 1917-12-06; Mont-Blanc explosives and crew abandoned; fire approximately 20 minutes, drift to Pier 6, onlookers; shortly after 9:04; near 2,000 deaths, 9,000 wounded, ~1,630 homes destroyed; approximately 2.9 kilotons; local and Boston relief, eye/burn legacy, Richmond public housing.
- Nova Scotia Museum https://museum.novascotia.ca/stories/explosion-narrows-1917-halifax-harbour-explosion : sections Collision, Rescue; port community, destruction including Turtle Grove, two of elder Jerry Lonecloud's children; six local/regional relief trains first day, Dr. Clement Ligoure and denial of hospital privileges. Local response **precedes Boston arrival**.
- Maritime Museum of the Atlantic https://maritimemuseum.novascotia.ca/stories/vincent-coleman-and-halifax-explosion : Coleman received warning and telegraphed stop, died; "Results" explicitly says passenger train No.10 rescue claim is **disputed**; his railway warning and ensuing response supported; six relief trains dispatched same day, US relief arrived two days later. Quote wording itself varies; narration does **not** invent/direct-quote it.
- Government of Nova Scotia https://novascotia.ca/treeforboston/history.asp : Boston relief train departed night of disaster; blizzard delayed arrival until morning Dec 8; supplies and respite for exhausted staff; tree sent 1918 and revived 1971, continued annually. `https://novascotia.ca/treeforboston/` confirms current tradition and 2026-12-03 ceremonial lighting; scripts do not assert any specific 2026 tree or delivery receipt.
- Nova Scotia Archives https://archives.novascotia.ca/explosion/ : actual original planning and construction records, Hydrostone planned fire-resistant housing, dedicated housing district; possible conflict with simplified "first public housing" descriptions → scripts avoid exact superlative. https://archives.novascotia.ca/halifax-explosion/ lists 1,782 *named* dead (not final consensus count).
- Canadian War Museum https://www.warmuseum.ca/firstworldwar/history/life-at-home-during-the-war/wartime-tragedies/the-halifax-explosion/ : official death count 1,963; supports round narration nearly 2,000. 
- Nova Scotia Medical regulator historical profile https://reports.cpsns.ca/2022-annual-report/medical-history-vignette/ confirms Clement Ligoure, Amanda Hospital and relief work. National institution/treatment consequence supported by Parks Canada above.
