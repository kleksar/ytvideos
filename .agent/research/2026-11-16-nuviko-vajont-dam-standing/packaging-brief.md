## Promise, opening, evidence, payoff

- **Viewer:** English-language history viewer without prior knowledge of Vajont, Italy or the valley geography. Genre: factual human-and-engineering disaster investigation, not fictional horror.
- **Promise:** A massive dam still stands, yet it was involved in a deadly flood. How can both be true, and why did monitoring and safeguards not prevent it?
- **05m opening:** "How can a dam destroy a town when the dam itself never breaks? And how could years of measurements fail to prevent a disaster that took less than a minute?" Lead into geographic location and the concrete arch, then reveal Mount Toc's movement. Not an empty tease.
- **First evidence in the narration:** clearly distinguish the **dam, lake, mountainside, downstream town**. Avoid placing the town directly against the water wall or implying it was built as a flood-control dam. First concrete historical warning is nearby Pontesei, followed by the old slide discovered on Mount Toc.
- **Development visible in packaging:** rock mass / moving slope → smaller 1960 landslide → bypass tunnel (for obstruction, not the surge) → physical model assumptions → 1963 acceleration → rapid collapse and overtopping.
- **Payoff:** the arch did not burst; the mountainside displaced water over it. Around 1,900 people died in the towns below; post-disaster inquiry documents decisions. No false "dam exploded" or claims of never-before-seen evidence.

## Where audience promise meets plot

- First 15–25 seconds: question and spatial setup; the arch's continued existence is stated, then Mount Toc appears as active risk.
- Middle: a measured ascent in actual warning and decision complexity, not generic mystery music.
- Main event: 22:39 on October 9, 1963; rock and lake wave. **No invented sound of a dam rupturing**.
- Resolve: destruction vs intact arch, and what inquiry could investigate. Last line brings focus from standing wall back to the actual danger. A separate 10-second long end-screen **after** narration is required by Nuviko's ChannelSpec, but no such media is planned/produced here.
