## Three competitively developed proposals

**A — Vajont, Italy, 1963.** First line: "How can a dam destroy a town when the dam itself never breaks?" First image: real-world reconstruction of a standing concrete arch above the valley; not a photo masquerading as archive. After hook: nearby reservoir warning in 1959; ancient slide identified; 1960 partial slide; bypass tunnel for the wrong kind of consequence; scale model whose assumed slide speed matters; rising slope movement and water management; rock fall; wave over crest; rescue and inquiry. Payoff: the dam held; the system protecting the valley failed. Strong objection: many prior "Vajont catastrophe" documentaries, and a structure-focused treatment may feel like engineering lecture. **Mitigation:** follow concrete decisions and the town's fate, not a catalogue of dam dimensions. Evidence: scholarly and Italian-government sources below.

**B — Aberfan, 1966.** First line: "Why was a mountain of coal waste placed above a school, even after people complained?" First image: the school, colliery tip, and hillside as separate historically grounded objects. After hook: dumping/underlying springs → collapse → difficult rescue → inquiry and incomplete accountability. Payoff: a foreseeable disposal practice, not a random freak storm. Strong objection: 144 deaths including children; re-enactment risks sensationalization, and the basic tragedy is revealed almost immediately. 2026 60th-anniversary coverage makes it a serious competitor, arguably **stronger than Vajont for immediate human empathy**, but less suitable than Vajont for a 10-minute visual causal investigation without a careful survivor-centered source pack. Sources: [Welsh Government 60th commemoration](https://www.gov.wales/wales-to-mark-60th-anniversary-of-the-aberfan-disaster), [AP, 21 Oct 2026](https://apnews.com/article/383cba4c5e0678844681107c8e1fc691), [Glamorgan Archives](https://glamarchives.gov.uk/blood/Aberfan/).

**C — Texas City, 1947.** First line: "Why did people gather to watch a ship burning at the dock?" First image: Grandcamp smoking at berth, with people kept observational rather than invented. After hook: fire in cargo hold → first explosion at 09:12 → damaged port and neighbouring fires → second ship's dangerous cargo → rescuers/accountability. Payoff: how one port fire produced a chain of destruction. Strong objection: documentaries about ammonium nitrate port explosions are crowded, and scientific chemical specifics need care. Its **second explosion is stronger than Vajont's final sequence as an extra plot reversal**, but the special "safe-looking intact wall" paradox better reframes a common viewer assumption. Sources: [Texas City official First Explosion](https://texascitytx.gov/464/First-Explosion), [US Army Corps 2008](https://www.usace.army.mil/Media/Fact-Sheets/Fact-Sheets-View/Article/4463139/114-the-corps-responded-to-one-of-the-worst-industrial-accidents-the-texas-city/).

Winner A **by angle and causal payoff, not proven demand**. Strongest losing alternative B (for empathy) and C (for a two-explosion story) remain real future candidates, not rejections. Vajont's weaknesses: a central fact is immediately known from the hook; the body must add actual decisions. All scripts answer the central question rather than hiding the existence of the wave until the ending.

## Vajont evidence, read/verified text not merely snippets

Every cited primary/scientific page below was inspected 2026-11-16 through available web content; page text is evidence, not license to copy phrasing or media. Do not treat 2026 modern retrospective descriptions as contemporary 1963 eyewitness interviews.

1. [Italian Civil Protection: The Vajont landslide](https://servizio-nazionale.protezionecivile.gov.it/en/pagina-base/vajont-landslide/): 1963 landslide, dam, Longarone, official casualty register 1,917, 400 not found. **Typographical discrepancy:** English page accidentally prints "270 m³" where scholarly literature consistently reports about 270 *million* m³. The scripts use scholarly magnitude.
2. [Italian national Fire and Rescue Service, published 2026-10-09](https://www.vigilfuoco.it/chi-siamo/memoria-storica/notizie-storiche/la-tragedia-della-diga-del-vajont): collapse at 22:39 on 9 Oct 1963, overtopping and Longarone devastation; 850+ rescuers, nearly 300 machines, three helicopters, **73 people rescued by fire service**, >1,000 recovered bodies. Its wave-height and landslide-volume estimates differ from papers; scripts avoid a single asserted wave height.
3. [Ghirotti et al., Italian Journal of Engineering Geology and Environment, 2013](https://rosa.uniroma1.it/rosa02/engineering_geology_environment/article/view/1300): 265.5m dam; about **270 million m³** of rock; rapid collapse under one minute; pre-existing slide surface and clay-rich layers 0.5–18cm; slow creep for 2–3 years; dam remained intact. Journal abstract visibly inspected.
4. [Dykes & Bromhead, Journal of Mountain Science, 2022](https://jms.imde.ac.cn/en/article/doi/10.1007/s11629-021-7098-3): multi-factor explanation and historically less-understood lake impact hazards; nearby Pontesei fatal landslide; differentiated causes/uncertainty. Publisher abstract and accessible indexed sections; avoid claiming all model parameters certain.
5. [David Bressan, Scientific American history-of-geology blog, 2011-10-09](https://www.scientificamerican.com/blog/history-of-geology/october-9-1963-vajont/): early filling, cracks, Nov 1960 slide around 700,000m³, bypass tunnel, displacement measurements, model limitations, last lowering, final collapse; identifies conflicts between interpreted geological scenarios. Author research commentary; secondary scientific narrative.
6. [Peer-reviewed modelling, Scientific Reports 2024, public indexed PMC version](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11554770/): scale model and its assumptions, water elevation 700m *above sea level*, actual event slightly above 700m. Full page displayed an anti-bot page on direct fetch; **do not claim full paper read**; indexed passage only, corroborated by Bressan and Dykes & Bromhead.
7. [Treccani, Tina Merlin, published 2018-10-08](https://www.treccani.it/magazine/atlante/cultura/Tina_Merlin_la_voce_che_denuncio_i_rischi_del_Vajont.html): journalist reported warnings and residents' complaints, court acquittal in 1960, follow-on warning in 1961. ### Claim boundaries and contraindications

- What broke: **mountain slope**, not concrete arch. "Standing dam" is a fact; claiming engineers intended to protect Longarone specifically by holding landslide-generated waves is misleading. Narration says the overall system failed to keep the downstream valley safe.
- Volume and timing: roughly 270 million m³, less than one minute (scholarly approximation), not the separate official 2026 firefighters' rounded 300 million figure.
- Wave estimates vary significantly across reputable models and interpretations; no single precise wave height is narrated. The likely existence of a pressured air wave is not dramatized as a measured cause of specific deaths.
- Geology: clay layers and ancient movement are supported; any exact one-factor explanation of acceleration would exceed consensus. Lowering the surface water does **not automatically** relieve groundwater pressure immediately.
- Journalistic and legal responsibility: Merlin's acquittal and investigations are sourced, but scripts do not invent unique guilt, defendants' intent or survivor dialogue.
- Images, footage, archive rights **not acquired**. Text-only proposals; no reference bytes or media claimed.

## Editorial rationale and exact promise

Viewer promise: "The dam remained standing. Why did a town below it disappear?" First on-screen evidence: standing arch, mountain and the downstream valley as identifiable separate real-world features. In **2m**, minimal geological warning → collapse → overflow → outcome. In **5m**, a meaningful causal ladder Pontesei → ancient slide → 1960 movement → bypass and physical-model assumptions → October event → inquiries. In **10m**, additional verified functions: public warnings/Tina Merlin, deep clay layers, lake-groundwater lag, model's risk of mistaken slope-speed assumptions, sourced rescue scope and archive/attribution. None relies on invented fictional dialogue or feelings.

Strongest objection to the winner: its answer ("a landslide pushed water over the top") is already visible in competing titles. The 5m earns attention only if the *chain of failed precautions* remains surprising; this is an author hypothesis and must be tested by a fresh reader. Film packaging must not promise footage of a broken dam or invented survivors. All sources are for internal research, not narration attribution or images.
