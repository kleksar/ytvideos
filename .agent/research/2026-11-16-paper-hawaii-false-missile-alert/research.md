## Three comparable *story proposals*, before full writing

**A — Hawaii, chosen.** *Exact opening:* “How could an entire American state be warned of a missile attack when there was no missile?” **First image idea:** a paper smartphone in a normal Hawaiian morning, with an unmistakable warning; physical paper emergency-room clock appears later. **After hook:** surprise drill crosses shift change → recording contradicts itself → live/test share controls → authorities learn truth → social post is not public alert → no ready retract template. **Payoff:** first warning took seconds; the same agency lacked a prepared public correction, causing 38 minutes. **Main objection:** publicly known outcome and large news-channel views are old, not evidence for a fresh English documentary; 10 minutes risks system-explanation drag.

**B — Chilean mine rescue, strongest alternative.** *Exact proposed opening:* “A drill finally found thirty-three missing men alive. Getting them out would take another fifty-two days.” **After hook:** proof of life, supply boreholes and underground living conditions, preparations for a narrow rescue capsule, final extraction. **Payoff:** all 33 reached the surface after 69 days. **Objection:** strong human arc, arguably *stronger for 10 minutes*, but heavily documented on video and highly recognizable; stronger human stakes do not prove better future performance or fit to a 2-minute Short. Sources: AP rescue clip and contemporaneous Guardian/Al Jazeera report below.

**C — NORAD training-data alarm (1979).** *Exact proposed opening:* “A military computer showed a Soviet attack that wasn't happening.” **After hook:** simulation input appears as threat, personnel check independent indicators, false alarm resolved before a statewide public message. **Payoff:** why independent confirmation prevented escalation. **Objection:** enormous stakes but weak directly verifiable human eyewitness arc for a Paper Short; harder to distinguish from common nuclear near-miss compilations. Source: NPS 2018 film excerpt below. Full institutional incident report not read.

**Why A over B:** A has a very specific *second causal reversal* after the audience already knows the launch alert was false: those who knew the answer could not send it through the original channel. It supports a compact complete Short and a meaningful 5m investigation, and modern phone alerts are a recognizable object. B has higher direct human stakes and probably better 10m range. This is a subjective editorial choice, not an audience measurement; B remains a valid future competing proposal.

## Promise and editorial limit

Audience promise: **not** “was a missile real?” (given away immediately), but “why did correction take 38 minutes even though officials knew within minutes?” Opening object: authentic-looking public phone-warning *reconstruction* in paper, clearly not presented as recovered screenshot. Payoff: ready-to-send threat vs missing ready-to-send correction; three failure points rather than one villain.

Sources support all key claimed times and causal safeguards, but exact audience perception, scenic details and individual internal thoughts are not filled in. Copyrighted source video clips, transcripts and photos are not licensed for reuse; no media imported. An independent reviewer could reasonably reject the 10m or the whole idea for repetitiveness — that is a real remaining quality gate.
