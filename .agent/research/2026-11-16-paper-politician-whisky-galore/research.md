## Three finalist treatments

**Selected — SS Politician:** 
- First scene: a cargo ship runs aground with **264,000 bottles** aboard.
- Question: why did a rescue become a court case, and why was the wreck deliberately destroyed?
- Midcourse changes: crew escape -> bottles moved ashore -> islanders/customs disagree on salvage -> searches and imprisonment -> Jamaican notes add a second investigation -> destruction of the wreck.
- Payoff: legal conflict does not destroy the story; an actual recovered bottle enters a museum; movie is a fictional adaptation, not evidence of specific dialogue.
- Strong objection: feature films' cultural fame is not verified demand for a new YouTube retelling, and the story is familiar in Scotland. Distinct angle puts the **conflicting meanings of 'salvage'** and documentary object at the center, rather than reproducing the film.

**Strongest alternative — Chaplin's coffin:**
- First image: empty cemetery grave two months after famous film actor's death.
- Midcourse: demand -> widow/lawyer stall while police trace changing public phones -> roughly 200 booths monitored -> two men caught -> forgotten field location recovered with detectors.
- Payoff: coffin recovered, grave reinforced with concrete; family not forced to pay ransom.
- Objection: grave violation and threats to children demand restrained treatment; 10m script risks lengthy procedural detail. Swiss National Museum https://blog.nationalmuseum.ch/en/2023/06/when-chaplins-corpse-was-kidnapped/ (2023-06) and contemporary reporting https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/politics/1978/05/18/swiss-police-recover-body-of-chaplin/584fbb0d-e807-4547-bf89-6634d0eb437d/ (1978-05-18).
