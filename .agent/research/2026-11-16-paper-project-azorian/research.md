# Research — Paper Detour: Project AZORIAN, K-129 and the Glomar Explorer

Research / editorial screening on **2026-11-16**. Author's reading and decision; **not** audience analytics, independent review, an official run, or authority to produce.

## Decision and the audience

English-speaking Paper Detour audience: a comprehensible physical spectacle that changes into an espionage failure with a human consequence. **Selected angle:** a vessel built to disguise a CIA submarine recovery passed the test of nearby Soviet observation, partially failed its extraordinary lift, and then lost its secrecy when documents from an office burglary reached reporters. **Why start:** “The supposed mining ship was stealing a submarine from three miles down.” **Why stay:** how the claw was hidden, the observers, broken load, burial, and seemingly unrelated robbery. **Full payoff:** only part recovered, six crewmen buried, and the planned return stopped by exposure. This is not a claim to have recovered the whole submarine.

Alternative editorial hypothesis: a 2m Short may be the strongest entry to the story; 5m can add the hidden-barge engineering and the second failure; 10m risks losing urgency unless real independent new events justify its time. The 5m draft is recommended only after author review, never measured viewer demand.

## Three competing final pitches, equal editorial treatment

**A — Project Azorian.** Opening: “In 1974, a ship that claimed to be mining the Pacific was trying to steal a Soviet submarine.” After hook: it must hide the huge claw; Soviets come close; a section snaps; the CIA considers returning, then a break-in and press revelations kill the second operation. Payoff: half-success in the ocean, failure of secrecy on land, six Soviet sailors buried respectfully. **Strong objection:** specialized engineering could become a machinery lecture; public accounts leave exact intelligence finds classified. Remedy: make two distinct tests, physical recovery and preservation of cover, drive the 5m, and refuse precise unverified finds.

**B — British Airways 5390.** Opening: “The captain was hanging outside the cockpit, and the plane still had to land.” After hook: the crew restrains him while an officer takes over; pilots choose an emergency landing. Payoff: the captain survives. **Strong objection:** survivor experience more immediate than Azorian, perhaps stronger for a 2m Short; but known retellings and focus on one continuous emergency make verified new layers for 10m harder. Do not invent inner thoughts, injuries or cockpit dialogue.

**C — Damascus Titan II accident.** Opening: “A tool dropped in a missile silo started a crisis around a nuclear-armed missile.” After hook: leak, response decisions, explosion, consequences for workers and policy. Payoff: distinguish serious rocket-propellant accident from the nuclear explosion some thumbnails falsely promise. **Strong objection:** human stakes higher than Azorian, and the PBS documentary has authentic material, but research/rights scope and likely audience misunderstanding require deeper checking before a six-text pack. It remains a valid **future** candidate, not a rejected owner topic.

**Selection:** A offers the most clearly sourced independent changes after the hook and between 2→5→10: loss, engineering cover, submerged loading, observer encounter, fracture, funeral, robbery, public exposure. B is the strongest quick-Short alternative; C has the strongest grave-stakes documentary alternative. The choice is editorial, **not** measured comparative CTR/retention. It could lose to B in actual audience tests. No candidate promoted on “rare” or “easy to draw”.

## Fact ledger — opened and read, 2026-11-16

- **AZ1 — CIA Museum**, [Project AZORIAN](https://www.cia.gov/legacy/museum/exhibit/project-azorian/), sections “About Project AZORIAN,” paragraphs on 1968 K-129, 16,500 ft / three nuclear missiles, 1970 engineering decision, Hughes cover/manganese nodules, moon pool, claw transferred from submerged barge, 60-foot pipe sections, arrival **July 4, 1974**, Soviet observers, broken section, **six bodies**, burial, film to Yeltsin in 1992; June 1974 office burglary, Feb 7 1975 Los Angeles Times and second expedition abandoned. **Supports all core scripts.** Agency retrospective is firsthand institutional account, not an independent measure of its intelligence success. It says the section broke **about halfway** up.
- **AZ2 — CIA**, [The Exposing of Project AZORIAN](https://www.cia.gov/stories/story/the-exposing-of-project-azorian/) (published **2020-03-17**), paragraphs beginning “On March 18, 1975,” “Almost immediately after,” through “With Glomar's cover blown.” Confirms Colby appeal, Feb 7 newspaper, **March 18, 1975** Jack Anderson television, June 1975 Soviet site watch, and White House canceling the next attempt. **Disagreement:** this CIA page says the breakup happened **one-third** of the way up, unlike AZ1's halfway. Narration intentionally says only “partway through ascent”; do not invent exact elevation/mechanical failure.
- **AZ3 — Smithsonian Magazine / International Spy Museum**, [During the Cold War, the CIA Secretly Plucked a Soviet Submarine From the Ocean Floor Using a Giant Claw](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/during-cold-war-ci-secretly-plucked-soviet-submarine-ocean-floor-using-giant-claw-180972154/) (published **2019-05-10**). Notes alternative underwater buoyancy scheme; 618-foot Glomar; too wide for Panama Canal, traveled around South America; claw “Clementine,” secret HMB-1 transfer, Soviet observation and partial lift; serious uncertainty on what intelligence was recovered. **Supports 10m-only details.** Secondary museum reporting; direct copied phrasing avoided.
- **AZ4 — CIA retrospective**, [Honoring the Mission of Project AZORIAN](https://www.cia.gov/stories/story/honoring-the-mission-of-project-azorian/), cross-checking agency account of recovered remains/participants. Archive availability and other detailed claims require review before production.
- **Exclusions / disputed**: cause of K-129 sinking is not established; exact part lost, depth at breakup, specific codebooks and nuclear warhead recovery are not publicly secure; do not assert any of these. Claims of success are not neutral measurements of classified intelligence results. Burial is not a rescue; all crew died years earlier. The documentary never claims the U.S. secretly acquired the **whole** K-129.
