I have eight birthday invitations to finish. The method that gives me the first sealed envelope fastest might lose the race for the whole stack. Which way should I work?

This is a simple made-up model, not a real timed experiment. I write a name for ten seconds, put a card in the envelope for six, and seal it for four. Changing between those three setups costs five seconds each time. One person, eight identical cards, and the same rules for both methods.

With A, I complete one invitation before touching the next. I write, switch, insert, switch, seal. First envelope ready in thirty seconds. If someone needs one right now, that's a useful lead.

But I have seven more to finish. Each new envelope sends me back to the writing setup. Across eight invitations, A makes twenty-three switches. The whole pile takes four minutes thirty-five.

I reset the desk. With B, I write all eight names first, insert all eight cards next, and seal all eight last. Just two setup changes. The first sealed envelope doesn't appear until two minutes twenty-two. By then, A has four finished.

And then the finish line flips. B completes all eight at two minutes fifty. A doesn't finish its fifth until that same moment.

The actual writing, inserting and sealing takes two minutes forty under either method. The difference is the repeated setup changes. For one urgent invitation, I'd choose A. To finish all eight, I'd choose B.

So when another pile lands on my desk, the question isn't simply which method starts faster. It's what I need finished first.
