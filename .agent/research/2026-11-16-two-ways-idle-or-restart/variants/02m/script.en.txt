My car is parked, but the engine is still burning fuel.

I'm waiting to pick someone up. I can leave the engine running, option A, or switch it off and restart once, option B. Which uses less gas? Pick one before I run the numbers.

I'm not claiming I did a road test. This is a simple model: the same warmed-up gasoline car, the same safe parking place, and the same ninety-second wait. No air conditioning, no extra trips. I assume it burns three-tenths of a gallon per hour at idle.

For the restart, I use a ten-second idling equivalent. That's a research-based rule of thumb, not a measured result for my car.

Option A runs for all ninety seconds. It burns about twenty-eight millilitres.

Option B uses no fuel while off, then about three millilitres for the assumed restart. That's roughly twenty-five millilitres saved. B wins this model.

Now the number that surprised me: repeat the stop a hundred times and the saving is only about two and a half litres. At three dollars fifty per gallon, that's roughly two dollars thirty-three. Not a fortune.

There is a much more important catch. This is a parked-car comparison, not permission to turn the ignition off in moving traffic. The US Department of Energy specifically excludes traffic from its manual switch-off advice. My car, weather, battery and need for heating or cooling can change the decision.

So my result is practical, but narrow: for a predictable wait while safely parked, switching off usually saves fuel. For a queue that may move at any moment, safety comes first.

And whenever I park safely for another pickup, I notice something I used to ignore.
