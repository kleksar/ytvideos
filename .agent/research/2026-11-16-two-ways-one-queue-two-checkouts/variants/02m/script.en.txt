Would one long checkout line get six shoppers out faster than two short lines? I drew both layouts, and the answer surprised me twice.

This is a model, not a test in a real supermarket. I start with the same six people, two identical cashiers, and no new arrivals. The first cart needs five minutes to check out. Each of the other five needs exactly one. I'm shopper six.

I want to know when the last shopper leaves. But I also care when I leave. Pick A: one shared line feeding either cashier. Or B: two separate lines, three people each.

In A, the slow cart takes the left register for five minutes. The right register finishes one small basket every minute. It serves shoppers two, three, four, five, then me. At minute five, I'm done. So is the slow cart. The entire group is out.

Now reset everything for B. The left line has shoppers one, three and five. The right has two, four and me. I reach the right register at minute two and leave at minute three. That's two minutes sooner for me.

But look left. Shoppers three and five are trapped behind the five-minute cart. They don't finish until minutes six and seven. The last person leaves at seven, not five.

So the shared line wins the group finish by two minutes. Yet the separate lines win for me by two minutes. Neither clock was wrong. I was asking two different questions.

In an actual store, cashier speeds, walking distances and arrivals can change the answer. These are only the stated rules of this model.
