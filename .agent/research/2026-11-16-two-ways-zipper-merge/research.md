# Zipper merge / Early merge — shared research
Observed: 2026-11-16. Channel key: `two_ways_lab`, canonical main profile **Two Ways Lab** (not unmerged Oddly Drawn PR #138). Editorial-only, no registered runtime.

## Why a viewer would stop, stay, and get an answer
Every driver recognizes the resentment of watching cars use an apparently unfair empty lane. Hook is the empty lane next to a stationary line; the question is whether joining early is polite or counterproductive. Development moves from **two methods** to an **uncomfortable double result**, a short physical queue but unchanged bottleneck clearance, then a traffic-density exception. Payoff gives conditional road guidance rather than an invented guaranteed winner. Original differentiator from the many public service explanations is the split between **road occupied upstream** and **actual throughput**, with two observable clocks. Strongest objection: popular existing explainers already cover zipper merging and the illustration's perfect cooperation oversimplifies reality.

## Search funnel and decision (same-day, bounded)
Considered 10 different everyday problem directions without claiming ten complete source reviews. Not three framings of one story:
1. Merge at first warning vs zipper at the cones — **finalist; selected experimental hypothesis**. Hook: "Am I cutting in?" Midpoint: 160 m versus 80 m but 40 s versus 40 s. Payoff: how to use signs and heavy-traffic context. Objection: strong competition and unverified new-video demand.
2. Dishwasher vs hand washing — **finalist**. Hook: which wastes more water? Middle: full-load vs partial-load changes comparison; wattage and hot water complicate. Payoff: conditional consumption. Objection: widely covered, local equipment dominates; independent equal-input measurements unavailable here. The previous editorial sweep located the ENERGY STAR guidance and a YouTube experiment, but no new measurements were performed in this run.
3. Start dry pasta in cold water vs drop into boiling water — **finalist/closest alternative**. Hook: can skipping the boil save time? Middle: pan/water volume, type of pasta and texture. Payoff: dry pasta can work differently from filled/fresh pasta. Objection: Delish and MinuteFood already cover useful exceptions; independent cooking measurements absent, making a fresh, credible 10-minute model difficult.
4. Wash clothes cold vs hot — initial candidate, rejected for uncontrolled fabric/stain/detergent inputs.
5. Grocery shop by store aisle vs nearest next item — potentially fair modeled routes, but existing channel backlog and very layout-dependent.
6. Elevator nearest cabin vs closest destination — strong optimization paradox, but building dispatch policy and accessibility assumptions unresolved.
7. Put ice in a drink as one big cube vs several small — physical model depends on surface/contact and comparative claims without lab conditions.
8. Unplug chargers vs leave them plugged in — high practical value but low plausible dramatic escalation and device-specific power draw.
9. Quick reply to every notification vs batch replies — attractive workplace question; interruption cost and personal consequences would be fabricated without data.
10. Fold clothes vs roll clothes in a suitcase — visual A/B, but material, suitcase and measurement uncertainties plus saturated public examples.

Top three were contrasted on genuinely recognizable stakes and independent payoff. Zipper merge narrowly beats cold-start pasta for a conflict the viewer has personally encountered, a simple exact arithmetic mechanism, and a counterintuitive **two-metric** ending. It does **not** beat the competing videos on measured reach: audience demand for this new treatment remains UNVERIFIED.

## Primary/factual evidence (opened public pages; scripts' factual claims)
- [MnDOT zipper merge](https://www.dot.state.mn.us/zippermerge/) (read 2026-11-16): in heavy/slow traffic with a lane closure, use both lanes until designated merge, alternate; reduced backup **by as much as 40%**; **early merging makes sense at highway speeds with no backup**. This is agency guidance with context, not a trial measured by our script.
- [INDOT zipper merge](https://www.in.gov/indot/safety/zipper-merge/) (read 2026-11-16; published page ~2026): defined merge point, alternate, benefits include speed-difference reduction, shorter queues by up to 40%, exception when freely flowing, signs required where technique is used. Exact local signs govern.
- [WisDOT zipper merge](https://wisconsindot.gov/Pages/safety/safety-eng/ZipperMerge.aspx) (read 2026-11-16): heavy slow traffic distinction, alternating vehicles, up to 40% shorter backups in suitable work zones; early merging with free flow.
- [AAA zipper merge](https://www.acg.aaa.com/connect/blogs/4c/auto/zipper-merge-keeps-traffic-moving) (2024-era article, read 2026-11-16): cooperation essential; do not race ahead to brake, conditional guidance. Secondary interpretation, not source of our model values.

All sample quantities in narration are **author-created deterministic model inputs**, not sourced transport observations: 20 stationary identical cars; 8 m occupied per car including gap; 1 car passes the remaining lane per 2 seconds; same 20 cars, service rate and starting scenario for A/B; cooperative turns. Therefore one lane: 20*8 = **160 m**. Two balanced rows: 10*8 = **80 m** maximum queue reach. In either case 20*2 = **40 s** group clearance; these are idealized counts (no acceleration, travel to exit or change in order). The 100 m side street is hypothetical illustration and NOT real junction safety evidence. At 30 cars/min capacity, 20/min arrivals produce no long-term growing backlog; 35/min arrivals produce +5 waiting cars/min, +20 after 4 min (160 m in one row, 80 m spread evenly in two), **only under fixed rates and ignored transients**. Disrupted 3 s service is an explicit *changed assumption* => 20*3 = 60 s, **not a controlled A/B result**.
