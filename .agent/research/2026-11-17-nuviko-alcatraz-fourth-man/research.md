## Three developed pitches (equal questions)
**Alcatraz:** stop — painted faces seem to be sleeping people, but the three men have vanished from an island. Stay — learn vents, hidden roof path, raincoat raft, and why West cannot pass his own grille. Payoff — escape verified to shoreline; survival forever unproved. Opening: “How do three prisoners vanish from an island watched day and night?” Strongest objection: famous topic and historically unsolved survival; must avoid false miracle reveal or sensational invented final proof.

**Flight 232:** stop — plane with engines working still cannot steer; stay — crew uses uneven thrust for control, unpredictable descent; payoff — life-saving effort and genuine casualties; first question “How can an airliner lose all three hydraulic systems at once?” Source: NTSB. Strongest objection: tragedy-heavy aviation niche and several nearby Nuviko flight draft PRs, not a lack of intrinsic interest. Remains eligible later.

**Eagle Claw:** stop — mission fails before reaching hostages; stay — sandstorms and breakdown force abort, then deadly aircraft collision at withdrawal; payoff — eight lost, mission failure and organisational reform. First question “How did a rescue mission end before the rescuers reached the city?” Source: USAF and museum. Strongest objection: heavy Iran hostage historical context and distressing human loss without success.

Selection is an **editorial hypothesis**, not measured ranking. Alcatraz's one-night failed fourth escape is a clean structural reversal after a tangible engineering sequence; unlike Flight 232 it leaves survival unknown but reaches a **known payoff** (three launched; one stayed; why police knew details). Don't inflate as a proven "successful escape." Rival Flight 232 has a stronger resolved ending; if audience responds better to aviation stakes, choice might reverse.
