Why did three men disappear from America's most famous island prison? And why was a fourth escape planner still in his cell the next morning?

June 1962. Alcatraz sits in San Francisco Bay, surrounded by cold water and strong currents. Frank Morris, John Anglin and Clarence Anglin share a plan with prisoner Allen West.

They enlarge the vents at the backs of their cells. Beyond the walls is a maintenance corridor, not a way to freedom. They still need to reach the roof, cross the island, and survive the bay.

They make false heads with painted faces and real hair, so guards looking at their beds will see sleeping prisoners. Above the cells, they assemble a raft from raincoats. An instrument becomes a pump.

On the night of June eleventh, the plan breaks at the smallest opening. West has not managed to remove the grille at the back of his cell. The other three get into the passage. West cannot follow.

Morris and the Anglins climb to the roof, get down to the shore and launch the homemade raft. On the morning of June twelfth, guards find three empty beds beneath their false heads.

The FBI searches the bay. Investigators recover pieces of equipment, but no confirmed trace of the men alive. They believe the three probably drowned. No one establishes that they reached land.

Allen West, the man who was left behind, helps investigators reconstruct how they got out. The escape itself was real. Their survival was never proved.
