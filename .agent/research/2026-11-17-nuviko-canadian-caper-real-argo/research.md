## Three comparable proposals before writing

**A / Canadian Caper (winner).** Viewer stops at a mismatch: six American embassy workers escaped a hostage siege, but needed legitimate *Canadian* documents and a *fake* Hollywood studio. Exact 05m cold open: “Why would a government issue genuine passports with names that were not genuine?” After the questions: homes of Taylor/Sheardown → journalists detect missing people → legal Canadian passports and airport plan → film-company office, ads and concept artwork → wrong Iranian-calendar date caught → rehearsed identities → routine Swissair departure despite mechanical delay. Payoff: the unreal movie made the real airport exit credible; the safe houses made the exit possible. **Objection:** major 2012 film *Argo* and 2013 documentary *Our Man in Tehran* mean public recognition is high but novelty for a new documentary LOW/MODERATE; no proof our 5m angle will outperform.

**B / Operation Eagle Claw.** First line candidate: “How could an American rescue mission fail before reaching the hostages?” Real progression: eight helicopters depart → dust and failures → insufficient aircraft → abort at Desert One → deadly collision; payoff why backup chain collapsed. [US Dept of State, original after-action report, doc 267](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1977-80v11p1/d267), sections “Execution and Abort”, accessed Nov 17. **Objection:** technical and casualty-sensitive; same general hostage-crisis subject may confuse or require much more context. This is a genuinely strong alternative, not selected to be easy to beat.

**C / Hyatt Regency.** First line candidate: “How did a small change to the way two walkways hung from a ceiling cause a public catastrophe?” Progression: original load path → altered hanger-rod detail → transferred load → connection failure → investigation/accountability. Payoff is a causal explanation with prevention value. [NIST historical investigation](https://www.nist.gov/el/walkway-collapse-kansas-city-missouri-1981), accessed Nov 17. **Objection:** the disaster and its victims need tact; an illustrated storyteller can accidentally trivialise a large loss of life. No full casualty ledger vetted here.

**Decision:** A gives a direct human choice (shelter) followed by multiple verifiable obstacles with different agents (publicity, identity, employer backstop, entry records, rehearsal, airport), ending with a meaningful contrast “real passports, nonexistent film”. B has greater immediate physical stakes; C may have higher explanatory value. A's downside is known film coverage and no observed current demand. Selection is editorial hypothesis, not views prediction or paid analytics.
