**Why Oceanos wins this pass:** compared with Damascus it offers several *human decisions* and an earned collective payoff, not a single mechanical accident chain. Compared with Tenerife the central unanswered question changes twice: who can evacuate the people when the crew's command falters, then how can helicopters rescue them after lifeboats become unsafe. Oceanos's **biggest risk** is simplification into a false sole-saviour tale; every edition actively rejects that framing. More source-rich does not mean higher measured demand.

**Full-episode through-line:** entertainers performing in bad weather → machinery and lights stop → the lower deck is flooded → partial lifeboat evacuation grows unsafe → officers absent from bridge → guitarist sends Mayday, cannot give precise coordinates → navy divers and pilots take over the airlift → all people survive, liner sinks. 5m adds the risky boat-to-hull transfers, captain's contested role and practical harness training. 10m adds the preceding storm/wedding decision, jammed passage, rope handrail, close passes at boat davits, final ship search and danger to rescuers. All new factual detail has source locations below.

## 5. Promise, opening, causality, payoff

- **Audience:** English-speaking viewer unfamiliar with the Oceanos and South African maritime rescue, interested in rescue decisions, surprising roles and actual survival. Names and jargon introduced by actions.
- **Promise:** how a guitarist became the emergency caller, what stopped conventional evacuation, and why nobody died.
- **02m precise opener:** “Why did a cruise ship's guitarist end up calling for rescue from the captain's bridge? And how did everyone get off a sinking liner in a storm?” First frame concept: realistic 1991 ship bridge/radio with storm beyond; 2D traveler optional, not survivor.
- **05m opener:** three specific linked questions, then storm and musicians before main hook of water flooding. **10m opener** likewise, deeper prior-storm context.
- **Causal change:** dark/engines quiet -> investigation -> flood -> attempted lifeboats -> dangerous list -> bridge empty -> radio without position -> limited merchant rescue -> helicopter airlift -> last people -> ship sinks.
- **Payoff:** all saved, not because one musician magically learned seamanship, but because entertainers and trained responders took complementary roles.
- **Main objection:** large prior documentaries and testimony disagreement mean the approach only works if specific barriers and crew/rescuer contribution are shown, not clickbait villainism.
- **Storyboard:** intentionally not prepared in detail because independent script review is unavailable; only recommended exact 05m variant may receive detailed prompts after its text/hash is checked.
