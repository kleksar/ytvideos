Why did a cruise ship's guitarist end up calling for rescue from the captain's bridge? And how did everyone get off a sinking liner in a storm?

In August 1991, the Oceanos left a South African port for Durban. Waves hit the ship hard enough to send furniture sliding across the floor. Moss Hills, a guitarist in the entertainment team, was performing with his wife, Tracy.

Then the lights failed. The engines went quiet. Passengers gathered in the lounge, but clear instructions did not come.

Moss and a magician went below to find out why. Watertight doors blocked their way. Later, Moss found a lower deck flooded.

Some lifeboats were already leaving, carrying passengers and crew. Moss, Tracy and other entertainers helped people aboard the remaining boats. But as the Oceanos leaned farther to one side, lowering more boats became dangerous.

Hundreds were still aboard.

They went to the bridge to find the officers. The bridge was empty. Moss began calling for help over the radio. The people answering wanted the ship's coordinates. He did not know them. He was a musician, not a navigator.

Nearby vessels passed the distress call on, and South African rescuers sent helicopters. Navy divers came down onto the rolling deck. They showed Moss and Tracy how to fit lifting harnesses. Passengers rose from the ship, two at a time, as the helicopters returned again and again.

Moss and Tracy were among the last to leave.

About forty-five minutes after the final rescue, the Oceanos disappeared beneath the sea. Not one person aboard died.

The ship had a captain and officers. But when the rescue had to be organised, a guitarist, his fellow entertainers and the rescuers ashore helped bring everybody home.
