## Three equal-strength finalist pitches before drafting

**A. United 232 — selected.** Opening: “What if a passenger jet loses every hydraulic flight control at once?” First image: DC-10 with both wing engines operating and cockpit control wheel failing to affect the airplane. Development: tail-engine rupture → all three hydraulic circuits fail → differential thrust turns aircraft imperfectly → off-duty DC-10 instructor joins → runway choice under inadequate left-turn control → high-speed crash → post-crash investigation reveals one failure defeated supposed redundancy. Payoff: 185 of 296 survived, *not* a safe landing; investigators regarded normal successful landing in those conditions as impossible. Strength: successive genuinely different problems and morally honest ending. Objection: the well-known crash has been covered at length; short reconstruction must not sensationalize deaths or borrow other channels' footage.

**B. Gimli Glider — strongest alternative.** Opening: “How can a brand-new airliner run out of fuel while its pilots believe it has enough?” First image: blank fuel indication and a calculation mixing pounds with kilograms. Development: fuel mistake → engines stop → powerless glide → former base turned racing track → safe stop. Payoff: all 69 aboard survived, per Royal Canadian Air Force historical account. Objection: answer to the initial mystery (unit error) is familiar and the multi-stage pilot actions need strong verified first-hand coverage to sustain ten minutes. Compared fairly: better uplifting outcome and instantly understandable math than A, while A has a sharper escalation (independent hydraulic backups lost) and deeper concluding paradox. Both strong; this is an editorial preference, not measured demand.

**C. Hubble repair — equally credible non-aviation challenge.** Opening: “How do you repair the eye of a telescope already in orbit?” Visual: blurred first stellar image contrasted with a sharp after image. Development: investigators isolate a mirror-polishing measurement error → design compensating optics → astronauts install WFPC2 and COSTAR in 1993 → sharp new images. Payoff: image quality restored without repolishing the orbiting mirror. Objection: the mechanical sequence after the diagnosis is explanatory and does not offer as many changing near-term decisions for this channel; NASA image rights/provenance still require checks. Source: NASA/ESA linked below. A wins more distinct human-stage reversals; C has superior verified archival illustration and a positive result.

**Decision before full scripts:** A. Strongest rival B remains eligible for future research; not owner-rejected.

## Primary fact ledger: opened and read, 2026-11-17

- [NTSB DCA89MA063](https://www.ntsb.gov/investigations/pages/dca89ma063.aspx), “What Happened” and “What We Found”: 19 July 1989, tail engine No. 2 uncontained fan-disk failure, all three hydraulic systems lost, 285 passengers + 11 crew, 110 passengers + one flight attendant killed; NTSB cause: missed fatigue crack originating in metallurgical defect. Appropriate for event, mechanism, official casualties and causation. No invented direct cockpit dialogue.
- [FAA lessons learned, N1819U](https://www.faa.gov/lessons_learned/transport_airplane/accidents/N1819U), “History of Flight”: departed Denver 14:09, engine failed roughly 15:16, emergency request ~15:20, off-duty DC-10 instructor arrived ~15:29, runway 31 proposed vs runway 22 actually reached, ground contact 16:00, right wingtip first, rolled inverted and burnt. Includes 111 fatalities, 47 serious and 125 minor injuries. **185 survived = 296 − 111**; this count uses the FAA/NTSB immediate official accident accounting. Other later publications may use a different fatality count and should not be blended silently.
- Same FAA, “Engines”: 1971 titanium fan-disk manufacturing defect, fatigue crack, six fluorescent penetrant inspections and undetected visible crack, very high-energy fragments. Avoid claiming inspectors knew about this specific crack.
- Same FAA, “Flight Crew”: right-turn tendency, asymmetric thrust, simulator studies found a successful landing not practically possible, crew actions described as highly commendable; teamwork/CRM described but not proof any single human saved every survivor.
- Same FAA, “Accident Board Recommendations” and “Resulting Safety Initiatives”: recommended improvements to rotating-part inspections, uncontained-engine risk protection, backup control research; postaccident research and improved standards. **Do not** claim all recommendations implemented or zero future occurrences.
- [Canadian government Gimli account](https://www.canada.ca/en/department-national-defence/maple-leaf/rcaf/migration/2018/from-the-avro-arrow-to-the-gimli-glider.html): 1983 metric confusion, former military base/raceway, 69 survived.
- [NASA first Hubble servicing mission](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/hubble/observatory/missions-to-hubble/servicing-mission-1/) and [NASA mirror flaw](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/hubble/observatory/design/optics/hubbles-mirror-flaw/): 1990 blurred images, flawed mirror, December 1993 corrective optics/upgrade, verified payoff.
