**Three finalists with verified facts and honest objections:**

1. **Antwerp diamond heist** — Opening: "How did a complaint about litter crack a $100 million vault robbery?" Visible first event: untouched outside security versus ransacked underground boxes. After hook: office rented inside the building, weekend theft, discarded documents and gemstones, arrest on February 21, competing later accounts and still-missing loot. Payoff: security failed to prevent burglary, refuse led to arrests, recovery mostly failed. Objection: recent competing documentary/Netflix coverage, earlier Paper #172 Gardner heist could cause genre overlap, several reconstructions contradict one another. **Selected** because everyday litter complaint unexpectedly turns into the breakthrough, and the split outcomes (solved arrests / missing assets) produce a second meaningful turn.
2. **Hubble mirror flaw and 1993 correction** — Opening: "Why did a billion-dollar telescope see a blur?" Actual first event: defective 1990 images, corrective 1993 spacewalk. Payoff: Hubble's scientific vision restored with WFPC2/COSTAR, compelling visual before/after. Objection: the result is obvious from question and many videos already cover it; explaining spherical aberration may stall a 2m version. **Strongest noncrime alternative**, particularly on visual transformation.
3. **D. B. Cooper's 1971 jump** — Opening: "Where did the man who parachuted from a hijacked airliner go?" FBI confirmed flight, ransom and unresolved whereabouts. Payoff: why physical finds do not resolve identity. Objection: unknown final fate could fail to give a complete ending, extremely saturated mystery market. Consider later with a different verified central payoff.

The winner is an editorial hypothesis, not demonstrated audience preference; a factual vault/how-to walkthrough would be weaker than the trash → investigation → unrecovered valuables structure. ## Causal event chain

Trading vault in Antwerp → pre-existing tenant access and apparent legitimacy (TIME, Guardian) → weekend February 15–16 break-in and >100 boxes (Antwerpist) → Monday discovery of losses (Guardian, TIME) → Van Camp finds discarded sacks on land, reports dumping (TIME, Antwerpist) → paperwork/gems tie refuse to heist and tenant (TIME, Antwerpist) → Notarbartolo arrested Feb 21 (Antwerpist) → 2005 conviction (People, Antwerpist) → substantial loot unrecovered and contested thief account (TIME, People). Causal evidence does not establish exact minute-by-minute vault technique or exact fate of individual diamonds.
