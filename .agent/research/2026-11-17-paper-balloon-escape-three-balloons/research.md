### Claim and interpretation cautions

- One *fabric prototype* in 1978 failed to become a usable aircraft; the Strelzyks' July 1979 flight with another balloon failed after cloud moisture; successful third envelope September 1979. Do not miscount: third envelope, second actual escape flight.
- Don't claim precise night takeoff minute: museum and participant differ. Preserve settled dates, approximate duration, distance and max altitude from Federal Archives.
- The July flight's exact shortfall varies across accounts. Say **short of the border**, no precise metres; don't invent a landmine close call or firefight.
- Police found the abandoned balloon via hunter; the published August list was an appeal for leads, **not proof** investigators had already identified the families. No dramatic “Stasi at their front door.”
- Construction labor was shared. Different recollections dispute which man invented or was first responsible for specific parts; narration avoids unilateral ownership.
- Burning fabric and leakage near balloon top are participant recollections, signpost in Long as Wetzel's account; don't derive precise fuel leakage volume, fuel mass or start time.
- Weather wind direction verified by museum; actual steering limited by physics. No statement that balloon crossed directly above a live guard's gun position.
- Final location West Germany near Naila, recognized by West German officers; no fabricated speech quotations (questions paraphrased).
- 10m: later restrictive measures against families/relatives and museum preservation are retrospective; friendship Peter Richter/Wetzel is a 2024 reporting layer and must not create a false “they met in 1979” implication.
- Script narration contains zero copied contemporary documentary phrases; proper names/measurements are plain historical facts. Paper visuals should be reconstructed as visibly paper dioramas, not labeled archival footage.
