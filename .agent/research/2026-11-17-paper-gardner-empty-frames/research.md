## Three real competing bids

| Candidate | Concrete cold start / image | Stay-after-hook sequence | Payoff actually supported | Biggest objection |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Gardner 1990 — selected** | “How do two men in police uniforms take half a billion dollars' worth of art…?” Empty gold frame in a paper gallery | Rule-breaking entry → two guards tied → 81-minute room-by-room theft → unusual inventory and weak evidence → artworks cannot simply be auctioned → official attempts and today's visible loss | Thirteen works missing; 1994 deliberate empty-frame display; case still open; reward up to $10m | **No recovered works or proved perpetrators**: the long cut may frustrate rather than resolve; investigate neither suspects' motives nor a fictitious mastermind |
| Antwerp 2003 — strongest alternative | A heavily guarded diamond vault breached and a suspect identified through carelessly discarded material | Detailed security bypasses → gems disappear → incriminating garbage left behind → police identify a participant | Investigation identifies Leonardo Notarbartolo; diamonds largely not recovered; 2025 documentary interviews him | Arguably **better physical payoff** than Gardner; but many device-level accounts conflict between thief and investigators, and detailed heist mechanics would need exact additional primary checking |
| Great Train Robbery 1963 | A postal train stopped by a false railway signal in rural Britain | Train attacked → cash moved → hideout traced → arrests and later escapes | Gang members caught; money recovery incomplete; lasting public case | Strong resolved plot but UK-heavy cultural familiarity and large cast slow global 5-minute introduction |

**Selection reason, not an arbitrary preference:** Gardner can open with an immediately legible disguise, turn through a sharply time-bounded robbery into the paradox of priceless art that cannot simply reenter the market, and end on a **still visible** museum object. Its current public investigation plus 2026 reporting provide a living follow-through without pretending to solve it. Antwerp may actually be stronger for a mechanistic 10-minute film, and would be the next serious competitor, not a rejected theme. The chief risk for Gardner is that an unresolved ending only satisfies viewers if the final empty-frame answer is delivered concretely and early enough. For that reason the **five-minute** treatment is preferred over ten.

## Causal spine and exact viewer promise

- **Before:** an intentionally arranged collection, guards at the employee entrance
- **Change:** fake police admitted against procedure → guards restrained
- **Next question:** what can the thieves remove in eighty-one minutes? → thirteen varied items, cut canvases, missing tapes
- **Second change:** famous stolen artwork cannot be marketed openly without detection; investigations not equivalent to recovery
- **Payoff:** the original curator's intended arrangement is still incomplete, so the museum **deliberately shows** empty frames; official case open, reward remains
- **Strong objection:** if viewer expects to learn the culprit's name, our title and thumbnail must not imply that payoff

No independently observed target audience performance. All three variants use this one verified evidence basis, and individual scores/hashes are in story-review.
