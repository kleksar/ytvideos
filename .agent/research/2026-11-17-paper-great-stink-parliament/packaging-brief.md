## Audience promise / packaging honesty

Viewer sees London Parliament beside polluted Thames, learns **why the money was suddenly approved**, then sees engineering that improved water sanitation over years. Do **not** say a sewer was constructed in 18 days, that politicians understood bacteria, or that cholera disappeared immediately.

- Proposed Title A: **London's Parliament Couldn't Escape the Smell**
- Title B: **The Stench That Forced London to Build Its Sewers**
- Title C: **Why London Fixed Its Sewers for the Wrong Reason**
- Primary thumbnail text (large handmade paper letters): **THE GREAT STINK**. Alternatively **18 DAYS?** only if context clearly means a **vote**, not construction. Avoid misleading counter/time.
- Paper thumbnail idea `PD-THRESHOLD-01`: view from a Victorian parliamentary window, one side richly lit paper room with a failed treatment cloth on the curtain, other side a smelly but **non-sensational** paper Thames; miniature MPs visible only if supported by general building occupancy, not invented personal reactions. Material thickness, folds, contact shadows and real paper depth; an authentic 1858 photograph not claimed. Canvas 16:9, full bleed, no decorative external frame.
- Visually legible promise: "They had ignored warnings. Now the mess reached their windows." *This is a proposed editorial line for review, not a documentary quote.*

## First events → payoff alignment

1. Opening EN asks why smell succeeded where warnings failed; first paper composition establishes river beside Parliament.
2. Within the opening section, an exposed waste-filled Thames beside functioning new flushing toilets explains **why a modern convenience worsened collective sanitation**.
3. A correctly historicized window/curtain scene verifies the Parliament stakes. Do not show exact named MPs fainting or fleeing without a source.
4. Simple three-dimensional paper cross-section shows intercepting sewer, gradient, lift station, downstream flow: **1858 authorization vs 1875 construction**, visual purpose to explain years of engineering.
5. Human-health payoff: two paper water routes, contaminated supply to early houses versus separate drainage system, and 1866 east London geographic limitation. **Avoid false universal treatment/disinfection iconography**.
6. End lands the conceptual reversal: they wanted cleaner air; longer-term health gain came from reducing dirty water.
