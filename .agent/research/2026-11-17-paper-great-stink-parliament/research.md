## Three strongest proposals compared before narration

### A. Great Stink (selected)
- Why stop: **“Why did a terrible smell get a law passed in just eighteen days?”** A familiar city and government in a visceral conflict.
- Hook picture: a Victorian Parliament window above a paper Thames shoreline, untreated waste on exposed banks, curtains inside.
- After hook: flushing toilets relocate waste rather than solve it → tide and heat magnify problem → chemical curtains fail → Parliament changes its financing decision → the engineer intercepts drainage → 1866 outbreak exposes the distinction between bad air and polluted water.
- Verified payoff: legislation passed in 18 days, infrastructure built over years through 1875, a public-health effect different from the miasma explanation. *No claim that 18 days built the sewer or that air pollution and cholera were fully eradicated.*
- Strongest objection: more than one prominent English documentary has already told this story; unique audience demand **UNVERIFIED**. Material for 10m exists, but greater historical detail may reduce attention and undercut the clean political reversal.

### B. Dawson Film Find (closest challenger)
- Opening: “Why did construction workers find hundreds of long-lost film reels beneath a Canadian hockey rink?”
- After hook: nitrate films disposed of when programmes could no longer be circulated → buried as fill → cold storage inadvertently protects forgotten copies → excavation exposes fragile material → preservation/identification.
- Payoff: film history restored from material that was thrown away, with actual discoveries independently documented.
- Objection vs A: why the reels existed and why they survived is intellectually rewarding, but the strongest visual proof is the actual archival footage, rights/availability **UNVERIFIED**. A documentary already foregrounds the same ending. In A the political decision and construction independently change the causal situation, enabling a useful 5m.
- Grounding: https://www.kpbs.org/podcasts/cinema-junkie/2017/06/16/dawson-city-frozen-time-revels-real-found-footage ; https://canadiangeographic.ca/articles/new-film-explores-history-of-dawson-city-through-lost-footage/ ; film *Dawson City: Frozen Time* (2016). Source pages observed, full footage **not** watched.

### C. London Beer Flood
- Opening: “How did a vat of beer kill people outside a brewery?”
- After hook: a porter vat bursts → neighbouring containers fail → a wave enters poor streets and cellars → fatalities and inquest.
- Payoff: a real industrial hazard, not the humorous harmless “beer flood” implied by title.
- Objection: deaths require careful human framing, while sufficiently grounded new twists for 10m are thin; not a deliberately weak rival but poorer fit for the repeatable 2/5/10 package.
- Evidence: https://people.com/in-1814-a-brewery-rupture-sent-a-deadly-15-foot-wave-of-beer-through-a-london-neighborhood-12188510 (published in Oct 2026, accessed 2026-11-17). No assertion that secondary source is a primary inquest.

**Decision:** A has the strongest balance of immediately legible hook, development beyond the premise, multiple proven turns, and a final re-evaluation; B has a stronger rediscovery visual, but its filmed-payoff rights and 10m structure remain material uncertainty. Scores below are editorial judgments, not forecasts.

## Primary institutional / historical evidence for A

Every source opened as text on 2026-11-17; statements here describe what the pages support, not a claim to have read archival originals or seen documentary videos.

1. **London Museum**, *The Great Stink of 1858*, https://www.londonmuseum.org.uk/collections/london-stories/great-stink-of-1858/ : London >2.5 million residents; flush toilets and old drains; tidal river receiving waste; hot dry 1858 exposed filth; chemically treated curtains; government bill by Disraeli became law in **18 days**; Bazalgette intercepting scheme, pumping at Deptford/Abbey Mills, completed by **1875**. Limits: museum interpretive retrospective, not contemporary bill transcript; numbers are broad historical estimates.
2. **Royal Museums Greenwich**, *Dickens and the Great Stink of 1858*, https://www.rmg.co.uk/stories/maritime-history/library-archive/dickens-great-stink-1858 : Parliament curtains soaked in chloride of lime; 18-day law; downstream discharge and riverside embankments, work finished 1875. Limits: literary retrospective and image captions are not reproducing licensed image rights.
3. **Historic England**, *The Great Stink: A Victorian Solution to the Problem of London's Waste*, https://historicengland.org.uk/images-books/archive/collections/photographs/the-great-stink/ : Bazalgette and river embankments; Crossness opening on **4 April 1865**; Abbey Mills station. **Historic England** photo record, https://historicengland.org.uk/images-books/photos/item/OP04641 : Crossness sewage pumped into a reservoir and discharged at high tide; early system shifted waste rather than modern treatment. Photo rights restricted until verified.
4. **London Museum**, *John Snow: Cholera & the Broad Street pump*, https://www.londonmuseum.org.uk/collections/london-stories/john-snow-cholera-broad-street-pump/ : 1854 water-source investigation, prevailing miasma thinking, Snow dates **15 March 1813–16 June 1858**, last major cholera epidemic **1866** in east London involving Old Ford contaminated supply and areas not yet served by sewer works. Limits: 1866 evidence supports waterborne transmission but does not alone establish an exact mortality counterfactual for all London.
5. **Michael Faraday**, letter to *The Times* **7 July 1855**, transcription with provenance *Life and Letters of Faraday* (1870), https://humanym.com/letters/faraday-to-times-1855 : public warning that Thames was effectively a sewer. Historical quotation not used verbatim in narration; transcript publication context differs from the 1855 printing.
6. **Historical caution:** Famous Snow pump-handle removal should not be stated to have immediately halted the outbreak; https://historiamedica.org/sources/john-snow-cholera-map/ (retrospective on dates and map). In 10m narration explicitly corrects this common simplification; 2/5m do not assign such a causal role.

Material qualifiers: **18 days is legislation, never construction**; sewage was redirected downstream, not instantly purified; false to claim every resident had clean drinking water; 1866 outbreak and Snow's death complicate a simplistic "Snow personally proved Parliament wrong" framing. No invented dialogue, intentions, private thoughts, direct quotations, or precise casualty claims.

## Claim → new information → cause → consequence → source

- Flushing toilets / booming population → sewage concentrated in Thames → tide + summers intensify local pollution → danger and visible/well-smelled consequence → London Museum.
- Cholera and miasma → Snow proposes water link (1854) → warning alone did not secure financing → political backlog persists → London Museum Snow and Great Stink; avoid monolithic "everyone rejected Snow".
- Heat and chemically treated curtains (1858) → MPs directly confronted → Disraeli legislation in 18 days → funds/authorization, **not** completed tunnel → London Museum + RMG.
- Bazalgette interceptor network and pumps → less sewage through central Thames → 1875 network, altered embankments → sanitation/public health, but untreated discharge downstream → London Museum + Historic England.
- East London cholera 1866 and old network's later limits → water transmission recognized, combined sewers can overflow → lasting but bounded improvement → London Museum Snow; modern issues not quantified.

## Editorial hypothesis / uncertainty

Title and first shot promise a legislative **reversal**, not "London's sewer built in 18 days". First confirmation: Parliament visibly beside polluted river and futile treated curtains. Full payoff: an **incorrect theory of bad air** helped trigger engineering that reduced **waterborne danger**; sanitary improvement took decades and was incomplete. Main risk: overly familiar event and 10m expansion sliding into generic sanitation lecture. A paper diorama can show river banks, curtains, interceptors, pumping station and physical map; imagery is production CONCEPT, not existing/owned media or evidence. The paper plane is a later scene-planning easter egg, not a fact or on-screen artifact yet.
