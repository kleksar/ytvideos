## Title / thumbnail promise / cold opening / first confirmation / payoff

**Audience:** general English-speaking viewer who doesn't recognize Bernie Webber, Cape Cod or WWII-era tankers. Do not require prior viewing of `The Finest Hours`.

**Title A:** `A Rescue Boat Saved 32 Men. Then It Had to Find Home.`

**Title B:** `33 Men on Half a Ship. Four Rescuers Had No Compass.`

**Title C:** `The 1952 Rescue That Almost Ended on the Way Back`

Choose after exact independent review, not as invented A/B winner. Do not state “all 33 survived” or “no one died”; include exact limitation in narration.

**Thumbnail concept (Paper PD-SCALE-01 / PD-THRESHOLD-01):** one physically coherent handmade/cardboard miniature showing the immense vertical steel-looking cardboard hull fragment at right and one tiny eleven-metre-looking wooden paper boat against dark sea at left; a rope ladder connects the gap. At top, a short thick paper-letter hook **“NO COMPASS”**. One small distant red harbour buoy can appear toward the dark far shore, not as an impossibly bright giant. With a storm-lit paper atmosphere, eye goes tanker → tiny boat → buoy. **The scene is interpretive illustration**, not an archival photo or exact scale model. Strong contact shadows, layered cut edges, intact hands/faces; no decorative frame and no 2D vector cut-outs. Preview actual 320×180 with duration badge and safe area after media is authorized.

**Alternative thumbnail (PD-HOOK-01):** wet paper navigation chart with an empty compass mount and a small red buoy in the distance; two facts that visibly matter. Caption **“FIND HOME”**. Requires clear tiny boat silhouette, not a stock map with misleading sea positions.

**Opening promise:** 05m exact initial questions:
> How could four Coast Guardsmen get thirty-three sailors off a tanker that had snapped in two? Why did another rescue, happening in the same storm, almost leave those men undiscovered? And what use is a successful rescue if you cannot find your way home?

**First documentary confirmation:** two T2 tankers break in one storm; Fort Mercer calls for help, Pendleton cannot. A short paper diagram of a missing forward radio room may illustrate, but an illustrative cutaway is not a real recovered blueprint. Avoid showing a working SOS at the Pendleton stern.

**Middle expectation changes:** Chatham Bar blows compass/windshield out; Fitzgerald restarts engine; tanker is found by noise/spotlight, with lights visible on one side; ladder transfer requires repeated steering; one real fatality; more people aboard than before.

**Full payoff:** after taking 32 people aboard, 36 total return without compass by identifying the red buoy they passed on the way out. Conclude with all four later receiving Gold Lifesaving Medals, but do not replace the human cost with a clean heroic win. Neither “everyone saved” nor Hollywood car-headlight signal is supported here.

**Accuracy / rights:** all explicit numbers and actions in `research.md`; no fabricated spoken quotes, rescue procedure or period footage. Web pages and film frames are not licensed reference assets. Source-content links belong in internal research, not automatic on-screen bibliography.
