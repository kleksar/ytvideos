## Three comparable serious proposals

**A — Pendleton, chosen (Paper, documentary Long).**
- Audience stop: a four-man eleven-metre boat goes out to a 150-metre-plus tanker that has broken in two; no SOS from its stern.
- Cold opening, exact 05m narration: “How could four Coast Guardsmen get thirty-three sailors off a tanker that had snapped in two?”
- After hook: another tanker occupied rescuers → broken radio made this ship invisible → Chatham Bar destroyed compass and windshield → repeated engine restarts → a rope ladder sways over the tiny craft → thirty-two survivors crowd the boat → a red buoy guides thirty-six people home.
- Payoff: the red harbour light they passed outbound becomes their only reliable location on return; **32 saved, one man from the stern lost, eight on bow also lost**. Two distinct rescues: from tanker and from sea.
- Strongest objection: substantial *The Finest Hours* film exposure and a fresh 67-minute English video from a specialist sailing creator; rescue action may be familiar to a maritime audience. Paper 5m can focus on causal choices without pretending it is a newly discovered event.
- Evidence confidence: **high** for main acts via museum/Coast Guard Foundation; **moderate** for chronology of discovery (radar vs aircraft reports coexist); do not narrate exact discovery minute.

**B — Canadian Caper, nearest strong alternative.**
- Viewer stop: “Why did six Americans fleeing Tehran need Hollywood advertisements for a film that would never be made?”
- Development: Canadians hide six diplomats → Canadian passports authorized → dummy production company, sci-fi advertisements and identity paperwork → rehearsal → Iranian airport exit.
- Payoff: real documentation and a fake film let six people depart; Canadian cooperation and CIA operational help both essential. Direct CIA archive confirms departure after a flight delay.
- Strong objection: popular 2012 film has fixed audience expectations, and many internet retellings borrow dramatic fictional checkpoints. Novelty and proof of interest for our specific telling **UNVERIFIED**; may still be excellent later.
- Verified source: [CIA Museum, Rescue of the Canadian Six](https://www.cia.gov/legacy/museum/exhibit/rescue-of-the-canadian-six-n-a-classic-case-of-deception/), observed 2026-11-17, exhibit paragraphs “About the Rescue”, including passes, cover and flight.

**C — Triangle Shirtwaist, strong alternate kind of story.**
- Viewer stop: “When a factory caught fire, why did workers reach a locked exit?”
- Development: fire on upper floors → locked stair door → collapsed fire escape and inaccessible exits → 146 deaths → public evidence and safety changes (the final regulatory details not yet vetted for a long version).
- Payoff: human reasons behind the preventable loss, and the labour-safety consequences, not merely a casualty count.
- Strong objection: heavy subject; careless thumbnail/hero framing would sensationalise deaths; full personal testimony and aftermath need additional primary records.
- Source: [US National Park Service, Triangle Shirtwaist Factory (Brown Building)](https://www.nps.gov/places/triangle-shirtwaist-factory-brown-building.htm), observed 2026-11-17.

**Why A over B?** B has a more instantly surprising setup, a satisfying safe exit and strong document trail. A has more independently escalating *on-screen actions* after the first surprise: compass loss, restarting motor, single-person pickups, one tragic loss, red-light return. The red buoy recontextualises a concrete detail already seen. Both are strong. The film's familiarity is A's biggest weakness; it does not receive a free “originality 10”.

## Promise and causal bridge

Promise: “They saved the crew from a broken ship. How did they save the rescue itself?” The first visible proof is the immense stern of Pendleton and tiny CG-36500, grounded in real proportions. Before viewers see all 32 rescued, show the broken compass and engine, then the moving rope ladder: each changes what success would require. Payoff is both the rescued count **with loss respected** and the red buoy recognized on the way home. **Not** “everyone survived”. The 05m version best preserves action density and the physical double-rescue structure. Full text-level comparisons in `story-review.md`; independent review and actual audio remain UNVERIFIED.
