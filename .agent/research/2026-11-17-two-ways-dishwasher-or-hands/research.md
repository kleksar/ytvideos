## Selection before scripting

Screened **nine distinct everyday decision problems** for a real, understandable A/B, meaningful stakes, evidence and a payoff long enough for 2/5/10 min: (1) handwash vs dishwasher (selected); (2) back-to-front versus window-first airline boarding; (3) split restaurant bill evenly versus itemised; (4) batch laundry versus wash small loads; (5) one grocery trip versus several nearby purchases; (6) toll shortcut versus free road; (7) tidy by room versus by task; (8) put clothing away immediately versus batch; (9) one box versus multiple packages for shipping. Items 4–9 received **only preliminary screening**, not researched finalists or claims of documented YouTube demand.

| **Split restaurant bill evenly versus itemised** | “I ordered a sandwich. **Strongest alternative:** airline boarding; wider existing viewer base and striking animation potential, but crowded and heavily sensitive to assumptions. Dishwashing won on a clearly defined **identical workload**, primary-source numbers and an honest two-stage reveal, not on guaranteed virality. Limit: topic already has substantial evergreen coverage; whether our illustrated delivery offers enough novelty remains unknown. Reject the selection rather than invent a result if the premise cannot sustain interesting production after independent review.

## Exact source claims

1. **Which?, 1 September 2026**, [“Dishwasher vs handwashing: which is the most efficient method?”](https://www.which.co.uk/news/article/which-research-reveals-how-little-water-dishwashers-use-compared-to-hand-washing-aUDng9Y2iK8E), section “Does a dishwasher or handwashing use more water?”, table for fourteen place settings and following paragraph. Hand method: estimated **4.5 litres/setting** on assumption **one 9-litre bowl per 2 settings** = **63 litres for 14**. Machine: **33 tested relatively recent dishwashers**, mixed types/models; **12.96 litres (main)**, **9.74 litres (eco)** per same fourteen settings. Difference eco 63−9.74=**53.26 litres**, reduction relative to hand estimate **84.54%**. The hand number is an **assumption**, not a parallel controlled measurement; the model samples/cycle type cannot prove an arbitrary home result. Current 2026 publication is a **fresh source of an evergreen question**, not proof of real-time search growth.
2. **University of Michigan Center for Sustainable Systems, 12 February 2020**, [“A guide to household manual and machine dishwashing through a life cycle perspective”](https://css.umich.edu/publications/research-publications/guide-household-manual-and-machine-dishwashing-through-life), abstract: model **4 loads/week, eight place settings/load, ten years**. Typical manual **5,620 kgCO2e**, typical machine **2,090 kgCO2e** under study assumptions. Behaviours, grid mix, water-heater type and pre-rinsing affect results. This is a distinct model, with different workload and metric from source 1.
3. **University of Michigan SEAS, February 2020**, [“Fighting climate change at the sink”](https://seas.umich.edu/news/fighting-climate-change-sink-guide-greener-dishwashing), passages on two-basin practice, running tap, pre-rinse. Under the paper's scenarios, **two-basin manual method emitted 18% less life-cycle GHG than recommended machine use**. The laboratory observation involved 38 Whirlpool employees; the work was supported in part by Whirlpool and U-M institutions. Treat the 18% as a conditional model output, **not an experiment on our 14-place-setting water figures**.
4. **ENERGY STAR**, [Dishwashers Key Product Criteria](https://www.energystar.gov/products/dishwashers/key_product_criteria), effective **19 July 2023**: current certification criterion for standard-sized units **≤3.2 US gallons per test cycle** (~12.11 litres). This is a **certification maximum**, not an average and not an output of Which?'s dataset. [Product guidance](https://www.energystar.gov/products/dishwashers) discusses scrapping leftovers, not pre-rinsing, and soil sensors.
5. **Illustrative sensitivity only, not experimental evidence:** assumed 6 litres/minute tap flow × 3 minutes pre-rinse = **18 litres**; 9.74+18 = **27.74 litres**, still below 63 in that hypothetical. No faucet, cleaning result or re-wash cycle measured. No precise universal threshold inferred.
6. **ConsumerReports**, [Don't bother pre-rinsing dishes](https://www.consumerreports.org/dishwashers/dont-bother-pre-rinsing-your-dishes/) (accessed 2026-11-17): additional guidance about pre-rinsing; use the ENERGY STAR source for normative recommendation.

## Exact story promise and objection

**First proof:** a single nine-litre washing-up bowl cannot be equated with the whole task without accounting for refills. **A/B rule:** same 14 place settings, litres, identical cleanliness target, handwashing routine expressly modelled versus published machine test average; state difference in evidence type. **Development:** 63 vs 9.74, recirculation, optional pre-rinse 18L model, one-mug nontransferability, then separate life-cycle GHG question with two-basin reversal. **Payoff:** under stated full-load water assumptions machine is more frugal; this does *not* prove all handwashing or all metrics are worse. **Strongest objection:** two different data-generating processes compared rather than a physical head-to-head. Address it prominently in narration and keep independent review UNVERIFIED.
