My dishwasher can run for two hours. I can wash a few plates in minutes. So which method actually uses less water?

After a big family meal, I have fourteen place settings to clean. I compare two ways of washing that same pile. A: I wash by hand, refilling a washing-up bowl when it gets dirty. B: I scrape away the food scraps, fill an efficient dishwasher, and choose its eco program. The score is litres of water. I don't get to change the metric after seeing the result.

I'd bet on A. A bowl looks small. The machine makes a racket for ages. But the sound of spraying isn't the sound of fresh water entering. A dishwasher recirculates water. When I empty a dirty bowl, I need another.

A September 2026 comparison from Which? estimated that washing fourteen place settings by hand takes sixty-three litres, assuming a nine-litre bowl serves two settings before a refill. Test averages for recent dishwashers gave the same load about ten litres in eco mode.

That is a difference of roughly fifty-three litres. B wins on water for this full load.

I haven't physically tested either method in my own kitchen. The handwashing figure is an estimate; the dishwasher figure is a test average. Different habits and machines can give different results.

There is another twist. A separate University of Michigan life-cycle study found that careful manual washing in two basins could produce fewer greenhouse-gas emissions than recommended machine washing under that study's conditions. Different score, different result.
