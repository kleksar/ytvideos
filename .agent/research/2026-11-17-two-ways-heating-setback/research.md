## Three prepared finalist pitches

### Heating setback (winner)
- Audience: English-speaking people paying a household energy bill; plain-language temperature explanation.
- Exact opening: “I'm leaving home for eight hours. Do I keep the heat at twenty degrees all day, or let the room cool and warm it up before I return? Pick A or B.”
- Opening reason: familiar disagreement that reheating "must" cost more.
- After-hook development: enforce same 20°C return, calculate A/B useful heat, explain shrinking heat leakage, then change insulation and conversion efficiency; different winner by purchased electricity.
- Payoff: A=14.4kWh vs B=12.8228kWh **useful heat** at equal conversion; not guaranteed cheaper bills; practical conditions stated.
- Strong objection: cannot responsibly recommend timing/temperatures for real heat pumps or people in cold/damp buildings based on invented coefficients; Short production target 35–50s and missing Long recipe block any immediate production; saturated evergreen topic, relevance not proven to translate into views.

### Bath vs shower (closest alternative)
- Exact opening: “I have one wash to take. Which uses more water, one full bath or my shower?”
- Development: measuring shower flow rate and duration vs real tub fill; 2 gpm × 8min = 16 gallons, bathtub around 30 gallons; a very long shower reverses.
- Payoff: comparison depends on actual fixture and habit, no universal "bath" vs "shower" ranking.
- Strong objection: the central threshold exhausts novelty fast; ten-minute honest narrative would require additional measured tests. Source: https://www.epa.gov/watersense/showerheads and https://www.portland.gov/water/water-efficiency-programs/save-water-home (observed 2026-11-17).

### Dehumidifier + airer vs tumble dryer
- Exact opening: “I need one dry load by tonight; does the cheaper-watt machine really save money?”
- Development: power over cycle length, moisture removal, matching endpoints and dehumidifier effect on indoor heat and humidity.
- Payoff: total purchased energy and time vary with appliance, room and initial load; possible reversal.
- Strong objection: no independently confirmed equivalent dryness data or matched appliance figures. Under the owner's rule, no fabricated winner; held as an alternate experiment, not a verified storyline.

**Comparative decision:** the heating model has a satisfying but counterintuitive first answer **and** meaningful second reversal that changes the definition of "cheaper". Bath/shower has the cleaner physical demonstration and could win as a short real-life experiment; its weakness is meaningful 5/10-minute depth. Winner is editorially experimental; viewer demand is UNVERIFIED.

## Model fully reproducible

For identical A/B: duration 8 hours, outdoor constant 5°C, indoor initial=final=20°C, one thermal mass C=3 kWh/K and loss H=0.120 kW/K, no gains or variable ventilation. Ideal heat source with **same energy conversion efficiency** for main comparison. Equation C×dT/dt = P(t) − H×(T−5).
- A: P=H×(20−5)=1.8kW throughout 8h → 14.4kWh.
- B: P=0 for first 6h → T6=5+15×exp(−H×6/C) = 16.799417916°C.
- Reheat B, hours 6–8: constant P=H×[(20−5−(T6−5)×exp(−H×2/C)) / (1−exp(−H×2/C))] = **6.411398394 kW**, assuming this thermal output is achievable.
- B supplied useful heat=2×P=**12.822796787 kWh**. Difference **1.577203213 kWh** (10.9528% of A) over this model window. Both finish exactly 20°C. This does NOT establish cost in currency or annual bill savings.
- Sensitivity: H=0.060 kW/K with C=3 ⇒ T6=18.303807°C, A=7.200kWh, B=6.787487kWh, saving 0.412513kWh. H=0.240 ⇒ T6=14.281751°C, A=28.80kWh, B=23.018949kWh, saving 5.781051kWh, B recovery power 11.509474kW. These models illustrate physical and feasibility limits, not safe setback temperatures.
- Different hypothetical electricity conversion: A COP=3 ⇒14.4/3=4.8kWh electric, B COP=3 ⇒12.8228/3=4.2743kWh; B COP=2 ⇒12.8228/2=6.4114kWh electric. An efficiency drop reverses **purchased electricity** winner. Neither COP assumption is a measurement.
- Unknown actual equipment: heat pumps and backup elements, slow underfloor heating, occupant vulnerability/pets/damp/frozen pipes, timed prices, room thermal heterogeneity. Do not infer universally cheaper fuel or temperature scheduling from this model.
