# Source research — rain: walk or run?

Observed 2026-11-17 UTC. External queries contained no private repo or channel names. This is an editorial research note, not video viewing, Youtube Analytics or a provider-paid experiment.

## Audience problem and candidate funnel

Target: English-speaking viewer who has been caught outside without an umbrella and has wondered whether to hurry; the actual promise is a conditional and useful answer, not a fabricated athletic challenge. Current Two Ways Lab ChannelSpec supports A/B first-person illustrated Shorts only (ordinary 35–50 seconds); 2m is an editorial Short proposal with duration compatibility UNVERIFIED, while 5m/10m Long variants have no configured recipe.

Rapid screening of 10 distinct candidate questions: (1) walk vs run through rain; (2) shower vs bath water use; (3) shared vs separate checkout queues; (4) sort laundry socks into piles vs direct matching; (5) packing a suitcase by largest vs heaviest first; (6) carry household items one by one vs basket batching; (7) walk vs cycle to a nearby errand including lock time; (8) grouped vs sequential chores with setup time; (9) long vs short printing batches; (10) prepare cooking ingredients in a batch vs dish by dish. #3 has strong real queueing evidence but overlaps the channel's checkout pilot and existing queue-related editorial; #2 borders the current household water-comparison PR; #4 has potential visual appeal but lacked checked observation data. None was excluded merely for difficulty of animation.

### Three strong competing applications (equal readiness before selection)

1. **Rain (chosen).** Opening exact line: “I'm one hundred metres from a dry doorway.” Interest: familiar rain dilemma, real model controversy and 2026 result. After hook: why running doesn't increase front collisions over fixed distance, arithmetic A/B, then narrow wind-dependent reversal. Payoff: run in ordinary safe conditions; rare well-aligned tailwind can favor slower pace in models. Strongest objection: the main walk/run answer was already made famous by MinutePhysics; one extremely successful old Long does not validate this new Short or length variants. The 2026 dynamic-body nuance is the new editorial contribution, not a claim of first discovery.
2. **One shared queue versus two queues.** Opening: “Two cashiers are free, but I choose the shortest-looking line and still leave last.” Development: arrival/service-time variance and blocking, pooling, then when separate lines might be practical for different services. Payoff: explain average wait and fairness rather than always assume a single queue. Strongest objection: E001 checkout and PR #118 escalator already cover waiting/capacity; differentiate only with a robust new human decision, not trivial labels. Evidence: [Simulatio Lab interactive queue comparison](https://simulatiolab.com/en/experiments/checkout/) and [queueing essay background](https://thekidshouldseethis.com/post/queue-smarter-not-longer-queueing-theory-and-the-math-behind-efficient-lines). This was a serious runner-up, not a filler.
3. **Bath versus shower.** Opening: “My shower looks quicker. When does it actually use more water than a full bath?” Development: define bath fill/flow rate and crossover, then change one water-flow condition. Payoff: break-even time and why a small showerhead changes the rule. Strongest objection: a single threshold carries most of the insight, and an already-open dishwashing PR occupies similar conservation space; reliable homes differ, a long version risks padding. Model can be honest, but the story isn't stronger than the rain reversal.

**Decision:** rain beats the strongest queue rival on non-overlap and evidence of a specific new scientific caveat; still faces a large existing competitor, so viewer demand is an **experimental hypothesis, UNVERIFIED**. No topic accepted merely to fill a schedule. One research base, one story, three independently written endings.

## Primary/academic evidence actually opened

- [Crespi & Manini, Physics Open 26 (2026), 100381](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.physo.2026.100381), February 2026, accessed 2026-11-17. Full publisher text available. Abstract and Results/Conclusions report numerical moving-body model, run usually better, walking advantage only limited tailwind/little-crosswind region; up to ~7% less intercepted water by walking in some modeled conditions, ~37% more for walking without wind; no claim of a real person's fabric wetness. Formula/details grounded there.
- [Zaegel et al., European Journal of Physics 45 (2024) 025802](https://researchportal.ip-paris.fr/en/publications/should-you-walk-run-or-sprint-in-the-rain-to-get-less-wet/), March 2024; accessed 2026-11-17. Abstract confirms realistic body, variable wind/drop sizes/velocities, running generally preferable, occasional light-rain/tailwind optimum, additional sprint effort not always worth it.
- [Ehrmann & Blachowicz, European Journal of Physics 32 (2011), 355](https://doi.org/10.1088/0143-0807/32/2/008), January 17, 2011; accessed 2026-11-17. Abstract confirms different answers depending on modeling assumptions and argues explicit parameter modeling.
- [2024 MATLAB simulation preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.12023), Jan 22 2024; abstract supports typical running advantage and direction-dependent horizontal winds. Not used to assert measured human soaking.

**Our distinct toy model, not empirical evidence:** fixed path L=100m; walking v=1.5m/s; running v=4m/s; top area T=0.2 m²; front area F=0.8 m²; downward drop speed D=5m/s; no wind; uniform liquid-water concentration c. For vertical still air, relative intercepted volume proxy Q=T×D×L/v + F×L. Walk Q=66.6667+80=146.6667; run Q=25+80=105. Running advantage versus walking (146.6667−105)/146.6667=28.4091%. Walking is 39.6825% greater than running in this toy, not the published 37% of a different body model. All figures in narration are rounded and called *air-volume/proxy units*, not litres of rain. Real concentration c was not supplied and thus cannot be turned into measured liquid volume. No in-field run was performed.

Wind mechanism: relative horizontal raindrop/body velocity matters; the toy box can have an optimum near a matching tailwind, but it is not used to predict percentages for real bodies. Academic 2024/2026 findings are cited as separate models, not inserted as if they were outputs from the toy. Crosswind, body deformation, wind variability and absorption limit generalization.
