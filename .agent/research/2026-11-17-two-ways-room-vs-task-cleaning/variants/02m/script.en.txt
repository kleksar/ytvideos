I have three messy rooms to clean. Do I finish one room at a time, or do the same job in every room before moving on? Pick A or B.

I haven't timed a real home. This is a model with the same rooms, the same work and the same cleaning cart. I start at the first doorway. The rooms are eight metres apart, and I finish at the far end.

In each room I have five jobs: clear the clutter, dust, wipe, vacuum and mop. Each job takes a minute in this example. The actual cleaning is fifteen minutes either way.

A is room by room. I finish all five jobs in the first room, then move to the second and third. That's the shorter walk: sixteen metres. But changing what I'm doing fourteen times costs thirty seconds each in this model.

B is task by task. I clear all three rooms, then dust all three, and repeat for the other jobs. I walk eighty metres, back and forth. But I change tasks only four times.

Which is faster? A finishes in twenty-two minutes sixteen seconds. B finishes in eighteen minutes twenty seconds. The longer walk saves me almost four minutes because I spend far less time switching equipment.

There is a catch. If I need just one clean room before a guest arrives, A has the first room ready in seven minutes. B doesn't finish its first room until sixteen minutes four seconds. Different goal, different winner.

And if a task change takes only five seconds, A even wins the full-house race by fourteen seconds. So the trick isn't always to walk less or always to batch. It's to count the interruptions.
