## Equal-footing three pitches

### Elevator versus stairs — winner
- **Why stop:** "Would I reach the fourth floor faster by taking the stairs?" Immediate everyday tension: faster-moving machine may finish later.
- **First visible fact:** one lobby, lift not present, adjacent stairs.
- **Why continue:** first stair win 64s vs lift73s → only wait reduces, lift37s → an extra stop flips advantage → on higher floors lift recovers.
- **Full payoff:** 39-second break-even wait for 4 floors, *not* a general claim; real study supplies external mechanism check.
- **Strongest objection:** a toy spreadsheet is not an experiment and the channel targets very short videos. We openly name the model; 5/10 minute variants remain unsupported Long editorial proposals. Historic interest is evergreen, not recent surge.

### Grocery route — strongest alternative
- **Why stop:** same five items, two ways through a supermarket, which is quicker?
- **First visible fact:** a store map with products in five separated aisles, one entrance and checkout.
- **Why continue:** nearest-next may trap me crossing back; a sweep might travel less overall.
- **Full payoff:** reproducible Manhattan-distance route lengths given the exact same graph.
- **Strongest objection:** the paths depend on an invented floor plan and the narration needs many coordinates to be fair; few direct comparable YouTube A/B examples confirmed. Sources: [Cart-ographer](https://cart-ographer.com/), [Grocery Route Pathfinder](https://github.com/DaltonPayne/Grocery-Route-Pathfinder), observed 2026-11-17. Both are method/resource examples, **not evidence a specific route wins**.

### Matching socks — second alternative
- **Why stop:** twenty mixed pairs, which method touches fewer items?
- **First visible fact:** mixed laundry basket versus two equal piles.
- **Why continue:** collect matching pairs immediately vs sort by colours first, then pair.
- **Full payoff:** deterministic item touches if initial order and operation definitions are published.
- **Strongest objection:** unless the order contains a real search trap, one strategy is obvious; colour sorting may not distinguish near-identical socks. ## Primary real-world evidence

[Sachin Shah et al., "Elevators or stairs?", CMAJ 2011; open full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3255141/), observed 2026-11-17. Four participants, fourteen trips between one and six floors, both ascending/descending; mean time per floor stairs 13.1s, elevator banks 37.5s/35.6s inclusive; sums for all trips stairs 10.3 min, elevator 24.6 and 19.6 min. Difference mainly waiting. The paper itself acknowledges observational limits, fatigue and differences by time/day. These figures are **empirical**, never assigned as if the illustrative building's parameters.

[2025 observed study on hospital transfers](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12715022/) references prior elevator delays but different setting; supporting context, not new measurement for this model.

## Reproducible invented A/B model — common research for all variants

**One start:** lobby entrance. **One finish:** fourth-floor landing after exiting car. **Metric declared before reveal:** total elapsed seconds. **A:** stairs at 16 seconds per floor; **B:** elevator 7s doors + 4s exit + 3.5s movement per floor + unknown waiting `w`; no intermediate stops unless explicitly varied. These are **illustrative assumptions**, not study measurements, provider metadata, a real test or video timing.

- At 4 floors: `A=4*16=64`. `B=w+7+4+4*3.5=w+25`.
- At `w=48`, `B=73` and A wins by 9s. At `w=12`, `B=37` and B wins by 27s. Equality `w=39`.
- An extra intermediate stop `s=18` adds to B; with `w=12`, B55 vs A64; with `w=48`, B91 vs A64; new wait threshold `w=21`.
- For varying floors `f`: `A=16f`, `B=48+11+3.5f` at fixed slow wait. `f=1: 16 vs 62.5`; `f=4: 64 vs 73`; `f=5: 80 vs 76.5`; `f=8: 128 vs 87`. Continuous crossover 59/12.5=4.72 floors, discrete change to B at fifth floor.
- **Extra 10m editorial uncertainty model:** two possible waits `w=12` and `w=48`, fictional 50/50 mix. B has 37/73s, mean `(37+73)/2=55`, A64. Under an *additional explicit criterion* "arrival within 65s in both assumed states", A guarantees and B does not. No fabricated real wait distribution.
- Switch decision after spending 30s waiting is **not** the same start condition; cannot compare the new option to the original A64 without accounting for elapsed time.

**Source/fact boundary:** numbers 16/7/4/3.5/48/12/18 and probability 50/50 are ALL model parameters. No quote from staff, unseen sequence, panic, health outcome or success guarantee invented. Published 2011 statistics accurately attributed and caveated. Never blend 13.1s observed walking per floor with the invented 16s scenario.
