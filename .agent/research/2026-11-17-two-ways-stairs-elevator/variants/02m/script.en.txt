Would I reach the fourth floor faster by taking the stairs?

I get to a lobby with two routes: A, the stairs; B, the elevator. I can see the doors, but I cannot see when the car will arrive. So instead of claiming I raced anyone, I draw two routes and run an open calculation.

The finish line is the same fourth-floor landing. I start both clocks at the lobby entrance. I'm counting the whole journey, not just the time spent moving.

For A, I allow sixteen seconds per floor. Four floors cost sixty-four seconds.

For B, the elevator takes fourteen seconds to rise. It also needs seven seconds for its doors, four seconds to get out, and, in this first case, forty-eight seconds to arrive.

Fourteen plus seven plus four plus forty-eight: seventy-three seconds.

The elevator travels faster. I still reach the fourth floor nine seconds earlier on the stairs. Its speed never recovered the time lost before the doors opened.

Now I reset the exact same model. Same person, same floor, same stairs and elevator. I change one number: the elevator wait falls from forty-eight seconds to twelve.

The stairs still take sixty-four. The elevator now takes thirty-seven. B wins by twenty-seven seconds.

That is the useful result, not a universal rule to take the stairs. In this model, thirty-nine seconds of waiting means a tie. Below that, the elevator wins; above it, the stairs win. I cannot know the actual wait just from seeing the doors.

Real journeys vary, and stairs are not an equally accessible option for everyone. This is a calculation, not a filmed race.

Next time I'm standing in a lobby, the old question has a more precise meaning. Would I reach the fourth floor faster by taking the stairs?
