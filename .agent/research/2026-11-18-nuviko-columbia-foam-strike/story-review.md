## Causal story maps and evidence for scripts

### 02m — STRONG_CANDIDATE (author only)
**Promise/hook exact:** “How could a space shuttle fall apart fifteen minutes before landing when the fatal damage happened sixteen days earlier?” Approximate 'fifteen' versus NASA's ~16 min is rounded general phrasing, not a new claim of exact minute; stronger edit might use sixteen consistently, but not a story-level factual contradiction. The narrative establishes Jan16 strike → image review → lacking improved photos → crew reassurance → entry structural failure → panel-gun test. **Payoff exact:** “Seeing the strike was not the same as finding out what it had done.” Finishes with the fatal sequence starting before orbit. Source support: NASA dated chronology/CAIB and Sandia. **Interest/development/payoff 9/8/8**, all ≥8; independent reviewer UNVERIFIED.

### 05m — STRONG_CANDIDATE / **recommended** (author only)
**Questions before immersion:** “Why did seven astronauts come home in a shuttle that had already been fatally damaged? And how could engineers see the danger on launch day, yet never get a clear look at the wing?” **Immersion:** clear science-only sixteen-day flight and tank/foam explanation. **Main hook:** sighting of collision in next-day film did not show actual damage. **Progress:** prior safely returned missions shaped judgment → imagery requests unmet → Jan23 email without verified wing inspection → Feb1 hot-gas entry → ground sensor anomalies → fatal breakup. **Payoff:** real-foam panel-test hole and board's distinction; later inspection change. **Last line:** “But the investigation showed exactly why that missing inspection had mattered.” Sources NASA, CAIB and Sandia. **9/9/9** in three non-compensable editorial gates. Visuals must not imply a photo of an existing hole was available in orbit.

### 10m — REWORK (author only)
**Questions:** photographs and 16-day delayed consequence. **Unique added developments:** separate STS-107 from ISS flight; why RCC differs from tiles; flight-day-two departing fragment **only recognized in later study**; historic imagery-assessment gaps; more specific sensor information; recovery/test; August 2003 independent board; retrospective Atlantis rescue-vs-repair assessment; 2005 return-to-flight changes/foam incident. **Payoff:** "It was between seeing the impact and proving the wing was safe." **Development scored 7**, insufficient to label strong despite high historical stakes: rescue counterfactual risks pulling focus away from the core physical failure and must remain qualified, plus repeated explanations. Preserve as full finished *editorial draft* but **do not represent as approved/final strong script**. Additional historical fact alone does not cure pacing.
