## Three genuinely competitive pitches

**Hyatt / winner.** Opening, exactly as proposed 05m: “Why did two suspended walkways collapse at a Kansas City hotel while a third one survived?” First image: atrium showing the second and fourth levels stacked, the third offset. Development: load paths → original continuous rod → two shortened rods → doubled box-beam connection load → unreviewed calculations → catastrophic collapse → rescue → NBS tested actual and original arrangements. Payoff: *even the original connection failed code*, and the independent third bridge had a different load path. Objection: engineering explanation risks becoming a static diagram lecture; keep physical consequences, people and exact changes on screen. Why stop: apparent mystery. Why stay: one construction change transforms what supports what, then an unexpected second finding. What get: intuitively understands load path and accountability.

**Eagle Claw / strongest alternative.** Opening candidate: “How could a rescue mission be called off before it reached the hostages—and still kill eight people?” First visual: damaged aircraft at remote desert staging area, no fabricated archival claim. Development: aircraft availability → weather/mechanical failures → mission abort → refuelling collision → subsequent investigations. Payoff: death occurred during withdrawal, not hostage rescue. Objection: enough documented decisions and causes must be read before attributing responsibility; also multiple prior spy-rescue/Argo angles in queue, increasing thematic adjacency. It may win for emotionally rich 10m; Hyatt wins for a sharply explainable surprise in 5m.

**Bhopal / strong alternative.** Opening candidate: “How could a gas leak during the night become a catastrophe far beyond the factory fence?” First visual: industrial plant behind a populated neighborhood, no invented victims. Development: tank/process failure → escaping methyl isocyanate → exposure → emergency response and accountability. Payoff: a city-scale industrial-safety disaster, not just a local plant accident. Objection: cause/blame/health totals contested and need deeper primary + survivor evidence; this run cannot ethically turn uncertain details into dramatic first-person scenes. Potential broad interest perhaps stronger than Hyatt, but fact/source readiness weaker.

## Fact ledger; actual reads, not snippets alone

1. **NBS/NIST original investigation**, *Investigation of the Kansas City Hyatt Regency Walkways Collapse*, NBS BSS 143 (1982): https://www.nist.gov/publications/investigation-kansas-city-hyatt-regency-walkways-collapse-nbs-bss-143 and NIST history https://www.nist.gov/el/walkway-collapse-kansas-city-missouri-1981 . Authoritative for fourth/second collapse, third surviving, weak box-beam/rod connection, code-required load, initial count **113 killed / 186 injured**. These numbers reflect an earlier reporting stage; not silently conflated with later final estimates.
2. **Digitized original NBS report conclusion** (University of North Texas government-document library, scan-derived text): https://digital.library.unt.edu/ark:/67531/metadc38324/m1/267/ocr/ . Supports movement of crowd not significant, connection inadequacy, and investigation constraints. Confirm exact page before any close quotation.
3. **1982 contemporary NBS conclusions paraphrased by UPI**: https://www.upi.com/Archives/1982/02/26/Here-is-a-summary-of-conclusions-by-the-National/6635097243860/ . Supports original connection also not code-compliant and changed configuration roughly doubled connection load; original likely would have carried actual event load. Distinguish from *code capacity*, not a claim the original was safe.
4. **ASCE retrospective** *The Hyatt Regency Walkway Collapse* (2007): https://www.asce.org/publications-and-news/civil-engineering-source/civil-engineering-magazine/article/2007/01/the-hyatt-regency-walkway-collapse . Supports two short rods versus a continuous rod, verbal shop change and design-review failures, **114 later deaths**, details of engineering accountability.
5. **ASCE ethics retrospective** (2011): https://www.asce.org/publications-and-news/civil-engineering-source/civil-engineering-magazine/article/2011/07/ensuring-the-safety-health-and-welfare-of-the-public/ . Supports two professional licence revocations; injuries later described as **more than 200**, unlike 1982 preliminary 186.
6. **Structure Magazine** modern forensic discussion: https://www.structuremag.org/article/the-hyatt-regency-disaster-revisited/ . Useful counterargument: collapse not just material quality, inspections and original structural code deficiency. More technical detail is included only where narratively causal.
7. **Kansas City Star** local rescue: https://www.kansascity.com/news/local/article748398.html . Survivor Mark Williams trapped for over nine hours (secondary report, not firsthand audio). Local photos retrospective: https://www.kansascity.com/news/local/article252830713.html ; memorial story: https://www.kansascity.com/news/local/article252831633.html .
8. **Eagle Claw** official museum historical overview: https://www.asomf.org/search/eagle%2Bclaw/ . Needs focused item/source before scripting.
9. **Bhopal** UK HSE industrial accident case: https://www.hse.gov.uk/comah/sragtech/caseuncarbide84.htm . Confirms central leak/disaster; incomplete for victim testimony, disputed long-term health estimates.
10. **Great Smog** London Museum public history: https://www.londonmuseum.org.uk/collections/london-stories/the-great-smog-of-1952/ . Alternative not fully selected.

**Narration safeguards:** The third-floor bridge was offset and independently suspended, not “miraculously stronger”. Box-beam connection load approximately doubled *because lower deck became suspended from upper deck*. Avoid “single rod was safe” and “dancers caused collapse”. Initial and final death/injury counts differ across authoritative sources; clean voice master says “commonly reported final 114 and more than 200”. 10m specifically calls out original 113. Neither source proves thoughts/dialogue of hotel guests, so none invented. No misleading quote of engineers. The only personal named rescue in 10m uses local retrospective rather than an imagined interview.
