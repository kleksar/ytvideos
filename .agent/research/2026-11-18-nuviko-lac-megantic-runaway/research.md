## Selection funnel: ten different incidents

1. **Lac-Mégantic runaway oil train (2013, Canada)** — selected. Fire extinguished → compressor stops → unnoticed insufficient hand brakes → runaway → town destroyed. Official TSB chain and human payoff.
2. **MS Estonia ferry sinking (1994)** — finalist. Bow-visor failure/water entry; compelling passenger stakes and public re-investigation, but technical reconstruction and uncertainty complicate a compact fresh angle.
3. **Bhopal gas disaster (1984)** — finalist. Failed layered chemical safeguards and extreme stakes; competing estimates of fatalities, disputed responsibility and medical/industrial complexity create significant risks of glib narration.
4. **I-35W Minneapolis bridge collapse (2007)** — credible NTSB investigation and high stakes, but bridge-structure explanation and prior engineering-content saturation make opening less immediately graspable.
5. **Kaprun funicular fire (2000)** — potential human stakes; complete and current primary causal sources not retrieved in this budget.
6. **Halifax explosion/dispatcher warning (1917)** — already in Nuviko PR #72 / #150; no duplicate.
7. **Gimli Glider (1983)** — Nuviko PR #86 / #137; duplicate.
8. **Lake Nyos gas release (1986)** — Paper PR #110; existing coverage, no cross-channel duplicate this time.
9. **Air France 447 (2009)** — Nuviko PR #225; duplicate.
10. **Deepwater Horizon (2010)** — Nuviko PR #213; duplicate.

## Three competing fully described pitches

| Candidate | Why stop / exact illustrative opening | Why keep watching after hook | Payoff | Strongest objection |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Lac-Mégantic** | “Why did a train with its brakes set leave a hill by itself?” Establish parked oil train, fire and town below | A brake test apparently passes; a fire is extinguished; the engine's hidden role and slow pressure leak reverse the outcome | TSB documented hand-brake test conducted with locomotive air brakes still engaged; not firefighters-as-villains | Tragic familiar disaster coverage; railway safety detail can become procedural and emotionally detached |
| **Estonia** | “What happened to the ferry's bow before hundreds of passengers went into the sea?” | Water ingress and competing reconstruction, then newer investigation | What investigators established, and what speculation does not prove | Long technical/legal controversy risks overpromising a new cause; strong primary story but uncertain fresh novelty |
| **Bhopal** | “The gas escaped after several barriers meant to stop it could not protect the surrounding homes.” | Rising tank pressure, disabled safeguards, release and response | How multiple protections failed to prevent a lethal chemical release | Known catastrophe, contested numbers/causation and medical claims; ten minutes can easily turn into unsupported blame |

Strongest alternative: **MS Estonia** on stakes and continuing investigation; Bhopal on scale. Winner: Lac-Mégantic for a single verified, cinematic *counterintuitive* turning point that can support 2/5/10 with three distinct levels (physical test → operational chain → safety system). Even here the 10-minute marginal value is weaker than 5; do not interpret topic selection as validation of that variant.

**Chronology / material limits:** Fire did not *cause* hand-brake insufficiency; it exposed it when locomotive air pressure fell. TSB did **not** conclude a second crew member definitely would have prevented the accident. No invented dialogue, inner thoughts or motives. Forty-seven is confirmed; short scripts avoid disputed exact minute between firefighting and runaway. Mechanism is locomotive independent air brakes, not proof that every tank-car air brake was applied. The human losses cannot be reduced to an entertaining mechanical trick. English and Russian texts use same underlying claims.

## Causal story map & promise

Main promise: an apparently successful brake test allowed a heavy oil train to look safely parked; the fire response stopped the engine supporting the air brakes, and the earlier defect became visible too late. Verified path: parked slope → seven hand brakes + air-assisted test → fire / engine shut off → pressure leak → unrestrained downhill acceleration → derailment, 47 dead → systemic safety findings. Shorter text focuses on reversal; 5m shows operational failure and independent holding test; 10m expands responsible safeguards, tank-car vulnerability, dangerous-goods classification and regulatory risk.
