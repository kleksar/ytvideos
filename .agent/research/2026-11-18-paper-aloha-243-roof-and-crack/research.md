### Three developed finalist pitches

**Aloha 243 — winner.** Stop: flight loses 18 feet of roof; stay: after a landing with 94 survivors, why was a crack visible to a passenger but not caught by maintenance? Payoff: progressive multisite fatigue at an inadequately inspected lap seam, plus neglected flight-cycle loading. Exact first beat: a passenger sees a lengthwise crack while boarding; next beat at 24,000 ft exposes the gravity of that detail. Strong objection: technical investigation after the landing might feel like a second lecture. Counter: 05m uses a single rivet/overlap mental model and ends as soon as the institutional failure is clear. **Do not make the passenger responsible for the failure.**

**BA 5390 — strongest alternative for raw curiosity.** Stop: captain partly outside cockpit in flight; stay: crew hold him while first officer lands; payoff: he survives, window-bolt maintenance error. Sources: [ABC News, 2023](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2023-01-15/ba5390-pilot-sucked-out-windscreen-the-ultimate-nightmare/101813438), [UPI contemporary report, June 10 1990](https://www.upi.com/Archives/1990/06/10/British-pilot-nearly-sucked-from-plane/8573644990400/). Objection: exceptionally vivid, but the holding-and-landing payoff is likely known from viral images; evidence for an original 10m Paper treatment needs further source checking. This candidate remains available, **not owner-rejected**.

**Air Transat 236 — strongest engineering competitor.** Stop: large jet with both engines out over Atlantic; stay: why did fuel vanish and could it reach land? Payoff: 19-minute unpowered glide to Lajes, no deaths; technical cause includes a fuel leak after component mismatch, and a crossfeed response that worsened loss. Sources: [FAA lessons learned](https://www.faa.gov/lessons_learned/transport_airplane/accidents/C-GITS), [contemporary operator retrospective](https://www.transat.com/getmedia/583479f2-7f91-425a-b0c0-601c0fc11409/Emergency-landing-of-Air-Transat-Flight-TS-236-on-August-24%2C-2001.pdf). Objection: spectacular, but existing unpowered-aircraft proposals and a longer fuel-system explanation reduce distinctiveness in a five-minute paper scene.

Selection rationale: Aloha has **two distinct verified answers** (survive without roof; why that section failed) and one memorable preflight detail tying the halves together. BA 5390 may be stronger for single-frame novelty; Air Transat may be stronger for longer technical aviation followers. ## First-party factual basis (applies to all six versions)

**FAA official explainer**: https://www.faa.gov/lessons_learned/transport_airplane/accidents/N73711 (read 2026-11-18), organized sections Accident Overview / Lap Splice Design / Widespread Fatigue Damage / Maintenance Practices / Findings / Safety Initiatives.

**NTSB investigation synopsis**: https://www.ntsb.gov/investigations/Pages/DCA88MA054.aspx (read 2026-11-18). Full PDF `AAR-89/03` linked by FAA; PDF text and appendices **not used** as unseen evidence.

Verified claim → precise source:
- April 28, 1988, Hilo → Honolulu, 13:25 departure, 13:46 structural failure at 24,000 ft; approximately **18 feet** lost → FAA Accident Overview and NTSB synopsis.
- **89 passengers + 6 crew, 95 aboard; 94 survive; 1 fatality** (flight attendant Clarabelle Lansing, swept overboard; never recovered); 7 passengers + 1 attendant serious injuries → FAA overview/NTSB. Avoid implying everyone survived.
- Captain Robert Schornstheimer took control from first officer Madeline Tompkins, emergency descent and diversion to Kahului, Maui, landing → FAA Accident Overview. No invented cockpit dialogue or bravura maneuver.
- Boarding passenger later reported a longitudinal crack near upper row of rivets, **not reported preflight** → FAA Accident Overview. This is retrospective evidence, **not** a warning officially delivered to crew; no invented motive or transfer of institutional responsibility to her.
- 89,680 flight cycles / 35,496 flight hours; short routes accumulate pressure fatigue cycles faster than hours-based planning reflected → FAA Accident Overview, Maintenance Practices. English short version says *almost* 90,000 cycles rather than precise figure.
- Early lap joints: overlapping skins, adhesive plus three rows of rivets; bond degradation shifts pressurization load and rivet countersinks concentrate stress; small cracks from multiple adjacent sites can link → FAA Lap Splice Design / Widespread Fatigue Damage. **Do not imply one crack alone caused every failure.**
- The Aloha maintenance program failed to detect significant disbonding/fatigue; FAA AD 87-21-08 mandated S-4 sites, **not S-10L** where this aircraft failed; Boeing SB included broader lap areas → FAA Accident Board Findings / Precursors.
- Segmented D-check **52 packages**, inspection training/documentation weaknesses; unable to establish whether some inspections were inadequate or absent → FAA Maintenance Practices. Preserve uncertainty.
- Postaccident fleet inspections found damage signs; two other planes scrapped/sold for parts, accident plane scrapped; resulting corrosion and fatigue safety attention → FAA Maintenance Practices / Resulting Safety Initiatives.

**Do not assert:** dramatic conversations, pilots looking at specific faces, seatbelt use as the sole cause of survival, wind speed at the hole, seconds of oxygen-mask deployment, any passengers' private thoughts, crew member's intent, or passenger's blame. FAA source says 95, not varying passenger counts from some secondary narratives. No direct quotes from cockpit or survivors.

### Causal map

Regular short routes → many pressurization cycles → weakening disbonded lap seam around rivets → undetected adjacent cracks → break at 24,000 ft → crew emergency descent/diversion → Maui landing, 94 survive / Lansing lost → investigation exposes airline inspection/FAA directive gaps → revised focus on structural fatigue and corrosion.

Long adds distinct verified nodes: dawn walkaround vs no between-leg inspections, cold-bond manufacturing issues, why S-4 mandate missed S-10, split 52-package overhaul, two other aircraft scrapped. Nothing requires fictional dialogues.
