## Narrative promise and material ceilings

- **Stop:** six teenagers stranded for fifteen months and a boat without a compass. General viewer needs “Tonga = Pacific island country” but no prior castaway knowledge.
- **Stay:** broken sail → wrecked boat → scarce water → found traces of settlement → shared responsibilities, disputes and injury → ship turns toward island → legal detention after rescue.
- **Payoff:** Warner found all six alive, radio confirmed families had held funerals, then jail/compensation; survival was supported by specific duties rather than an invented moral or flawless group harmony.
- **Strong objection:** story famous online and the “Lord of the Flies” counterpoint might feel stale/patronizing; scripts never present Tongan boys as a psychological experiment, and don't call them naturally peaceful or morally superior. Opening is an actual navigation/survival question with a new post-rescue reversal.
- **02m:** concise causal outline, all central answers.
- **05m:** fuller sequence of discoveries, fire rotation, personal conflict, broken leg and legal twist; each new beat changes ability to survive.
- **10m:** historical prior settlement and raiding, sustained daily routines, failed raft, depiction/archival-source reliability, Warner's later connection and conditional coda. These new facts have sources but may slow second act. Independently judge if worth ten minutes.
