## Promise, first confirmation, answer

Target: curious English-speaking new viewers, no cave tourism knowledge assumed. The honest promise is not a miracle rescue: **a light stone makes an ankle impossible to free, and rescue workers ultimately reach the person too late**.

- Suggested canonical title **“A 27-Pound Rock Trapped Him for 17 Days”**. This points to the documented physical anomaly and gives a concrete reason to investigate. NPS labels the ordeal 17 days; script itself says more than two weeks, not a fictitious exact survival interval.
- Strong alternative title **“They Dug a New Tunnel. He Was Already Gone.”** This gives away outcome and shifts the question to *why* one rescue took so long; effective if opening promises a causal explanation.
- Third honest alternative **“How a Small Rock Defeated an Entire Rescue”** — broader and more abstract, less preferred.
- No promise that a person was alive at breakthrough; no invented first-person quote, photographs of Collins in distress or other people's expressions used as purported archival stills.
- Proposed 05m first questions already fixed: “Why did thousands of people gather around a cave to watch one man being rescued? How could a twelve-kilogram rock hold him there? And why did a rescue shaft reach the right place, but fail to save him?” First confirm with scene of narrow physical passage and 27 lb stone. Do not show an unrelated crowd before the viewer understands the trapped ankle.
- Main causal hook after informative entry: **Feb 4 collapse seals off the route that had allowed food and direct aid**. Payoff: **Feb 16 shaft breaks into cave; Floyd is dead, foot still fixed**, followed by one human consequence and closing sentence. No emotional fabricated rescue/reunion.
