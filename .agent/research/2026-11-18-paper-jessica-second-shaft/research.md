## Competitive briefs: equally substantive alternatives

### A — Jessica McClure, selected

- **Why stop:** “How did workers reach an 18-month-old trapped 22 feet below an ordinary backyard when the well was only eight inches across?” A clear, relatable helplessness; no prior identity needed.
- **Opening EN:** “Why would rescuers drill a tunnel below a trapped child instead of straight toward her?” Begins with an apparently backward decision, then supplies the precise rescue geometry.
- **Why remain:** crying on microphone → too narrow to enter → deeper parallel shaft → caliche defeats drill bits → on Friday morning the rescuers physically reach her → trapped leg means they still cannot extract → careful widening → successful lift.
- **Payoff:** touching the trapped child was not the same as releasing her. Workers created room to free the pinned leg, O'Donnell assisted her out and Forbes carried her through the new shaft. On October 16 she survived after about 58 hours, with subsequent medical treatment.
- **Strongest objection:** 1987 national rescue story is widely familiar to US older audiences and has been covered on TV, and describing drilling can become repetitive. The 05m angle specifically turns at the *false finish* after breakthrough; 10m is separately penalized for duplication/exposition.

### B — Quecreek mine, strongest alternative

- **Stop:** nine miners communicated nine taps through a small probe; the main rescue drill subsequently broke.
- **Stay:** flooded workings → miners in air pocket → nine tapping response → broken rescue bit → attempted backup drilling → renewed drilling and eventual extraction.
- **Payoff:** all nine men survived after approximately 77 hours; mapping error exposed the trapped group.
- **Strongest objection:** 2m/5m oral narration needs a map/cutaway to distinguish shafts and drill locations; stronger than Jessica for an engineering 10m, not weaker overall. CBS Pittsburgh **2022-07** timeline https://www.cbsnews.com/pittsburgh/news/20-years-later-timeline-quecreek-mine-rescue/ ; older PennLive video https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cqRXLgyzAec (publisher date/metrics not confirmed anew here). This was a competing real event, not intentionally weak.

### C — Floyd Collins, Sand Cave 1925

- **Stop:** a 27-pound rock pinned a caver's ankle in a tight crawl.
- **Stay:** family and local teams attempt removal → passage collapses → dig parallel 55-foot shaft in bad weather → news carnival and disputes → tunnel finally reaches cave.
- **Payoff:** rescuers found Collins dead when they reached him after over two weeks. This ending cannot be softened into a fictional successful rescue.
- **Strongest objection:** bleak payoff and ethically tricky contrast with family-child survival, but the NPS chronology has more distinct causal turns than a typical “strange fact.” Primary factual account: US National Park Service https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/tragedy-at-sand-cave.htm ; institutional biography https://home.nps.gov/people/floyd-collins.htm . A public YouTube archive https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Tan4Of3Ow1k was indexed, but matched creator baseline, duration and comments unavailable.

**Choice vs best rival:** Quecreek has richer technical escalation and perhaps a better ten-minute version, while Jessica's familiar child/household setting and false breakthrough to her gives a more immediate 2–5m human hook for Paper. Floyd has an extraordinarily strong consequence but a different tone and ending. **No measured claim** that Jessica outperforms them on public YouTube.

## Final editorial hypothesis and story design

Viewer: new English-speaking general audience; no assumption of knowing Jessica, caliche or 1987 news history. Primary promise: *why reaching her did not mean rescuing her*. 02m delivers geometry/false finish/outcome. 05m deepens the deliberate lower route, rock and final leg obstruction, fully causal. 10m extends long-time live broadcast vs underground work, recovery and the question of what mass spectators saw, but overlap increases and should be REWORK rather than stretched further. No direct retention, public comments or thumbnail experiment data.
