A Soviet fighter jet pulled alongside a rented Cessna, then flew away. Hours later, that little plane was parked beside the Kremlin.

On May 28, 1987, eighteen-year-old Mathias Rust left Helsinki in Finland. He had filed a flight plan for Stockholm. Over the water, he turned east instead, toward the Soviet Union.

His stated aim was to make a personal appeal for peace during the Cold War. But this was restricted airspace. He had no permission to enter it.

Finnish controllers noticed the change in direction and lost contact. They eventually began a search, fearing a crash.

Soviet radar crews also detected the plane. Missile units went on alert, and fighters were sent to investigate. One MiG pilot actually flew beside Rust. Yet his report did not produce an order to stop the Cessna.

The plane then passed through different radar sectors. In one, a controller treated it like a training aircraft. Elsewhere, it was mistaken for a helicopter. By the time officials near Moscow understood the problem, Rust was already overhead.

He couldn't simply land in Red Square. He found a nearby road bridge, lowered the Cessna onto it, passed traffic and rolled toward St. Basil's Cathedral.

Passersby gathered around. Rust was arrested, received a four-year sentence, and was released after about fourteen months. Soviet military leaders lost their jobs.
