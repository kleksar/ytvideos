## Three contesting full proposals

**A — Quecreek (selected as experimental evergreen, not proven demand).** Stop: “One wrong hole flooded a mine; a second hole saved nine men.” Develop: the old mine's boundary is wrong, escaping crew vs trapped crew, narrow air bore, knocking, larger drill breaking, pumps/air, confirmation of nine, individual cage lifts. Payoff: all nine emerge; investigators find a supposedly final map in a museum attic, but even it did not show the breach room. Audience: English-speaking general-history viewers, no mining knowledge needed. Strong objection: this resembles other recent Paper trapped-and-drilled stories, while public YouTube examples show modest exposure.

**B — Beaconsfield (strongest alternative).** Stop: “Two miners were found alive five days after a collapse, but still couldn't be brought out for another nine.” Develop: a remote-controlled device locates the miners, crews cut a path, 14-day extraction. Payoff: Webb and Russell leave alive; fellow worker Knight did not survive. Source: ABC Australia survivor interview 21 Aug 2024, https://www.abc.net.au/news/2024-08-21/beaconsfield-mine-disaster-todd-russell-i-was-actually-there/104245960; 2026 20th anniversary coverage excerpt https://www.dailymotion.com/video/xa6yh90. Objection: heavy emotional stakes and near-identical trapping/drilling mechanics to several recent Paper story PRs. A stronger candidate if the channel prioritises current Australian discussion over the map/misplaced-boundary ending.

**C — Chile 33 (strong alternative).** Stop: “33 miners were found alive after 17 days, but it would take many more weeks to get them out.” Develop: finding shelter, limited food, communication, drill engineering, individually lifted capsule. Payoff: 33 rescued on 13 Oct 2010. Evidence: https://www.theguardian.com/world/2010/aug/27/trapped-chilean-miners-video and https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5kejd1Y6bpc. Objection: very famous and extensively documented, the Paper angle must beat dominant live/video archives without pretending novelty.

**Decision:** A offers the clearest double payoff (rescue plus investigation of missing workings), and a ten-minute version can provide genuinely additional verified map history. B has the fresher commemorative angle; C has higher cultural familiarity. This is an editorial hypothesis, not a measured competitive advantage. If independent review concludes that another mine rescue will feel repetitive, REJECT without publishing; do not inflate scores.

## Source ledger — read pages, claims and limits

- **F1 — U.S. Department of Labor / MSHA, 22 Jul 2022:** https://beta.dol.gov/news-events/blog/20-years-later-look-back-quecreek-mine-rescue. Paragraphs 13–22: nine trapped for 77h; maps led miners to think old workings were hundreds of feet away, later federal appropriation $10m for digitization and detection; no automatic claim that any single map was perfectly accurate.
- **F2 — Federal Mine Safety and Health Review Commission, 28 Oct 2010:** https://fmshrc.gov/sites/default/files/decisions/commission/PENN2004-152-10282010.htm. §I.A paragraphs 43–47: breakthrough on 24 July 20:45 into Harrison No. 2, one crew escaped, another trapped, last refuge, notes and rope, cold/low oxygen. §I.B–C paragraphs 51–77: missing final certified maps, reliance on partial documents, post-rescue Windber museum attic find, even this map failed to show exact breach room. A legal decision is NOT a simple blanket final verdict of criminal liability.
- **F3 — CBS/KDKA Pittsburgh, 22 Jul 2022:** https://www.cbsnews.com/pittsburgh/news/20-years-later-timeline-quecreek-mine-rescue/. Timeline: 25 Jul narrow air drill/taps, 26 Jul larger drill bit broke and backup drilling, 27 Jul breakthrough/communication, 28 Jul extraction until 02:45. Reporter synthesis, some clocks approximate. The 150-million-gallon figure is not used as volume that actually entered the working mine.
- **F4 — The New Yorker, Peter J. Boyer, 11 Nov 2002:** https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2002/11/18/rescue-at-quecreek. Contemporary reporting: Arnold farm drilling location, surveyors, farm cleared, small hole for communication and air; not reproduced or quoted at length.
- **F5 — WTAE, 28 Jul 2022:** https://www.wtae.com/article/quecreek-mine-rescue-20-years-later/40746440. Search-visible excerpt says air pumped through bore and surface pumping, broken bit; full page not accessible from current web host (451), so only F2/F3/F4 govern script facts.
- **F6 — MSHA federal investigation report:** https://arlweb.msha.gov/quecreek/QueCreekInvestigationReport.pdf. Identified but NOT analysed or screenshot-reviewed in this pass; no fact depends exclusively on that PDF.
- **F7 — Mine safety commission legal record:** same F2; after rescue documents do not establish an intentionally concealed room or a single person deliberately placing miners in danger. All causal descriptions avoid that implication.

## Causal map for all variants

Incomplete old mine map -> working crew cuts through flooded workings -> one crew escapes, nine are blocked -> nine move to highest refuge/air pocket -> surface survey targets farm -> narrow pipe establishes contact and supplies air -> rising water requires pumps -> large escape hole required -> broken drill bit delays it -> hole reaches miners -> two-way voice confirms nine -> one-by-one rescue -> map investigation shows incomplete records -> money appropriated for mapping/detection (10m only).

Do not imply: the lost museum map would certainly have prevented this specific break (it also omitted the exact room); the men could climb out through the narrow hole; the entire adjacent mine's water entered at once; an anonymous rescue worker knew how much air time remained; or a historical photograph grants reuse rights.
