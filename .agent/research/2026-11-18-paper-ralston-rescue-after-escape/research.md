## Three competitive briefs

### A. Aron Ralston — selected

**Stop:** "He was free of an 800-pound boulder. Why did he still need rescuing?" The widely known amputation is *not* the ending.

**Stay:** wedged boulder → hours of rock-chipping → ineffective pulley → absent route disclosure → water gone Tuesday → Thursday release → one-armed 60-foot rappel → several hours of hiking → encounter with a family → the impossible final ascent → helicopter.

**Payoff:** actual rescue happened only after he exited the trap and moved through the canyon to reach people. On May 4 rescuers needed a hoist/jack to move the same boulder, a corroborating visual coda. 2004 first-person CNN recounts meeting a family and a helicopter arriving.

**Strongest objection:** popular feature film `127 Hours` already spoiled the most famous event, and a ten-minute presentation may grow repetitive or graphic. Countermeasure: foreground post-escape obstacles and the search timing, not shock detail; still rate the 10-minute text separately.

### B. Quecreek — strongest alternate

**Stop:** "Nine miners tapped their number through a tiny hole. Then the drill meant to rescue them broke." This is a compelling new threat after the first sign of survival.

**Stay:** water from old flooded mine → men gather in air pocket → exploratory hole/air → nine taps → rescue drill breaks → second location → final communication and individual cage rescue.

**Payoff:** all nine emerged alive after ~77 hours; official MSHA findings also expose the inaccurate map behind the inundation.

**Strongest objection:** multiple similar-looking men, rigs, shafts and chronology would require more explanatory exposition in a 2m/5m paper story; perhaps stronger at 10m, not weaker in absolute terms. Sources: MSHA investigation (PDF, not used for visual analysis here), https://arlweb.msha.gov/quecreek/QueCreekInvestigationReport.pdf ; contemporaneous later account https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2002/11/18/rescue-at-quecreek ; 2022 timeline https://www.cbsnews.com/pittsburgh/news/20-years-later-timeline-quecreek-mine-rescue/ . No full rival narration written.

### C. Poon Lim — strong current-video alternative

**Stop:** "The ship sank in wartime. One sailor survived on a raft for 133 days."

**Stay:** emergency raft supplies run down → water collection and fishing improvisation → prolonged drift → sighting by Brazilian fishermen.

**Payoff:** survival and rescue after four months at sea.

**Strongest objection:** repeated daily sea survival needs enough individually verified turning points for 5m/10m, and some public retellings differ on details. This was not promoted on the basis of an unverified retelling. Recent 2026 online documentaries are evidence that others publish the subject, not proof of our CTR or audience fit.

**Selection:** Ralston wins for this **specific two-to-five-minute angle** because there are distinct observed causal problems *after* the familiar headline and unusually strong first-person plus contemporaneous official sources. Quecreek has a stronger technical/mechanical ten-minute structure, and Poon Lim has arguably better fresh niche-video signals. No claim the selected topic would outperform either on YouTube.

## One central promise and evidence-linked causal map

Audience: broad English-speaking viewers interested in compact survival histories without requiring outdoor-climbing vocabulary.

- **Promise / exact title idea:** `He Escaped the Boulder. But the Canyon Wasn't Finished.`
- **First image:** small folded-paper climber between high sandstone walls; pinned right arm, a clear stone mass, only one narrow way out; original art, not an archival image.
- **Hook** (05m): "How do you leave a canyon when a boulder has pinned your arm for five days? Why didn't the search teams simply find you?" Answers appear at the points described below.
- **Event → obstacle → altered expectation → evidence:** boulder rolls and traps hand → force fails → tool chipping, then pulley fail (GQ). Nobody knows precise route → hoped-for rescue uncertain (GQ/NPS). Water gone Tuesday → time shrinks (NPS). Thursday emergency amputation → free, *not* safe (NPS/CBS). Rappel then hours of walking → still needs outside help (CBS/CNN). Three hikers give support → steep final climb remains → helicopter lifts him out (CNN/NPS).
- **Payoff** (05m): "His rescue wasn't one single moment of impossible courage. It was a series of real obstacles, and the final one required other people." Actual medical outcome, not a miraculous unhurt finish.
- **Surprising verified coda:** equipped rescuers had to return on May 4 with hoist/jack to move the stone (NPS); appears in longer versions and reinforces scale.
- **Strongest critique of our treatment:** post-escape angle is also discussed in some existing Ralston media, so it is a proposed framing rather than a provably fresh discovery. We cannot promise cinematic AV seam or audience response.
