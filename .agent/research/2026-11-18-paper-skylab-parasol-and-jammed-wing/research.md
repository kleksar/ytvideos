## Viewer, premise and selection

Audience: English-speaking viewers who enjoy real engineering under pressure but don't know the details of Skylab. No assumption they know what a solar wing does. Why stop: America's first station lost its thermal covering during launch; the rescue involved *sewing a reflective shade and extending it through a very small opening*. Why stay: heat and loss of electricity were **separate** failures; the first solution does not solve the second; the first attempt to free the wing fails; even cutting the obstruction doesn't fully open it. Payoff: parasol cools the station, then crew frees the hinge on a June 7 spacewalk and completes a productive 28-day flight followed by two more crews.

The distinctive visual is a handmade *fabric parasol* rescuing a metal space station; Paper Detour can show an entire physical paper/card diorama per scene, with the real fabric and apparatus as reconstructed subjects, not alleged archival photographs. The subject has an actual cause-and-effect sequence, unlike a list of unusual facts.

### Three competing pitches before writing

**Skylab** — opening from exact proposed 05m: “How do you repair a space station that has lost its protection from the Sun?” Specific changes: 63-second launch damage → two failed systems → ground teams fashion parasol → first wing attempt fails → parasol cools but cannot make electricity → strap cut yet hinge remains stuck → rope pull opens wing. Payoff: usable station for three crews. Strongest objection: unrecognized 1973 spacecraft, more engineering than personal stakes; NASA interest alone does not establish interest in a new paper-diorama channel. Evidence advantage is documented multi-stage causality with real people.

**Quecreek** — first sentence proposal: “Nine miners were alive under a flooded Pennsylvania mine. Why couldn't the rescuers just pump the water away?” Middle: floodwaters/air pocket, drilling and communication, pressure and rescue capsule; payoff: all nine rescued. Strong objection: too similar in feeling to the existing trapped-person Paper rescue queue. This alternative likely has *more immediate human stakes* than Skylab; it loses this time on portfolio repetition, not on fabricated lack of merit.

**Apollo 12 lightning** — first sentence proposal: “A rocket was struck by lightning twice, but the crew didn't turn around.” Middle: scrambled instrument readings and the obscure SCE power switch instruction; payoff: systems restored, mission continues to Moon. Strong objection: main cause-and-effect payoff resolves early; the 10-minute version may become a broader Apollo 12 documentary without adding value to that question. Does not win simply because NASA already provides footage.

**Decision:** Skylab offers two interlocking, independently resolved threats, concrete human action at ground and orbital level, and a contrast between a fabric shade and a technical EVA. It is an editorial **hypothesis**, not established viewer demand. Independent first listener may reject all three.

Key event → consequence chain used by all texts:
- May 14, after 63 seconds, shielding tears off → thermal problem and two power-wing failures [NASA 1].
- Crew delayed roughly ten days while ground repairs devised → canopy from scientific airlock and separate release tools [NASA 2, Smithsonian 4].
- May 25 crew arrives, first attempt fails and docking strains first day → cannot occupy station normally [NASA 2, Smithsonian 4].
- Parasol opens and temperature falls → shelter achieved but electrical deficit remains [Smithsonian 4].
- June 7 strap cut, hinge stuck, rope pull suddenly opens wing → electrical power restored; tethers protect astronauts [NASA 3].
- June 22 crew returns, two later crews complete missions → full promise resolved [NASA 5 and 6].

No first-person thoughts, dramatic quotations, or made-up causality assigned to real participants. The scripts' physical metaphors are explanatory narration, not reproduced speech.

## Storycraft and visual truth boundaries

- Intended first image: a full-frame physical paper model of a damaged Skylab with one wing missing and another folded. **Concept only; no generated reference, asset/hash or NASA image used.**
- Long opening answers promised: why shield broke (launch aero), why shade fit (folded parasol through scientific airlock), why EVA needed (panel stuck / missing power).
- The main hook follows early grounded immersion: launch achieves orbit while station remains uninhabitable/underpowered. Stakes develop again after shade works, because power is still short.
- Title/thumbnail must not imply astronauts were aboard Skylab *during* initial launch or that the parasol physically powered the station. Avoid suggesting both large wings were restored; one was lost forever.
- Public-domain/NASA visual assets are **not imported by research**; if any actual reference/portrait/photo is later used in production, obtain bytes, verify rights, hashes and Paper diorama transformation under channel rules. No image provider use now.
