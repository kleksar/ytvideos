## Selection, audience promise, and counterarguments

English-speaking casual audience who recognizes a stalled aircraft boarding line. The **payoff** is not merely "window seats first" (widely explained elsewhere); it is a transparent flip: 18 seconds saved inside a tiny cabin disappear when grouping the queue costs 20 seconds. Viewers can change the assumption and find the tie at 18 seconds. In the five-minute version a further counterexample shows the *same* back-to-front policy tied with windows-first if row-level arrival order changes.

**Three competing pitches, evaluated at a comparable level:**

1. **Boarding.** Opening (02m exact): “Which boarding line gets me into my airplane seat sooner: filling the back rows first, or letting window passengers board first?” Development: fixed row order → two aisle-seat stand-ups → window-first removes them → a *newly priced gate sorting step* flips winner. Payoff: 130 vs 112, then 130 vs 132, and the 18s crossover. Strongest objection: huge established CGP Grey/TED-Ed coverage; eight serial passengers are nothing like a real plane. Distinctive answer is the priced setup-cost threshold. No claims of operational forecasting.
2. **Shared queue vs separate lines.** Opening: “I reach two free cashiers: one queue, or one line for each?” Development: uneven service times create an idle cashier then an inversion if a queue has a joining cost. Payoff: compare completion time and max personal wait. Objection: channel's E001 already compares shopping queues; weak differentiation unless a new, genuinely more valuable premise appears.
3. **Group photos.** Opening: “We're taking six group pictures. Why does everyone keep sitting down and standing up?” Development: keep same photo requests, reorder shots to reduce moves. Payoff: total position switches, trade-off with order demanded by host. Objection: a preselected photo list could engineer any winner, and verified comparable interest was not found.

**Decision:** boarding outranks the nearest alternative by subject novelty within Two Ways and an externally demonstrated *category* of viewer interest. Checkout may have better immediate everyday familiarity but repeats the pilot. Boarding itself remains experimental for this channel: an 8-person serial cabin and visible split-group overhead must be demonstrated clearly, without implying measured flight time. Independent listener can still reject. Do not select on production convenience alone.

## Fully reproducible toy model — input, state, calculations

One **empty** cabin each trial, front row 1 / back row 4, two seats per row W (window) and A (aisle). Eight fixed participants, all with one identical bag. Starting point is the first boarding announcement; metric is **time until the last person is seated**. Queue processing is serial: one passenger completes walk/bag/sit before the next begins, 14s each; a person already seated at A who must stand for W creates 9 additional seconds. There is no simultaneous stowage; this is a *fictional simplifying rule*, not aircraft physics.

Method A fixed queue: `4W → 4A → 3A → 3W → 2A → 2W → 1W → 1A`. Rows 3 and 2 incur aisle-standing interruptions, count 2.

Method B fixed queue: `4W → 3W → 2W → 1W → 4A → 3A → 2A → 1A`. No seated aisle passenger blocks W, count 0.

| Counterfactual scenario | A | B | Result |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Both queues already organized | `8 × 14 + 2 × 9 = 130s` | `8 × 14 + 0 × 9 = 112s` | B 18s faster *inside model* |
| Sorting B's gate queue costs 20s more than A | 130s | `112 + 20 = 132s` | A 2s faster overall |
| B sorting premium `d` seconds | 130s | `112 + d` | Tie at `d=18`; B wins if `d<18`, A wins if `d>18` |
| A order amended to W then A in each row | 112s | 112s | Tie under serial rule |

**Important methodological caveat:** this is not a claim about average airline boarding. The two fixed queues are not the uniquely best representatives of their broader strategies; assigning seat order differently inside A could erase B's advantage entirely. The extra B sorting time is introduced as an explicit second scenario (not hidden after seeing the result) and A's setup is held at zero by assumption. The model holds all other inputs fixed across A/B and clears seats before rerunning. This supports a conditional payoff, not a general recommendation.
