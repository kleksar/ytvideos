Which boarding line gets me into my airplane seat sooner: filling the back rows first, or letting window passengers board first?

I made a small model to find out. Eight passengers, four rows, one window and one aisle seat per row. Same people, same bags, same seats. This is a model, not an airport stopwatch.

Method A fills the back row, then moves forward. Within each row, people follow a fixed arrival order. In two rows, the aisle passenger sits before the window passenger.

Now watch what happens. The window passenger arrives. The person by the aisle stands up again. That happens twice.

Method B sends all four window passengers first, then the four aisle passengers. Nobody has to get out of a seat for someone else.

For this deliberately simple model, I allow fourteen seconds per passenger to reach their row, handle one bag, and sit. Every seat interruption adds nine seconds. A takes a hundred and thirty seconds. B takes a hundred and twelve.

So window-first wins by eighteen seconds. But I have counted only what happens after the line is ready.

Suppose making two boarding groups adds twenty seconds at the gate. Nothing else changes. B now takes a hundred and thirty-two seconds, while A still takes a hundred and thirty. The winner flips.

Real planes let people do several things at once. Families, priority boarding, large bags, and two doors can change the result. My numbers don't predict an airline's time.
