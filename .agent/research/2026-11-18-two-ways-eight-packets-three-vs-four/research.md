## Three finalist applications, with counter-argument

**1 — Eight screw packets.** First line of 02m EN: “I've got eight sealed packets of identical screws.” Strong visual opening: eight sealed packets, left-right balance and two untouched packets. After-hook: A weighs 4:4, then 2:2 then 1:1; B starts 3:3+2, its *balanced* branch eliminates six at once, and either unbalanced branch selects three. Payoff: B two weighings for every one of eight possible lighter packets versus A three. Viewer chooses before reveal. Strongest objection: exact classic fake-coin puzzle is already online; a two-pan balance and identical screw-packet mass may feel contrived. Therefore **viewer demand/freshness UNVERIFIED**, and the piece must not present itself as a genuine workshop experiment.

**2 — Scarce cable allocation.** Proposed opening: “I have six devices waiting and only two compatible chargers. Which do I plug in first?” Development would progressively reveal incompatibility constraints; payoff could be two fully powered devices versus one, conditional on a public device-cable matrix. Objection: unknown actual cable/charging rules are a hidden rigged experiment unless the exact hardware matrix is verified. No full text, no outcome assigned in this run.

**3 — Teaching a helper.** Proposed opening: “I can finish this furniture kit alone in 20 minutes, or spend the first 10 minutes teaching someone. What if there are six kits?” Development would track model setup time versus repeated faster work; payoff would be a break-even kit count, not a fabricated productivity fact. Objection: percentages and time savings would be arbitrary without observations, potentially undermining fairness. No experiment or winner claimed.

**Decision:** #1 wins on bounded reproducible rules, two real causal paths and a precise payoff before interpretation. #2 is the strongest alternative for natural everyday recognition, but unverifiable connector compatibility presently lowers confidence in its proposed surprise. Even winner has a material objection: *known coin riddle disguised as screws* can weaken the click and recognition. This editorial selection is an experimental hypothesis, not a validated strong-market signal.

## Exact model and reproducible proof

- Eight closed packets indexed 1–8, one and only one is missing exactly one screw and therefore lighter. All other contents and packaging masses equal. A perfect two-pan balance distinguishes equal, left lighter and right lighter. No real weights, times, views or locations measured.
- A: first compare {1,2,3,4} against {5,6,7,8}. Any outcome narrows to four (can't balance if one is light); second compare two of the four against the other two; third compare the final pair. **3 weighings in every case**. 8+4+2=14 packet placements on pans if each pan placement counted; excludes removals and walking.
- B: first compare {1,2,3} against {4,5,6}, leaving {7,8} off. If left lighter, only {1,2,3} could be wrong; if right lighter, only {4,5,6}; if equal, only {7,8}. Compare 1 vs 1 within selected trio and leave the third off; for selected pair compare pair directly. All eight possibilities uniquely identified in **2 weighings**. Exactly 6+2=8 packet placements on pans; this placement count does not measure time.
- Information bound: one weighing at most three distinct observations; two at most 3×3=9 leaf outcomes. At least two for eight known lighter possibilities. Nine solvable with groups 3/3/3; ten impossible with two under same model (10 choices, at most nine observation paths).
- Sensitivity: packaging variation, insufficient precision, a heavier rather than lighter packet, two underfilled packets, or use of a numerical kitchen scale invalidate one or more stipulated inference steps. Do not generalize to real hardware without new evidence. All scripts name the hypothesis and its limits.
