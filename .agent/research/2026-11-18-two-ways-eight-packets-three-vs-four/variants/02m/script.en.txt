I've got eight sealed packets of identical screws. Seven have the right number inside. One is missing a screw, so it's lighter.

I can't open them. I have a two-pan balance, and I want to find the short packet using as few weighings as possible.

Would you choose A, split the packets in half, or B, leave two packets off the scale?

This is a logic model, not a filmed test. The packets and their contents are otherwise exactly equal, and I know there's precisely one lighter packet.

A feels obvious. I put four packets on each side. The lighter side tells me which four contain the problem.

Now I weigh two against two. That gets me down to a pair. One against one finally identifies the lighter packet. Three weighings. It works every time.

For B, I put three on the left, three on the right, and leave two on the table.

If one side rises, the missing screw is in that group of three. I weigh one suspect against another. The lighter one wins; if they balance, it's the third.

But what if those first six packets balance? Then the missing screw must be in the two I never touched. I weigh those two against each other. Done.

Two weighings, whatever the result. B wins by one weighing, not by a claimed number of seconds.

A balance gives me three useful answers: left lighter, right lighter, or equal. Splitting into halves wastes that third answer. Leaving packets untouched isn't skipping information; it's using the balance result to get it.

Real bags have packaging differences, and a scale might not detect one screw. This result only holds under the stated model.
