Four gifts. One sheet of wrapping paper. And one cutting plan that leaves the last gift uncovered.

I'm not pretending to measure how paper folds around a real box. In this model, I've already worked out the flat piece each identical gift needs, including its folding allowance: a rectangle 25 by 35 centimeters. My sheet is 60 by 90. A gift counts only if it gets one complete rectangle. I can't tape scraps together.

I can cut every piece with its 35-centimeter side across the sheet, or turn the templates so the 25-centimeter side goes across. Same sheet, same four gifts. Which would you choose: A or B?

I try A first. Only one 35-centimeter piece fits across 60 centimeters. Along the 90-centimeter length I can cut three rows, 25 centimeters each. Three wrappers. The fourth gift stays bare.

Now B. I turn every rectangle. Two widths of 25 fit side by side. Two rows of 35 fit along the length. That's four full wrappers, in a two-by-two grid.

Nothing stretched. I just stopped wasting the narrow side of the sheet.

There's an important catch. These are pre-measured flat templates, not proof that turning any real present always saves paper. Different box sizes, folds and patterns can change the answer. I would measure those before cutting.
