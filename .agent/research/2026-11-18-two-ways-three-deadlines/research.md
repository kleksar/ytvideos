## Audience promise and reason to watch

An ordinary English-speaking viewer with more things to do than a morning accommodates, not an operations-research specialist. **Promise:** the most urgent task need not be the first when the declared goal is to finish the **largest number** of jobs before their deadlines. **Exact opening 02m:** “I can finish two jobs on time by leaving my earliest deadline until last.” First image: a desk clock at 9:00 and three separate task cards, still unrevealed outcomes, A cyan / B amber. **Development:** the counterintuitive A result 2/3 → B only 1/3 → contrast between count and worst lateness → move deadlines (clearly a different model case) and B 3/3. **Payoff:** choose the metric before proclaiming any ordering rule superior. **Main weakness:** broad productivity viewers may see the model as contrived classroom mathematics rather than a lived problem; no verified current demand for this exact format.

## Three finalist pitches on the same test

| Finalist | Exact cold opening proposal | What changes after hook | Complete result for audience | Strong objection |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Deadline order** | “I can finish two jobs on time by leaving my earliest deadline until last.” | A scores 2/3, B only 1/3; moving due times makes B 3/3 | Objective changes which order is defensible; same exact three cards each A/B trial | Numbers and jobs hypothetical, video demand niche |
| **Boarding** | “I want this boarding line to move faster. Why might filling the back rows first make everyone wait?” | Aisle blockage/seat access under a controlled setup, later re-order | Distinguish aisle blocking and seat blocking, admit simulations don't model all real flights | CGP Grey and others already cover it well; hard to create an original evidence-comparable result without claiming real measurement |
| **Cable matching** | “Three cables fit my three devices, but one easy choice can leave a device unplugged.” | If the flexible device takes the scarce cable, the dedicated device is stranded; scarcity-first can assign all | Match the least flexible device first in this explicit compatibility graph | The selected compatibility arrangement itself may dictate the surprise; no independent tested real hardware |

**Tradeoff:** boarding has dramatically stronger *public interest evidence* and likely stronger spectacle than task ordering; refusing to hide that. This run favors deadline order on **originality of this explicit decision, honest checkability and personal usefulness** without borrowing a crowded presentation. That is a deliberate **experimental hypothesis**, not a data-supported ranking of probable views. Cable assignment is mechanically sound but narrower. A neutral first listener could still reject deadline order as insufficiently entertaining; independent review UNVERIFIED and no claim of 9/10 demand.

## Exact model: transparent state, objective, reset and results

All times are offsets from 09:00, one worker, no preemption, no simultaneous jobs, zero setup time, fixed known durations, all available at start. **Metric chosen before comparison: count completed by their individual due times, including a finish exactly at due time**. A = shortest duration first, tie between email/form resolved email then form. B = earliest due time, tie email then form. No change between the A/B resets.

| Job | Duration | Deadline | Internal due offset |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Slides P | 40m | 09:40 | +40m |
| Email E | 20m | 09:50 | +50m |
| Form F | 20m | 09:50 | +50m |

| Scenario | Ordered full completions | On time | Worst positive delay | Result |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| **Initial A** | E 09:20, F 09:40, P 10:20 | **2/3** | 40m (P) | A wins declared metric |
| **Initial B** | P 09:40, E 10:00, F 10:20 | **1/3** | 30m (F) | B protects earliest P |
| Only E/F due 10:00, A | E 09:20, F 09:40, P 10:20 | 2/3 | 40m | Tie with B |
| Only E/F due 10:00, B | P 09:40, E 10:00, F 10:20 | 2/3 | 20m | Tie with A on count |
| Only E/F due 10:20, A | E 09:20, F 09:40, P 10:20 | **2/3** | 40m | B beats A |
| Only E/F due 10:20, B | P 09:40, E 10:00, F 10:20 | **3/3** | 0m | B wins after explicitly stated change |

Exhaustive check of the *six* original permutations:
`PEF 1, PFE 1, EPF 1, EFP 2, FPE 1, FEP 2` (on-time tasks). Therefore **2/3 is optimal under initial model**, with formal short argument: P must occupy 09:00–09:40 to meet its deadline; then only 10 minutes remain to complete either 20m task before 09:50, so 3/3 impossible. Both orders always finish all 80m at 10:20. Optimizing **maximum tardiness** favors B (30 vs 40), a **different, explicitly named objective**. With illustrative weights P=3, E=F=1, weighted on-time rewards are A=2, B=3, again a changed objective, not a retroactive A/B victory.

Separate second illustrative morning **only in 10m text**, with *new stated inputs* (not a hidden change to original A/B):
P 30m due 09:30; E 5m due 10:00; F 10m due 10:00.
A E09:05/F09:15/P09:45 -> 2/3. B P09:30/E09:35/F09:45 -> 3/3. Total 45m either method. This checks that the sign of the result is not a universal property of shortest-first or deadline-first.

All results are deterministic paper arithmetic, author independently enumerated permutations, not field measurements or predictions under interruption, uncertain durations, team work, parallelism, customer penalties or a user-provided real calendar. No outside source is credited for the imaginary slide/email/form times.
