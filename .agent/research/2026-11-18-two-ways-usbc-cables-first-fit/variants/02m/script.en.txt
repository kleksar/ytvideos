My phone gets the best USB-C cable on the desk. That sounds harmless—until my monitor stays black.

I have four devices and four cables. Every plug looks the same. But the cables don't all do the same jobs.

I'm comparing two ways to connect everything in one pass, without unplugging anything afterward. The score is simple: how many devices work? These are labeled cables in a made-up desk model, not a test of real hardware.

One cable can handle video, fast data, and high-power charging. Another handles high-power charging. A third handles data. The last is basic charging. They're lined up in that order.

My phone can use any of them. My drive needs data. My laptop needs the high-power option. And my monitor needs the video cable. Assume the computer ports and chargers support all four jobs.

Pick A or B before I connect anything. A means I start with my phone, then drive, laptop, and monitor, taking the first compatible cable each time. B means I start with the device with the fewest choices.

With A, I hand that video-capable cable to my phone. The drive takes the data cable, the laptop takes the power cable—and the monitor is left with a charging-only cable. Three devices work. One dark screen.

I reset every connection. With B, the monitor gets the only video cable first. The laptop takes the power cable, the drive takes the data cable, and the phone uses the basic one. Four out of four.

I could rescue A by swapping the phone's cable with the monitor's. So this isn't a claim that A can never work, or that B is faster in seconds. It's a first-pass comparison with a fixed cable order.
