## Three serious pitches — same questions asked of each

### A. Great Escape — chosen, with a material counterargument

- **Audience:** general English-language historical-story audience, without assumed knowledge of Steve McQueen or Stalag Luft III.
- **Cold open:** “Why did seventy-six men get out of the Nazi prison camp, but only three reach safety?” (2-minute script uses the exact independent question).
- **First proof:** tunnels named Tom, Dick, Harry; exit misses tree cover; rope signalling beneath a tower.
- **Develops after hook:** discovered tunnel → deep timber-shored shaft → forged identities/rail carts → unexpectedly exposed exit → rate-limited launch → nationwide manhunt.
- **Payoff:** 76 escaped the camp, 73 were recaptured, and 50 of those were deliberately murdered. The tunnel and surviving the pursuit are separate things.
- **Best reason to reject:** globally famous film and established documentary market; as recently as October 2026 an independent 114-second publication uses the *same 76 out / 3 safe* angle. The distinctive editorial work must live in the human/cause sequence, not a novel statistic. Honest risk of saturation.

### B. Operation Anthropoid — strongest alternative

- **Cold open:** “The gun jammed in front of one of Hitler's most powerful men. The attack was not over.”
- **Progression:** clandestine preparations → gun failure at Heydrich's car → grenade → death days later → hiding and final stand of attackers → Nazi retaliation against civilians.
- **Payoff:** assassination succeeded, and the retaliation including the destruction of Lidice reveals its terrible cost. Cannot end as simple triumph.
- **Strongest argument for choosing over A:** named protagonists, greater immediate life-or-death stakes and morally substantial consequences.
- **Strongest objection:** risk of using civilian atrocities for suspense, complex context in 2 minutes, many existing WWII depictions, a potential invented motive if written loosely. No same-author comparable audience baseline verified.
- **Fact evidence:** US Holocaust Memorial Museum, `https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/timeline-event/holocaust/1942-1945/assassination-of-reinhard-heydrich`; corroboration on Lidice `https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/lidice`. Source reviewed; not written into scripts.

### C. Tunnel 57 — another serious alternative

- **Cold open:** “A woman crawled from East Berlin into a bakery cellar under the Wall. Fifty-six more followed.”
- **Progression:** failed first tunnel and imprisoned girlfriend → students dig beneath Wall from disused bakery → 57 flee in two days → shooting and debate about border death.
- **Payoff:** family reunification, and the political aftermath rather than only tunnel engineering.
- **Best argument for choosing over A:** immediate human specificity, less filmed/memed than Great Escape, accessible geography.
- **Objection:** detailed shooting causality requires additional primary research; same structural tunnel escape may be less distinctive for a queue with many rescue/engineering stories.
- **Fact evidence:** Smithsonian Magazine, Jessica Camille Aguirre, 2014-11-07, `https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/most-successful-tunnel-escape-history-berlin-wall-180953268/`.

**Selection decision:** A beats B only on a tighter verified structure with 2/5/10 independent length-value and accessible multiple institutional sources; B has more severe ethical and attribution risks in the Short-length synopsis. A beats C on explicit 200→76→3 / 50 second payoff and ready primary fact coverage. This is an **editorial hypothesis**, not evidence of higher viewers, CTR, retention or public interest. B remains viable for a separate future selection, not rejected by owner.

## Primary account / fact evidence actually read

- **RAF, “Great Escape 75,” 2019-03-24:** `https://www.raf.mod.uk/news/articles/great-escape-75/`. - **RAF Benevolent Fund, “The Great Escape,” updated 2024-03-20:** `https://www.rafbf.org/great-escape`. 24–25 March 1944, Bushell, 200 planned, 76 out, 73 caught, 50 shot by Gestapo. Distinguish escaping the camp from reaching safety.
- **RAF Benevolent Fund veteran interviews:** `https://www.rafbf.org/great-escape`, links to Jack Lyon and Charles Clarke accounts. Distinguish primary testimony from independent event measurement.
- **RAF Museum, “Remarkable RAF men…”:** `https://www.rafmuseum.org.uk/blog/remarkable-raf-men-in-our-new-london-exhibition/`. Paul Brickhill's memoir and later popular culture; claustrophobia prevented his participation in the escape line, so his own experience differs from those who passed through Harry.
- **HISTORY, “The Great Escape,” history explainer:** `https://www.history.com/articles/great-escape-wwii-nazi-stalag-luft-iii`. Context: sandy site/raised huts, sand distribution, improvised engineering, interrupted breakout, distinction between true events and film. Secondary account; check individual precise details against stronger sources before production.
- **UK National Archives:** `https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/explore-the-collection/stories/roger-bushell-and-the-great-escape/`. Search metadata available but direct body returned 403 during this research; **not** treated as read source evidence. Can serve later source cross-check only.
- **War crimes:** RAF article and RAF Benevolent history support fifty murdered *after recapture*, not deaths in a fight at the fence. Do not dramatise shooting or invent dialogue/thoughts.
- **Named survivors:** Per Bergsland, Jens Müller, Bram van der Stok in RAF account. Other specific personal details require careful source review.
- **All full variants:** no imaginary quotes, opinions attributed to protagonists, unsupported first-person participation, actual film footage or misleading archival treatment. The EN/RU wording may need independent detailed source verification before approval.

## Narrative factual chain and risks

1. Escape-proof design: sandy raised-hut camp → digging harder/detectable → 3 deep shafts to distribute risk (RAF/HISTORY).
2. Tom discovered, Dick as store → only Harry remains (RAF).
3. Hundreds prepare documents, disguises, air supply, rails → 200 chosen (RAF).
4. Harry misses the tree line → rope signals slow departures; darkness ends → 76 make it out (RAF).
5. Manhunt captures 73; 3 get away → Hitler orders murder of 50 recaptured, not 50 killed on escape night (RAF/RAF Benevolent).
6. Postwar investigations and book/film memory → two endings, the physical tunnel and human aftermath (RAF/RAF Museum).

**Unverified details needing careful independent audit:** precise order of occasional tunnel interruptions and partial collapse (secondary account), exact number/time per wave of escapees, names and station where Bushell caught, film's artistic embellishments. Sources should not be used to portray the reconstructed scene as actual footage. Never invent survivor quotes or what prisoners felt. The 10m film distinction is illustrative and may be cut if proof inadequate.

**Copyright:** scripts are independently written. Other providers' video footage, archive images, newspaper scans and music have NOT been imported. Rights and approved actual reference bytes remain a future production prerequisite.
