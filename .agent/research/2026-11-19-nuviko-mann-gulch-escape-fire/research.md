# Research and selection — Nuviko / Mann Gulch (19 November 2026)

## Scope and ethics

Audience: English-speaking viewers who need no knowledge of US wildfire history. Main question: **How did starting a second fire let a trapped firefighter survive, while his colleagues could not outrun the first fire?** Explain the independent refuge of two survivors, without reducing thirteen deaths to a clever trick or imagining their motives. The `escape fire` is a documented action, **not self-help survival advice**. All external search terms were generic; no private repo content, account credentials or unpublished materials sent to the web.

## Three finalist proposals, concrete narrative check

| Candidate | Opening (exact draft line / scene) | After hook | Payoff | Strongest objection |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mann Gulch | “Why would a firefighter deliberately start another fire while being chased by one?” Open on running slope, then a match and a smaller circle of fire | River route blocked; team climbs; Dodge removes fuel; other men use rock; 13 fatalities | Fuel-free patch and rocky refuge explain survival, later safety work | Fresh Oct 2026 major-channel video already uses Mann Gulch; fire danger could be romanticized as a trick |
| Great Smog | “What if the air you breathed inside your own home was killing you?” Show street and bus disappearing | 5–9 Dec thickening; coal combustion and inversion; death toll noticed afterwards; policy shift | 1956 Clean Air Act; visibility vs invisible hazard | Few independent character actions; exposition may peak too early |

**Comparison:** Great Smog is the strongest *different* option; it offers an immediately relatable environmental question and a clear public-policy payoff. Mann Gulch has the stronger central contradiction and distinct mid-story survival routes, allowing meaningful 2 → 5 minute expansion. It is not a proven viewer-demand winner; the likely saturation caused by a large creator's fresh 2026 episode is a serious counter-signal.

## Selected story map and promise

- **Promise:** Why would the leader start *another* fire when flames were chasing his sixteen-person crew, and how did anyone survive?
- **Opening:** exact questions in each Long EN script. Early visible action: dry grassy slope, line of smoke, diverging routes; never the 2D traveler impersonating an actual 1949 firefighter.
- **Causality:** assignment → blocked river route → exhausting uphill flight → Dodge's escape fire removing fuel → rocky refuge for two → 13 deaths → lessons about fuel and routes.
- **Payoff:** first answer (no fuel for main flame on burned patch) plus distinct survival path (rock with sparse vegetation). Human death toll presented without triumphal tone.
- **Risk:** “escape fire” mistaken as a recommendation. Keep factual distinction, especially in 10m. Illustrations must avoid unsupported cinematic details.
- **Closest competing idea:** Great Smog, evidence above. Chosen for deeper action sequence, **not** because historical truth or popularity guarantees entertainment.
