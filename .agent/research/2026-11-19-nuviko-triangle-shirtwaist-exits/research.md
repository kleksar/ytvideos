## Three equally serious pitches

**A / Triangle (selected).** Stop: *How could the building remain standing when 146 people could not escape?* First EN question is present in each exact script. Stay: eight-floor fire and evacuation → tenth-floor warning and improvised roof crossing → ninth-floor no alarm, locked door, fading alternatives → inadequate fire equipment → surprising criminal acquittal. Payoff: doors/alarms/egress reform and precise distinction between structural survival and occupant survival. Worst objection: frequently told history, including documentary videos from several authors; generic "locked door" packaging would be indistinguishable. Our angle is the **contrast of three adjacent floors and their warning windows**, with court outcome not used to imply exoneration of unsafe conditions.

**B / Aberfan (closest challenger).** Stop: *Why did a mountainside of mining waste reach a school at the start of the day?* Start with the location of tip no.7 over Pantglas Junior School and underlying water. Stay: slope movement → impact on homes and school → frantic rescue → official inquiry and multigenerational memory. Payoff: mechanism and preventability; no invented child dialogue. Best advantage over A: **fresh 60th anniversary archival exhibition opened 2026-10-10**, not just evergreen traffic. Worst objection: mass child deaths are difficult to handle responsibly in AI-generated imagery; strong fresh interest is not independently measured YouTube demand. Sources: [National Library of Wales, timeline](https://www.library.wales/discover-learn/digital-exhibitions/archives/aberfan-disaster-1966), [National Library exhibition 2026-10-09](https://www.library.wales/news/article/aberfan-disaster-1966), [Museum Wales Aberfan 60](https://museum.wales/aberfan60/). **Not rejected by owner**; preserve as next independent candidate if available.

**C / Great Smog.** Stop: *Why could a few days of ordinary coal smoke cause thousands of deaths?* Start with visibility collapsing across London in December 1952. Stay: stagnant weather and coal smoke → health crisis → changing official mortality count → air-pollution law. Payoff: why an invisible hazard demands standards; no personified villain. Worst objection: epidemiological estimates change across sources; hard to turn a diffuse phenomenon into a clear, distinctive 2-minute sequence. Primary institutional background: [London Museum](https://www.londonmuseum.org.uk/collections/london-stories/the-great-smog-of-1952/). Not rejected.

**Decision:** A narrowly wins *causal visibility of specific actions / multiple turning points / distinct court-and-law payoff* for Nuviko's illustrated 2/5/10 scripts. B is plausibly stronger on **freshness** and may outperform A; this has not been measured. A's evergreen interest and ownership of an original angle are hypotheses, not CTR or retention measurements. No competing subject was scored as proven weak merely to elevate A.

## Evidence ledger: verify the exact claims, not search snippets

Read original institutional source text on 2026-11-19. Each row states what it supports **and what it does not**.

1. [U.S. NPS — Triangle factory / Brown Building](https://home.nps.gov/places/triangle-shirtwaist-factory-brown-building.htm), **overview of March 25, 1911 + aftermath**. Supports fire beginning on eighth floor among cloth scraps; rapid spread; employees on tenth warned while ninth not warned; no working alarm and emergency hose; repeated elevator rescue trips (**at least 150 by account**); tenth-floor roof / adjoining NYU student ladders; one ninth-floor stairway locked; fire escape collapse; inadequate firefighting; **146 fatalities**. Does **not** establish which person discarded a cigarette or that *all* exits were locked.
2. [U.S. Census Bureau — March 2016 history feature](https://www.census.gov/about/history/stories/monthly/2016/march-2016.html), **paras factory growth, fire and trial**. Supports company and owners Max Blanck/Isaac Harris, >1,000 garments/day by 1908, factory labor/strike conditions, ladders only reaching sixth floor, approx half-hour fire, **146 deaths**, trial and **Dec 27, 1911 acquittal because knowledge of locked exits was not proven**, 1911 Sullivan-Hoey fire-prevention legislation and additional reforms. Also supports **1914 civil settlements ~US$75 per victim**; do not suggest value-of-life conclusion.
3. [NPS — 200 years of labor history](https://www.nps.gov/blrv/learn/historyculture/200-labor-events.htm), **Triangle entry**. Corroborates ninth-floor lack of warning, insufficient exit, 146 fatalities and 1909 worker action. Distinct NPS page, not independent forensic research.
4. [NPS — Mink Brigade](https://www.nps.gov/people/mink-brigade.htm), **1911 section**. Corroborates lock use to discourage unauthorized breaks and worker advocacy; not authority for imputing criminal knowledge to owners.
5. [Cornell ILR Triangle Fire collection](https://trianglefire.ilr.cornell.edu/), **archive landing and trial records**, with [trial introduction](https://trianglefire.ilr.cornell.edu/primary/trialRecords/introduction.html). Primary proceedings collections and how the criminal charge narrowed to knowledge. Narration uses no unverified courtroom verbatim quotes.
6. [PBS American Experience: Triangle Fire](https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/americanexperience/films/triangle%20/), **program description, historical articles and 1909 strike sections**, documentary contextual corroboration. Trailer/page metadata read; **full film not watched**.
7. For long-version late memorial: NPS Brown Building historical account states final six victim names were established by Michael Hirsch in 2011. This is used as an archival correction, not as a mystery claim.

**Uncertainty/counterchecks:** likely match/cigarette ≠ identified cause; “fireproof” refers to building structure, not fabric or exits. “Jury acquitted” ≠ “owners did nothing unsafe”. 1909 strike was a garment-industry event; do NOT assert each individual victim had struck. No invented thoughts, dialogue, exact victim journey, or immediate rescue orders. The scripts include all material facts for their specific length; no source wording is copied into the narration.

## Three concrete story maps

- **02m:** question comparing floors → fire on eighth → tenth warned/roof → ninth uninformed → exits and response fail → 146 dead → owners acquitted under narrow test → safety changes. Exact payoff ends with walls standing/doors needed. A complete short **Long**, not a preview.
- **05m:** questions → factory/1909 conflict + structural misconception → ignition → stair/roof warnings → ninth's time loss → elevators and collapsing escape → ladders unable to reach → criminal court standard → safety rules. This extra causal chain, especially the **two elevator operators' repeated trips**, justifies added time.
- **10m:** questions → labor and business context → three-floor timeline → separate warning, stair, elevator, fire escape and fire-service mechanisms → trial and settlements → legislative process → later identification of six names. Its extensive post-climax court-law-memorial discussion risks weaker momentum; not preferred.
