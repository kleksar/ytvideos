## One story, audience and thesis

**Central viewer question:** A father says a six-year-old was carried away by a homemade flying saucer. The entire nation watches it land empty. Where was the child? Why did a rescue later turn into a criminal investigation and then a pardon?

**Hook promise:** flying silver object + missing child; **first established action:** on 2009-10-15 a helium craft broke free and Falcon was missing; **first shift:** the family thought he boarded, but the footage never showed him; **second:** landing empty increases fear, not relief; **payoff:** child found alive hiding in garage attic; subsequent hoax investigation/pleas, disputed intent and 2020 pardon. A pardon is neither a record of the child aboard the balloon nor a definitive adjudication of every disputed claim about planning.

**Why Paper rather than specialist history:** parent/child, search, twist and public judgment require no niche knowledge. An English viewer does not need to know the Heene family or U.S. criminal procedure before the story begins. 2009 media coverage and Netflix's July 2025 return show long-lived awareness, but do not prove current CTR, retention or demand for handmade paper visuals.

**Strong objection:** 2017 Internet Historian and the 2025 Netflix documentary have extensively covered this case. A new Paper retelling risks being derivative; 10-minute evidence discussion can become an exercise in secondhand uncertainty. Distinct editorial promise is **two separate mysteries**: child location conclusively answered; adult intent disputed even after guilty pleas and pardons. This is not a court verdict. If reviewers think the two-part treatment is uninteresting or legally misleading, reject the idea.

## Story map and promised answers (one factual research shared by 2/5/10)

1. **A family builds a silver saucer-like balloon** — camera-ready unusual object but real purpose per family's own account; the older child thought Falcon boarded. *Source: CNN, 5280*.
2. **Balloon takes flight, child missing** — television can show flying object but cannot confirm the passenger. *CNN, 5280*.
3. **Landing is empty** — supposed rescue fails; now the feared outcome is a fall. *CBS, 5280*.
4. **Attic recovery** — full physical mystery resolved, six-year-old hiding in box. *CNN, 5280*.
5. **TV interview reinterprets the event** — “for the show” leads to scrutiny; don't stage faux footage of a private plan. *CNN*.
6. **Investigation and pleas** — legal consequences and investigator conclusion; family disputes intent and coercion circumstances. *5280, CBS, Washington Post*.
7. **2019 case notes and 2020 pardon** — documentary evidence interpreted by reporter but disputed; clemency reduces lasting consequences, not factual re-adjudication. *5280, Washington Post*.
8. **2025 family revisit** — adult Falcon's recollection; no general verdict. *Netflix, PEOPLE*.

This is documented causal movement, not a format trick. **Two-minute:** physical answer + criminal/pardon paradox compactly. **Five-minute:** proper turning points, investigation, notes and pardon. **Ten-minute:** original broadcast/tether evidence, layered documentary uncertainty, detailed legal and 2019 case-file reconstruction; editorial risk long post-landing investigation. All three tell the **same** story, not three separate themes.
