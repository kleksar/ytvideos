A five-year-old asked to be Batman. So San Francisco turned itself into Gotham City.

His name was Miles Scott. He had spent much of his childhood receiving treatment for leukemia. By November 2013, his cancer was in remission, and the Make-A-Wish foundation asked what he wanted most.

Miles didn't ask to meet an actor. He wanted to fight crime as a superhero.

On November fifteenth, a man dressed as Batman arrived with a costume for him. Outside waited a black sports car dressed up as a Batmobile. Then the police chief called: Gotham needed help.

It was a carefully staged adventure, but the missions were real things Miles got to do. He helped rescue a woman from cable-car tracks. He caught the Riddler at a pretend bank robbery. Later, the Penguin kidnapped Lou Seal, the San Francisco Giants' mascot, and Batkid went after him at the baseball stadium.

The surprise wasn't the costumed villains. It was the crowd. Thousands of people filled the streets to cheer for a child they had never met.

Police and firefighters joined in. The local newspaper printed a special Gotham edition. At City Hall, the mayor handed Miles a key to the city. Even President Obama sent a message.

It was one day, not a cure. But it gave a boy finishing years of treatment a day in which he could be the hero of his own story.

And ten years later, Make-A-Wish reported that Miles remained cancer-free. His mother had become a volunteer granting wishes to other children.

The people playing Gotham's citizens helped one child. Then that family's kindness carried on.
