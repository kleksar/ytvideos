### Three genuinely competing pitches

**1. Ever Given — selected.** Opening: “Could one tiny excavator free a ship that blocked the Suez Canal?” A photo of tiny equipment against a giant bow tells the problem at a glance. *After* the photo: bow stuck in soil → diggers and dredgers loosen sand → tugboats cannot immediately pull free → larger tugs and high tide synchronize → stern swings before bow releases → canal reopens → second obstacle: compensation detention until July. Payoff: the tiny excavator was real, not a miracle or a joke; the actual answer was interdependent work, and the rescue didn't free the ship from its legal predicament. **Counterargument:** a huge 2021 meme, already saturated by coverage; new viewer may know the answer “tugs plus tide”; long aftermath risks becoming legal lecture. Chosen because everyday-scale image plus a real second consequence provide distinct changing expectations and a visual paper miniature, unlike merely explaining a maritime term.

**2. Bangladesh Bank — strongest rejected alternative, still available.** Opening: “A missing letter helped stop a billion-dollar theft. Why did $81 million still vanish?” After hook: fraudulent instructions move through banking system → transfers to Philippines go through → Sri Lanka payment raises spelling query and returns → different destinations split the outcome. Payoff: typo halted one transaction, not the completed $81m; it is inaccurate to claim the typo stopped all theft. **Objection:** explanations of interbank messages are abstract; 2m may simplify the system and 10m risks becoming a technical procedure rather than a human-scale plot. Reuters contemporaneous reporting: https://www.theguardian.com/business/2016/mar/10/spelling-mistake-prevented-bank-heist and https://www.yahoo.com/news/millions-bangladesh-bank-heist-disappeared-110325441.html . YouTube CyberArk https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MLqh3-NNO7s (2017-03-23; 18,529 public views observed via search; a technical channel, 14.3k subscribers) is **not** a direct baseline for Paper Detour.

**3. Fake Hitler diaries — also plausible.** Opening: “Sixty notebooks sold as historic diaries; an archivist discovered modern paper.” After hook: exclusive media release → rapid and apparently convincing expert reaction → Bundesarchiv gets original notebooks → forensic tests expose materials made after the alleged dates → public retraction. Payoff: famous newspaper claims could not overcome the physical properties of the objects. **Objection:** presentation must not glamorize the dictator or rely on faux quotations; older and more niche than the universal Suez photo, and archive discovery alone leaves a short 2m variant stronger than a long narrative. German Federal Archives primary record https://www.bundesarchiv.de/themen-entdecken/online-entdecken/geschichtsgalerien/pressekonferenz-des-bundesarchivs-zu-hitler-tagebuechern/ (May 6, 1983 discovery); a small English YouTube comparator https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F7ALIchGYKw (2024-07-22; 739 public views, 36.9k subscribers observed via search).
