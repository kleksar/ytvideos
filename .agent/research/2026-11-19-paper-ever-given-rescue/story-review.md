## Three distinct story maps and audience value

**02m:** Question: can one miniature digger free a huge vessel? Real bow embedding → shoreline digger image → underwater dredging/tugs → high tide and more pulling power → release March 29 → second outcome: ship held until July → answer: visible digger was only one real part of the team. **Payoff before loop:** “The ship moved when the digging, the pulling and the tide finally worked together.” Cold opening is understandable without any preceding clip. Closing “That famous picture made the whole operation look much simpler” returns to the initial excavator question as a reinterpretation, not merely a matching word. EN and RU have been read against each other twice by the author; **independent second-cycle and AV seam UNVERIFIED**. Risk: a news-savvy viewer may know the combination immediately and not need two minutes. Unmatched ending-to-opening picture timing requires later QA.

**05m:** Opening distinct documentary questions: how did it stick, why not pull it, why remained in Egypt? Necessary context on single-lane geometry → visible diagonal grounding (main hook) → bow trapped, dredging needed → tug difficulties/queue pressure → alternative container-unloading considered → more power + high tide → premature celebration as stern turns but bow stays stuck → complete refloat → law/court/settlement hold → two separate forms of being stuck. **Payoff:** “The small machine helped. It was never the whole answer.” The legal second trap answers third opening question without inventing an agreed sum. Risk: a short explanation of soil/buoyancy can feel like a mechanics lesson; keep action and physical space visible in paper staging.

**10m:** Three opening questions → geography and one-lane constraints → grounded bow as hook → buoyancy and dredging → tug force & coordination → hundred-ship queue and supply chain uncertainty → container unloading vs ballast contingency → forecast tide and two seagoing tugs → partial stern turn → complete release → queue clearing → April detention, demands vs paid amount, July departure → distinct physical and legal resolution. **Payoff:** “The first problem was a hull stuck in sand. The second was a bill nobody had agreed to pay.” Risk: middle stretches about ballast, trade estimates and settlement carry repeated logical reminders before the second trap; “proof” is solid but rhythm is too instructional for a broad audience.
