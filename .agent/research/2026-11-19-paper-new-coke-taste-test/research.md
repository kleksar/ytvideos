## Three researched finalist pitches, fair comparison
### A — New Coke, winning test vs missing choice
**First EN line:** "Coca-Cola made a sweeter drink that won its taste tests. Then customers started hoarding the one it was meant to replace." Longer opening can begin with the documented May 31 customer hotline exchange.
**First visual:** two physical paper-cola bottle silhouettes; preferred tasting cup in foreground, familiar original bottle absent from a store shelf. **After hook:** Pepsi's test campaign → Coke's multi-year testing → *replacement rather than addition* → complaints, stocked cases, hotline staff working overtime while executives defend 55–45 result → reversal 79 days later. **Payoff:** original returned as **Classic**, new still marketed beside it; taste preference ≠ acceptance of losing previous choice. **Why audience:** universal consumer attachment / product choices, recognizable without a history lesson. **Strongest objection:** not visually high-stakes as a physical rescue; can sound like a business lecture. Our chosen 5m builds human actions (hoarding, actual complaint call, expanding hotline) instead of generic brand strategy, while 10m is explicitly weaker.

### B — Balloon Boy, empty balloon and hidden child
**First EN line (pitch only):** "A silver balloon landed in Colorado. The boy everyone thought was aboard wasn't inside."
**First visual:** paper weather balloon, open empty landing site. **After hook:** rescue response → search for child → discovery at home → police theory and guilty pleas → subsequent pardons/parents dispute account. **Payoff:** child not in balloon, legal aftermath not a proven single motive. **Why audience:** parent-child stakes and a mystery with real resolution. **Strongest objection:** the core question is answered fairly early; 10m needs disputed legal detail and cannot honestly assert all adults colluded. Strong recent 2025 documentary competition. Sources: Reuters 2020-12-24 https://www.reuters.com/world/us/balloon-boy-parents-pardoned-by-colorado-governor-over-2009-hoax-2020-12-24/ ; Washington Post 2020-12-24 https://www.washingtonpost.com/nation/2020/12/24/balloon-boy-pardon-colorado/ ; TIME 2025 https://time.com/7302213/trainwreck-balloon-boy-netflix/ . Legal narrative not fully reviewed, so not chosen.

### C — Harrison Okene, diver grabs a living hand
**First EN line (pitch only):** "A diver came to recover bodies from a sunken tug. One hand grabbed his back."
**First visual:** cutaway paper submerged boat, bubble in dark cabin, glove and hand. **After hook:** sinking → air pocket → divers looking for bodies → shock at touch → decompression ascent. **Payoff:** cook survived; 11 others did not, and returning to surface required controlled pressure. **Why audience:** immediate human stakes, real rescue recording available for study. **Strongest objection:** our Paper queue already includes Quecreek #232, Baby Jessica #218 and Floyd Collins #224; further similar survival episode dilutes channel variety; footage rights and reuse **UNVERIFIED**. Primary-ish near-event reports: https://www.theguardian.com/world/2013/jun/12/nigeria-sailor-survive-air-pocket (2013-06-12 interview), https://www.itv.com/news/2013-12-02/miraculous-ship-rescue-new-footage-emerges (2013-12-02), AP via https://www.houstonchronicle.com/news/nation-world/world/article/man-survives-3-days-at-bottom-of-the-atlantic-5032727.php (2013-12-03).

**Choice with equal-weight adversarial comparison:** Harrison is stronger for instant physical peril and a captured reversal; New Coke is stronger for underserved genre variety, broad daily-life stakes, more independent factual material for three script lengths, and a twist that changes the apparent meaning of the experiment. Balloon Boy has the strongest opening/recency but the weakest uncontested ten-minute path. **New Coke wins provisionally**, not from a proven CTR/retention advantage.
