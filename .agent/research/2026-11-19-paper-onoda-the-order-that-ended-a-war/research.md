## Audience and concrete reason to watch
For a new viewer the promise is independent of knowing Onoda's name: a soldier on an inhabited island continued armed activity until 1974, almost 29 years after Japan surrendered. Leaflets and letters failed; an unrelated civilian found him; an ex-major who had become a **bookseller** came back to revoke the order. The surprise is not simply time spent in a jungle. What changed after the hook: comrade surrendered in 1950, family letters delivered 1952, two armed companions died in 1954/1972, Suzuki succeeded where state searches failed, Taniguchi issued an actual release order. Final payoff has two outcomes: an end to Onoda's holdout and an end to a long threat facing Filipino families. Strong objection: the familiar hero myth often omits victims, and readers may perceive a contrary account as a bait-and-switch. Include civilian testimony with source limits, never invent a motive or present belief as objectively certain.

## Three serious pitches on equal terms
**Onoda.** First line: "Why did a Japanese soldier need an order to stop fighting in 1974?" First image: paper relief order presented by an elderly former commander before a tropical hill. Source-backed middle: dated leaflets/letters, 1950 departure and 1972 death, 1974 Suzuki photo/contact; payoff Taniguchi's March 1974 order and Filipino memory. Objection: risk of heroic myth or didactic victim epilogue.

**Great Escape.** First line: "Seventy-six prisoners got through a tunnel; only three reached freedom." Middle Roger Bushell's plan, escape, search, recapture and killings; payoff escape achieved for only three and 50 murdered. Source: [UK National Archives Great Escapes](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/whats-on/exhibitions/great-escapes/), [Roger Bushell](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/explore-the-collection/stories/roger-bushell-and-the-great-escape/). Objection: well-known film association and claustrophobic repeated tunnel/soldier beats.

**D.B. Cooper.** First line: "He walked off a passenger plane in midair, with ransom, and was never found." Middle four parachutes and 200,000-dollar demand, release of passengers, night jump, cash found in 1980; payoff investigation still can't verify his identity/fate. [FBI original case](https://www.fbi.gov/history/cases-and-criminals/db-cooper-hijacking), read. Objection: unresolved human ending and large established competitor videos.

**Decision:** Onoda beats closest alternative Great Escape for this Paper package by offering a clearer unusual physical end mechanism and a shift in perspective at the very end; Great Escape arguably has more direct official engineering detail for a 10m version and remains a legitimate future candidate. Not evidence Onoda will get more views.
