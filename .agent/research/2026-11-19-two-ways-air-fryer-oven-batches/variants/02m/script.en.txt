I have six people waiting for fries. Do I use my little air fryer, or the big electric oven?

I'll choose A, the air fryer, or B, the oven. Same frozen fries, same amount, and one rule: the clock stops when everyone's food is ready.

This is a calculation, not a video of me cooking. An independent kitchen test gives me a starting point. For two portions, the air fryer took twenty-three minutes. The oven took thirty-three.

That sounds like an easy win for A.

But my fryer holds only two portions in this example. Six people mean three rounds. Twenty-three, then another twenty-three, then twenty-three more. Everyone gets fed after sixty-nine minutes.

For B, I assume the oven can take all six portions together and finish in forty minutes. That's a model assumption, not a lab result. Now B wins the clock by twenty-nine minutes.

Wait. Electricity tells a different story.

The real two-portion test used 0.287 kilowatt-hours in the fryer. If I assume each of my three rounds uses the same amount, that's 0.861. My modeled full oven load uses 0.92. So the slower fryer still uses slightly less electricity.

And if eight people arrive? Four fryer rounds use 1.148 kilowatt-hours. In my assumed oven scenario, one larger load uses 0.98. This time the oven wins both measures.

The real answer depends on basket size, oven loading, and what an electricity meter shows. I haven't tested these larger meals.

My choice for six is B if everyone's dinner must be ready together. For just two, I choose A. And before I can put the appliances away, six more people walk into my kitchen.
