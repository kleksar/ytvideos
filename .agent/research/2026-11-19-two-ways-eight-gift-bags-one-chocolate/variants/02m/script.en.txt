I have eight identical gift bags in a drawing. One is missing a chocolate. Which method finds it faster: weighing each bag, or weighing groups?

This is a model, not a real kitchen experiment. Seven bags weigh 120 grams each. The one without chocolate weighs 110. My digital scale gives an exact total, and I know exactly one bag is short.

I choose bag number six as the hidden problem.

Method A is simple. I put bag one on the scale. Normal. Then two, three, four, five. All normal. Number six reads 110 grams. Found it, after six readings and six bag placements.

For method B, I reset the same eight bags. I weigh numbers one through four together: 480 grams. They're fine. I weigh five and six: 230 instead of 240. Finally, I weigh five alone. It's normal. So six is missing the chocolate.

Just three readings. But I placed seven bags on the scale across those readings.

Now comes the surprise. If each reading takes eight seconds and moving each bag takes one, A costs 54 seconds. B costs 31. Grouping wins.

If readings take one second but moving each bag takes eight, A costs 54 and B costs 59. The winner flips. Neither number is a real measured time.

The bags never changed. The missing chocolate never moved. I changed only which action takes longer.

So the faster method isn't always the one with fewer readings. I reset all eight bags for another round, and the choice is mine again.
