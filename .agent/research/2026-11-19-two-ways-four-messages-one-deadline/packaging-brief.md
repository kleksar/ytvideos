## Recommended 02m: one central promise

**Working title A:** `Reply Now or Later? One Message Changes the Winner`  
**Title B:** `Four Messages. Two Methods. One Missed Deadline.`  
**Title C:** `I Ignored 4 Messages. The Clock Said I Won—Until...` (**risk:** “ignored” only means hypothetical; the question mark and model language in video must clarify no experiment was filmed).

Most accurate candidate: B, with appropriate model frame. Title must not imply measured 12m human improvement or a real office test.

**Thumbnail concept:** two adjacent illustrated phone-and-task clocks, cyan A with `80` and amber B with `68`; a *separate* small deadline icon near the first sender changes the reading, avoid a visual that falsely makes raw 68 appear numerically slower. The visible `80`/`68` must be understood as **hypothetical minutes**, not watch-time claims. Actual text-only graphics must follow channel image-origin policy; no programmatic type overlays here.

**First literal EN:** “Do I answer each message as it arrives, or wait until I finish an hour of work?”  
**First literal RU:** “Отвечать на каждое сообщение сразу или дождаться, пока я закончу час работы?”

**Visual first event / reveal:** I sit at a task bar with 60 work minutes remaining; four future alerts appear marked at 10/20/35/50. Screen split A left, B right. First alert appears on A while the B line continues. Design with real time *model* minutes, not documentary footage.

**Midstory mechanism:** A's fourth return pushes task finish to 80, B's answer queue ends 68. Before disclosing winner, viewers have the same starting inputs and can choose A/B. Avoid overprinting dozens of small numbers on a phone screen.

**Payoff promise:** B 68 vs A80 as combined finish, **but** first message completed t62 under B vs allowed deadline t25; B misses it. Last beat: different acceptable reply times produce different valid choices. No universal rule.
