Do I answer each message as it arrives, or wait until I finish an hour of work?

I want to get one task done, but four messages are coming. So I draw two timelines and make the rules the same for both. This is a model, not a stopwatch test on a real person.

The task needs sixty minutes of actual work. Messages arrive ten, twenty, thirty-five and fifty minutes after I start. Each answer takes two minutes. If I stop working to answer, I also need three more minutes to find my place again. That last number is an assumption, not a universal fact about our brains.

Method A: I answer immediately. At minute ten I stop, reply, find my place and continue. It happens again at twenty. And again at thirty-five. By the fourth reply, I've spent eight minutes writing messages and another twelve getting back into the task.

The work that should take an hour finally ends at minute eighty.

Method B: I leave the messages alone until the task is finished. I work for sixty minutes, then answer all four in eight more. Everything ends at minute sixty-eight.

I'd pick B. It finishes everything twelve minutes sooner. But I haven't checked what the people messaging me need.

Now I change just one condition. The first message needs an answer by minute twenty-five. A replies by minute twelve. B doesn't answer it until minute sixty-two. B is faster overall, but it fails the deadline.

So there isn't one winner for every inbox. If the replies can wait, batching wins this model. If one reply is urgent, waiting can be the wrong choice, even when the final clock looks better.
