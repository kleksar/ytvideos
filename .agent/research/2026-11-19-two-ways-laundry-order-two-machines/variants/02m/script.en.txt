The dryer helps decide whether I start with shirts or towels. Two different orders can finish twenty-five minutes apart with exactly the same laundry.

I'm comparing two rules on a made-up schedule, not claiming I measured a real washing machine. Method A starts with the shortest wash. Method B starts with the longest dry. Both loads use the same machines, and each has to finish washing before it can enter the dryer. My score is the minute when the last load is completely dry.

The shirts need twenty minutes to wash and forty-five to dry. The towels need fifty to wash and seventy to dry.

With A, I wash the shirts first. At minute twenty they enter the dryer, while the towels enter the washer. Shirts finish drying at sixty-five. Towels finish washing at seventy, then dry until minute one-forty.

With B, the towels go first. Their washing ends at minute fifty, and they occupy the dryer until minute one-twenty. I wash the shirts from fifty to seventy. Then they sit wet for another fifty minutes, waiting for the dryer. Everything finishes at minute one-sixty-five.

Method A wins by twenty-five minutes. Same loads, same total work. I only changed the order.

But what if those shirts need just ten minutes to dry instead of forty-five? A still finishes at one-forty. B finishes at one-thirty. Now B wins by ten minutes.

At exactly twenty minutes of shirt drying, both methods finish at one-forty. So the winner isn't decided by the shorter wash or the bigger basket alone. I need both cycle lengths, not just the shortest wash. So when I choose the first basket, which machine should I check?
