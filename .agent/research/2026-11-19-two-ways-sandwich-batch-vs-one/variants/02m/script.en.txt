The method that finished all four of my sandwiches first made me wait the longest for the first one.

I need four identical packed lunches. I could finish each sandwich before touching the next one, or line everything up and do the same step four times. Which would you choose: A, one at a time, or B, in batches?

I'm not filming a real kitchen test. I'm running a small model with fixed times. Each sandwich needs ten seconds for bread, fifteen for filling, and five to wrap. Changing stations takes six seconds. The batch method also needs twenty-five seconds to set out and label its trays. Same four sandwiches, one person, same ingredients.

I start with A. Bread, filling, wrapper. One sandwich is ready in forty-two seconds. Then I move back to the bread station and repeat. The second is ready at ninety seconds. The third at a hundred and thirty-eight. The last at a hundred and eighty-six.

Now B. I spend twenty-five seconds preparing trays. I lay out all four breads, add all four fillings, and wrap all four. I only change stations twice instead of eleven times. The entire batch is done at a hundred and fifty-seven seconds.

B wins the race to finish everything by twenty-nine seconds.

But look at the first lunch. Under B, I don't wrap it until a hundred and forty-two seconds. Under A, I handed it over at forty-two. The method that finishes the order faster makes the first person wait a hundred seconds longer.

With just one sandwich, batch setup is pure waste. And if I reduce the station-change penalty, the winner can flip again.

So I won't call B the universally faster way. If all four lunches must be packed together, B wins this model. If someone needs one right now, I deliberately pick the method that finishes last.
