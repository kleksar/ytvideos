## Main promise and payoff

Viewer promise: a shower that appears to save water can consume more than a filled bathtub if it runs long enough. In an explicitly hypothetical, fixed-input model, the same 2.5-gallon-per-minute shower uses **20 gallons at eight minutes** but **37.5 gallons at fifteen**; the bath is **30 gallons**. At twelve minutes the methods tie. Changing only showerhead flow to 2.0 gallons/minute makes the fifteen-minute wash tie as well. Real tubs/heads vary.

First visible action: the same person needs to wash, split into A cyan (running shower) and B amber (filled bath). Explicit “MODELED WATER USE” near the comparison so a viewer doesn't mistake it for physically measured footage. The numerical flow and fill values must be displayed only through authorized generated graphics at actual storyboard time, according to the current channel visual profile. No fake gauge photograph, “real test” claim, or unverified hero/reference bytes.

Core audience reason: it is a common, useful daily choice with a counterintuitive conditional result. The video should visibly *reverse* one winner by changing the declared running minutes, not reveal a winner selected for dramatic convenience.

Complete 02m payoff: conditional winner and 12-minute threshold **before** the final semantic loop. Its literal end EN “That's why, whenever I choose the shower just because I assume it saves water, I have to ask:” joins to first line “Can my shower use more water than a bath?” RU has the equivalent causal joining.
