## Variant-specific map, exact hook and payoff

**02m — STRONG_CANDIDATE / recommended editorial duration.** Opening: “Can my shower use more water than a bath?” Then an everyday choice, explicitly a model; A at 8m and 2.5 gpm uses 20 gallons against B's 30; at 15m A uses 37.5 and loses; the 12m break-even explains the reversal; at 2 gpm the same 15m ties; limitations before the ending. English final line “That's why, whenever I choose the shower just because I assume it saves water, I have to ask:” connects syntactically and causally to the opening question. RU final line similarly leads to its opening. Full payoff appears before the loop, including that a shower is not inherently more or less wasteful. Weakness: a simple threshold can feel like an explainer rather than an event, and 2m greatly exceeds channel's typical Short runtime.

**05m — STRONG_CANDIDATE, not recommended.** Opening questions introduce water use, time limit and showerhead; a running tap is compared with a fixed fill; same 8→15m reversal; 12m threshold; efficient head and a shallow 24-gallon fill shift the break-even to 15m and 9m36s respectively; final instructions for measuring actual water flow. Payoff is the changeable threshold and what a viewer can measure. Weakness: explaining every arithmetic step and second recalculation after the main reversal may lose a general viewer; Long recipe not configured.

**10m — REWORK.** Opening asks a broader question; additional measured versus assumed distinction, 24-gallon shallow fill, low-flow showerhead, on/off running time, possible warm-up waste, 100 repetitions and how to measure household inputs. These mechanisms are distinct and honest, but they make the story a lecture after the 12-minute threshold has already answered its main question. The added 100-repeat multiplication and warm-up sensitivity need a more distinctive viewer motive to justify ten minutes; none is fabricated. Long recipe not configured. Do not silently promote to STRONG_CANDIDATE on the strength of having 1325 words.
