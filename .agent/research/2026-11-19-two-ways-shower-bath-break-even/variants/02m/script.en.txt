Can my shower use more water than a bath?

I usually choose the shower when I want to save water. But I want to compare what actually comes out of the tap, not which option sounds greener.

I make a simple model, not a filmed measurement. Same bathroom, one person, one wash. A is my shower. B is a bath filled with thirty gallons. The only score is water drawn. Comfort and cleaning aren't part of this test.

First I pick A. My showerhead runs at two and a half gallons a minute, and I stay for eight minutes. That's twenty gallons. B needs thirty, so the shower wins by ten.

Easy. Except I often stay longer.

I reset the comparison and leave the exact same shower running for fifteen minutes. Now A uses thirty-seven and a half gallons. The bath is still thirty. B wins, even though I haven't changed the bath at all.

The switch happens at twelve minutes. Twelve times two and a half is thirty. At that point it's a tie; after that, my shower uses more.

Now I swap only the showerhead in the model for one that uses two gallons a minute. Fifteen minutes takes thirty gallons again. The same long shower is no longer worse than this bath; they're level.

So the winning method isn't fixed. The shower can win, lose, or tie, depending on the minutes and the flow. My own tub might hold less or more, and a real tap would need measuring.
