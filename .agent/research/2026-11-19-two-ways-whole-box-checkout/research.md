### Three matched finalists: stop / stay / payoff / strongest objection
- **Value-size basket:** first line: “I needed five hundred grams of berries for dessert. Two boxes were on the shelf. The larger one was cheaper per kilo. Yet buying it would cost me more.” Stay: first 500g A wins, at 750g B wins, at 1000g A wins again, then mixed purchase at €6 undercuts both. Payoff: calculate whole-box checkout, not only €/kg; depending on how much is needed, even the same fixed unit-price discount cannot identify the cheapest of these two pure methods. **Objection:** looks like basic arithmetic instead of a story; 10m risks lecture, real viewers may know bulk caveat. Shorter version should be preferred absent evidence the deeper proof keeps attention.
- **Bus vs walk:** opening option “A bus is coming. If I walk, will I reach the station before it?” Stay: different departures, delay at stop, street crossing, arrival-time tradeoff; payoff: compare total door-to-door, not vehicle speed. **Objection:** requires unverified transit/wait/signal parameters and may resemble stair-vs-elevator #175; public analog/baseline weaker, not chosen.
- **Roll vs fold:** opening “Both suitcases have the same clothes. Only the packing rule changes.” Stay: fill, count, compress, unpack; payoff: actual capacity and wrinkles or number of moves. **Objection:** no reproducible measured physical data or validated geometric packing model; visually easy to fake a winner. Not chosen.

Selected over the bus alternative because the numerical claim can be fully verified from declared inputs without invented physical observations; not because pricing is automatically more viral or easier to draw. Selected over fold/roll because it has a truthful decisive result under the available inputs.

## Exact model assumptions and reproducible numerical ledger

Identical hypothetical berries; A: €1.80 for 250g (7.20 €/kg), B: €4.20 for 750g (5.60 €/kg). Need `q` grams; **pure A** may buy only whole 250g boxes, **pure B** only whole 750g boxes. No discounted multi-buy, taxes or store-specific prices; identical availability. Compare **minimum checkout amount for at least q today**, ignoring credit for leftovers. B *does* have lower per-gram price, but total is discontinuous.

Formula `A(q) = 1.80 * ceil(q/250)`, `B(q) = 4.20 * ceil(q/750)`. Full ledger (recalculated from programmatic integer counts, not observed shopping):

| Need | A boxes | A (€) | B boxes | B (€) | Pure-rule winner | Leftover A / B |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|---|
| 500g | 2 | 3.60 | 1 | 4.20 | A | 0g / 250g |
| 750g | 3 | 5.40 | 1 | 4.20 | B | 0g / 0g |
| 1000g | 4 | 7.20 | 2 | 8.40 | A | 0g / 500g |
| 1250g | 5 | 9.00 | 2 | 8.40 | B | 0g / 250g |
| 1500g | 6 | 10.80 | 2 | 8.40 | B | 0g / 0g |
| 1750g | 7 | 12.60 | 3 | 12.60 | tie | 0g / 500g |
| 2000g | 8 | 14.40 | 3 | 12.60 | B | 0g / 250g |

Independent calculation of the mixed basket (not silently admitted as A or B): for q=1000g, one small + one large gives exactly 1000g and €6.00, below pure A €7.20 and pure B €8.40. The head-to-head is a test of two **heuristics**, not a search for global optimum.

Known failure modes: mixing sizes changes winner, leftover used later changes objective, spoilage unmeasured, discount/prices/store inventory unknown, household consumption not observed, budget makes method infeasible. No universal “always buy small” claim. Real prices/physical shopping **UNVERIFIED**.

## Story map and promise

Audience: English speaking shopper who makes regular small grocery decisions; RU is full editor translation. Central question: how can an actually cheaper €/kg box produce a higher bill, then win, then lose, without any change of prices? Thumbnail object: two distinctly sized berry boxes and two totals for the 500g need. First evidence: real arithmetic for 500g. Stakes: a verifiable budget decision, NOT invented peril or actual financial loss.

**02m**: cold 500g choice → rules/price per kg → A €3.60 vs B €4.20 → reset 750g B wins → reset 1000g A wins → honest limitations → checklist. Proposed verbal seam: final “It's the first line of my recipe:” → opening “I needed five hundred grams of berries for dessert.” This is an author-designed callback to the decisive requirement, not verified by an independent reader/MP4. Not mere identity of objects; the final line specifies the reason the opening quantity matters.

**05m**: explain declared measure and true unit discount → first reversal at 500g → more guests at 750g → another step at 1000g → further steps 1250/1500 → mixed basket beats pure A/B → leftovers distinction → narrower applicable rule. Added value: shows why there is NO permanent cutoff and why heuristics aren't global optimization.

**10m**: explicit numerical test plus budget feasibility, several jumps/tie, round-up rule, hidden third basket and conditional future value of leftovers. Added value: precisely understand step-function and alternative constraints. Counterpoint: may feel like long classroom lesson after answer; recommendation remains 02m.
