## Three comparably strong verified pitches

**A — Andrea Doria (winner).** Audience: English-first viewers interested in choices under pressure; no nautical vocabulary assumed. **Opening:** “Other ships sent lifeboats to an ocean liner that already had enough for everyone.” **First scene:** relief lifeboats approach a severely listing Italian liner, with an illustrated reconstruction clearly not called archive footage. **After hook:** radar disagreement → collision and list → port-side boats blocked → first Cape Ann two-boat shuttle → Stockholm receives survivors → Île de France turns back and launches thirteen → 1660 Andrea Doria survivors and the losses. **Payoff:** equipment capacity did not equal actual access; survival required multi-vessel transfers and a liner that altered its route. **Strong objection:** dramatic 2026/2022 feature-length competing work, no verified same-format Short baseline, and visible 2026 weak small-channel result. **Sources:** Noble Maritime museum sections “July 25, 1956” and “Bonsoir...” and US MARAD Gallant Ship Award entries below.

**B — General Slocum (nearest emotional alternative).** **Opening:** “A New York Sunday-school excursion began with more than a thousand people aboard. The safety gear would not save most of them.” **After hook:** fire on the boat, failed firefighting and life-saving equipment, improvised rescues around North Brother Island, accountability. **Payoff:** 1021 deaths and a documented safety failure. **Strong objection:** an intensely grim, largely preventable mass-fatality story; a ten-minute version would need sober firsthand reconstruction rather than sensationalizing children. **Sources:** Smithsonian “A Spectacle of Horror – The Burning of the General Slocum,” https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/a-spectacle-of-horror-the-burning-of-the-general-slocum-104712974/ (eyewitness and aftermath); History Editors last updated 2025-05-27 https://www.history.com/this-day-in-history/June-15/river-excursion-ends-in-tragedy (chronology and disputed figures). Its usable YouTube baseline and viewers' present interest remain UNVERIFIED.

**C — R101 airship (strongest visual/institutional alternative).** **Opening:** “The British government sent its new airship toward India. After one night, it was burning on the ground in France.” **After hook:** inadequate lift after test flights → added gas cell and length → final overseas departure in poor weather → crash/fire → deaths and aftermath. **Payoff:** 48 dead out of 54, a public symbol of the abandoned British airship project. **Strong objection:** the visual crash is well known, but a full technical causal finding cannot be attributed to the short UK Parliament pages alone. **Sources:** UK Parliament https://www.parliament.uk/about/living-heritage/building/palace/westminsterhall/contemporary-context/lyings-in-state/what-was-r101/r101-accomodation/ (48/54, route, final departure), https://www.parliament.uk/about/living-heritage/building/palace/westminsterhall/contemporary-context/lyings-in-state/what-was-r101/r101-return/ (public memorial). The 2025 documentary video below shows old evergreen interest, not recent growth.

**Comparison at equal pitch resolution:** Slocum has larger stakes and the most painful accountability, R101 has the largest single moving object and a cleaner engineering icon. Andrea Doria has the best *post-hook chain of changing choices* and a payoff about **how people were saved**, not only why a disaster occurred. ## Primary / institutional evidence read (2026-11-20)

1. **Noble Maritime Collection, museum exhibition “Andrea Doria: Rescue at Sea”** — https://noblemaritime.org/andrea-doria-home . Sections “Pride of Post-War Italy”, “July 25, 1956”, “Bonsoir...”: 1951 launch, 1953 service, 1706 aboard, radar on both bridges, opposite passing predictions, reinforced Swedish bow, 46 deaths aboard Andrea + 5 aboard Stockholm, list and 11 hours afloat, lifeboats for all but half unusable; 1660 Italian-ship survivors (1706–46); Cape Ann 129, transport Thomas 159, Stockholm 545, French ship 753; Captain Calamai persuaded to leave aboard US Navy Edward H. Allen; sank shortly after 10 AM on July 26; contemporary news images exist. **Limit:** museum's per-vessel tallies are not a disjoint arithmetic partition; boats also moved people between vessels. Do not sum numbers to “prove” 1660 or imply all were unique. The radar interpretation is an explanatory museum reconstruction, not a judicial determination of guilt.
2. **US Department of Transportation MARAD, Gallant Ship Award: SS CAPE ANN** — https://www.maritime.dot.gov/history/gallant-ship-award/ss-cape-ann . Original award citation + expanded history: first relief vessel, SOS response ~15 miles through fog, two boats, 129 survivors; within half an hour order to abandon; left-side boats unavailable, right-side lowered before boarding; 40 survivors in early trip, two rescued doctors assisted, cargo ship drifted and returned, later ferry to Île, nine seriously injured aboard Cape Ann. **Limit:** exact exchange time and personal feelings not inferred from official recognition.
3. **US DOT MARAD, Gallant Ship Award: USNS PRIVATE WILLIAM H. THOMAS** — https://www.maritime.dot.gov/history/gallant-ship-award/usns-pvt-william-h-thomas . Two motorboats, 159 brought aboard, US troop/dependent passengers, radio coordination until morning Coast Guard arrival. **Limit:** do not imply Thomas alone directed every boat.
4. **US DOT MARAD, Gallant Ship Award: SS ÎLE DE FRANCE** — https://www.maritime.dot.gov/history/gallant-ship-award/ss-%C3%AEle-de-france . Award describes a course change and return, **13 lifeboats**, 753 received (with help from other ships' boats). **Limit:** 753 is number carried aboard French liner, not its lifeboats' unique rescues.
5. **PBS Secrets of the Dead: “The Sinking of the Andrea Doria”, episode page, 2006-07-26** — https://www.pbs.org/video/secrets-of-the-dead-the-sinking-of-the-andrea-doria/ . Public series synopsis, runtime 54m21s, says fault debate continues. **Page only; episode not watched.**

**Fact and causality boundaries:** no invented on-deck dialogue, crew motives or missing radio transcripts. Do not claim the Swedish or Italian side alone officially “caused” collision; the museum describes differing predictions. Source tallies are per reception ship, overlapping small-boat transfers. The 46+5=51 figure is museum-based; other retrospective indexes give inconsistent aggregate mortality, so exact sum must be attributed and not treated as universal adjudication. 10m corrected from false “gone by daylight” to **“gone by late morning”**; record confirms shortly after 10 AM. Maritime image provenance, rights and official archive usage not verified; no borrowed screenshots/artwork/audio.

## Verified development chain shared across variants

1. Radar contact in fog → two conflicting expectations for passing → collision (museum).
2. Reinforced Swedish bow, Italian starboard hole → severe list; lifeboats inaccessible even though nominal enough places (museum + MARAD).
3. Distress request for more boats → nearby Cape Ann first, then Thomas and still-afloat Stockholm (MARAD + museum).
4. Limited shuttle capacity → Île de France turns back, launches 13 and receives 753 → evacuation completes overnight (MARAD award).
5. Ship sinks after 10 AM, 46 Italian-ship dead, 1660 Italian-ship survivors + 5 Stockholm dead; choice-and-access rather than a simple shortage of inventory is the payoff (museum).
