### Three compared proposals

**A: August 14, 2003 blackout — winner.** First full 05m line: “How did an electrical problem near Cleveland stop subway trains in New York and cut power in Toronto?” Concrete first image: stopped subway carriage in an unlit tunnel (explicit *historical reconstruction*, not archival footage). Why stop: ~50m people across two countries, normal commute fails. Why remain: Eastlake generator → two separately degraded monitoring systems → 15:05 and 15:32 tree contacts → power shifts → cascading disconnections → 413 subway trains evacuated and elevator rescues. Payoff: task force identified **four linked failure groups**, not single bad line/program; lessons shaped mandatory, enforceable reliability standards. Strong objection: Practical Engineering already has a sophisticated, widely watched 21m treatment; a five-minute documentary could be derivative or too compressed. Original narration deliberately focuses on ordinary emergency rescues and multiple failed safeguards, not another author's technical formulation.

**B: 1952 Great Smog — strongest alternative.** First line proposal: “Why did people keep dying after London's deadly fog cleared?” Fog + cold + coal heating under high-pressure system → badly reduced visibility, buses stop, fog enters houses → ~4k deaths during event and later analyses up to ~12k → slow governmental response and Clean Air Act 1956. Payoff: the worst effects were counted when the visible crisis had passed. Strength versus A: health and human stakes are easier to recognise without engineering literacy. Weakness: main mechanism remains one sustained toxic exposure, and ten-minute storytelling may turn into an abstract policy history. Sources: https://www.londonmuseum.org.uk/collections/london-stories/the-great-smog-of-1952/ and https://www.london.gov.uk/media-centre/mayors-press-release/Seventy-years-on-from-the-Great-Smog-Mayor-warns-that-air-pollution-is-still-a-matter-of-life-and-death-in-London . 2012-12-04 London Museum YouTube https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=k6Ww0ONdhg4 (~27,698 indexed lifetime views) and Fascinating Horror https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hmrjwAkMveE (~1.29m third-party snapshot). Distinct authors/formats, no direct current demand estimate.

**C: 1989 Quebec solar blackout.** First line: “Why did a storm that began on the Sun switch off Quebec in less than a minute?” On March 10 solar wind leaves Sun, March 12 voltage disturbances, March 13 02:44 fast protection trips, >9h electricity loss, grid modified. Payoff: even protection settings designed for local disturbances may react to unexpected solar weather, revised afterward. Weakness: explanation requires careful electrical physics; false visuals could turn protective grid tripping into science fiction; 10m may be filler. Official Hydro-Québec https://pannes.hydroquebec.com/learning/notions-de-base/tempete-mars-1989.html and Australian space-weather service https://www.sws.bom.gov.au/Educational/1/3/12 . YouTube demand and exact format baseline UNVERIFIED for C.

**Decision:** A has stronger escalating actions/obstacles and material consequences for a global audience than C; B is very competitive on human stakes, but lacks the cross-location causal mystery and concrete multi-stage recovery choice. A is a **content hypothesis** with general evergreen interest evidence, not a measured viral hit. A lost an early rival (Halifax) due to occupied PRs; duplicates not silently recycled.

## Primary / reliable factual evidence actually read

1. US Department of Energy, “August 2003 Blackout” (access 2026-11-20), https://www.energy.gov/oe/august-2003-blackout — eight US states + southern Ontario affected; rough 50m figure. This page uses “customers” inconsistently; Lawrence Berkeley / report summary uses “people,” so scripts say about 50 million people, not exact accounts.
2. DOE, “Timeline of Events: 2003,” https://www.energy.gov/lm/timeline-events-2003 — collapse around 4:10 EDT August 14; some locations without electricity up to four days, Ontario rolling blackouts over a week.
3. Canadian government, **Canada-US Task Force final report press release**, dated 2004-04-05, https://www.canada.ca/en/news/archive/2004/04/canada-task-force-presents-final-report-blackout-august-2003.html — four cause categories: inadequate system understanding; inadequate awareness; inadequate tree trimming; inadequate regional diagnostic support. Report identified seven violations of then-voluntary reliability rules and recommended mandatory/enforceable standards.
4. Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory official repository record, https://gridintegration.lbl.gov/publications/final-report-august-14-2003-blackout — ~50m people, 61,800 MW power, days of restoration, parts of Ontario rolling outages. Abstract used, not PDF downloaded or depicted.
5. Federal Highway Administration public HTML transportation study, https://ops-dr.fhwa.dot.gov/publications/fhwahop08015/lit5_9_3.htm — 413 NYC subway trains evacuated in about 3h; 19 PATH trains stranded (16 in tunnel); procedures used for evacuation.
6. NYC Mayor's office 2003 municipal press release, https://www.nyc.gov/html/om/html/2003b/pr234-03.html — verified Empire State Building separate stuck-elevator rescues 52nd/32nd floor and backup emergency dispatch staff member working 22h.
7. Associated Press, 2003-08-17, published by Deseret News, https://www.deseret.com/2003/8/17/19742194/2003-blackout-timeline/ — line trips 3:05 Harding–Chamberlin, 3:32 Hanna–Juniper, 3:41 Star–South Canton; generator Eastlake. Early reporting subject to later refinement; final causal verdict comes only from government final report.
8. Practical Engineering own published 2022 educational article (READ textual page, NOT watched video) https://practical.engineering/blog/2022/2/9/what-really-happened-during-the-2003-blackout — technical outline of MISO state estimator and FirstEnergy alarm failures, attribution for technical background only; no scene, dialogue, phrasing or beat order copied.
9. FirstEnergy filing with SEC https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/352049/000103129608000049/ex13_1.htm — company describes task-force Ohio-area findings; not used to override the independent report.

**Fact constraints:** No "one tree caused the whole blackout," no "hackers" or "one bug proves everyone blind"; FirstEnergy alarm failure and MISO state-estimator outage are distinct. The transmission system *could* tolerate an individual fault under some conditions; no claim every individual line was a sole trigger. No eyewitness dialogue, invented motives, private victim identity, or exact death count. NYC 413 trains and 19 PATH refer to transport agency report, not randomly guessed viewers. Only original narration text is in this PR; no third-party images, video bytes, voice clips, or usage rights.
