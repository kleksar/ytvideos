### Three genuinely competing finalist pitches

| Dimension | Eastland — selected | Operation Halyard — strongest alternative | Boston Molasses Flood |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opening exact intended line | “How could a ship kill 844 people in water barely twenty feet deep?” | “A transport plane came for men hiding behind enemy lines. The airstrip had to be built almost in plain sight.” *(pitch only; do not narrate without matching a verified source)* | “In Boston, a storage tank broke. What followed wasn't water.” |
| Why stop | 844 dead within sight of pier, an immediate, universally intelligible impossibility | Concealment and improvised evacuation under occupation, human rescue | A wave of molasses with real deaths, physical shock |
| Why stay after hook | shifting balance → water enters → ship rotates → radically different outcomes above/below decks → why lifeboats never launched | how airmen hid → collected on farmland → made runway → evacuation and political complications | ignored leaks/design → bursting tank → rescue difficulty → accountability |
| Full payoff | the danger was **entrapment inside a rolled hull**, not distance from land; safety gear existed but could not be deployed; no single cause proved by these sources | rescue worked through network and airlift, while exact figures and politics require attribution | multiple tank-design failures produced catastrophe and legal accountability |
| Strongest objection | old, traumatic, and already covered in 45–60m documentaries; 10m can be repetitive | political alliances and number of rescued are contested; 2m can distort | very familiar viral topic and impressive existing videos |
| Why loses/wins | best compact paradox + varied human perspectives with sources; new 5m angle prioritizes **two simultaneous escape realities**, rather than encyclopedic history | probably stronger upbeat human rescue; loses on constrained clarity and contemporary politicization, not on inherent quality | visually striking but highly familiar and payoff can be exhausted very early |

**Decision:** Eastland 05m is the preferred editorial experiment; no predicted success, CTR, retention or guaranteed 9/10 audience fit. Halyard is preserved as a viable future candidate and was *not* rejected by the owner here.

Historical society reconstruction, not unmediated footage; timestamps approximate and not necessarily independently measured.
2. Survivor testimony is reported recollection, not omniscient camera narration. Narrative does not borrow verbatim lines.
3. Smithsonian Magazine / Susan Q. Stranahan, [“The Eastland Disaster Killed More Passengers Than the Titanic and the Lusitania”](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/eastland-disaster-killed-more-passengers-titanic-and-lusitania-why-has-it-been-forgotten-180953146/) (2014-10-27; accessed 2026-11-20), paragraphs describing **E.W. Important: article states the added equipment was a stability concern; it does **not** justify presenting a single new lifeboat as the unique proximate cause. Some accounts differ in timing, crowd distribution and liability.
4. US National Archives, [Steamship Eastland Disaster](https://www.archives.gov/chicago/highlights/eastland): federal criminal case, ownership, crew, transfer to Grand Rapids; archived primary-case material exists, **not individually read**. 6. EDHS [Financial Relief](https://eastlanddisaster.org/history/financial-relief): **Western Electric allocated $100k** that day; Red Cross relief appeal; 540 family payments totaling $170k by Sept. 18, 1915; 69 families' trusts; principles of means assessment. No claim that these payments compensated loss or came from culpability award. In narration financial figures deliberately omitted to preserve listening clarity.
7. US Air Forces in Europe, [Operation Halyard 75th anniversary](https://www.usafe.af.mil/News/Article-Display/Article/1966338/us-serbians-commemorate-75th-anniversary-of-daring-wwii-rescue/) (2019), and Smithsonian Air & Space [Great Escape](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/air-space-magazine/the-great-escape-67380188/), alternative's source basis. 8. City of Boston, [centenary of Great Molasses Flood](https://content.boston.gov/news/100-years-ago-today-molasses-crashes-through-bostons-north-end), primary civic retrospective (2019); **21 deaths**, failing tank and city impact; finalist only.

**Conflict boundaries:** “never left dock” is misleading if understood as literally no movement: the stern moved away while still alongside the wharf. Scripts say it never reached **Lake Michigan** and lay beside the pier. Extra lifeboat weight was a concern among several factors, not declared sole cause. The 844 fatality total is from EDHS/Smithsonian; some online lists differ by one, therefore do not overclaim an independently reconstructed complete list. Repa and Goyette scenes rely on recorded recollections. No unverifiable private emotion, omniscient dialogue or novel testimony.

## Promise and causal evidence chain

**Promise:** A steamer beside the shore becomes a lethal trap in moments. The answer is not “deep water” but sudden roll, movement of people/debris, blocked escape and equipment too slow to use. The five-minute version gives the listener *different physical experiences on the same hull*, not a string of unrelated facts.

**Hook frame concept:** photographic 1915 Chicago pier with capsized steamship on one side, clear pier distance; tiny illustrated narrator (if needed) is an external observer, never a participant. Scene is a proposed reconstruction, **no actual source image or model-generated bytes**. Historical archival images need licensing/provenance review.

**Five-minute beats with source:**
1. annual Western Electric excursion → public, ordinary situation (Smithsonian/EDHS)
2. documented early list and ballast delay → correction attempts have limits (EDHS timeline)
3. furniture sliding/openings submerged → capsize irreversible (EDHS timeline)
4. upper exposed hull versus lower trapped cabins → why small distance did not save all (Smithsonian/EDHS)
5. shore rescue, Repa at hospital → response vs irreversible loss (Smithsonian/EDHS)
6. lifeboats not launched and safety-equipment paradox → no silver-bullet explanation (EDHS/Smithsonian)
7. toll/legal aftermath → complete human payoff (Archives/HISTORY).
