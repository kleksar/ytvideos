## Viewer promise and fit
Audience: broad English-language history viewers, no technical prerequisite. Genre: documented historical mystery with a human cost, **not** speculative apocalypse. Primary promise: the same bomber dropped two nuclear weapons, but opening a parachute triggered part of one bomb's arming sequence while the other did not follow it. Full payoff: two distinct mechanisms for avoiding nuclear detonation, not a fantasy mushroom cloud. Three crew died; partial underground components and periodic water testing are important reality checks.

First narrative questions, verbatim from proposed master: “How could two nuclear bombs fall from the same aircraft and behave so differently? Why did a parachute make one bomb more dangerous instead of simply slowing it down? And what did the recovery teams leave in the ground?”

First verifiable story action: right-wing damage to the B-52 during aerial refueling, two-hour offshore hold while burning fuel, the attempted landing and breakup. Main hook: two MK39 bombs dropped on countryside, only one parachute open. Resolution: the switch for the first stayed SAFE; the second did not complete the same sequence. The story does not imply nuclear explosion occurred.
