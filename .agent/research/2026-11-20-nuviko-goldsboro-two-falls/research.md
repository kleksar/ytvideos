## Audience, promise and screen test
Nuviko History, English-language factual entertainment, new viewer unfamiliar with B-52, Sandia or Goldsboro. One understandable premise: two hydrogen bombs fall from **one** crashing airplane, yet a parachute makes their internal arming paths different. Why stop? A familiar safety device behaves counterintuitively in the one situation where safety matters. Why stay? Wing failure → two airborne paths → different physical/electrical sequences → an overlooked buried legacy. Payoff: neither weapon caused a nuclear explosion; one progressed toward arming until a switch stayed SAFE, the other did not run the same sequence. Three crew died in the aircraft crash. No invented countdown, detonation, survivor quote or all-East-Coast certainty.

### Equal-strength applications
**A / Goldsboro.** Exact opening of 05m: “How could two nuclear bombs fall from the same aircraft and behave so differently?” After: damaged wing, two-hour offshore hold, fatal breakup, parachute deployment activating parts of sequence, other bomb breaking apart, different recovery. Payoff: the evidence does **not** describe two identical “one switch saved us” near-detonations. Serious objection: highly covered; stale counts and the strongest viral single-switch narrative may dominate; no demonstrated demand for *our* explanatory split-path angle. Worst moment: first nuclear detonation explanation could sound like technical lecture.

**B / Halyard.** Potential opening: “How could a rescue plane land in occupied Serbia without a proper runway?” After: villagers extend field, transports approach, crew and resistance keep it usable, hundreds evacuated. Payoff: shared rescue effort, plus the political and historical consequence of disputed credit. Strong objection: rescue total 417 from aircraft records versus 512 in OSS/public commemorations, need make distinctions rather than choose biggest number; political context risks swallowing rescue in a 5m narration. Stronger human story than Goldsboro, but weaker directly evidenced 2026 English viewer-interest signal under our limited tools. [SOF News, Aug 21, 2026](https://sof.news/history/operation-halyard/), [MiGFlug, Sep 3, 2026](https://migflug.com/afterburner/operation-halyard-1944-airmen-rescue-pranjani-serbia/).

**C / Norwegian rocket.** Potential opening: “A scientific rocket was detected in Russia like a missile.” After: launch notification, detection, assessment and stand-down. Payoff: it did not cause a nuclear exchange. Strong objection: the near-launch narrative is contested, and the public summary cannot substantiate a specific retaliatory decision. Do not turn alarm into a fabricated human countdown. Distinct from A/B, but insufficient verified middle for 10m. REJECT pending better records; not an owner-rejected subject.

**Decision:** A wins this specific batch on **directly documented two-track causal progression** + at least some public interest signals. B remains a legitimate future option, not “owner rejected.” C fails factual strength, not visibility.
