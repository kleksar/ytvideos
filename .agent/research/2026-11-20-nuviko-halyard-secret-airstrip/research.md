## Three competitive pitches, before full drafts

**A. Operation Halyard — selected.** For an English-language viewer unfamiliar with the Balkan campaign, promise: a shot-down American hides under a bed while a stranger walks through the house; the stranger is never positively identified. How do hundreds of isolated men get home? **Opening:** `An American airman was hiding under a bed in an occupied Serbian village.` **After hook:** village hosts → radio team → rock-filled hilltop field → night aircraft aborting a landing → 48 out → next-day daylight/escort dilemma → hundreds flown out. **Payoff:** runway is the visible end of the rescue, not its beginning: families sustained the men long enough for any runway to matter. **Strongest objection:** serious political complexity around royalist Chetnik forces, an established English-language 16-minute competitor, weak direct fresh view signals. No false claim that the airfield action alone saved everyone.

**B. Operation Frankton — strongest alternative.** **Opening:** two British commandos paddle folding kayaks through a guarded estuary at night. **Development:** one kayak damaged before deployment, tidal hazards eliminate others, only two crews reach Bordeaux, ships attacked, the men try to escape to Spain. **Payoff:** two survive out of ten, and damage to shipping is documented. **Main objection:** the survival outcome is grim, limited location/character context for an English general audience, precise damage claims differ in accounts, known Cockleshell Heroes treatment. The single-pair escape has excellent human stakes; **could beat Halyard in a suspense-focused 10-minute edition**. Sources: [Musée Mer Marine Bordeaux](https://www.mmmbordeaux.com/en/operation-frankton/) sections 'Mission' and 'Que se passe-t-il réellement?' and [Sky HISTORY](https://www.history.co.uk/articles/the-cockleshell-heroes-operation-frankton). Do not dismiss for being less familiar.

**C. London Great Smog — finalist.** **Opening:** people walk into a familiar kind of fog, and pollution becomes a deadly five-day event. **Development:** coal smoke, inversion, urban response, later clean-air policy; **payoff:** seemingly everyday urban pollution causes mass deaths and long-term regulatory change. **Main objection:** ARTE 53-minute documentary is already a substantive direct competitor; reported death toll varies from immediate to later estimates and requires careful causality, Long likely documentary exposition rather than personal decisions. Source: [ARTE, Als London im Smog verschwand](https://www.arte.tv/de/videos/120440-000-A/als-london-im-smog-verschwand/), 53min DE; Museum of London video page via [Londonist](https://londonist.com/london/videos/video-the-great-smog-of-1952). No pretend video viewing.

**Decision:** Halyard strongest combination of one individual POV, verifiable sequential reversals, personal human ending and multiple complete-length arcs. Frankton is a credible alternative and arguably has a sharper dangerous physical journey. Halyard's political sensitivity and weak fresh audience evidence remain material counters. Selection is an **experimental editorial hypothesis**, not verified demand.

## Halyard fact ledger (read actual content)

1. [Smithsonian Air & Space, Phil Scott, **The Great Escape**, Jan 2011](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/air-space-magazine/the-great-escape-67380188/), read body 2026-11-20. Veteran interviews of Clare Musgrove, Arthur Jibilian, others; retrospective oral history, *not original 1944 flight manifest*. Early paragraphs: Musgrove as B-24 ball turret gunner, engine failure, nine other crew captured, late parachute; the two women/two boys with sheep, shared food, the host hiding him under bed, stranger's **boots that looked German to Musgrove**. **Visitor's identity uncertain**. Later: OSS Musulin/Jibilian/Vujnovich, aid from villagers and Mihailović's movement; 9 days to clear meadow, 2,100 × 150 ft (approximately 640 × 46 m), forest/cliff, C-47; 9 Aug first abort/another landing and near-missed edge; **48 evacuated first night**, **12 C-47 next morning**, escorted by P-38/P-51; late August later flights; Nick Lalich replaces Musulin after transfer of two sick Chetnik patients; Ray Weber's **1955 letter**. Avoid copying veteran quotes or detailed narrative phrasing. This is primary witness *as mediated by a 2011 article*.

### Material limits and script implications

- Do **not** identify Musgrove's mysterious visitor as a confirmed German soldier; the eyewitness saw boots he interpreted as German.
- Chetnik role acknowledged, but the wider political/military assessment **not adjudicated** in this rescue narrative. Historical and modern memory disputed; no moral whitewash or generalised claim all Chetniks acted the same.
- Smithsonian witness source may exaggerate detail/sequence through memory; numerical audience/political/rights claims are not independently corroborated. Additional archival validation needed before formal script gate, especially exact 1944 rescue totals and later mission date.
- The large-scale American involvement and villagers' sheltering are supported by independent institutional accounts. Nothing narrated as a first-person historical quote without available evidence.

## Narrative causal map / promise

Main question: how can stranded Americans escape occupied territory when there is no usable runway? **Start:** one man hidden under bed (known firsthand account) → **wider obstacle:** hundreds hidden in farms (supply, patrols) → **attempt:** OSS radio team coordinates meadow → **physical obstacle:** boulders/cliff and short strip → **turn:** one night landing abandoned, then others land → **escape:** 48 + further transports → **human payoff:** houses made the eventual airlift possible. 10m adds conflicting totals and the later private letter as verified context, not mandatory 05m filler.
