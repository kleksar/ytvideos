An American airman was hiding under a bed in an occupied Serbian village. He could see the visitor's boots, but he didn't know whether the man had come to arrest him.

In 1944, Allied bombers flew from Italy to attack oil refineries in Romania. Some were shot down on the way home. One gunner, Clare Musgrove, parachuted away from the rest of his crew and landed in Serbia.

Two women and two boys tending sheep led him to a farmhouse. The family shared food with him. Later, when another visitor arrived, his host pushed him into a bedroom and told him to hide. Musgrove survived that encounter, but he still had no way out of German-occupied territory.

He wasn't alone. Serbian villagers and fighters loyal to Draža Mihailović were sheltering many stranded Allied airmen. An American intelligence team learned where they were and planned an airlift.

There was no airport waiting for the rescue planes. Near Pranjani, villagers and airmen cleared stones, filled holes and levelled a meadow. They needed a strip long enough for a C-47 transport to land, load men and take off again.

On the night of August ninth, the first aircraft came in. One overshot and had to try again. Other pilots landed. Forty-eight airmen flew out that night.

The next morning more transports arrived with fighter escorts. Further flights followed. By the end of the mission, hundreds of Allied personnel had escaped by air.

The runway made their departure possible. But the escape had started long before an aircraft touched that meadow, when a family chose to hide a stranger beneath its roof.
