## Three pitched finalists (same selection standard)

**A. Hotel New World (winner, Singapore 1986).** Audience: English-language general history and disaster-explanation viewers, no prior engineering knowledge required. Opening: "A woman was trapped beneath a collapsed six-storey hotel in Singapore. Firefighters reached her by digging through the wreckage instead of lifting the concrete above her." After hook: first-day tunnel and broken basin → continuing rescues under unstable rubble → last extraction after more than three days → subway workers both help rescue and later face an investigated but excluded construction-causation theory → investigators trace inadequately designed supports and ignored cracks. Payoff: 17 alive, 33 dead, rescue method and structural root cause both explained. Strongest objection: English indexed examples modest; National Geographic and 2019 CNA have already covered the event. Not demonstrably fresh/viral.

**B. R101 (UK/France 1930).** Opening: "A British airship set off for India with the minister responsible for the project aboard. It never got past France." After hook: lack of lift → extension/additional gas cell → single further test flight → stormy night departure → crash and fire. Payoff: 48 of 54 died; UK abandoned airship policy. UK Parliament's public historical entry: https://www.parliament.uk/about/living-heritage/building/palace/westminsterhall/contemporary-context/lyings-in-state/what-was-r101/r101-accomodation/ ; [Grove archive documentary](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Oa2KuUh7c_A), dated 2025-04-13, index ~38,373 views, full video not watched. **Nearest serious competitor:** its initial visual is stronger than A, but causal explanation of flight conditions and the precise failure chain needs more original inquiry material before a ten-minute narration would be reliable. UK Parliament gives a strong spine, not an exhaustive verdict on flight decisions.

**C. SS El Faro (US cargo ship, 2015).** Opening: "Thirty-three sailors sailed into a strengthening hurricane. Investigators later recovered the recordings of the last hours." After hook: weather warnings → navigational choices → failure of propulsion and abandon-ship delay → recorder recovered at depth → NTSB findings. Payoff: none survived; identifiable failures in storm avoidance and crew-muster timing. NTSB primary investigation https://www.ntsb.gov/investigations/pages/DCA16MM001.aspx ; NTSB talk/video https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3x0FGuqWCGo (2024-04-01, indexed 892 views; 28:17 El Faro segment, not viewed). Strongest objection: bleak outcome, complicated nautical exposition, and likely weaker clear 2-minute character resolution. **Not rejected as a subject**, only a losing finalist today.

**Why A over B (closest):** R101 has the better instant spectacle and potentially wider English fascination; A has an unusually concrete rescue choice, an identifiable survivor, a physical payoff at midnight, a second long-developing structural-cause mystery, and a satisfying contrast of subway workers helping after the subway construction was scrutinised and cleared. A supports genuinely distinct 2/5/10 without copying an existing story PR. This is an editor judgement, not a fact about future CTR. B remains eligible for future rounds.
