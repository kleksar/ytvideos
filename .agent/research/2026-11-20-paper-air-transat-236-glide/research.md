## Three seriously competing pitches

**Air Transat (selected):** For a broad audience, a large passenger jet with 306 people loses both engines, but the surprising middle is that an attempt to fix unequal tanks helped drain them. Opening line (05m): “What happens when a plane still has fuel on its instruments, but the fuel is leaving through a hole?” First image: paper diorama of A330 crossing black Atlantic, distinct near-empty tanks indicated in a diegetic cockpit display. Development: oil reading → imbalance alert → crossfeed → leak worsens → diversion → two shutdowns → approach too high → full turn → runway → investigation finding. Payoff: all 306 survive, two passengers seriously injured in evacuation, and both the maintenance mismatch and failure to apply leak procedure are disclosed. Strongest objection: aviation disasters have many established long documentaries, and Paper Detour is not a specialist aviation channel.

**Carpathia (strongest alternative for human drama):** Titanic is gone when the rescue ship reaches the lifeboats; the captain first prearranges doctors, meals, blankets, extra watch and infant slings. Opening: “The rescue ship arrived to find no Titanic—only boats scattered among icebergs.” Development: radio distress → turn → prepare crew → first boat difficult alongside → ice field → all aboard 08:30 → New York. Payoff: over 700 brought to safety; the rescue is not the same as saving Titanic itself. Objection: 1912 story is already saturated; direct Carpathia-specific videos with usable comparable audience data are scarce. Primary: US Senate 1912 Captain Rostron testimony https://www.titanicinquiry.org/USInq/AmInq01Rostron01.php and https://www.titanicinquiry.org/USInq/AmInq01Rostron02.php ; Senate rescue report https://www.titanicinquiry.org/USInq/USReport/AmInqRep08.php.

**Chicago Loop Flood:** Fish swimming in a basement while streets remain dry is a clear cold open. Development: freight tunnels under river, earlier photographed crack, delayed fix, April 1992 river water entering downtown buildings, valves and barriers, huge cleanup. Payoff: enormous infrastructure damage without casualties. Objection: much interest is local Chicago history, low observed performance of local PBS YouTube excerpt. Authoritative evidence: USGS https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70017945 and WTTW https://www.wttw.com/chicago-stories/downtown-disasters/a-comedy-of-errors-how-a-small-leak-became-the-great-loop-flood-of-1992 .

**Decision:** Air Transat offers three different causal turns (maintenance, cockpit misdiagnosis, gliding landing) and a complete emotional result. Compared with Carpathia it has a specific 2026 public audience signal; compared with Chicago it crosses geographic/language context more readily. Carpathia would be a defensible next candidate and is NOT rejected by owner. Weakness: its uniqueness versus established aviation reconstructions must be in the angle and pacing, not fictional dialogue.

## Factual evidence — important claims

**P1 primary:** Portuguese Aviation Accidents Prevention and Investigation Department (GPIAA), `Final Investigation Report 22/ACCID/2001`, issued **18 Oct 2004**, published by FAA:
https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/2022-11/AirTransat236_AccidentReport.pdf (103 pages). Read report abstract p.2; history pp.7–9; maintenance pp.45–50; analysis and engine-out operation pp.58–63; findings pp.84–85. Actual page screenshots checked p.2, p.7, p.84, p.85 (1-based printed pagination). These are evidence for **all versions**, not independent viewings of film.

- p.2 and pp.7–9: 293 passengers, 13 crew; 05:33 imbalance warning; 05:45 diversion Lajes, 06:13 right engine stops, 06:26 left stops about 65 nmi out, 06:45 landing on runway 33, aircraft damaged, rescue crews extinguish minor wheel fires, 16 passengers + 2 cabin staff hurt on evacuation. Two passenger injuries serious per 1.2 / Air Transat fact sheet.
- p.84 causes 1–7: mixed old hydraulic tube with new fuel tube at right engine replacement; contact/fracture and fuel leak. **No fictional technician dialogue or intent.**
- p.84 causes 8–12: crew did not recognize fuel leak; balancing by memory allowed left tank fuel to feed right-engine leak; not using leak procedure key factor in fuel exhaustion.
- p.85 risks 5–11: oil warning confusion; automatic 3.2t trim-tank forward transfer delayed advisory ~15min; no unambiguous leak alert, workload & training effects.
- p.85 risks 12–13: captain's skill averted catastrophe; first officer supported effective engines-out approach.
- pp.61–63: height on arrival, 360-degree turn and S-turns; limited braking; do not add any unverified cinematic 'last second' warning.

**P2 operator's own summary (not neutral investigation):** Air Transat, `Emergency landing of Air Transat Flight TS 236 on August 24, 2001`, PDF:
https://www.transat.com/getmedia/583479f2-7f91-425a-b0c0-601c0fc11409/Emergency-landing-of-Air-Transat-Flight-TS-236-on-August-24%2C-2001.pdf . Actually inspected page image and parsed text. Says leak begins 04:38, 05:45 diversion, two engines at 06:13/06:26, 19 minute 65nmi glide, 06:45 landing ~200 knots, 90-second evacuation, all survive. Notes some **initial press coverage was inaccurate**. Operator advocacy means use GPIAA for causal findings.

**S1 report-indexed specialist recap (secondary, checked page not video):** https://airlineincidents.com/incidents/air-transat-236-2001/ (2026-10-07), cites FAA investigation page by page. Details: oil warning, pump/crossfeed sequence, cabin visual check with lights dimmed, ram-air turbine, 360-degree circle and S-turns, approach/gear, 2 serious evac injuries. Its own reconstruction is not substituted for primary report.

**Limits / controls:** Exact oil readings and transfer details appear in 10m only with P1 p.85 backing. No invented speech, subjective fear, weather storm, engine fire, flight recorder transcript or fatalities. The flight was at night with **good** visual weather (P1 p.2); do not call it a blind landing. Operator's 47.9t at gate vs report's 46.9t at takeoff are distinct timestamps; script only says fuel more than required. 200 knots approximate. Modelled alternate fuel procedures are findings from report, NOT an experiment or actual action. Aircraft final stop damaged wheels, not claim every tire exploded. No footage or other copyrighted material downloaded or claimed licensed.

## Narrative contract and factual chain

05m opening asks about fuel escaping while displayed, correction making worse, powerless landing. Immersion: Toronto–Lisbon 306 aboard. Main hook: technicians had installed mismatched tubes, introducing unseen leak. Development: first warning is oil, then imbalance, crossfeed actually feeding leak, desperate diversion, engine failures, power from emergency systems, high-on-approach turn. Payoff: landing and full survival, followed by report's dual conclusion: fuel handling failed but final glide skill succeeded. 02m simplifies the chain without hiding reason; 10m adds separate evidence-backed mechanical and system layers.

**Counterargument to recommendation:** 05m still cannot beat specialist footage for aviation enthusiasts; Paper needs visual production quality, actual viewer response unknown. Could choose 02m if independent reviewer finds the preflight mechanical passage slows 05m.
