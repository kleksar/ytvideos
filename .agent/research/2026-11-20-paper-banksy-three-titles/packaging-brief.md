## One honest promise

An artist's painting partly cut itself up after a million-pound auction. Instead of undoing the sale, the buyer took it, a new artwork was authenticated, that same object later sold for £18.582m, and its title changed once more. The script explains what happened to the **same physical object**. Do not promise an explanation of Banksy's private motives, a secret inside-job, a working shredder demonstration or a repeatable way to multiply art prices.

## First narrated events and verified payoff

- Opening EN words: "Why would anyone pay a million pounds for a painting that had just been cut into strips?" Visual promise: an *illustrative* framed child/balloon beside an auction lot ticket.
- Early factual grounding: Banksy design, Sotheby's October 2018, £1,042,000 including fees. Show hammer, *then* cutting strips; don't put the price of the later sale in the earlier room.
- First significant change: "That last detail changes the story. The final artwork was only partly shredded." Reported rehearsal shows the machine *could* cut a test image fully, not what definitively caused the stoppage.
- First fulfilled promise: "the anonymous buyer agreed to go ahead with the purchase at the original price." No anonymous buyer portrait, reaction or dialogue invented.
- Full price payoff: "The final amount, including fees, was £18,582,000." It must be **2021**, separated from the 2018 sale, with 2021 estimate displayed before reveal.
- Last turn: "Girl Without Balloon" in South Korea 2023, reported 2024; clarification that titles and dates refer to **one physical object**.
- Long closing: "The auctioneer had said the sale was over. For this picture, that was when the next story started."
