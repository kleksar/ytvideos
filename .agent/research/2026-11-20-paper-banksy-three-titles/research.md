# Research — Paper Detour | The painting that kept changing

Observation date: 2026-11-20. Channel: `paper_detour` (English original, Russian editorial translations). This is **editorial research**, not a production run, measured viewer demand, video review or independent acceptance.

## Editorial decision

**Selected:** Banksy's single physical `Girl with Balloon` canvas: October 2018 partial shredding after a £1,042,000 sale; the original buyer completed the transaction; the authenticated re-titled object was exhibited, re-sold for £18,582,000 in October 2021, and later catalogued under a third title in 2023. A viewer who already knows the familiar shredding clip still has two consequential questions: *why did the buyer keep it, and why did its identity continue changing?*

The core answer is not "destroy your art to become rich". It is that the altered object was authenticated, its documented material state became the thing sold, and later catalogues recorded another title/date change. Prices are **two historical observations with buyer premiums**, not an estimated intrinsic value or a repeatable market return. No artist motive or bidder's thoughts are invented.

**Runner-up:** *Carpathia* answering the Titanic's distress calls and rescuing passengers. It has exceptional real human stakes, strong actions, and a complete rescue payoff. The Titanic brand is heavily saturated; unlike Banksy, the angle overlaps broadly with an open Titanic rescue study (#123). The Banky angle's 2023 third title is under-discussed and lends itself to a genuine third act. This is an editorial preference, not proof of larger viewer demand.

## Three finalist pitches — concrete functions before narration

### 1. Banksy: the million-pound painting that changed identity twice

- **Stop:** "A painting started shredding itself the moment its buyer won it for £1 million."
- **Stay:** strips stop halfway; artist later shows a full-shred rehearsal; auction terms become uncertain; Pest Control authenticates the transformed object; anonymous collector keeps the agreed purchase; 2021 sale at almost eighteen times the price; 2023 catalogue renames and redates it again.
- **Payoff:** three titles, two verified sales, and the same physical frame/canvas. The destruction attempt became the defining event in a documented artistic object, without proving the artist engineered the resale increase.
- **Strongest objection:** global 2018 viral story, well-known trick; could be no suspense after the first 20 seconds. The third naming event and the original buyer completing payment supply a verifiable second/third act, but comparable Paper 5m demand is UNVERIFIED.
- **Specific first visible action:** framed girl and balloon in one paper diorama; descending strips and an auction hammer only when narrated. Avoid recreating an allegedly authentic photo.
- **Core sources:** Sotheby's 2018 account, Pest Control certificate/2021 lot catalogue, Sotheby's 2021 result, and 2024 museum-title investigation.

### 2. Carpathia: the ship that turned around for Titanic

- **Stop:** A ship receives an SOS and turns toward a freezing sea where other passengers are waiting.
- **Stay:** Captain Arthur Rostron's preparations, night transit and lifeboat pickup; inquiry testimony reconstructs what could and could not be done.
- **Payoff:** real survivors transferred aboard *Carpathia*, not an invented last-second Titanic rescue.
- **Strongest objection:** mass of existing Titanic films; source/fact angle must be separate from open Nuviko #123 Titanic lifeboats work. No confirmed fresh comparable viewing signal.
- **Sources screened:** [U.S. Senate Titanic investigation](https://www.senate.gov/reference/reference_item/titanic.htm), [contemporary inquiry testimony (Rostron)](https://www.titanicinquiry.org/USInq/AmInq01Rostron04.php).
- **YouTube discovery:** [Soul Bridge `ytwrp-r1IUs`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ytwrp-r1IUs) — indexed 2023-12-19, 17,392 views; publication/crawl metadata require direct revalidation; this single video does not establish a baseline.

### 3. Thailand cave rescue in 2018

- **Stop:** Twelve boys and their coach are found inside a flooded cave, but finding them is not the same as getting them out.
- **Stay:** rising water, pumping, narrow submerged routes, planning, staged evacuation.
- **Payoff:** all thirteen were rescued; note the human costs accurately, not as invented dialogue.
- **Strongest objection:** substantial pre-existing documentaries and Hollywood attention; depth of source verification and safety/technical framing required for 10m; production complexity not a demand metric.
- **Sources screened:** [Euronews/Reuters contemporaneous report, 2018-07-12](https://www.euronews.com/2018/07/12/grit-daring-and-beating-terror-how-thailands-cave-boys-were-rescued), [Guardian visual explainer, 2018-07-03](https://www.theguardian.com/world/ng-interactive/2018/jul/03/thailand-cave-rescue-where-were-the-boys-found-and-how-can-they-be-rescued), [PBS NOVA, 2018](https://www.kpbs.org/news/arts-culture/2018/11/08/nova-thai-cave-rescue).
- **YouTube indexed example:** [CBS `1oLVfHosrwk`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1oLVfHosrwk) — indexed July 2018 ~164k views in earlier search; current count, format baseline and transcript **UNVERIFIED**.

**Conflict handled:** Sotheby's 2018 item is labelled with a 2006 date, 2021 catalogue says "Completed in 2018", and the reported 2023 South Korean exhibition associates the object with 2021. These are **successive documentary descriptions of one object**, not three newly painted canvases. The exact reason for changing to 2021 is unexplained. Do not present the 2024 article as reporting a new sale. The 2021 auction price was an artist record **then**, not assumed to be the latest record in 2026.

## Narrative/source mapping and deliberate omissions

1. **Artwork becomes object:** wall stencil → unique canvas in large frame (Sotheby's 2018 + Guardian 2021). Hook in any length must explain the painting and price early.
2. **Sale changes under buyer's feet:** hammer → mechanism → only partial cut (Sotheby's 2018 + ITV). Main escalation; don't pretend to know what anyone felt.
3. **New identity rather than repair:** Pest Control certifies; buyer completes agreed purchase (ITV 2018 + Art Newspaper 2024). This is the first full reversal of viewer expectation.
4. **Historical consequence:** museum display 2019–20 (Sotheby's lot provenance/exhibitions).
5. **Financial reversal:** estimate £4–6m → 9 bidders → £18.582m in 2021 (Sotheby's 2021 + Guinness). **Payoff** not probabilistic advice.
6. **Further surprise:** 2023 Korean exhibition reported 2024 new name `Girl Without Balloon` and date 2021; unexplained (Art Newspaper 2024). Distinguishes this from the viral original clip.
7. **Boundaries:** cannot verify why the shredder stopped, Banksy's precise motive, any private buyer thoughts, later financial ROI, the actual AV loop, or claimed 2026 audience demand. None should be invented in narration.
