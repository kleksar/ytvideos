## Audience and selection

The proposed viewer has no required familiarity with the 1912 inquiry: everyone knows *Titanic* sank, but not what another ship's crew actually did while rushing toward it. Central promise: the attempted rescue was not just a fast ship — the captain reorganized his passenger liner as a rescue vessel *before* he knew what awaited him. Payoff: Carpathia could not reach the ocean liner, but reached and received survivors from its boats. No claim that faster sailing would have saved all victims.

## Three genuinely competitive finalists

### A. Carpathia — selected, experimental demand hypothesis

**Proposed EN opening**: “The rescue ship found lifeboats where Titanic should have been.” Visual: a small real paper lifeboat illuminated beside the empty ocean and the much larger Carpathia; no photographic reenactment presented as archive.

**Watch-after-hook**: Cottam receives the radio call while preparing to go to bed → Rostron verifies it and turns → ice threat and double lookouts → doctors distributed, warm food, chairs/bags and ship boats prepared **before** arrival → Titanic sinks unseen → arrivals and multiple boats 4:10–8:30 → return to New York and wireless aftermath. Ten-minute alternative has independently sourced 1918 coda; do not claim that event is the reason for 1912 decisions.

**Payoff**: the preparations explain how the survivors could be received and cared for. An opportunity to reframe a hugely famous disaster from a less-repeated, human-scale viewpoint. **Strongest objection:** the Titanic universe is crowded and the same “hero captain” headline is not new. The winning *angle* must be work done before the arrival, not the fact that Titanic sank.

### B. Lake Peigneur — strongest alternative

**Opening candidate**: “The lake disappeared, and the miners inside the ground were still alive.” **Next steps**: oil drilling, salt mine roof collapse and sudden draining, evacuation of miners and rig workers, disputed initiating cause, subsequent settlements. **Payoff**: no injuries were reported despite catastrophic loss of equipment and lake drainage; do not say a rig definitely struck a particular shaft without respecting the legal/geological disputes. Contemporary UPI [1980-11-21](https://www.upi.com/Archives/1980/11/21/Drilling-rig-breaks-into-salt-mine-cavity-draining-lake/6665343630800/), [1980-11-22](https://www.upi.com/Archives/1980/11/22/Texaco-says-its-oil-rig-was-in-right-place/2760343717200/), and [1983-07-07 settlement](https://www.upi.com/Archives/1983/07/07/Settlement-reached-in-Jeff-Island-accident/4485426398400/). Real contest: higher immediate visual surprise than Carpathia; weaker confidence in a straightforward causal account, and many retellings deliver the whole payoff in their title.

### C. Aloha 243 — other credible finalist

**Opening candidate**: “A plane landed with part of its roof missing — but one crew member never came home.” **Next steps**: 24,000-foot structural separation, cockpit response and diversion to Maui, NTSB finding about hidden fatigue and defective maintenance. **Payoff**: the crew landed the damaged 737 but did not save everyone. [NTSB DCA88MA054](https://www.ntsb.gov/investigations/Pages/DCA88MA054.aspx). **Objection:** physical peril and robust primary source are strong, but the Paper queue already contains another airliner near-disaster (#297); topical overlap is a real product cost, not a ban on aviation.

**Selection:** Carpathia narrowly over Lake Peigneur because each subsequent preparation changes the viewer's understanding of how a rescue can work; verified human stakes and primary operational testimony support a substantial 5m middle. Lake Peigneur has a possibly stronger first image. This is **editorial judgment**, not a quantified probability or validated demand.

## Beat causality mapped to source

1. Radio nearly missed → [Cottam testimony] → why another vessel knows at all.
2. Captain verifies and turns → [Rostron testimony] → mission begins toward known ice.
3. Extra lookouts/full speed → [Rostron + Senate report] → own ship at risk; preparations needed.
4. Doctors, hot drinks, lifting gear, own boats → [Senate report] → receiving survivors is different from reaching coordinates.
5. Last radio answer disappears; Titanic sinks → [Cottam/Rostron + historic timeline] → rescue shifts from liner to lifeboats, without a fabricated decision moment.
6. Ice hazard, 4:00 arrival, first boat 4:10, last about 8:30 → [Rostron/report] → payoff from advance planning.
7. Turn back toward New York, name/wireless burden → [Rostron/Cottam] → survivors still need care and news.
8. Optional 10m: 1918 loss of Carpathia and Snowdrop rescue → [Hurd 1919] → genuine later echo, separated by six years.
