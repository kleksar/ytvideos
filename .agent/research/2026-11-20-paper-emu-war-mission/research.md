## Three equal-effort finalist applications

**A — Emus (selected).** Stop: a machine gun on a truck chasing a bird. Opening EN: “How do you stop thousands of birds from eating a wheat harvest?” Remain: the flock scatters, first attempt suspended, mobile gun fails, second campaign still cannot secure farmland. Payoff: *the goal was protected crops, not a high kill count*; barriers eventually work better than culling. Objection: meme saturation, animals suffer and easy “emus won” framing falsely turns culling into a war. Reply: retain farmers and the failed-fit of tool to task, not a cute animal army.

**B — Beer Flood (runner-up).** Stop: a huge brewery vat bursts into houses. Remain: unheeded hoop warning, wave strikes crowded St Giles dwellings, the wake becomes its own tragedy, legal inquiry. Payoff: families receive little institutional protection while brewery receives a tax concession. Objection: one physical failure is followed by mostly legal exposition; surviving evidence for 10m without needless victim detail is thin. Stronger emotional sting than A, weaker sustained action.

**C — Eiffel Tower (finalist).** Stop: a con man pretends the Eiffel Tower is being sold for scrap. Remain: forged authority, select buyers, paid victim, secret exit; risk of exposure. Payoff: the tower remains, the buyer loses money, institutional embarrassment suppresses disclosure. Objection: specifics of 'sold twice' and exact dialogue need stronger independent sources; 10m invites second unrelated con. Stronger character-led scam but less secure causal detail.

Choice is *editorial*, not observed demand. A has the strongest series of **observable changes to the problem** across 2/5/10 without a new unrelated story. B remains very competitive; no stacked weak rivals.

The actual original letter has NOT been opened; wording is attributed to the archives' summary, not to an inspected document.
3. **[State Library of Western Australia catalogue, item slwa_b6580504, “Western Australia declares war on emus”](https://purl.slwa.wa.gov.au/slwa_b6580504)**, Item Detail: archived newsreel description of soldiers shooting and men chasing one emu in a car. **Catalog description read; actual moving images NOT watched.** Copyright restrictions expressly apply. Source confirms an archival record, not permission to reuse the footage. Avoid describing exact frames beyond the catalog.
4. **[Britannica, “Emu War”](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Emu-War)**: indexed institutional overview; page could not be opened in this check, **not** treated as freshly read primary evidence. No claim exclusively based on it.
5. Historical rounds/kill ratio in the 10m is explicitly a disputed **report**, not independent mortality; the precise report from Meredith was not inspected. Numbers not needed in 2m/5m; important uncertainty remains close to the 10m figure.

**Interpretive claim:** a weapon optimized for concentrated fire solves a different problem than preventing further access to crops. Marked as narration inference, not a historical quotation. No fabricated soldier thoughts, orders to birds, or re-created dialogue. Context source supports that farmers continued to request help in later years.

## Promise, factual causality and weak spots

Promise: “Why did an Australian team with machine guns fail to keep birds out of wheat fields?” Confirm immediately through factual emu/weapon contrast. New information: scattered birds → ineffective fire → truck without stable aim → renewed attempt with disputed body count → farm protection still unsolved → fencing. This is a problem-solving chain, not an assertion that birds consciously fought.

**Strong objections:** overplayed meme; 10m slow context; institutional summaries are not opened primary documents; exact 1932 operational report and direct archive footage inaccessible; public search lacks comparable author baselines. None is masked as PASS.
