## Selected factual spine — primary and institutional sources

1. **1937 painting and museum error:** [Museum Boijmans Van Beuningen, *The Supper at Emmaus*](https://www.boijmans.nl/en/collection/artworks/101464). The museum explicitly says it bought the painting as a Vermeer in 1937 and identified van Meegeren after his confession. **Source conflict:** same catalog displays acquisition date 1939, credit "Loan ... 1939". Therefore scripts say `in 1937 ... museum acquired/celebrated` (descriptive account); they **do not** use 1939 as date of sale or invent legal transaction details. The 10m version discloses catalog/date ambiguity.
2. **Scale and other works:** [museum research publication *Van Meegeren's Vermeers*](https://shop.boijmans.nl/products/van-meegerens-vermeers), 2011, by Friso Lammertse and collaborators: museum and other collectors, Rijksmuseum, and Göring bought works falsely attributed to Old Masters; experts were convinced by missing period in Vermeer oeuvre. Not a source of precise profits.
3. **Method/false age:** [International Arts & Artists, *Intent to Deceive*: Van Meegeren](https://www.intenttodeceive.org/forger-profiles/han-van-meegeren/), curator/exhibition archive. Resin / phenol formaldehyde (Bakelite family), heating, artificial craquelure, alcohol and heated needle checks. Use as explanatory source; **not** directions to make sellable counterfeit works. Its timeline dates transfer to dealer Alois Miedl to 1942, then Göring. One passage says demonstration occurred "before ... a courtroom"; archival photo locates observed painting at an **Amsterdam easel/studio in 1945**, so scripts do **not** claim live in-court painting.
4. **Contemporaneous evidence of watched painting:** [Rijksmuseum collection RP-F-2005-10, ANEFO photo dated Oct 1945](https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/en/collection/object/Han-van-Meegeren-werkend-aan-Christus-temidden-van-de-schriftgeleerden-om-te-bewijzen-dat-hij-de-Emmausgangers-van-Johannes-Vermeer-had-vervalst-Amsterdam--0e917ba1a8b3398a8af9ee009ccca8df). Actual contemporary caption describes his claim to have forged Emmaus, his composition `Christ among the Doctors`, presence of witnesses, provided seventeenth-century canvas, brushes and pigments. A photograph and caption are evidence of the exercise and claim, not of **alone** establishing every work's authorship.
5. **Arrest, case and outcome:** [Smithsonian Magazine, *Showcasing Shams*](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/arts-culture/showcasing-shams-46600747/) on police accusation and witnessed work; [Smithsonian Magazine, *Everything in This Museum Is Fake*](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/travel/nothing-art-museum-real-180964918/) on Göring-linked sale, 1947 one-year fraud sentence, death before serving. Van Meegeren's defense did **not** erase culpability for misleading collectors. 1947 date/source sufficiently cross-confirmed by exhibition archive. Avoid death date (some public sources disagree).
6. **Current museum identity:** Boijmans present catalog labels author Han van Meegeren. Physical canvas remains in collection (the 5m/10m ending).

Claims not presented as fact: invented dialogue or revenge motive; Göring's imagined thoughts; personal emotions of witnesses; 100% certainty on exact historical scientific test sequence; an unverifiable "courtroom live proof"; a fabricated painting sold **directly** to Göring; any exact buying price, death date or unsupported "hero" verdict.

**The point of the story is not that fakes are admirable.** It is how expert authentication was made plausible and then reversed by the forger for self-preservation. Investigation concerns a work, not a verified act of resistance.
