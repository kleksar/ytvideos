Three genuinely competing free angles for final consideration: toys, beer, dancing. Source/interest coverage is strongest for toys and beer; dancing has disputed deaths and uncertain causal interpretation. The short list is not a ranking of evidence quality alone.

**Winner — Friendly Floatees.** Cold first event: a real bath duck washed up in Alaska and is now catalogued as oceanography. What changes: loss of twelve containers -> arrival after ten months -> beachcomber evidence tests drift model -> researchers distinguish verified finds from proposed distant routes -> actual museum object -> plastic-pollution consequence. Payoff: a toy with known origin and reliable landing is a data point, NOT a GPS tracker. Main objection: the 1992 ducks were covered by TED-Ed/BBC/SciShow, so angle novelty and demand remain uncertain; much longer than 5 minutes may become an explainer rather than a story. Audience: English-speaking general curiosity viewers, no existing background required. Why this over the beer disaster: a physical museum payoff and a second turn about evidence limits allow a consistent accessible Paper Detour treatment, while beer offers a more instantly shocking hook but requires serious treatment of fatalities and is recently saturated by viral Shorts. This is an editorial preference, not measured reach.

**Strongest alternative — London Beer Flood (1814).** Opening: a worker notices an iron hoop fall from a huge vat; later a wall of porter destroys nearby dwellings. Development: overlooked warning -> vat rupture -> flood -> eight deaths -> inquest/no fault. Payoff: industrial failure and who bore its cost, not a beer party. Objection: strongly covered, including a viral September 2026 short; vulnerable to morbid comedy. Critical distinction: 28,800 is the number of mixed toys (yellow ducks/green frogs/blue turtles/red beavers), not the duck count. The academic original abstract says about 29,000; Royal Meteorological Society states 28,800. BBC's teaching page says 7,000 ducks, not 28,800 ducks. In scripts use approximately 29,000 mixed toys. The 1994 paper establishes near-Sitka beach finds after about ten months and comparison against computer simulation; does not track every individual object's route. The model can be checked against arrivals, but no single duck transmitted GPS data. Arctic/Atlantic routes: only prospective / later accounts; do not present a verified identical-object chain of custody. Museum object 2005-393 and related objects are verified by museum collection records. Later pollution inference is the persistence of plastic, not a fabricated animal injury. ## Story causality
1. Cargo vessel storm and twelve overboard containers (original 1994 paper) -> many bath toys enter water.
2. Identifiable group and known point/date (original 1994 + science museum) -> potentially traceable test markers.
3. First confirmed Sitka sightings roughly ten months later (original 1994) -> independent real endpoints.
4. Prior shoe work + computational drift modeling (AGU 1994, UW 2009) -> scientists compare model to endpoints.
5. Wind and incomplete observation mean no exact individual path (Guardian 2003 interview; model limitations) -> spectacular trans-Arctic narrative cannot be treated as verified.
6. Surviving specimen in Science Museum (museum 2005-393) -> concrete proof of science value and physical payoff; persistence of plastic is the sober aftertaste.
