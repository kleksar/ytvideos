How could a publisher already be manufacturing Tetris cartridges and discover that he might not have the right to sell them? And how did that mistake help put the game on Nintendo's Game Boy?

In 1988, Henk Rogers found Tetris at a trade show in Las Vegas. He published games in Japan and arranged to release this one on Nintendo's home console. The agreement came through other businesses. His cartridges were being made, and, by his later account, family property helped finance the deal.

Nintendo then wanted Tetris for its new handheld, the Game Boy. Rogers travelled to Moscow in 1989 to get the necessary rights.

Programmer Alexey Pajitnov had invented Tetris in the Soviet Union in 1984. But a Soviet state agency called Elorg controlled its international commercial licenses.

Rogers showed officials his Japanese cartridge. According to his recollection, they told him they had not authorized a version for home consoles.

The problem was hidden in an earlier contract. Its rights covered computers. A computer with a keyboard was not necessarily the same thing as a cartridge-based game console. Businesses had been passing on rights that the original agreement might not have included.

Rogers suddenly faced a risk to the cartridges already being made. He also had competitors after the game. So he negotiated directly with Elorg instead of relying on the old chain. He obtained the handheld rights for Nintendo. Nintendo also secured separate home-console rights.

But Atari's Tengen label was already selling its own Tetris cartridge for Nintendo's console. It believed it had a license. Nintendo challenged that claim in court. In June 1989, a judge stopped further distribution of Tengen's version, finding the earlier computer rights did not cover that console.

Tetris, meanwhile, accompanied the Game Boy's launch and became one of its defining games. The inventor's puzzle hadn't changed. What changed was who had permission to place it on each machine.

Rogers had flown to Moscow in search of a new license. The shock was learning what might be wrong with the one he already had.
