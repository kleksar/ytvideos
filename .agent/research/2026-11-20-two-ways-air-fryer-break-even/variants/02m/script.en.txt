Would buying an air fryer save me money if I already own an oven?

I have one ordinary job: cook the same 320 grams of frozen chips. Option A is to buy a thirty-pound air fryer. Option B is to keep using my existing electric oven. My score is total money spent, including buying the machine.

I haven't tested these machines myself. I'm using published measurements for one air fryer and one oven. For this example, I assume my thirty-pound fryer performs like the one in that test. That's a model, not a promise about the cheapest model in a shop.

For one batch, the air fryer used 0.287 kilowatt-hours. The oven used 0.863. At my assumed electricity rate of twenty-six pence per unit, the air fryer saves almost fifteen pence every time.

So A must be cheaper, right? Not for dinner number one. I would spend thirty pounds just to get started. B costs me only the electricity, because I already have the oven.

After one hundred identical batches, the oven is still about fifteen pounds ahead. At two hundred batches, the two totals are almost equal. On batch two hundred and one, the air fryer finally pulls ahead.

Twice a week, that's nearly two years before the purchase pays back. A more expensive fryer takes far longer. And if I cook bigger portions, the published small-batch numbers may no longer apply.

So A wins on electricity for this measured portion. B wins on total cash at first. Only enough repeat dinners change that answer.
