## Equal-standard shortlist and strongest competing idea

**Selected: warmer-looking bowl beats colder freezer.** Exact opening: “My freezer is twenty degrees below zero. That sounds like the obvious place to put a warm can.” Visible first beat: warm illustrated can between a -20°C freezer and a bowl marked ice+water+salt; model label **published experiment, not our test**. Development: disclose identical starting can and finish line, reveal plain-ice victory, then salt+ice's second reversal; explain physical why and crucial boundaries. Payoff: 2.5min vs 60min, **24× in this particular test**. Strongest objection: the 2015–2024 salt-and-ice trick is established and several YouTube demos have middling/weak observed views; the 2025 source does not prove demand in late 2026 or an exact match for real soda. Also requires substantial salt and ice.

**Strongest alternative: detour for cheaper fuel.** Exact opening pitch: “I can save five cents per litre, but the cheaper pump is farther away.” Development: adding round-trip miles and vehicle consumption makes sticker savings disappear; price and quantity may change threshold. Payoff: a clearly declared break-even in a *hypothetical* model. Objection: many input assumptions, often an arithmetic lecture rather than escalating everyday action. `https://wealthvieu.com/personal-finance/budgeting/saving-money/is-driving-further-for-gas-worth-it/` (2026-04-01) examines the real question; `https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=h0r3BL3vkIg` (MathWithNick, 2024-05-19, indexed 416 views / 12 likes) is a caution, not demand evidence.

**Third finalist: folded versus rolled clothes.** Exact opening pitch: “I'm packing the same suitcase, and one method looks as if it creates extra space.” Development: compare identical sets of clothes, dimensions and accessible space; payoff: fit count and unpacking time. Primary comparable `https://www.cntraveler.com/video/watch/how-i-pack-flat-packing-vs-rolling` describes its own casual, not controlled, comparison. Strongest objection: cannot cite a portable numeric result without actually measuring identical clothes and bag; a claim of universal winner would be fiction.

**Decision:** cooling has the clearest documented causal reversal and most memorable measured payoff. The detour model is flexible and universal but its best scene is a spreadsheet; cooling can show a direct visual reversal. Neither source accuracy nor easy illustration measures audience interest. Selected as an **experimental editorial hypothesis**; comparable current demand **UNVERIFIED**. Source 2025 is an older evergreen comparison, not evidence of 30–90-day surge.

## Verified source base, how each claim is anchored

1. SINTEF original experiment by Kristian Solheim Thinn, published **2025-12-19**, last edited 2025-12-29: https://blog.sintef.com/energy/efficient-way-to-cool-a-drink/ . Water-filled 0.33L aluminium can and 1.5L bottle; 21°C start; centre thermocouple; for can target 5°C: salt ice bath **2.5min**, ordinary ice bath **10min**, freezer air **-20°C / 60min**, snow **30min**, fridge **10h**; ice/salt bath about -16°C with 5L water +1.25kg salt. Nine-degree target in salt bath roughly 2 minutes. For 1.5L bottle ordinary ice bath 20–25min, freezer 90min; **no stated salted-bath big-bottle measurement**. Wet paper wrap in his freezer test did not improve time. Never convert this into a claim of our own conducted experiment or a guaranteed commercial soda result.
2. Australian National University College of Science / Science Lab https://science.anu.edu.au/engagement/science-lab-experiments-home-school/chill-drinks-faster . Independent educational method for ice vs ice+salt, freezing-point depression and heat-transfer explanation. **Does not independently corroborate the precise SINTEF timings**, which belong to SINTEF.
3. SINTEF institutional release `https://www.sintef.no/en/latest-news/2023/how-to-cool-drinks-efficiently-during-the-holiday-season/` older context for ice versus freezer, not identical salted test. Do not falsely state it independently verifies 2025 salt figures.
4. Museum of Science public lesson `https://www.mos.org/video/how-cool-your-drinks-fast` educational mechanism, not same controlled result.

**Model semantics:** A freezer, B ice-water bath with/without salt as a declared adjustment. Fixed object: 330mL water-filled can, start 21°C, target centre 5°C, published fixed test environment; value measured = reported elapsed cooling minutes. Calculated factor 60/2.5 =24; 10/2.5=4; neither model predicts alternative setups. Ice/salt availability and handling costs *not measured*, no universal energy or cost winner. 10m includes a proposed new *reproducibility protocol*, not a fictional actual run.
