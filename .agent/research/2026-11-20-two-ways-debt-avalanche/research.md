## Three finalist promises (not just names)

**Debt payoff (winner, editorial experiment).** Opening: "I erased one debt in three months. The other method made me wait until month eleven..." First event: two strategies allocate the same $195 discretionary money differently while each pays $205 required minimums. Development: one account disappears sooner under A, high-interest balance declines sooner under B; reverse apparent winner on declared **interest cost** metric. Payoff: month 21 both, B $117.97 less interest. Primary objection: common personal-finance explainer, not novelty or an established Two Ways audience demand; debt is not universal to everyone, and very similar "eight months earlier first win" example exists in public community content. Cannot claim originality of mechanism or predicted views.

**Free shipping threshold (strongest alternate).** Opening concept: "I added an extra item to avoid shipping and spent more overall." Development: calculate two fully stated carts and then quantity-dependent reversal; payoff total price, not the label "free". Objection: closely resembles earlier price/discount PRs #272/#279; an indexed May 2026 niche video around the theme attracted few views, not evidence of fresh interest. No extended script written.

**Appointments with versus without buffers.** Opening concept: "I filled every appointment slot, but two late sessions made the last person wait." Development: same simulated customer durations with fully stated schedules, buffered plan uses fewer advertised slots and can finish waiting earlier; payoff compare wait versus capacity, not manufacture a universal winner. Objection: research and search results concern providers, not an everyday viewer; hard to write compelling 10-minute first-person story without padding. No extended script written.

Selection: Debt is strongest for immediate personal stakes, a concrete irreversible first win, and meaningful opposing cost and motivation measurements. Free shipping is simpler and potentially broader but would repeat existing channel concepts; appointments has better operational depth but weaker casual-audience relevance. **Actual audience demand remains UNVERIFIED**.

## Reproducible fictional model

Balances / APR / fixed minimums:
- account 1: $600 / 5% / $30;
- account 2: $2,400 / 29% / $75;
- account 3: $4,000 / 16% / $100.
Fixed monthly budget: $400 (minimums total $205; discretionary $195). Annual rates fixed; accrue all active balances **once at the start of each month** at APR/12, cent-round with decimal ROUND_HALF_UP. Pay min(minimum, balance) on each active account, then spend remaining budget on smallest active balance (snowball A) or highest APR active balance (avalanche B), and spill unused money to subsequent account **in same month**. Pay no more than remaining balance, never change the budget, no fees/new debt/missed payments.

Independent pure-Decimal loop re-run locally to generate results (calculation authorial check, **not a live financial experiment**):
| Extra to $205 minimums | Total budget | Snowball month / interest | Avalanche month / interest |
|---|---|---|---|
| $0 | $205 | 52 / $3,512.95 | 52 / $3,512.95 |
| $100 | $305 | 29 / $1,801.05 | 29 / $1,662.51 |
| $195 | $400 | 21 / $1,256.93 | 21 / $1,138.96 |
| $400 | $605 | 13 / $778.08 | 13 / $696.55 |

For $400 budget: A first debt closed month 3 (account 1), high-rate account month 12; B first debt closed month 11 (account 2), both finish month 21. At month 1 high-rate account A $2,383.00 vs B $2,188.00. At month 3 high-rate A $2,277.47 vs B $1,748.51. Equal APR stress test (all 16%, $400 budget): both charge $1,024.25 total interest. These figures are a closed fictional example, not observed financial behavior or offered product prices.

Calculation pseudocode:
```text
for each month:
  for each active account: balance += HALF_UP_CENTS(balance * APR / 12)
  budget = monthly_budget
  for each active account: pay min(balance, fixed_minimum); budget -= pay
  while budget > 0 and any balance > 0:
    target = min(balance) if A else max(APR)
    pay min(balance[target], budget); budget -= pay
  end when all balances = 0
```
