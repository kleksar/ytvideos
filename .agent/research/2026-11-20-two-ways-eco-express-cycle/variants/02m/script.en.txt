Does the one-hour dishwasher cycle really use less electricity than the three-hour one?

I have one load of everyday dishes and two buttons to choose from. A says Express, one hour. B says Eco, three hours and thirty-five minutes. Before choosing, I set one rule: the winner is the program that uses less electricity for one cycle.

This is a comparison of published figures, not a test I filmed in my kitchen. Both numbers come from one Bosch dishwasher model.

Express uses 1.3 kilowatt-hours. Eco uses just 0.645. That's almost exactly half.

I expected the motor running longer to mean a bigger bill. But the clock measures how long the program lasts, not how much electricity it draws. Heating water takes energy. Giving dishes longer to soak and wash at a lower temperature can reduce that demand.

There's a second result. On this model, Express is listed at ten and a half litres of water. Eco is nine. So Eco saves water too, but makes me wait two hours and thirty-five minutes longer.

Does that settle which program washes better? No. These figures measure consumption, not how clean my particular dishes will be.

In a separate test of three other dishwashers, the consumer group Which? found the quick programs scored worse on average for cleaning than the main programs. That doesn't measure this Bosch or guarantee a result in my kitchen.

I choose Eco when I can wait. When I need clean plates in an hour, Express has a real advantage: time.

And the longest program isn't automatically the cheapest. This same machine lists a four-hour Silence cycle that uses more electricity than Eco.
