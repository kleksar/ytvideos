# Shared research — Two Ways Lab / gas-station detour

**Observed:** 2026-11-20. **Scope:** Two Ways Lab everyday one-state A/B experiment, EN first-person, RU equivalent, two honest methods; exactly one compared metric. **Evidence boundary:** public web pages and indexed YouTube metadata only, not played videos or watched/transcribed comments. No vidIQ paid searches: balance observed **0 credits**, renews 2026-12-06; free-source research was still performed. No personal/account-specific queries were sent to public search.

## Coverage and exclusions before selecting

Twelve distinct real-world task directions reviewed (not twelve rewritten titles): (1) close vs far fuel stop; (2) bulk perishable groceries vs individually bought portions; (3) add an unwanted item to unlock free delivery vs pay delivery; (4) teach a helper then divide repetitive packing vs work alone; (5) heat on constantly vs timed setback; (6) shortest task vs earliest deadline; (7) immediate messaging vs batch responses; (8) air fryer purchase versus oven energy; (9) frozen drink by ice/salt vs freezer; (10) wash dishes by basins vs dishwasher; (11) direct bus versus a transfer; (12) near versus far parking.

Actual GitHub search and recent story PR catalog rejected occupied angles: #195 heating, #217 deadlines, #269 messaging, #299 air fryer, #296 cooling, #303 dishwasher, #285 transit and #127 parking. They are NOT new topics. Free delivery is a strong practical financial question but one arithmetic difference and a checkout click exhaust its story quickly. Helper training has a real time-versus-upfront-investment turn but its simulated learning assumptions would dominate the supposed observed result. **Strongest alternative: bulk perishable groceries** — more broadly relatable and with some visible category-scale YouTube interest; a meaningful quantity threshold and expiry can change the result, but the central payoff arrives before the narrative has much action. Earlier alternative candidates are not owner-rejected by implication.

## Exact common model / reproducible calculation

This is an **original hypothetical model**, not real station prices, real road measurement or a filmed test. One home start and finish, one equivalent car, equal fuel quality, same fuel tank gain at home, no queues, tolls, rewards, price changes or route-specific consumption. Enough starting fuel and tank headroom are conditional feasibility constraints. Prices USD per litre; distances km, common fuel burn 8 L/100km.

| Input | A: close | B: far |
|---|---:|---:|
| Pump price | $1.80/L | $1.65/L |
| One-way home→pump | 2 km | 12 km |
| Round trip | 4 km | 24 km |
| Fuel consumed by round trip | 0.32 L | 1.92 L |
| Litres purchased for net **40L** at home | 40.32 | 41.92 |
| Exact bill for net 40L | $72.576 | $69.168 |
| Rounded bill for net 40L | **$72.58** | **$69.17** |
| Litres purchased for net **10L** at home | 10.32 | 11.92 |
| Exact bill for net 10L | $18.576 | $19.668 |
| Rounded bill for net 10L | **$18.58** | **$19.67** |

For goal `L` net litres at home: `cost_A=(L+0.32)*1.80`, `cost_B=(L+1.92)*1.65`. Exact savings A−B = `0.15*L−2.592` dollars. At 40L savings **$3.408 → $3.41** for B; at 10L **−$1.092 → −$1.09** (A wins). Exact crossover `L=2.592/0.15=17.28L`. Do not derive saving by subtracting costs rounded to cents before calculating: the results here happen to agree but the exact amounts are authoritative.

10m conditional expansions, still not new experiments: 6 L/100km crossover 12.96L; 12 L/100km crossover 25.92L. If B is $1.75, crossover 55.68L and at 40L B costs $73.36 versus A $72.58. If B is $1.50, at 40L B costs $62.88 versus A $72.58; at 10L B $17.88 versus A $18.58. Hypothetical twenty extra minutes for B gives $3.408/(1/3 hour)≈$10.22 per additional hour for the 40L case. This is a way to express marginal time tradeoff, **not a measured wage/time value**.

**Causal boundary:** buying the same pump volume at both stations would NOT give the same final fuel volume because the round trips differ. All scripts instead equalize fuel volume at home. Actual per-route consumption, queue/time, vehicle wear, tank capacity and a preexisting trip can change the choice; none is silently measured.

## Creative promise, honest uncertainty

- Viewer stops: two different price signs and the surprising possibility that the cheaper pump is not cheaper overall.
- Viewer stays: B wins for 40L then unexpectedly loses for 10L with every other condition fixed, followed by the exact 17.28L threshold.
- Viewer gets: a fair at-home, equal-tank formula and decision boundary; clear distinction between money at pump, marginal travel distance and personal time.
- First visible action: my route-choice screen from a common home point, two labelled A/B pumps; no footage of a real drive.
- Counter-claim: the setup uses assumed prices and constant fuel consumption; if real route or fuel prices differ, the winner may differ.
