### Three competing full pitches / strongest objections

**Winner — thermostat.** Stop: “Do I leave the heating at twenty degrees all day, or turn it down … and warm the house up again?” Watch: room cools gradually, the final high-power warm-up seems to erase savings, then a full-window energy comparison disproves that for the assumed building; in longer cuts a real test house and conditional heat-pump electric-backup reversal. Payoff: reduce empty-house heat loss without sacrificing return-time comfort, but equipment behaviour matters. Strong objection: UK heating advice and fresh 2026 coverage already answer the generic always-on question, and genuine public evidence for demand for *this A/B model* or the English illustrated Shorts format is absent; 10m risks becoming an efficiency lecture.

**Alternate 2 — replace battery or phone.** Stop: $80 repair versus hundreds for a new phone; new stakes when software support ends. Watch: age, official battery service, software security, resale. Payoff: lower total cost at chosen horizon. Objection: without one exact model and vendor evidence, everything relies on invented numbers and a trivial choice.

**Alternate 3 — home printing or shop.** Stop: buy a printer for “cheap” pages or pay a counter for one emergency print. Watch: ink cartridge and page-yield threshold, quality needs, pickup/shipping. Payoff: break-even volume under named assumptions. Objection: price and availability fluctuate, and additional 10m may be just formulas; conflicting promotional vendor price lists.

## Reproducible hypothetical A/B model (NOT real measurements)

**Single input state and declared metric before any result:** empty hypothetical building starts at 20°C 08:00; outside fixed 5°C; end 16:00 must be 20°C in both A/B. Metric 1: **total useful heat delivered (kWh)** from 08:00 to16:00, not billed kWh of gas/electricity. House heat loss coefficient `U = 0.16 kW/K`, lumped heat capacity `C = 1.6 kWh/K`, heater max useful heat `Pmax = 8 kW`, controller bang-bang `P = 8 kW if T < setpoint else 0`; no internal gains, sunshine, windows or varying weather. A setpoint20 throughout; B setpoint16 until14:30 (6.5h elapsed), then20. Euler time step `dt=1/600 hour` (6 sec) with:
 `P_loss = U * (T - 5)`;
 `E_delivered += P * dt`;
 `T += (P - P_loss) * dt / C`.
 Initialize `T=20` and `E=0` independently for each A/B. At step `i=0..4799`, use `t=i*dt`, choose setpoint, then update. Numerical readout from actual local calculation:

| Time | A temperature | B temperature |
|---|---:|---:|
| 09:00 (1h) | ~20.00°C | 18.5724°C |
| 10:00 (2h) | ~20.00°C | 17.2808°C |
| 11:00 (3h) | ~20.00°C | 16.1120°C |
| 14:30 (6.5h) | ~20.00°C | 16.0023°C |
| 16:00 (8h) | 19.9986°C | 19.9982°C |

End-round `E_A=19.20 kWh`, `E_B=15.64 kWh` (rounded), `E_A-E_B = 3.56 kWh` useful heat. Since B is cooler, cumulative heat leakage is lower; final warm-up is included. On an assumed constant 90% boiler efficiency, useful heat divides by 0.9 to get illustrative fuel input `21.33 vs17.38 kWh`; at an **invented $0.10 per kWh fuel** and **100 identical days**, saving ~$0.40/day or ~$40/100 identical days (10m only; NO bill promise).

**Separate variant, same underlying useful-heat totals but hypothetical heating technology change:** model uniform heat pump COP3: `A=19.20/3=6.40 kWh electricity`, `B=15.64/3=5.21`. If **only B** calls resistance backup for 2kWh useful heat during catchup, `B_electricity=2/1+(15.64-2)/3=6.55 kWh > A=6.40`. This is **conditional scenario and new equipment assumption**, not an empirical A/B observation. Does NOT prove every heat pump calls for backup, or guarantee a new winner in real housing. Heating/safety, comfort, moisture and tariff-by-time all limit transfer.

**Reproduction code (stdlib Python 3, no packages):**
```python
U, C, POWER, OUTDOOR = 0.16, 1.6, 8.0, 5.0
dt = 1 / 600  # hour, 6s
for case in "AB":
    temp, delivered = 20.0, 0.0
    for step in range(8 * 600):
        hour = step * dt
        target = 20 if case == "A" or hour >= 6.5 else 16
        heating = POWER if temp < target else 0.0
        delivered += heating * dt
        temp += (heating - U * (temp - OUTDOOR)) * dt / C
    print(case, round(delivered, 2), round(temp, 4))
# A 19.20 19.9986
# B 15.64 19.9982
```

**Why this is honest:** figures come from defined equations and actual arithmetic, not claimed house, meter or filmed experiment. Holding temperature at precisely 20°C at endpoint is numerical tolerance 0.002°C, not an exact physical guarantee. No inference about emission or bills without fuel prices, efficiency, timing and equipment.

## Closing selection, coverage, risks

Short hook: "Do I leave the heating at twenty degrees all day, or turn it down …?" 2min development: lower thermostat → cooling → costly-looking warm-up → full 8h heat result → heat-pump condition. Payoff: conditional B in the modeled conventional system, but real heat pumps need their own controls. 5min development: independent Salford trial that answers a different question, and mathematical conditional electricity reversal. 10min: full model steps, test comparison, boiler fuel conversion and equipment limitations; facts are adequate but pacing less convincing. Most serious objection: topic is widely covered already and published **2026-11-19** YouTube-adjacent explainers provide same general lesson; originality of precise illustrative A/B may not be enough to hold attention. No fabricated effect sizes or independent viewer judgement.
