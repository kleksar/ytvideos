## Three full competing pitches — evidence before writing

### A. 442nd / 100th, chosen

- **Audience:** English-language general history audience who can follow a small human rescue story without knowing U.S. unit structure.
- **Stop:** `The soldiers sent to rescue the trapped Americans had barely been allowed to rest.` Immediate contradiction: another exhausting mission before recovery.
- **Stay after hook:** the 141st outruns support and is surrounded → earlier rescues fail → supplies miss the pocket → the Japanese American 100th/442nd marches in complete darkness → tank counterattack and ridge traps → last mined roadblock → two separate units contact the pocket.
- **Payoff:** Company I patrol under T/Sgt Takashi Senzaki and a Company B platoon reach the pocket on October 30; **211 survive**. The cost and subsequent orders change how the rescue is understood. The discrimination context is verified and does not substitute for the rescue.
- **Strongest objection:** extensively covered military-history event, specialist unit names, disagreement across published casualty totals, and limited recent comparable view evidence. If narrated as mere awards/statistics, it becomes a military list. Specific source-backed actions are the remedy; no invented dialogue or motives.
- **Primary core:** 1946 combat-unit history at https://442sd.org/learn/history/campaigns/the-vosges-battles-as-experienced-by-the-442nd-regimental-combat-team/ (date sections October 24–31, especially 27, 28, 29 and 30); 100th Battalion historical organization https://www.100thbattalion.org/history/battalion-history/european-campaigns/france-2/; U.S. National Park Service https://www.nps.gov/perl/learn/historyculture/japanese-american-units-of-world-war-ii.htm .

### B. SS General Slocum, strongest challenger

- **Audience value:** a familiar question — why didn't life jackets and lifeboats save a ferry full of families?
- **Stop:** a packed New York excursion boat catches fire, but protection equipment fails when needed.
- **Stay:** fire spreads → poor response and failure to beach in time → life preservers ineffective / boats inaccessible → mass casualties → investigation and conviction.
- **Payoff:** safety measures and ignored risks mattered more than the ship simply being old.
- **Strong objection:** story has exceptional stakes but a grim and visually difficult ending; currently serviced by an almost identical long-form competitor; likely less suited to a calm 2D-host historical channel than a rescue with a survivable central payoff.
- **Primary:** Library of Congress research guide (accessed 2026-11-21), June 15 1904, >1,000 dead, the actual causes and indictment timeline: https://guides.loc.gov/chronicling-america-slocum-steamboat-tragedy .

### C. 1979 Fastnet Race

- **Audience value:** a normal competition becomes a coordinated rescue operation; survival decisions have clear consequences.
- **Stop:** sailors start a race; a severe storm overturns their expectations.
- **Stay:** weather worsens → yachts are abandoned → ships, lifeboats, helicopters search under risk → separate rescues unfold → safer sailing practice.
- **Payoff:** human rescue and changes to safety culture rather than a generalized storm headline.
- **Strong objection:** many different yacht crews without one clear protagonist, complicated geography and split casualty figures (15 participating sailors versus 21 deaths including spectators), and fragmented public comparable videos.
- **Primary contemporary evidence:** Royal National Lifeboat Institution Autumn 1979 journal: 60 people saved by RNLI boats and 74 rescued by helicopters; these are **different service counts**, not necessarily unique summed persons: https://lifeboatmagazinearchive.rnli.org/volume/46/470/notes-of-the-quarter-by-patrick-howarth . Reporting on participants and 21 total: https://www.itv.com/news/westcountry/2019-08-12/fastnet-40-years-on .

**Decision:** 442nd is selected over the strong General Slocum challenger because the rescue's exact breakthrough, widening obstacles, human cost, and verified unequal treatment each changes the meaning of what precedes it. General Slocum has greater immediate tragedy and easily recognized location, so choice is an editorial hypothesis, **not measured demand**. Do not claim it would outperform.

| 28 October hollow ambush, Yamashiro roadblock | A “cleared” stretch can still cost lives | 05m/10m |
| 29 October mined ridge and simultaneous flank protection | Literal final barriers to reaching pocket | All; detailed 05m/10m |
| 30 October two contacts (I patrol + B platoon), 211 survivors | Full rescue payoff, not delayed to replay | All |

**Editorial decision on length:** 05m has the strongest balance of successive source-backed decisions, named people and a real resolved ending. 02m is complete but its last→first spoken seam is only a thematic return and needs another pass before Short review; do not call Short ready. 10m has extra verifiable route details and aftermath, but needs an independent test of whether extended battle detail sustains a general-audience viewer. Both 05m and 10m are editorial candidates, neither is authorized production.
