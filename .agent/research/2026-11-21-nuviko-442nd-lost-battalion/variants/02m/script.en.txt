On October 26, 1944, American soldiers in France were called back into battle just after receiving hot food, dry clothes, and time to sleep.

In the Vosges, roughly 275 men of the 141st Infantry had advanced along a wooded ridge. German troops cut in behind them. Other rescue attempts failed, and much of the food and ammunition dropped from planes missed their position.

The Army turned to the 100th Battalion and the 442nd Regimental Combat Team. Most of their infantrymen were Japanese Americans. Back home, the government was still confining many Japanese American families in camps.

Before dawn on October 27, the rescuers moved through darkness, holding the packs of the men ahead so they would not lose the column. That afternoon German troops counterattacked with a tank. Private Matsuichi Yogi disabled it with a bazooka. He was killed two days later.

The route narrowed into mined ridges covered by machine guns. Breaking one position only exposed another. Even after the advance on October 29, a final roadblock separated the rescuers from the surrounded men.

On October 30, artillery hit that roadblock. A Company I patrol led by Takashi Senzaki reached the pocket. Minutes later, men from the 100th Battalion arrived from another direction.

Two hundred and eleven survived to be rescued. The 100th Battalion's historical organization estimates fifty-four rescuers killed and about three hundred wounded during the five-day operation. The wounded were not three hundred more deaths.

The trapped men could come out. But on October 31, the rescuers received new orders to hold or attack more ground. It was not the first time that week they had been sent back before they could recover.
