### Equal-weight finalist pitches
**Courrières:** First EN action: “Thirteen miners came out of a French coal mine twenty days after an explosion that had killed more than a thousand people.” After that reveal, Danglot's wrong rockfall assumption → poisoned air → abandoned shafts → pipes and hand-clearing rubble → rescue difficulty → search suspension and strikes → father/son among thirteen → fourteenth man. Payoff: not “everyone saved” but the painful mismatch between what aboveground people concluded and what survivors were still doing below. Strong objection: public EN videos have low observed counts; actual English demand UNVERIFIED and dramatic retrospective accounts need careful attribution.

**Great Smog:** First possible action: a London commuter sees the street vanish into smog as transport stalls. Development from weather inversion and domestic coal smoke to hospitals and retrospective death estimates; payoff air-quality reform, longer tail. Strong objection: source-rich but harder to make vivid and human over five minutes without inventing a single protagonist; familiar educational subject. Stronger on broad UK recognizability, weaker on a contained rescue reversal.

**Cocoanut Grove:** First possible action: customers at a Boston nightclub reach an exit that cannot handle them as fire spreads. Development from blocked exit paths and combustible fixtures to rescue and code changes; payoff changed egress rules. Strong objection: overwhelmingly fatal, risk of exploitative thumbnail and factually contested ignition, less personal survival payoff than Courrières.

**Selection:** Courrières wins the exact after-hook development and layered reversal; Great Smog remains a serious alternative for wider public relevance, not a manufactured weak opponent. If independent first-listener review fails, reject or rework instead of claiming a proven demand winner.

### Evidence chain / post-hook causal beats
Blast across connected workings [1,3] → Danglot assumes rockfall [4] → horse's inability to walk signals bad air [4] → bodies/gas prevent direct shaft route [4] → coats/pipe/blocked gallery change tactics [4] → dangerous rescue conditions and German teams [1,2] → premature-search perception and strikes [2] → thirteen appear twenty days later, including father and son [1,2] → another appears 4 April [1,2] → contrast with 1,099 fatalities [1,3]. Avoid false claims that German rescuers found the thirteen, that all survived by one exact diet, or that different search decisions certainly would save extra people.
