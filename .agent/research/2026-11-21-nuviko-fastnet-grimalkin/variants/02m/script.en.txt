Nick Ward was found alive on a yacht his crewmates had abandoned in a storm. They believed he was dead. Why had they left him there?

In August 1979, six men on Grimalkin were caught in a violent storm during the Fastnet Race. Seventeen-year-old Matthew Sheahan was sailing with his father, David. Waves repeatedly rolled the boat. Nick Ward and Gerry Winks were growing weaker.

Around dawn, Matthew and David went below to send a distress call. Another wave overturned the yacht. Loose equipment flew through the cabin, and David suffered a severe head injury.

A later wave flipped the boat again. Matthew was trapped underwater by his safety line. He fought free. When the yacht righted herself, Nick and Gerry lay motionless among tangled ropes in the flooded cockpit. Matthew saw his father in the sea.

Two crewmen had launched the life raft. Matthew later said Nick and Gerry looked dead and could not be moved. One of the others believed the damaged boat was sinking. Matthew joined them on the raft.

A Royal Navy helicopter rescued the three. Matthew told its crew that two men remained aboard Grimalkin. It could not return immediately because of limited fuel.

Hours later, another helicopter found Nick alive. Gerry had died. Grimalkin was still afloat and was eventually recovered.

That outcome sparked a dispute. Nick remembered waking up abandoned; Matthew described a decision made among people he believed were dead. The boat's survival did not give the crew advance knowledge that it would stay afloat.

And Matthew had not simply forgotten the men left aboard. He kept asking rescuers to look for them. The search was still under way when a helicopter finally reached Grimalkin.
