The rescue helicopter found a man alive on a yacht that his own crewmates had abandoned. The men who left believed he was already dead.

August 1979. Six sailors aboard the British yacht Grimalkin were racing across the Celtic Sea when a violent storm struck the Fastnet fleet. Among them were seventeen-year-old Matthew Sheahan, his father David, and crewman Nick Ward.

Wave after wave rolled their boat. Water filled the cockpit. Gerry Winks and Nick grew weaker. Matthew and his father tried calling for help, but another enormous wave overturned the yacht. Loose equipment flew across the cabin. David was struck and badly injured.

Then the boat rolled again. Matthew escaped from beneath the overturned hull. When it came upright, Nick and Gerry lay motionless in the flooded cockpit. Matthew saw his father's body in the sea.

Two other men had launched the life raft. Matthew joined them. He later explained that they could not lift the two motionless sailors out of the tangled ropes, and they believed the damaged yacht might sink. From the raft, they could not know if their decision was right.

A Royal Navy helicopter rescued the three men from the raft. Matthew urgently told its crew that two people remained on Grimalkin. The helicopter could not return immediately, but the information was passed on.

Hours later, another crew reached the yacht. Gerry had died. Nick Ward was alive. And the broken boat everyone expected to sink was still floating. It was eventually towed into port.

Fifteen competitors died in that Fastnet storm. Nick survived, but the question of why the others left has never had a simple answer. Matthew's account and Nick's later account differ. Both are part of the history.

The yacht stayed afloat. Nobody aboard her could have known that at the moment of the decision.
