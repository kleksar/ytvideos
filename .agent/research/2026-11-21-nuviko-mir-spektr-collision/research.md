## Three competing proposals at the same level

**A — Mir: A Station Saved by Losing a Room.** Hook (2m exact): "A cargo ship hits a space station. Air starts escaping. And three people have to decide which part of their home they can afford to lose." A remote piloting test causes a real hull breach. Cables block the emergency hatch; removing them saves air but loses solar power, while the station spins. The crew estimates the spin using visible stars, and a later spacesuited internal repair partially restores power. **Payoff:** the station survives but Spektr is permanently isolated. Intended audience: general English-speaking viewers who understand an air leak and loss of electricity without spaceflight familiarity. **Strong objection:** the event has already been extensively covered; an overtechnical electrical explanation could become a tedious failure report.

**B — Castle Bravo: The Blast Was Far Stronger Than Predicted.** Opening proposal: "The US tested a bomb expecting roughly six megatons. The reading came back fifteen—and radioactive fallout reached people outside the planned danger zone." Explain yield prediction, fallout and consequences for nearby islanders and the Japanese fishing crew, without inventing motives or dialogue. **Payoff:** the original miscalculation makes the supposed safety perimeter fail. Sources: US DOE history and National Academies record below. **Strong objection:** ethically charged narrative, likely broad competition, extensive distinctions between forecast, exposure, injuries and later compensation; cannot compress into a sensational shortcut.

**C — Great Smog: When the Fog Came Indoors.** Opening proposal: "London's fog was so thick it entered houses. Five days later, it had become a public-health catastrophe." Development: coal burning, trapped pollutants, illness, contested immediate and later mortality, 1956 Clean Air Act. **Payoff:** visible weather phenomenon reveals invisible policy failure. Source: London Museum below. **Strong objection:** 4,000 immediate and estimated up to 12,000 total deaths are not interchangeable; individual chronology must be sourced before dramatization.

**Winner over B:** Mir has a clean, sequential set of *verified actions by named people* (remote piloting → impact → hatch cables → power failure → spin correction → internal EVA), and an ending that changes the meaning of the emergency choice. Castle Bravo has arguably greater historical human significance, but a 5m presentation that properly handles consequences and rights requires more distinct sources and a carefully established affected-person perspective. This is editorial preference, **not** proof of audience demand. Strongest objection to Mir remains the prior Curious Droid coverage and technically heavy middle. A topic about loss of a room, not merely the crash, is the differentiating promise.

## Primary evidence ledger — read, with claim bounds

All accessed 2026-11-21. Internal attribution; none should appear as boilerplate in narration.

1. **NASA Shuttle–Mir: Progress Collision Animation**, https://www.nasa.gov/history/SP-4225/multimedia/progress-collision.htm — remote TV control, poor visual velocity estimate, solar-array hit followed by module/hull damage; supports cause sequence but not a claim that commander deliberately endangered anyone.
2. **NASA, NASA-5 Michael Foale chapter**, https://www.nasa.gov/history/SP-4225/nasa5/nasa5.htm — eyewitness synthesis including audible hissing, falling pressure, Soyuz escape option, cables physically blocking hatch, difficulty releasing cover, power loss, Foale stars/thumb and miniature model/flashlight, condensate and aftermath. Memoir-level recollections are clearly distinct from telemetry.
3. **NASA, Mir expedition summaries**, https://www.nasa.gov/history/SP-4225/documentation/mir-summaries/nasa5/toc-nasa5.htm — June 25 accident, closed hatch stabilization, loss of Foale's possessions/experiments, August new crew, September EVA, October 6 return. No assertion that Foale personally entered Spektr in August.
4. **NASA, Progress collision/Mir emergency animations**, https://www.nasa.gov/history/SP-4225/multimedia/animations.htm — visual/technical explanation of Soyuz emergency escape and solar/power spin dependence.
5. **NASA Mir-24 weekly reports**, https://www.nasa.gov/history/SP-4225/documentation/mir-summaries/mir24/wr.htm — September damage inspection external EVA and power condition. No assertion the puncture location was positively identified.
6. **NASA Mir-24 status reports**, https://www.nasa.gov/history/SP-4225/documentation/mir-summaries/mir24/mr.htm — August partial restoration of electricity, separate life-support recovery from other modules.
7. **PBS NOVA Mir / Spektr**, https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/nova/mir/spektr.html — Spektr American living quarters, four solar panels, roughly half station's pre-impact generation; August internal EVA by Solovyev and Vinogradov reconnects circuits via modified hatch; October second internal work. Do **not** imply the August event restored every panel.
8. **NASA STS-86 mission highlights**, https://www.nasa.gov/history/SP-4225/documentation/mission-summaries/highlights.htm — Foale back October 6, 1997; his personal experiments isolated. The later September EVA was **Foale with Solovyev**, not with Vinogradov.
9. **NASA Mir history**, https://www.nasa.gov/history/SP-4225/mir/mir.htm — Mir continued until planned deorbit in 2001.
10. **The National Academies**, https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK225000/ — Castle Bravo actual 15Mt, unexpected fallout, exposure to Lucky Dragon and island communities; finalist only.
11. **U.S. Department of Energy, Nevada Test Site History**, https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/edg/media/NTS_History.pdf — Bravo > 2× expected 6Mt; source inspected as text/search extract, **PDF visual contents not used for claims**.
12. **London Museum**, https://www.londonmuseum.org.uk/collections/london-stories/the-great-smog-of-1952/ — dates 5–9 December 1952, coal-smoke trap, immediate 4k versus later estimated 12k, 1956 law. Strong contender, not narrated in the selected scripts.

**Fact boundaries:** no fabricated cockpit dialogue, deliberate sabotage, specific number of minutes until loss of consciousness, confirmed hull-hole diameter/location, full recovery of lost electricity, or repair of the Spektr atmosphere. The 10m Foale photograph and internal spacesuit work are described by NASA/PBS and contemporary reporting. The 2m, 5m and 10m scripts share the same event sequence, while detail levels and framing differ.
