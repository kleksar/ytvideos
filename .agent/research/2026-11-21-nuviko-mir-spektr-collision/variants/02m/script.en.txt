An unmanned cargo ship slammed into Mir. Air began leaking from the space station.

On June 25, 1997, three people lived aboard Mir: commander Vasily Tsibliyev, cosmonaut Aleksandr Lazutkin, and NASA astronaut Michael Foale. The cargo ship Progress was making a remote-controlled practice approach. Tsibliyev watched its camera feed, but could not judge how fast it was closing.

Progress struck a solar panel and then punctured the Spektr module. The crew heard hissing. Pressure was falling, and abandoning the station was a real possibility.

Foale and Lazutkin hurried to isolate Spektr. But electrical cables ran across its hatch. They carried power from Spektr's solar panels to the other rooms. With the cables in the way, the hatch could not close.

The two men disconnected and cleared the lines. They freed a cover and sealed the opening. The pressure stopped falling in the rest of Mir. Spektr lost its air, along with Foale's sleeping place and some experiments.

Now the station was losing power. The disconnected panels had supplied much of its electricity. The collision had also set Mir spinning, making it harder for the remaining solar panels to face the Sun.

With the computers down, Foale tracked stars past a window against his thumb. His estimate helped controllers slow the rotation.

Later, two replacement cosmonauts entered the airless module in spacesuits and reconnected part of the power supply through a modified hatch. They never restored Spektr as a living room.
