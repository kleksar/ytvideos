## Historical source ledger, claims and limitations

1. [Sultana Disaster Museum — Ann Annis](https://www.sultanadisastermuseum.com/ann-annis): transcription of **May 11, 1865 sworn Hoffman Investigation statement**. Covers mother/husband/seven-year-old Belle; blocked cabin exit, life preservers, rope, being knocked into hold, husband/daughter jumping first, Ann holding rudder and small board. **Separate 1880 newspaper retrospective** on same page adds later rescue recollections; script 10m explicitly identifies this as later account. Do not assert sight of husband/daughter after the jump or their precise final moments. The website's square-bracket expansions are editorial context, not Ann's verbatim words.
2. [Sultana Museum — James Harvey Kimberlin](https://www.sultanadisastermuseum.com/james-kimberlain): 1913 reprint of a survivor's **later recollection**; overly crowded decks, riverside jump, removed shoes/clothes, tied floating wood, fire forced departure, Bostona stopped wheels, drifted and launched boats. Numbers of people he thought saved and relative survival rates are estimates, not representative controlled data. Distinguish memory written decades after 1865 from contemporaneous direct testimony.
3. [Sultana Museum — chief engineer Nathan Wintringer](https://www.sultanadisastermuseum.com/nathan-wintringer): **May 16, 1865** interview under the Washburn investigation. Confirms boiler leak and patch, his belief repair held, boat on fire, jump with shutter, later plank with four soldiers, one lost. His explanation of rocking/uncovered hot boiler surfaces is **a proposed hypothesis**, not established physical evidence. Museum introduction says repair patch did not itself cause explosion.
4. [Encyclopedia of Arkansas — Sultana](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/sultana-steamboat-2269/): contextual 376-passenger capacity, 2,000+ aboard, military return, Civil War aftermath, April 27, seasonal flood, casualties estimated up to 1,800, contemporary national attention, annual remembrance. It itself qualifies the high casualty number. Library-of-Congress-associated historic photo provides a compositional research reference, not licensed/obtained media bytes.
5. [American Battlefield Trust — The Sultana Disaster](https://www.battlefields.org/learn/articles/sultana-disaster): important counter-evidence: quotes **1,195 of 2,200** dead, while Encyclopedia of Arkansas cites **up to 1,800** and survivor Kimberlin estimated ~1,500. Accordingly **all scripts say more than one thousand and note disagreement**; no numeric headline/thumbnail such as "1,800 confirmed" is authorized. Trust asserts the patched boiler was the cause, while museum explicitly denies that particular patch failed; avoid a fake settled causal chain.
6. [Sultana Disaster Museum](https://www.sultanadisastermuseum.com/museum) / [Remember the Sultana](https://www.rememberthesultana.com/): scope and historical memory; the latter is a creator's presentation, not independent confirmation of disputed corruption or failure mechanism.
7. [Texas State Historical Association — New London School Explosion](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/new-london-school-explosion): March 18, 1937 school blast, official cause and rescue aftermath. Finalist corroboration.
8. [Canadian Museum of History — Empress of Ireland](https://www.historymuseum.ca/exhibitions/canadas-titanic-the-empress-of-ireland): May 29, 1914 collision; 1,477 aboard; ~1,012 deaths; sank in under fifteen minutes; eight-year-old survivor recollection. Finalist corroboration.

Causal audit:
- The *overloading* is historical fact. Inferring a single bribe or proven intentional killing from promotional films is unsupported and **NOT in narration**.
- The *explosion* is historical fact. The precise boiler failure mechanism is contested; no mechanical animation presented as historical proof.
- Kim­berlin's observation of rescuers stopping paddle wheels is attributed to his late published account; never claim direct observed audio/film.
- Ann's family was separated; husband and child never recovered. No reconstruction of their deaths or invented final conversation.
- A surviving story's personal memories can conflict in detail with later retellings; don't combine them as if recorded simultaneously.
- Historical illustration is not camera footage. Source photos/images and possible public-domain status require provenance/rights verification before production.

## Story-beat proof and failure mode

Opening (5m): Ann and child survival puzzle plus paddle-wheel rescue question. Immersion: prisoners finally going home and overcrowded riverboat. Main hook: boiler blast blocks Ann's cabin exit. After hook: rope descent, mother knocked into hold, family split, choice on burning deck, swimmers in water, Bostona halting wheels. Payoff: Ann survives without her family; rescuers save lives by stopping dangerous paddles. The strongest objection is **two narrative strands competing for 5 minutes**; future independent listener can reject it if the Bostona mechanical payoff feels like a second unrelated topic. No claims of predicted CTR, audience retention or success.
