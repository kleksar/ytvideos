## Three competing pitches, each with a hard objection

| Story | Why stop (exact English opening proposal) | Why stay / verified event chain | Payoff / strongest objection |
|---|---|---|---|
| USS Tang | "How can a submarine be sunk by the last torpedo it fired?" | Circular-running last torpedo, 20 seconds, rear flooding, 180-foot descent; thirteen escape via forward hatch, only five of those rescued; four other survivors; nine prisoners survived war | Real escape from a vessel sunk by its own shot. **Objection:** already covered by major English channels; commercial audience demand for a new shorter treatment unverified |
| Apollo 12 | "Lightning struck a Moon-bound rocket twice. | HMS Thetis | "A test tap said the torpedo tube was dry. It wasn't." | Royal Museums Greenwich: painted-over test tap, tube already open to sea, flooding, failed support, 99 dead and four survived | Irony of a tiny obstruction and cascading rescue failures. **Objection:** difficult to give a humane story without graphic/extended fatality detail, and the payoff is tragic rather than transformative |

**Decision:** USS Tang, because its hook is visually graspable without technical preparation and *two* subsequent questions genuinely matter: "can the boat evade the weapon?" (no), then "can anyone leave at depth and survive?" (nine). The final turn—picked up by the enemy, imprisoned but survive—is supported, not invented. Apollo 12 is a comparably serious contender and might outperform this choice with a technology-focused audience; no analytics justify certainty. HMS Thetis is well sourced and may warrant a separate future pitch.

**2026-11-21 primary-source follow-up:** Navy NHHC [H-038-2](https://www.history.navy.mil/about-us/leadership/director/directors-corner/h-grams/h-gram-038/h-038-2.html) explicitly accounts for **five picked up after thirteen forward escapes, three men rescued from the bridge, and one free escape from the conning tower**. The indexed passage of the original [US Navy War Damage Report No. 58](https://www.history.navy.mil/research/library/online-reading-room/title-list-alphabetically/w/war-damage-reports/submarine-report-vol1-war-damage-report-no58.html) separately corroborates three bridge survivors and one conning-tower survivor. **The full War Damage Report page returned 502, so the entire primary record was not verified.** The [National WWII Museum overview](https://www.nationalww2museum.org/war/articles/history-through-viewfinder-15) summarizes the outside/inside survivor split differently. **Uncertainty requiring care:** National WWII Museum's other retrospective essays describe the bridge/inside survivor split differently from NHHC's H-gram (examples: five outside and four inside, versus four outside and five inside). The scripts choose NHHC's described five from thirteen through the forward hatch and three bridge plus one conning tower, but the discrepancy is material and should undergo independent source reconciliation before production. Different sources date the final engagement to 24 or 25 October across the midnight change; narration says only "October 1944". ## Story mechanics and promise

- Promise: **Can a submarine sunk by its own last torpedo leave anybody alive?**
- First image (proposed, not generated): nighttime ocean, a torpedo's wake bending back toward a surfaced submarine; no false archival label.
- Opening 05m text: "How can a submarine be sunk by the last torpedo it fired? And what can its crew do when the boat comes to rest on the seabed?"
- Development (documented chain): experienced boat -> last two weapons for crippled transport -> circular run -> emergency evasive order -> collision at 20 seconds -> aft flooding -> ~30 forward survivors -> pressure-matched escape -> thirteen outside/five recovered -> four outside hull -> POW survival, 78 not.
- Payoff: nine rescued prisoners survived war, alongside 78 deaths; no heroic rewrite or suggested rescue of all thirteen.
- Weakness: familiar existing viral retellings and audience likely knows "own torpedo" reveal. Hence **open with it**, and make the forward escape and captivity the remaining dramatic reason to watch.
