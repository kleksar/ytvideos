The last torpedo fired by the American submarine USS Tang turned around and came back toward the boat.

October 1944. Tang was hunting Japanese ships near Taiwan. Her crew had just fought through two nights of attacks. Only two torpedoes remained.

The first went toward a damaged transport. Then the second broke the surface, curved left, and began circling.

Commander Richard O'Kane ordered an emergency turn. It was too late. Twenty seconds after launch, Tang's own torpedo struck her.

Water flooded the rear compartments. The submarine sank stern first and came to rest about fifty-five metres below the surface.

Some sailors had been on the bridge when the weapon hit. Others were trapped inside, still alive in the forward part of the boat.

Those inside had an escape device called a Momsen Lung. It helped a sailor breathe during an ascent through the water. But first they had to leave a damaged submarine with enemy ships overhead.

Thirteen men made it out through the forward escape hatch. Only five of them were later picked up alive.

Four more survivors came from the bridge and conning tower. Japanese sailors rescued all nine and took them prisoner. Some of the men aboard the rescue ship had survived vessels Tang had attacked. The American prisoners were beaten, but all nine survived the war.

Seventy-eight of Tang's crew did not survive the sinking.

The weapon's exact failure is not settled by the rescue story. What is certain is what the crew saw, and how little time they had to react.

A submarine's last shot was supposed to travel away from it. On Tang's final night, the direction changed everything.
