## Three competing pitches on comparable footing

**1. Baker Street (selected).** Audience: English-speaking viewers who like a surprising mistake and an ingenious, visually legible operation. First line (02m): “A man walked into a London bank carrying an umbrella.” First frame: customer measuring inconspicuously with an umbrella in a physical paper vault. Second beat: tunnel reaches concrete, jack and lance fail; next change: walkie-talkie intercepted; decisive reversal: police visit the **correct** branch but cannot open its time-locked vault; ending: 268 boxes broken, investigators follow lease in a real name. Payoff: locked vault door hid an intrusion through the floor, not a magical conspiracy. Strongest objection: robbery was depicted in a 2008 feature film and major documentary content; 2024 short with millions of views is a large-creator outlier, and fresh comparable English Short demand isn't established.

**2. Great Train Robbery (strongest alternative).** First line: “The robbers stopped a mail train with a battery and a glove. Then their driver couldn't move it.” First frame: cut-paper red signal over nocturnal train. Progression: victim drivers attacked -> fake signal works -> replacement engineer fails -> injured driver must move locomotive -> human chain removes mailbags -> farm and Monopoly lead to police discovery. Payoff: the supposedly perfect railway plan contained a basic operational flaw and a visible forensic trail. Verified central events in British Transport Police [official account](https://www.btp.police.uk/police-forces/british-transport-police/areas/campaigns/our-history/crime-history/great-train-robbery/). Strong objection: well-known, heavily covered story; more characters and physical violence to depict, and its outcome may be familiar. It arguably has **higher human stakes**, so Baker is not automatically better. Baker wins the hook-to-payoff unity: one radio + wrong side of the same vault creates a clean causal irony throughout the whole film.

**3. Hitler diaries forgery.** First line: “The supposed diary of a dictator contained words that couldn't have existed when he wrote them.” First frame: a paper diary dated in the 1930s beside post-1955 adhesive-test evidence. Progression: publisher buys/cache promoted -> historians tentative endorsement -> forensic exam -> postwar publication copied into supposed period diary -> public retraction. Payoff: the illusion of authenticity falls to evidence the forger couldn't fake. Strong contemporaneous evidence: US [Washington Post, 1983-05-07](https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/politics/1983/05/07/bonn-brands-7-hitler-diaries-fake-after-tests/4a2e2df6-6f34-4b5e-a6ec-66adf78100d5/) and [Chemical & Engineering News, 1983-05-16](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/cen-v061n020.p008a) (abstract only; full PDF paywalled, not read). Strong objection: Holocaust-denial content embedded in documents requires fuller context; the topic is less congruent with light Paper Detour pacing. Do not treat chemical evidence alone as a viewer-demand signal.

Winner therefore Baker Street, with Great Train as a genuinely competitive fallback. Neither competitor is rejected by the owner.
