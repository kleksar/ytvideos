## Three credible pitches before selection

**A. Ecce Homo — winner.** First spoken line: "Why did people travel to a small Spanish town to see a painting that looked ruined?" Immediate first frame: old wall-picture and widely circulated altered face as two distinct **reproductions** (licenses to be checked later). After hook: water damage → Cecilia's incomplete repainting and trip → global before/after joke → her hurt in 2012 interview → travelers arrive → organized paid entry → 2022 wages/elder-care support → 2025 local remembrance. Payoff: a monetized joke helped vulnerable elderly residents, without making humiliation or inexpert intervention good. Strong objection: audience already knows meme, perhaps before/after payoff appears "done" after 20 seconds; a 2026 English Short exists and the topic's demand on Paper is unknown.

**B. Virginia lottery syndicate — strongest suspense competitor.** First line: "They tried to buy every lottery ticket. Then the shops stopped selling." First visual: huge ticket stacks versus closing purchase window. Development: investors and ticket-production machinery → days to place millions of tickets → retailer limits left combinations uncovered → $27m jackpot winning ticket → state investigates legality → 1992 lottery decides to pay. Sources: contemporaneous Washington Post 1992-03-03, UPI 1992-03-10; retrospective Washington Post 2019-03-27. Payoff: the supposedly guaranteed purchase was incomplete and still won; the lottery restricted later bulk purchases. Main objection: repeats the fresh #330 Paper story about beating a supposedly random prize system, reducing differentiation; some accounts exaggerate the number of combinations purchased and Mandel's exact corporate role. No strong comparable creator-normalized 2026 video baseline verified.

**C. Million Dollar Homepage — entrepreneurial alternative.** First line: "He sold a million tiny squares of a web page. The last squares cost thirty-eight times the price." Visual: grid progressively filled with company icons. Development: 2005 concept → first customers → news attention → last thousand pixels auctioned for $38,100 in January 2006 → site disrupted by extortion-driven DDoS → ad links subsequently age and break. Source: WIRED 2006-06-01; corroborating early media / archive. Payoff: ~$1.037m gross, with the strange fragility of a supposedly permanent online billboard. Main objection: a satisfying number, but weaker stakes once sale is revealed; limited evidence of contemporary audience appeal for a paper-diorama story. Public YouTube search surfaced small retrospective channels, not a trustworthy current demand baseline.

**Decision.** The art restoration has an unusually strong second payoff beyond the obvious visual joke, and a real person with consequences; it beats Mandel on novelty relative to #330 and beats the homepage on emotional and material change. **Counter-signal is serious:** the 2026 English Short of the subject was below some of its author's other art Shorts. This is an **editorial hypothesis**, not verified demand/CTR/retention.
