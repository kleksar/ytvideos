A painting made a small Spanish town famous. But the woman who painted it became an internet joke.

In 2012, Cecilia Giménez was trying to protect a fading picture of Jesus on a church wall in Borja, Spain. Moisture had damaged the old image, called Ecce Homo. Cecilia was an amateur painter. She had cared for the picture before, and this time she began repainting the face.

Then she went away before she had finished.

Someone saw the work in progress. Photos of the original and Cecilia's version spread around the world. People called the new face Monkey Christ. The jokes were everywhere.

Cecilia was eighty-one. She said the work wasn't finished, and she was deeply upset by the ridicule. When she spoke on Spanish television that September, she no longer wanted to go back and complete it.

But the crowds came anyway. People who had never heard of Borja traveled there to see the painting for themselves. They didn't come to see a masterpiece. They came to see the picture they recognized from the internet.

The town kept the altered painting. Visitors began paying to enter the sanctuary, and souvenirs brought in more money. By 2022, local officials said the income helped pay staff to keep the site open and support care for older people who could not afford it.

Cecilia died in 2025, aged ninety-four. The town's mayor remembered her kindness and how much pressure she had endured.

The face people laughed at stayed on the wall. And the joke that hurt one woman ended up helping other people in her town.

It was a very different ending from the one those first photographs suggested.
