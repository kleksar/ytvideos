Michael Larson's first spin on television hit a Whammy, a square that could erase his winnings. Then he won $110,237.

On Press Your Luck in 1984, contestants stopped a flashing light on a board of eighteen squares. Where it stopped decided their prize. The light seemed to move at random.

Larson, an ice-cream-truck driver from Ohio, had recorded the show for months. He rewound tapes, followed the light instead of the prizes, and found just five repeating paths. At home he practiced hitting pause at the right moment.

Two squares were especially useful. In the second round, landing there meant money and another spin. The trick was to recognize a path and stop the light on one of them, then do it again without hitting a Whammy.

His first try in the studio failed. On the next turns, he began stopping on the paying squares. One prize bought him another spin; the next spin brought more money. Backstage, staff noticed the same positions kept paying.

They discussed stopping the show. But he hadn't touched the equipment or broken a rule. The producers had put those five paths into the machine themselves.

Larson played long enough to fill two broadcasts. CBS reviewed the game, paid him $110,237 in cash and prizes, then changed the patterns.

At the start, the people in the control room saw an ordinary contestant pressing a button. They couldn't see months of rehearsal behind his first press.
