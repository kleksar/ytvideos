## Three comparable finalist pitches

**1 — Galaxy Note7: the replacement was recalled too**

- Why stop: "They recalled a dangerous phone, sent out a fix, and then recalled the fix." Familiar object and meaningful contradiction.
- First spoken line in chosen 05m version: "Why would a company ask you to return a smartphone, give you a supposedly safer one, and then tell you to return that one as well?"
- First image: two recognizable phone-shaped paper cutouts, original stamped RETURN and replacement stamped RETURN, no real victims or fake photographic evidence.
- After first hook: launch → first reports → first official recall → green-icon replacements → October 5 plane evacuation on the ground and further reports → full recall → aviation ban.
- Payoff: January 2017 investigation found **different** defects in original versus replacement batteries; response changes from swapping cells to withdrawing product and redesigning safety tests.
- Strong objection: globally famous "exploding phone" topic, many explanations already made; a double-failure angle needs genuinely clear mechanics and cannot claim this audience will click.
- Source anchors: Samsung 2016-09-02 statement, US CPSC 2016-09-15/2016-10-13, DOT 2016-10-14, Samsung 2017-01-23.

**2 — Dasani UK: "purification" and a new contaminant**

- Why stop: A global drink maker sells processed municipal water as a premium branded product and then finds bromate above legal limits.
- Proposed cold start: "A drinks giant put tap water in a premium bottle. Then a problem appeared after it had purified the water."
- After hook: early criticism about source → bromate found in testing → roughly 500,000 bottles voluntarily removed → French/German launches postponed.
- Payoff: legal limit breached, but the UK FSA said **no immediate public-health risk**; responsible story is failed quality control and brand trust, not proven mass poisoning.
- Strong objection: narrow UK historical context and single sharp reversal; genuine new material for 10m is weaker than a second product failure.
- Verified: https://www.theguardian.com/uk/2004/mar/19/foodanddrink ; https://www.theguardian.com/business/2004/mar/20/medicineandhealth.lifeandhealth ; https://www.theguardian.com/business/2004/mar/24/food.money .

**3 — Apollo 12: an unfamiliar switch saved the Moon mission**

- Why stop: Two lightning strikes in the first minute make Mission Control's data unreadable while the launch continues.
- Proposed cold start: "Lightning struck the Moon rocket twice. The engines kept running, but Mission Control was losing the information it needed."
- After hook: 36.5s and 52s strikes → scrambled instrument data → John Aaron recognizes a pattern → SCE-to-AUX restores readings → mission reaches Moon.
- Payoff: obscure electrical workaround under pressure; not proof that the rocket was about to explode.
- Strong objection: spectacular opening and human stakes may exceed Note7, but space missions are highly represented in the queue, and the switch explanation is less universal than a recalled household device.
- NASA sources: https://science.nasa.gov/resource/lightning-strikes-twice/ ; https://www.nasa.gov/centers-and-facilities/kennedy/repeat-performance-apollo-12-achieves-second-moon-landing/ .

**Decision:** Galaxy Note7 wins narrowly on universal stakes and two independently verified reversals (replacement, then aviation prohibition), plus a substantive explanation not exhausted by the title. Apollo 12 is the strongest alternative, not a straw man: its decisive operator action and successful Moon landing are better as an uplifting rescue.
