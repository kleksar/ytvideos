## Three genuinely competitive proposals

**Piltdown:** Hook (2m): “Someone filed an ape's teeth until they looked human.” A fraud seemingly confirms a popular theory, critics challenge the joins, tests expose the work, forensic DNA later identifies the same orangutan across two places. Payoff: the *second* site, once supporting the specimen, becomes the clue to the forgery's authorship. Audience: anyone curious how expert institutions can be fooled while some experts object. Objection: science history is less immediately everyday than the rival; longstanding successful explanations have already covered it; freshness and channel-specific appeal not proved.

**Taco Liberty Bell:** Hook: “On April 1, a fast-food chain announced it had bought the Liberty Bell.” The ad triggers calls and anger; company reveals its prank and a real conservation donation. Payoff: the actual bell was never sold; joke earned national exposure. Primary contemporaneous UPI report: https://www.upi.com/Archives/1996/04/01/Taco-Bell-stages-April-Fools-hoax/8400828334800/ ; historical local report https://www.inquirer.com/history/taco-bell-april-fools-day-prank-20250331.html . Objection: obvious April Fools reveal; no verified meaningful 10m second act; U.S. history requires explaining the bell to international viewers.

**London Beer Flood:** Hook: “A brewery's giant vat burst and beer rushed into homes.” Events: vessel fails, adjoining vessels release liquid, wall breaks and local residents die; contemporary inquiry concluded accidental deaths. Payoff: a brewery disaster, not a funny drinking story. Basic source https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Beer_Flood ; primary inquest and modern accounts not thoroughly cross-checked in this run. Objection: weak source depth for dramatic individual beats; avoiding exploitation of victims imposes stronger evidence burden.

**Why Piltdown beats the closest rival:** Taco Bell stops scrolling more immediately, but Piltdown has a stronger verified progression after its first fact—1912 assembly → early technical objections → 1949 chemical doubts → 1953 teeth revelation → 2016 cross-site clue. The additional five-minute passage changes what the audience understands about the original “evidence,” not merely the duration of a prank. The rival wins on broad recognisability; choice is a **testable editorial hypothesis**, not an established audience preference. If independent first listener finds no reason to stay past the filing reveal, reject/rework despite sources.

**P2 / criticism and chronology:** same NHM 2016, “Uncovering the fraud” explicitly says some anatomists doubted from the beginning; Dawson died 1916 and no more Piltdown objects appeared. NHM collection says fluorine testing in 1949 undermined age and 1953 Weiner/Le Gros Clark/Oakley re-examination exposed filed teeth / staining. **Nuance:** NHM 2016 says extent of most staining was additionally reported 1955; don't assign every detail's first discovery unambiguously to 1953.

**P3 / science and 2016 forensic:** original peer-reviewed research, De Groote et al., *Royal Society Open Science* (2016), DOI 10.1098/rsos.160328, open metadata/abstract https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/1508682/ ; NHM news https://www.nhm.ac.uk/discover/news/2016/august/piltdown-man-charles-dawson-likely-fraudster.html under “New discoveries” and “Charles Dawson”. ## Causal map

1912 mixed-looking finds → teeth seem human, supports large-brained ancestor claim → critics see mismatch yet the display/interpretation gains fame → “another site” gives apparent reinforcement → other discoveries and fluorine age testing weaken fit → microscopic filing and stains show deliberate construction → a human skull plus orangutan jaw is no ancestor → 2016 the same orangutan and repeated working method link both sites → apparent corroboration becomes a clue about the fabrication → limits: Dawson likely but sole responsibility and motive uncertain.

## Editorial evidence addendum — 2026-11-22, PR #348, 02m exact revision

No new historical claims beyond this source register. The new short makes the **second site** the early apparent corroboration, then asks why two sites contained related remains after the 1953 filed-tooth discovery. NHM 2016 (P1–P3) supports Dawson's report of a second site with molar/skull pieces, early anatomical dissent, no new Piltdown finds after his death, 2016 DNA linking canine and molar teeth from two sites to the same orangutan, plus similar gravel/putty preparation. The institutional NHM collection supports 1949 fluorine dating and the 1953 microscopic file marks; its chronological summaries differ about *when* all staining became clear, so the revised Short does not date staining to 1953. The investigative inference strongly implicates Dawson, but no direct proof that he acted alone or explanation of his motive is claimed.
