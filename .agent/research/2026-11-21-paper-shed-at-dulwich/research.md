# The Shed at Dulwich — research and selection (2026-11-21)

## Source, context and boundary
Channel: Paper Detour, English illustrated narrative; RU for editorial only. Main 131ef24068d75967b1e43aa88e5f1370f6775054. PR #124 already merged, thus editorial main, not the old proposed revision. No production run. Research date 2026-11-21. No proprietary data searched on public web.

**Premise:** Fake review listing for a London garden shed advanced to TripAdvisor #1 without serving a meal; after a real, staged one-night dinner of dressed-up supermarket food, some real guests asked to return. Two contrasting outcomes are necessary: one unhappy couple and one who requested a second visit. This is NOT a blind food test, real functioning restaurant, or demonstration that all reviews are fake.

## Three finalists, independently comparable
| Pitch | Exact opening idea; development; payoff; strongest objection |
|---|---|
| **The Shed** | "Would you book dinner at a restaurant that had never served anyone?" Fake menu/photos → false reviews and actual booking inquiries → #1 → one-night dinner → dissatisfied guests AND repeat booking. **Objection:** famous VICE stunt; new retelling must add more than the #1 twist, and most evidence comes from the stunt organizer |
| Taco Liberty Bell | "What happens when a fast-food brand says it bought a national monument?" Newspaper advertisement → official and public reactions → reveal as April Fools, corporate aftereffect. **Objection:** punchline is quickly exhausted; historical details alone may not support 5/10m. [UPI primary-era report](https://www.upi.com/Archives/1996/04/01/Taco-Bell-stages-April-Fools-hoax/8400828334800/) |
| Judgment of Paris | "Why did an anonymous tasting reverse French experts' expectations?" Blind tasting setup → unexpected scores and disagreement → later reappraisal. **Objection:** technical wine names and disputed meaning of a one-day tasting; requires nuanced context. [Smithsonian](https://www.americanhistory.si.edu/explore/exhibitions/food/online/wine-table/judgment-paris) |

Strongest competitor = Taco Liberty Bell for universal recognizability and shorter immediate hook; The Shed wins on **documented second act** (real diners) and bittersweet contradictory payoff. Actual audience interest remains a hypothesis, not validated CTR/retention.

## Causal chain and story promise
1. Prior paid fake reviews → Butler tests fiction itself (S1) → question: how build fake listing?
2. Phone, no number, mood menu, staged inedible foods (S1) → listing approved.
3. Coordinated fake reviews → ranking rises → genuine people call and are told no tables (S1/S2); exclusivity is Butler's interpretation, not independently tested causality.
4. #1 November → new decision to serve real people for one night (S1) → can manufactured expectations survive actual experience?
5. Ready meals, actors, DJ, free dinner, Californians visibly unhappy, others ask to return (S1) → payoff is **mixed reaction**, not claim that food objectively excellent.
6. TripAdvisor's stated anti-fraud distinction and subsequent removal → scope of inference narrow.
Short delivers booking punchline + natural return to question; 05m explores staged dinner and two competing reactions; 10m adds consequences, mechanics, official response, later reappraisal and attribution. No fabricated dialogues.
