London's number-one restaurant had never served a single guest. It had no dining room. Just a shed in a south London garden.

In 2017, writer Oobah Butler wanted to find out whether fake online reviews could make a restaurant seem real. He bought a phone, made a website, and listed only the street, without a house number. The Shed at Dulwich was supposedly available by appointment only.

Its menu offered moods instead of dishes. Photos showed painted sponges, shaving foam, and an egg arranged on Butler's foot. It looked like fashionable food. It wasn't edible.

Friends and acquaintances began posting invented reviews. TripAdvisor approved the listing in May, placing it last among more than eighteen thousand London restaurants. Then it started rising. A real person called to reserve a table.

Butler said there were no openings for six weeks. More people called anyway. By November, The Shed was ranked first, though it hadn't cooked a single dinner.

So Butler invited real people to his garden. Friends played happy diners. A DJ played restaurant sounds over a microwave. Guests were served decorated supermarket meals. The food was free.

The responses weren't all glowing. Butler described one visiting couple leaving visibly unhappy. But another couple asked if they could return.
