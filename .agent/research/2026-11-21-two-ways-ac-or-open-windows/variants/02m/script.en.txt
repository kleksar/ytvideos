I'm about to switch on the air conditioning. But would opening my car windows use less fuel?

I can choose A: windows down, AC off. Or B: windows up, AC on. The car, route and speed stay the same. My score is fuel burned over the same distance. Which side would you pick?

I haven't run this drive myself. I'm comparing published tests, not pretending that this animation is a road experiment.

There's a reason both sides sound plausible. Open windows make the car push through more air. AC asks the engine to do extra work. At first, speed seems like the answer: the faster I go, the worse those open windows should become.

But researchers at Oak Ridge National Laboratory tested two real vehicles, a Toyota Corolla and a Ford Explorer. With the AC working at maximum cooling, windows down used less fuel in both vehicles between forty and seventy miles an hour.

Then the Corolla changed the story. At seventy-five, the two methods were about equal. At eighty, closing the windows and using maximum AC finally did better. The Explorer didn't show that same crossover in the tested range.

So why does the US government's own driving advice say windows down at low speed, AC on at highway speed? Because the lab's comparison pushed the AC to maximum. Real drivers can set it to a comfortable level instead. And actual cooling demand changes with sunlight, humidity and the car.

For the Corolla in that particular high-speed test, B wins. At lower tested speeds with maximum AC, A wins. Neither result is a rule for every car.

On the next hot drive, I'll reach for the AC button again before deciding.
