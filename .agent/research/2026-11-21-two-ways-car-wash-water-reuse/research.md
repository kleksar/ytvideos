## Three comparable editorial pitches

**1 — Hose versus reclaiming conveyor (chosen).** Audience: car owners and anyone who has left water running. Exact proposed opening: "A garden hose can use a hundred gallons while I wash one car." First picture: one dirty car, open hose on cyan A, commercial spray line amber B, both pending result. Stop: why would the large automated machine draw less *new* water? Stay: 100 modeled open hose vs 30 metered California conveyor sample, reveal reuse → shutoff home example 30 ties → one 11-gallon home counterexample refutes a categorical claim; consider runoff. Payoff: two variables worth checking (hose open time; commercial reclaim). Strong objection: the A and B measurements aren't paired on the same car, real cleaning parity and current facility performance UNVERIFIED; public interest in this exact comparison also weakly evidenced. Why chosen over hand dryer: two independently sourceable changes to the *same freshwater metric*, not climate-versus-hygiene conflation; a real practical action reverses the intuitive outcome. 10m adds separate flow accounting but may still be excessive.

**2 — Hand dryer versus paper towel.** Proposed opening: "I reach for a paper towel because an electric dryer must be using more energy. Does the whole life cycle agree?" First image: same hands/one wash, A towels, B electric, declared life-cycle impact. Stop: a new October 2026 report suggests the electricity-consuming tool has a lower overall environmental footprint; stay: manufacturing/disposal vs electrical mix and drying time; payoff: different procurement outcomes by device and power. Objection: CE Delft gives a general finding but the exact appliance and towel case, hygiene and power source need matched inputs; the most visible comparable YouTube discussion is about hygiene, not the specific footprint. This is a genuine strong alternative, not a deliberately weak runner-up.

**3 — Sock pairs: direct matching or color bins first?** Proposed opening: "I emptied forty socks on the table. Should I pick pairs as I see them, or group colors first?" First image: same fixed sock arrangement, two empty work areas and predeclared number of comparisons/hand moves. Stop: everybody recognizes the pile; stay: sorted grouping may reduce searching but requires extra moves, so result may flip when socks look identical. Payoff: a replicable counting rule for one predefined arrangement, without pretending the agent performed a physical test. Objection: sorting algorithm model dominates the observed result and long versions would have to invent multiple distributions. No verifiable observed broad viewer demand. Third place, not a fake competitor.

## Model boundary and falsification

**Task:** One already-dirty hypothetical car; A home wash, B commercial conveyor. **Declared metric:** gallons of *new* water entering the wash per comparable completed clean; not total spray, price, time, or total life-cycle impact.

**Published scenario 1** A open hose: 10 gallons/minute × 10 minutes flowing = **100 gallons** (Maryland illustrative flow-rate calculation). B: 2017 field average **30 gallons** freshwater/car from **six** Northern California conveyor sites with reclaim. Illustrative difference: **70 gallons** in favor of B. These are **not paired comparable-site observations**; assume equal cleaning only to ask the question, without evidence that home use, California business and narrator's car correspond.

**Published scenario 2** A bucket+shutoff guidance example **30 gallons** vs same B field sample **30 gallons**: *illustrative tie* with source/method limitations. PolitiFact's measured **11-gallon single home wash** is a **counterexample** to "all home washes use more", not evidence the narrator's A is 11 gallons or equal to B's observed sample.

**Separate federal planning sensitivity** DOE conveyor **16.9 with reuse** vs **34.0 without**. Do not substitute DOE estimate as 2017 metered sample. Pairing 30-gallon careful home *guidance example* with DOE no-reuse **34** produces a conditional A advantage of **4**, but it is **not a controlled test**. 10m uses this only with explicit source separation.

**Arithmetic check:** 100-30=70; 10×2=20, 10×3=30, 10×4=40; 34-30=4. Gal-to-litre conversion deliberately omitted from narration to minimize cognitive load; numbers remain source-native.

**Known weaknesses:** Wash quality/equivalence, specific equipment, water meter, wastewater routing, soap/chemistry, reclaim operation and contemporary local figures are unobserved. Recycled throughput is not a metric substitute for freshwater intake. Do not claim one permanent environmental winner, guaranteed cleaning performance or actual narrator experiment. For stronger proof, a real matched A/B with water meters at both sites and verified equal outcome would be required; not authorized here.

## Decision

Select **car-wash water-reuse** as editorial hypothesis due to an understandable visual paradox and two grounded conditional changes on one honest metric; **hand dryer remains a serious alternative** with more recent formal research. The topic's most serious objection remains unpaired datasets and poor topic-matched video baseline. If the opening or independent first listener fails, **reject/rework** rather than release the story because it was scheduled.
