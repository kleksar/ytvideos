Will dark mode save my phone's battery?

I open the same app on the same kind of OLED phone. I have two choices: A, the white screen, or B, the dark screen. I keep the task and brightness level the same. The score is the phone's power use, not how nice either screen looks.

I expect the black background to win by a mile. On OLED, dark pixels need less power. But a phone also runs its processor, radios and other parts. Changing the colours doesn't switch those off.

Here's what surprised me in published tests from Purdue University. At ordinary indoor brightness, around thirty to fifty percent, switching to dark mode saved an average of only three to nine percent of the phone's power.

That's a real saving. It just isn't the huge battery-life boost I imagined.

Now I take the same choice outside. Bright daylight pushes the screen towards maximum brightness. In the researchers' high-brightness tests, dark mode saved thirty-nine to forty-seven percent of phone power on average.

Same A-or-B setting. Very different answer.

There is one more catch. This comparison is about phones with OLED displays. On an LCD, a backlight illuminates the whole screen, so black backgrounds don't produce the same power-saving effect.

I haven't run a battery-drain experiment here. I'm comparing published research, and those percentages are not a promise about every phone or every app.

My decision? On a bright OLED screen, I would try dark mode for battery savings. Indoors, I would choose the theme I prefer and pay attention to brightness.
