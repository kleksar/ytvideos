I have one minute before my subway train leaves. But the exit I need at the next station is at the other end of the platform. Do I board here, or walk toward the last car?

I'm comparing two choices in a simple made-up station. It's a model, not footage of a real journey.

Option A: I get on at the closest door. Option B: I walk one hundred and fifty metres along this platform first, so I get off near the exit. I walk at one and a half metres per second. Reaching that better door takes me one hundred seconds.

Here's the trap. This train leaves in seventy-five seconds. If I insist on option B, I miss it.

With A, I catch this train and walk two minutes after I arrive. With B, I wait for the next train, four minutes later, and only walk twenty seconds at the end.

Counting the identical train ride, I reach the exit in eight minutes and fifteen seconds with A. With B, it's ten minutes and thirty-five seconds. The supposedly clever door costs me two minutes and twenty seconds.

But change just one thing. Now the train leaves in two minutes and forty-five seconds. I have time to walk to the last car before it arrives.

Both choices catch the same train. A still has two minutes of walking at the destination. B has twenty seconds. This time B wins by one minute and forty seconds.

The remarkable part is that both options involve the same total amount of walking. Moving it into waiting time is what saves me time, not walking faster.

So I can't pick a winning car without knowing when the train leaves. I check the countdown, keep clear of the platform edge, and never chase closing doors.
