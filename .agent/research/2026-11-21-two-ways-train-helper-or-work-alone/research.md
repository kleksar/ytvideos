## Finalists, same decision criteria

### A. Teach a friend to assemble party boxes (**selected**)

**Who cares:** anyone faced with doing repetitive work or explaining it to someone else. **Stop:** "I have 90 minutes to finish 12 boxes; is losing 18 minutes to a lesson a mistake?" **First actual EN spoken line:** “I've got twelve flat-pack party boxes to assemble before the guests arrive.” **First visible event:** 12 identical unfinished kits and a large deadline timer in an illustrative model; two clearly labeled methods.

**Middle after hook:** for five boxes, B trains and loses by 2min; at six tie; at seven B begins to win; at twelve solo misses deadline, trained team finishes 16min early. **Payoff:** after 18 minutes training, 7 boxes with me and 5 with friend in parallel, total 74min versus solo 96min. **Strongest objection:** training an adult to make party boxes for 18min is a deliberately chosen model assumption, possibly unnatural. Actual teaching time, quality, fatigue, equipment bottlenecks, willing helpers and physical task complexity are unknown. Public exact-topic viewer demand UNVERIFIED.

### B. One pooled queue vs two separate queues (**strongest alternative, not owner-rejected**)

**Stop:** my shorter line stops moving while the other line clears. **First possible EN line:** “I picked the shorter checkout line. Why am I still the last person waiting?” **Middle:** fixed service-time order, one customer delays a dedicated server, pooled queue shares servers; no changed arrivals to favor either. **Payoff:** compare completion times and the longest unlucky wait. **Objection:** checkout/queues were already the channel pilot, and public queue comparisons are well covered, so novelty weaker despite a visceral opening.

### C. Suitcase rolled vs folded clothes

**Stop:** which method fits one extra shirt? **First possible EN line:** “Both piles have the same clothes. Can folding them differently fit another shirt?” **Middle:** pack identical items two ways and show actual remaining volume and access. **Payoff:** a specific demonstrated capacity, with crease/packing-time tradeoff. **Objection:** without a controlled physical test or faithful 3D simulation, inventing a packing winner would violate the channel's honest comparison rule. We do not manufacture a test result.

The chosen story beats the closest rival on **freshness relative to Two Ways E001 and an original human decision**. It loses on **measured audience interest**: no comparable proven outlier or independent first-listener data. Label a transparent editorial experiment, not confirmed demand.

## Reproducible model / arithmetic truth

All model values below are **synthetic assumptions**, not facts borrowed from videos:
- One batch: `n` identical flat-pack party gift boxes; two volunteers: me and an initially untrained friend. All usable finished boxes count; zero defects by model assumption.
- I assemble 1/8min; friend after training 1/10min. Training takes 18min **simultaneously from both of us**, no output while training, no overlapping start.
- Once trained, separate stations, unlimited independent supplies, no shared-tool or space bottleneck, no change in skill during production. The clock runs from initial setup until last box finished, strict finish-time metric **announced before A/B**.
- A completion time `T_A(n)=8n`; B for integer split of `k` boxes to me and `n-k` to friend: `T_B(n,k)=18+max(8k,10(n-k))`. The team split is selected by minimizing this exact function over `k=0..n` (no fractional boxes).
- For n=5: A40, best B `18+max(8*3,10*2)=42`: A faster by 2.
- n=6: A48, best B `18+max(8*3,10*3)=48`: tie.
- n=7: A56, best B `18+max(8*4,10*3)=50`: B faster by 6.
- n=12: A96, best B `18+max(8*7,10*5)=74`: B faster by22. Hypothetical party deadline **90min** gives A six minutes late, B sixteen minutes early.
- n=12 equal 6/6 split B `18+max(48,60)=78`, 4min worse than optimal 7/5.
- Sensitivities (10m independent textual variation): training 30min, rate10 => `30+max(56,50)=86`. Training18min, helper rate15min, optimized 8/4 => `18+max(64,60)=82`. Both changes => `30+max(64,60)=94`. All numbers re-derived by formula, no measured person.
- Not a general delegation rule, optimal labor-cost solution, measured productivity, quality guarantee or genuine deadline.

**Material caveat:** 18min for learning to assemble a cardboard gift box may be unrealistic; this deliberately awkward box with inserts is a hypothetical model and must **not** be depicted as a real completed experiment. More evidence about actual assembly learning could change the story's applicability, not the validity of its conditional math.
