### Three matched finalist pitches

**1 — Herald (chosen).**
- Viewer: general English-language historical audience, no ship-design knowledge assumed.
- Stop: *“How could a ferry leave port with doors large enough for cars wide open?”* Exact scene: ordinary 1987 Zeebrugge vehicle loading; bridge without position indicator.
- Stay: asleep assigned operator → ambiguous loading/officer duty → no warning to captain → free water on vehicle deck → capsize and improvised rescue → earlier fleet near misses and written requests reveal preventable system weakness.
- Payoff: the inquiry did not assign sole blame to a sleeping worker; company management had been warned, and departure was allowed to depend on silence. **Opposing objection:** film/drama/history already extensively covered, and tragic stakes require restrained delivery. Differentiate through contemporaneous inquiry's actual 1983–86 record, not sensational recreation.
- Source: Sheen formal investigation and 1987 Hansard below.

**2 — Wahine (strongest alternative for dynamic footage and rescue).**
- Stop: ferry only a short distance from shore, yet people die while reaching it.
- Stay: Cyclone Giselle, Barrett Reef, propulsion damage → water and free-surface movement → abandoned in rough seas → rescuers cannot reach victims quickly; inquiry rejects simplistic claim that earlier evacuation would save more.
- Payoff: outcome depends not only on sinking but where survivors emerge. **Opposing objection:** storm dominates agency, likely requires licensed imagery to avoid a generic reenactment; local NZ specificity may be less universal.
- Sources: NZ Ministry for Culture and Heritage https://nzhistory.govt.nz/wahine-wrecked-wellington-harbour and inquiry https://nzhistory.govt.nz/culture/wahine-disaster/court-of-inquiry. Its 53 total reflects two later deaths, not 53 immediate.

**3 — Empress of Ireland (strongest alternative for existing cross-language viewer interest).**
- Stop: liner sinks in less than 15 minutes after collision in fog in Canadian waters.
- Stay: intersecting courses, collision, rapid loss of interior access, incomplete escape.
- Payoff: a major disaster with 1,012 dead, much less familiar than Titanic. **Opposing objection:** Oceanliner Designs' 2023 real-time 14-minute treatment gives a stronger direct visualization; relying on the same format would be derivative.
- Source: Canadian Museum of History https://www.historymuseum.ca/exhibitions/canadas-titanic-the-empress-of-ireland .

**Why Herald wins over Wahine:** stronger, explicitly documented causal *decision chain* after the obvious shipwreck, plus distinct reveal that previous crews had already sailed with doors open and company management ignored warnings. Wahine has stronger storm/shore visuals and may win when licensed archive material is available. **Why over Empress:** 2023 real-time treatment already leads with the most immediately captivating format; Herald has a differentiated question about responsibility that holds a 5-minute standalone story. The greatest risk is that a documentary-heavy niche has already saturated the incident.
