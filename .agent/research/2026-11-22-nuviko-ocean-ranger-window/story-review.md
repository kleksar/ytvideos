## Three genuinely standalone versions and central answer

**02m — ordinary warning → fatal failure chain → revisiting the warning.** First report: **“At nine that evening, the Ocean Ranger reported a broken window in its ballast-control room. The glass had been cleaned up. It did not seem serious.”** Immediately afterward the audience learns none of the 84 workers would survive. The story then progresses through storm preparation, an exposed steel cover, wet electric controls, apparently misunderstood manual rods, forward flooding, a Mayday, the only evidenced launched lifeboat, and a rescue vessel unable to recover men in the storm. Closing statement **“The first warning, though, had sounded almost ordinary.”** returns to the first radio assessment with changed significance. **05m — early warning, reversible mistake, fatal second failure.** Opening questions: **“How could one broken window bring down an enormous offshore drilling rig? Why did the crew lose control of the very system designed to keep it afloat? And when rescue ships arrived, why did nobody make it home?”** Intro quickly identifies workers, semi-submersible and impending storm. Main hook after context: **“Then something happened that made the problem far more dangerous than a wet floor.”** Stakes change: apparently cleaned-up window → shocks and uncontrolled valves → hand rods apparently reverse intended action → growing bow flood → desperate evacuation → a lifeboat capsizes despite a standby ship → official findings on design, testing and rescue. Payoff is not one blameworthy worker: **“The storm did not create each of those weaknesses. It exposed them at the same time.”** Weak point: first half contains an explanatory section about tanks and controls; without vivid images the independent listener may tire before the rod reversal. Last two sentences restate inquiry rather than return to a specific person; consider alternative only if actual reviewer finds them flat. Documentary Long intro follows questions→meaningful context→main hook, not fake one-minute padding.

**10m — reconstruct where each protection failed.** Opening questions: **“What does it take to sink a drilling platform built for the North Atlantic? Could a broken window really start the process?”** Extra information: precise field/crew connection, the reason for draft-mark readings, storm preparation decision, neighbouring rigs as independent radio sources, differentiation between controlling valves and knowing their state, chain-locker flooding, mechanics of rescue transfer, regional consequences and post-inquiry changes. Causal turn: **“An attempt to shut off water could admit more of it.”** Payoff reaches its core at **“No one on board survived. All eighty-four workers were lost”** followed by investigation of independent safety barriers. Final idea: **“The Ocean Ranger story is the distance between a safety feature being installed and a person actually being protected by it.”** Defect: after that main loss, the five-paragraph debrief repeats the short version's core proposition (cover, manual control, lifeboat), approaching an extended safety lecture. The extra evidence is real but not obviously worth twice the viewing time; long documentary saturation is high.
