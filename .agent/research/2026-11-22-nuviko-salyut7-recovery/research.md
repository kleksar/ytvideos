## Finalist one-page pitches, objections and decision

**Salyut 7:** Why stop: a stranded *empty* station has to be boarded, although no radio and no powered docking support exist. First spoken image: "How do you dock with a station that cannot answer your radio?" Main change after hook: manual docking is achieved, but the crew find a frozen station, dead batteries and solar arrays that cannot aim without battery power; Soyuz breaks the deadlock, then thawed water threatens the repairs. Payoff: attitude control June 13 and a real Progress cargo ship June 23, followed by renewed orbital work. Strong objection: extensive existing coverage (2017 Curious Droid, 2023 Zem TV, 2025 Russian narration); no proven incremental Nuviko demand. Selected narrowly for a documented chain with a second-half that changes the opening promise; not because "rare" or easy to illustrate.

**USS Squalus:** Why stop: 33 people trapped below 240 feet of water, with a chamber lowered to their hatch. Opening: rescue chamber descends to a submarine where 33 people survived the flooding. Development: 25 rescued in three trips; fourth cable trouble, last people still waiting; fifth dive checks the flooded aft compartment. Payoff: 33 survivors saved, 26 did not survive the initial disaster. Source: [US Navy ship history](https://www.history.navy.mil/research/histories/ship-histories/danfs/s/squalus-ss-192.html), [1939 Commander Momsen lecture](https://www.history.navy.mil/research/histories/ship-histories/danfs/s/squalus-ss-192/squalus-ss-192-sinking-rescure-of-survivors-and-salvage/lecture_squalus_rescue.html), [official original rescue report](https://www.history.navy.mil/research/histories/ship-histories/danfs/s/squalus-ss-192/squalus-ss-192-sinking-rescure-of-survivors-and-salvage/rescue_ops_report.html). Strong objection: after the chamber reaches the hatch, the main action repeats trips; strong final cable crisis, but less editorially diverse than Salyut. It remains viable, NOT rejected.

**Soyuz 11:** Why stop: three astronauts complete a station mission, capsule lands normally, rescuers discover all three dead. Development: re-entry pressure-valve failure and later pressure suit redesign. Payoff: not just disaster, concrete changes to crew protection. Sources: [NASA 50 years later](https://www.nasa.gov/history/50-years-ago-remembering-the-crew-of-soyuz-11/), [NASA Mir Hardware Heritage overview](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/history/SP-4225/documentation/mhh/mirhh-part2.pdf) p.64. Objection: its most powerful reveal occurs at the end, with limited agency during the fatal failure; risk of emotionally manipulative delayed answer. Not promoted here.

## Salyut 7 primary factual evidence (actual opened/read)

1. [NASA, *Mir Hardware Heritage*, Part 2](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/history/SP-4225/documentation/mhh/mirhh-part2.pdf), printed **pp. 98–100**, inspected page images p.99/p.100 on 2026-11-22. P.98-99: final crew left Oct 2 1984; in February 1985 communication abruptly stopped; March 2 Pravda said mission finished/deactivated; recovery decision by end March; crew Dzhanibekov/Savinykh launched June 6; manual laser-aided approach to slowly rolling Salyut, visual exterior check, first Soyuz dock with inactive station **June 8**; electrical connections dead, breathable very cold air, frost. P.100: all **8** batteries dead, **2** destroyed; failed solar-pointing sensor prevented recharge; Soyuz used to orient station into sunlight; **June 10** air heaters, **June 13** attitude control restored; wall heaters delayed to avoid frost-water hitting equipment; water tanks thawed end June, humidity end July; television light used to heat fluids because water heater frozen/damaged; **Progress 24 docked June 23**, carrying three batteries, water heater, solar extensions and other spares; **August 2** EVA with added panel sections; Savinykh remained while Dzhanibekov later departed.
2. [Sven Grahn, 1985 radio-monitor's firsthand account, *Salvaging Salyut 7*](https://planet4589.org/space/archive/sven/trackind/Salyut7/SalvaSal7.htm). Observed retransmission, reported docking June 8 and amateur detection of voice signals on June 12; good independent contemporary corroboration of communication restoration, but NOT proof of an exact moment/motive or of private dialogue. Record as recollection.
3. [Spacefacts, Soyuz T-13 mission](https://spacefacts.de/mission/english/soyuz-t13.htm), secondary mission chronology: June 6 launch, June 8 manual docking, optical instrument and panel orientation. Source used to corroborate chronology, not rights to use images.

[Curious Droid's own site, 2017 feature and narration](https://curious-droid.com/262/salyut-7-forgotten-rescue-dead-space-station/), **2017-06-30**, secondary; illustrates significant already-covered story and potential cinematic/myth conflation. ## Reason to watch and substantive beats after hook

**Promise:** the rescue is *not* the docking; viewers see why entering, charging and thawing are separate obstacles. Hook specific first action: an unresponsive station slowly rolls and the Soyuz crew must measure distance and match rotation manually. After hook: (1) docking reveals no electricity; (2) air check/frost; (3) failed sensor creates orientation/charging deadlock; (4) visiting Soyuz breaks loop; (5) melting frost creates a new hazard; (6) real Progress dock proves station is functional again. Payoff is observable change from dead object to receiving a cargo ship.

**Strongest objection:** audience has seen "dead station rescued" before; a poor script repeats the same miracle in three ways. The differentiator is verified sequence *after* docking; must resist empty exposition. If selected five-minute editor review fails, decline without padding.
