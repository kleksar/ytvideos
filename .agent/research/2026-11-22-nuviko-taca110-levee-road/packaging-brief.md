## Audience / central promise

English-language general history viewers who may not know the airline or the event. Promise: a passenger jet loses thrust in *both* engines, survives on a flood levee, then departs from an old wartime runway that had become a road. **Do not** depict a Boeing flying out of the same grassy levee without repairs, or a landing on an ordinary city street.

- **First narrated questions** (exact 05m beginning): “What do pilots do when both engines of a passenger jet go silent? How can they land on a place that was never built for airplanes? And even if everybody survives, how do they get the aircraft home?”
- **First tangible proof:** documentary FAA 1988 incident; hail strikes, both engines produce no usable thrust; grass levee beside canal near NASA Michoud. First image is a proposed reconstruction, never a claimed archival shot.
- **Payoff:** “The Boeing accelerated along that former runway. It lifted off and flew about seventeen minutes to New Orleans International Airport.” Emergency landing and planned ferry departure must not be combined into one imaginary flight.
- **Viewer's take-away:** relief for all 45 and an actual technical reason the engine testing/design later changed. Do not claim this proves routine landings on any field are safe.
