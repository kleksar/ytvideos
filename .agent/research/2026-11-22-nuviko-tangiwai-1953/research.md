## Sources read and fact ledger (primary or official institutional)

**T1 — Government chronology:** Manatū Taonga / New Zealand Ministry for Culture and Heritage, [“Wrong place at the wrong time”](https://nzhistory.govt.nz/culture/tangiwai-railway-disaster/wrong-place), accessed 2026-11-22, last noted update Aug 2026. 
- 24 Dec 1953: train departed Wellington ~15:00; crater lake rim burst **20:02**; lahar struck bridge between **22:10–22:15** and washed out pier 4; train passed Tangiwai station **22:20**; derailment **22:21**; Car Z fell ~**22:30**; main flood diminished ~**22:35**.
- Weather fine with little recent rain; goods train crossed at ~19:00; crater release ~2m cubic metres, six-metre wave; train ~65 km/h, engine and five second-class coaches fell; last three first-class coaches remained. Driver Charles Parker's emergency brakes appear to have been applied ~200m before bridge; investigators said braking prevented further carriages going in. NOT certain his action was caused by Ellis's torch.
- 1925 lahar had weakened bridge; amateur geologists raised crater-rim risks; 1954 inquiry no legal blame/prosecution, recommended upstream flood warning. 2007 lahar caused little damage/no injuries with warning system.

**T2 — Government rescue:** [“Search and rescue”](https://nzhistory.govt.nz/culture/responding-to-tragedy/tangiwai-1953), accessed 2026-11-22, updated 2024.
- Motorist **Cyril Ellis** found road bridge flooded, saw train lights, ran with torch. Guard **William Inglis** and Ellis entered hanging first-class **Car Z** to move people rearward; coupling broke, wagon went into river. Ellis/Inglis/passenger **John Holman** lifted people out through smashed windows. **21 of 22 passengers in Car Z survived**; human chain to bank after water fell. 
- **Arthur Bell** helped another coach, wife raised alarm; constable **Leo Smidt** reacted to unusual roar, organized early effort; farmers, forestry, engineering, navy, police, military from Waiouru. Main flood declined within ~45m but recovery continued; **20 bodies not recovered**. Awards: George Medal Ellis and Holman, British Empire Medal Inglis and Bell (script doesn't need titles).

**T3 — Government basic event:** [“Tangiwai railway disaster”](https://nzhistory.govt.nz/tangiwai-railway-disaster-0), 285 passengers/crew, **151 deaths**, train route and Christmas timing. Do not call all 285 passengers: includes crew.

**T4 — Government human stakes:** [“Death at Tangiwai: a class affair”](https://nzhistory.govt.nz/culture/tangiwai-disaster/class-affair), accessed 2026-11-22: **148 of 151 victims were travelling second class**, 28 of more than 170 second-class survived, last three first-class coaches relatively intact; **Barbara Mahy, 17, and brother John moved by guard from second to rear first class**, almost all original neighbors died. No invented thoughts/dialogues.

**T5 — Government aftermath:** [“Dealing with the dead”](https://nzhistory.govt.nz/culture/tangiwai-disaster/dealing-with-death), accessed 2026-11-22, updated 2026: prime minister Christmas radio announcement, Queen's sympathy, Prince Philip 31 Dec memorial for 21 unidentified victims, later corrections to identities. Avoid sensational bodily descriptions; no purported first-hand quotations.

**T6 — Government educational film/transcript:** [“Tangiwai rail disaster — roadside stories”](https://nzhistory.govt.nz/media/video/tangiwai-rail-disaster-roadside-stories); official textual transcript and notes *are available and read*, not claim video/archival sound playback. Copyright for archival audio explicitly reserved; NOT media source permission. One simplification in that government transcript implies Ellis's warning caused brake; our scripts and review avoid definitive causal attribution.

**T7 — Contested documentary counter:** [NZ On Screen, *The Truth About the Tangiwai Rail Disaster* (2002)](https://www.nzonscreen.com/videos/the-truth-about-the-tangiwai-rail-disaster/), description/limited excerpt metadata consulted, not entire film watched: documentary disputes whether Ellis could physically warn driver. This disagreement reinforces **UNVERIFIED causal link**; Ellis's running and emergency brake evidence must remain separate facts in every language/version.

**W1 — Wahine official counter:** [“Wahine wrecked in Wellington Harbour”](https://nzhistory.govt.nz/wahine-wrecked-wellington-harbour), NZ History: 10 Apr 1968, Barrett Reef, only 4/8 lifeboats could launch, severe storm and shore impact, 51 died immediately, two later for eventual **53**; avoid incorrect “53 died that day”.

**M1 — Morro Castle official counter:** US Naval History and Heritage Command, [*Tampa II* ship history](https://www.history.navy.mil/content/history/nhhc/research/histories/ship-histories/danfs/t/tampa-ii.html); Sep 1934 fire, Shark River surfboats rescue ~120 first, Tampa arrives later and participates, towline fails and fouls its propeller. More individual first-person rescue evidence needed for a full rival package.

**No cinematic inventions:** no made-up dialogue between Ellis and driver, exact timing of first visual torch signal, passengers' thoughts, hero's emotional states, quote from Crown/PM, or unverified death scenes. Official chronology yields 6–11 minutes between bridge strike and train; don't claim exact eleven. 2007 monitoring system gives comparison, not proof same magnitude/storm. Railway inquiry no individual legal blame is separate from earlier technical warnings.

## Story map / packaging promise

**Audience:** global English history/survival viewers. Explain “lahar” once through moving debris, “Car Z” as named passenger carriage. No assumption of New Zealand railroad knowledge.

**Promise:** A flood from a volcano destroyed a bridge without local rain; train's response was too late to stop the wreck, but different positions and decisions allowed one carriage to be rescued. What was later changed so a similar flood could be detected sooner?

**Short start/payoff/loop:** exact EN opening “A night express was already braking when a railway bridge gave way beneath it.” First visual scene needs accurate photoreal period locomotive and bridge with Nuviko 2D traveller only as external observer if present. Short full payoff includes 151 deaths, 21/22 Car Z surviving, 2007 early-warning success before final last→first semantic transition. EN final “And only minutes after the river struck the bridge, another sound came down the tracks.” → opening reintroduces the braking express; RU equivalent. Author two-cycle textual review only; actual AV seam UNVERIFIED.

**Long question-led open:** exact 05m: “Why did a passenger train fall into a river on a clear Christmas Eve? Why did some carriages stay on the rails while others disappeared? And what did one man with a torch manage to do when he saw the train coming?” Questions before immersion; first main hook when bridge loses support with train minutes away. All promises answered in narration.

**Verified cause→effect:**
crater rim break 20:02 (T1) → lahar travels down river despite clear local weather → pier washed 22:10–15 (T1) → train 22:21, brakes applied (T1) → leading second class coaches destroyed, rear 3 remain → Car Z hangs then falls 22:30 → Ellis/Inglis/Holman rescue 21/22 (T2) → total 151 deaths / disproportionate second class (T3/T4) → inquiry's 1925 damage, geologists' concern and warning recommendation (T1) → monitored 2007 lahar with little harm (T1). Do not infer Ellis's torch **caused** brakes (T7 contest).

**Strongest unresolved tension:** 151 deaths, including attempted warning — an independent listener may consider it overfamiliar, grim or insufficiently novel. Avoid triumphalist tone or portraying one carriage's rescue as “everyone survived.” 10m has extra confirmation of survivor movement and government response but less payoff per minute after 05m climax.
