| **Apollo 11 bag** | “A bag carried on Apollo 11 sold at a government auction for just nine hundred and ninety-five dollars.” White fabric / federal receipt | NASA proves lunar origin, then retains the bag; Kansas court says erroneous disposal cannot simply cancel good-faith purchase; Texas orders return | $1,812,500 Sotheby's sale on landing anniversary, government defeated by its own completed auction | Very easy to turn into dry legal summary; space memorabilia niche, many existing news clips and 2025 short |

| David Phillips / pudding miles | Engineer buys thousands of cheap pudding cups to earn airline miles | Finds mail-in UPC early bird rate, organizes collection of labels and donations, flights earned | About 1.25m miles, actual charity donation; concrete everyday arithmetic | Delightful central reversal is told in first 15 seconds, subsequent narrative can easily be a coupon calculation, not a full second act |

**Selection:** choose Moon bag. Against the very strong Selbee alternative: bag's physical proof creates a **second revelation after the expected scientific payoff**, and a court fight between the agency that identified the item and the buyer who lawfully paid for it. Both have a public outcome with contemporary evidence. Selbee may have **wider baseline appeal** and should not be labeled rejected; our winner is an **editorial hypothesis**, not a popularity claim. Versus Phillips, moon bag has more credible development after the fun purchase fact. Strongest counterargument to the choice: many viewers may not care who legally owns a museum artifact, and a five-minute explanation may lose them once the price and identity are revealed.

## Verified historical evidence (read content, not only result snippets)

- **S1 — U.S. District Court, District of Kansas, *United States v. Ary*, 224 F. Supp. 3d 1186, decision 2016-12-14.** [Full opinion](https://openjurist.org/224/fsupp3d/1186/united-states-v-ary). Paragraphs 4–7: curator's offenses, seized property and recorded bag part number; ¶¶9–14: forfeiture and inventory mix-up; ¶¶22–29: NASA apparently owned bag before forfeiture but was not given adequate notice, no proof buyer was in bad faith, court does not find power to unwind completed sale. **Critical limitation:** Kansas court **did not** itself issue the final order directing physical return from NASA in Texas. Source type: primary court record, not narrative transcript.
- **S2 — Sotheby's 2017 lot 102, [catalogue](https://www.sothebys.com/en/auctions/ecatalogue/2017/space-exploration-n09759/lot.102.html).** Lines under description/provenance: outer decontamination bag, Apollo 11, white Beta-cloth/polyester, brass zipper, 12 × 8.5 in, printed part/serial number, remnants of lunar dust; $2–4m is *estimate*, not actual hammer. Source type: auctioneer's first-party listing; promotional words about absolute rarity not used as scientific fact.
- **S3 — collectSPACE, 2017-07-20, [post-sale report](https://www.collectspace.com/news/news-072017b-sothebys-space-auction-moon-bag.html).** 1969 mission role (contingency sample protection), uncertain museum custody path, bag mixed with Apollo 17 inventory; earlier attempts to sell; Carlson's Feb 2015 $995 purchase; 2017 sale **$1,812,500 including buyer's premium**, hammer $1.5m, 48 years since landing. Source type: specialist contemporary reporting including sale outcome.

## Causal story map / promise

**Question:** how could NASA lose ownership of an Apollo 11 bag through a federal auction, and why couldn't it simply retrieve it after testing?

**Stops scroll:** dramatic gap between Apollo 11 and $995, physically recognizable labeled bag/receipt. **Keeps:** laboratory proof creates apparent happy ending, but NASA retains bag and buyer goes to court. **Gives:** lawful buyer protected against government reversal; Texas handover and $1.8125m recorded resale. Not a generic “rare thing worth lots.”

| Beat | Before → evidence → change → next question |
|---|---|
| 1969 | Emergency lunar sample protected by outer bag (S2–S3) → historically genuine object → how did records disappear? |
| custody | Curator/forfeiture, two similar bags confused, route into curator possession not proven (S1/S3) → government mistake → how did buyer get it? |
| 2015 | After failed offers Carlson pays $995, requests NASA test (S1/S3) → returned scientific proof → agency returns? |
| NASA | NASA identifies and retains bag, offers reimbursement (S1/S3) → property conflict → whose paperwork governs? |
| Kansas | Proper notice to NASA absent, but bona fide purchaser is protected; judge declines to rescind (S1) → decision doesn't resolve Texas possession → will she get the bag? |
| Texas | 2017 physical return order (S4) → she possesses it → what becomes of historical object? |
| Sotheby's | 2017-07-20 new sale $1,812,500 with fees (S3/S6) → complete payoff → why original receipt matters more than huge number |

**Best objection / guardrail:** this is a legal rather than adventure story; use concrete objects, test, refusal, receipt, and clear decisions instead of legal procedural citations in spoken text. Verified facts and tested provenance do not prove audience interest. This is not an argument that every NASA topic works.
