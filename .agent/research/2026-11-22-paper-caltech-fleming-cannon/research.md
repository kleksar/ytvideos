## Finalist A — Caltech cannon: exact audience promise and sources

**Why stop:** the campus guards accepted papers for a *cannon*, not a package. Cold opening in 02m: “At half past five in the morning, security guards stopped a crew moving a cannon off a college campus. The crew showed work orders. The orders were fake.”

**Why stay:** suspicion points to the wrong college (1986 Harvey Mudd precedent); reveal arrives nine days later across America; elaborate engineering prop escalates; then Caltech organizes its own cross-country recovery, where police ask the owners for evidence, and the last object reverses the scale.

**Payoff:** Caltech brings the large cannon home, MIT keeps the giant ring, Caltech leaves a miniature cannon under glass. Ring subsequently enters MIT Museum. Core reversal is not merely a stolen cannon: both campuses finish the joke with different-size objects.

**Strongest objection:** almost no directly comparable YouTube evidence; highly recognizable institutions are not enough to predict audience demand. Intro with 2005 retaliation might feel insular without early visual evidence. Ten minutes risks sounding like a chronology of campus logistics rather than an escalating prank.

## Decision

Cannon over Ankrom: the **second action** (Caltech physically recovering the stolen object) creates a new problem and new ironic answer *after* the MIT reveal, whereas the sign story peaks at the moment official workers leave a forgery in place. Ankrom may have the more general topic but weaker documented additional plot. Cannon over Dreadnought: two self-contained campus choices, opposite directions, tangible scale reversal and unusually strong institutional sources. Weak point of winner: interest outside alumni and technology-prank communities remains experimental.
