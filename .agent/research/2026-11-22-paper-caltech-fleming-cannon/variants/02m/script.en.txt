At five-thirty in the morning, guards at Caltech challenged a crew moving an antique cannon off campus. The movers showed work orders. The orders were fake.

It was March 2006. The cannon was a landmark of Fleming House, a student residence in California. Twenty years earlier, students at a nearby college had taken it as a prank and later returned it. So when it vanished again, Caltech first suspected the same neighbours.

The trail led much farther away.

Nine days later, the cannon appeared at MIT in Massachusetts, about three thousand miles from Caltech. It was pointed toward California, with an enormous gold-plated copy of MIT's class ring around its barrel.

A year before, Caltech students had targeted MIT's welcome event for prospective students. They handed out shirts with MIT on the front and a joke mocking the school on the back. The cannon appeared during that event the following year. It was a very large answer to a small shirt.

Caltech's students wanted their landmark back. Twenty-three students and seven alumni flew east. They arrived with a truck.

Then MIT campus police demanded proof that the visitors were entitled to remove the cannon. The people reclaiming it now had to show they had the right to take it.

They managed to load it. MIT kept the giant ring. Caltech left a tiny model cannon under glass in the empty spot.

The real cannon went back to California. The toy remained at MIT. But in Massachusetts, the people returning it had to justify taking it away. In California, those who removed it had faced a much easier test.
