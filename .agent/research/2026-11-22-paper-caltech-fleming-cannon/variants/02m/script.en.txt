At half past five in the morning, security guards stopped a crew moving a cannon off a college campus. The crew showed work orders. The orders were fake.

This was Caltech in California, March 2006. The cannon belonged to a student house, and it had vanished once before. Twenty years earlier, students from a nearby college had taken it as a prank.

So when it disappeared again, Caltech initially suspected the same neighbours.

They had the wrong school. This time, the people behind the move were students from MIT, on the other side of America.

Why go that far for a cannon? A year earlier, Caltech students had visited MIT and given prospective students shirts that looked like ordinary MIT souvenirs. On the back was a joke at MIT's expense.

MIT's reply was much heavier.

Nine days after the cannon vanished, it appeared in front of an MIT building, three thousand miles away. Around its barrel was an enormous gold-plated version of MIT's class ring. The cannon was even pointed back toward California.

Caltech's students flew east. With alumni helping, a group of thirty came to collect it. MIT students were waiting, but instead of another theft, there was a send-off.

Caltech took the cannon home. MIT kept the giant ring. In its place, the visitors left a tiny toy cannon under glass.

The original had travelled across a continent because a crew showed up before sunrise looking exactly like people with permission to move it.
