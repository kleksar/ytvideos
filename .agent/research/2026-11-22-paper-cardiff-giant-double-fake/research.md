**Evidence chain (confirmed event → change → consequence):**
- Hull commissions gypsum likeness, works and stains surface → object that can pass as old for spectators (Ksepka / Live Science)
- Associates transport and bury figure in 1868 → hired diggers uncover a real buried object in 1869 (Ksepka / Smithsonian)
- Newell erects tent and charges → audience and investors turn discovery into travelling attraction (Ksepka / Smithsonian)
- Scientists Marsh and others publicly challenge authenticity → skeptical viewers have reason to doubt; do not assert every spectator was fooled (Live Science / Smithsonian)
- Barnum fails to buy stake and makes copy → original exhibitors face rival claiming the same attraction (Ksepka / Smithsonian)
- Investors seek injunction; it fails → continuing double-fake rivalry; no invented direct judicial quotation (Live Science / Smithsonian)
- carvers' story and Hull's role public → no ancient giant; object later becomes museum exhibit (Live Science / Ksepka)
