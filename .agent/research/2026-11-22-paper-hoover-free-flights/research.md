### Three genuine competing pitches

**Hoover — chosen:** Opening: “A vacuum cleaner in Britain cost £119. The two ‘free’ return flights offered with it could cost Hoover more than £500.” First frame paper shop price tag and flight tickets. Retention reason: forms and complaints, abrupt rescue intervention and actual booked flights after promise exceeds product price. Payoff: £48m reported campaign cost, 220,000 **flown OR booked**, many satisfied bookings while others disputed terms. Main objection: business exposition, old frequently retold subject and no demonstrated Paper Detour demand.

**Pudding Guy — strongest rival:** First frame: a buyer surrounds himself with inexpensive pudding pots, planning to acquire airline miles rather than dessert. Stay for shopping/documentation/donation, finish with claimed miles. Broadly corroborated by [Value Tactics](https://www.valuetactics.com/tag/the-pudding-guy/) and travel reporting. Strong comedy and possibly stronger two-minute story, but exact original offer terms and fresh 10-minute causal progression not fully verified here; do not invent detail.

**Taco Liberty Bell:** First frame: full-page newspaper ad claiming a fast-food chain bought the Liberty Bell. Stay for callers and official response; payoff April Fools reveal plus real $50k preservation donation. Read contemporary [UPI 1996-04-01](https://www.upi.com/Archives/1996/04/01/Taco-Bell-stages-April-Fools-hoax/8400828334800/) and [Washington Post 1996-04-02](https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/business/1996/04/02/a-joke-that-rang-true/748a6749-53d7-4952-ba23-e3a5f8daceca/). Objection: reveal too early, likely padding beyond Short.

**Decision before writing:** Hoover earns the 5-minute version through real later acts—demand, paperwork, protest, rescue plan, money, qualified outcome—not only a clever premise. It wins over Pudding for documented middle and over Taco for multi-stage human/financial consequences. The rival could outperform in Short; no guaranteed winner/CTR.
