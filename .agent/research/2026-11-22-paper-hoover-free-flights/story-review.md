## Story map, original opening and answer

**02m Short.** Hook **“A vacuum cleaner in Britain cost £119. The two ‘free’ return flights offered with it could cost Hoover more than £500.”** Immediate price/reward contradiction. Beats: 1992 offer → cheapest eligible appliance → paperwork → complaint campaign → Maytag intervention and flight purchasing → report £48m, 220k **flown OR booked** → exception for dissatisfied claimants → shelf price still embodies mismatch. Final words “Every customer could see it on the original shop label:” lead literally into the first £119 sentence. **Negative loop test:** there is an actual causal callback/linguistic completion rather than merely repeating the topic, and payoff already delivered; but ending may feel like an engineered lead-in on repeated full speech. Cold start stands alone; two full spoken cycles and actual AV seam **UNVERIFIED**. Weakest moment: years of response and complaints compressed into a list, little room for any individual decision; not yet a strong independent Short.

**05m Long.** Three opening questions ask why tickets were offered, why shoppers bought unwanted appliances and how promotion cost tens of millions; contextual Hoover/1992 shop scene follows. Main hook/raise: **“The giveaway had a catch, although not a hidden one.”** Forms and dates first change the audience's expected outcome; complaints → public parliamentary complaints → Maytag reserve → purchase/charter flights → £48m → 220k bookings and still disputes. Payoff **“Hoover did not promise flights and give absolutely none. Many people received or were booked for travel.”** Last action/interpretation returns to **£119 shop label with tickets** as the cause of both demand and expense. Main weak point: public complaints passage slightly formal; a fresh listener may stop there despite new stakes. Long intro questions are answered and main hook follows real set-up, not the first word.

**10m Long.** Three questions: how an appliance shop became an airline booking operation; whether claimants completed the process; who bore the disputed costs. Context: British product market, offers, European then US expansion. Hook after explanation: **“The journey might be worth more to a customer than the machine that supposedly justified it.”** Beats: asymmetric reward, expected applications vs actual; paperwork and reported “wising”; identified complaint sample; Maytag reserve/executive shake-up; 2,200-flight/charter plan; Harry Cichy complaints; 1994 parliamentary case allegation; £48m and **flown OR booked**; careful Candy 1995 non-causal warning; BBC 2004 retrospective catalogue; return to shop price. Payoff already achieved at the £48m/220k reveal. After it, sale/corporate/legacy sections reduce momentum. 10m adds real detail but less incremental attention. **REWORK**, no automatic long production.
