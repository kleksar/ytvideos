## One audience promise

In 2004, a real invoice described an alleged Mark Rothko as passing through a private Swiss collection, for **$8.3m**. Federal prosecutors later established that the purported Swiss owner **never existed**. The story follows how the forged ownership trail helped a source in Queens become a multimillion-dollar artwork. Audience value is not “fakes are bad,” but the concrete distinction between a convincing painting, a prestigious seller, and independent proof of origin.

**Counter-signal:** this case already appears in *Made You Look* (2020) and extensively in press. Broad interest/format fit to Paper Detour and any current view trend remain unverified.

## Claim ledger: verified statements and limits

1. [DOJ SDNY guilty plea, 2013-09-16](https://www.justice.gov/archive/usao/nys/pressreleases/September13/RosalesGlafiraPleaPR.php): Rosales admitted participating in a scheme involving **more than 60 fakes** sold to two galleries for **~$33.2m**, later sold by those galleries to victims for **more than $80m**. **Do not add the two amounts** as separate losses. 1994–2009, Pollock/Rothko/Motherwell, Queens painter, false signatures and simulated aging. Swiss client nonexistent; Spanish supposed collector never owned paintings.
2. [De Sole v. Knoedler 2015 judicial opinion (transaction evidence)](https://cases.laws.com/nysd/7316330/): 2004 invoice for alleged 1956 Rothko, price **$8.3m**, private Swiss collection provenance, promise of supplement to scholarly catalogue. Invoice/source proves what buyers were told, not authenticity.
3. [The Art Newspaper, 2016-03-01, trial testimony](https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2016/03/01/parties-settle-in-knoedler-case-as-court-hears-fakes-helped-keep-business-afloat): 1994 doubts from Diebenkorn-associated expert; **2003 IFAR unable to authenticate** purported Pollock and questioned provenance; 2008 conservator found materials in two purported Motherwells **inconsistent with claimed dates**. **Not all fakes were lab-tested.** 2016 buyers' suit settled; Knoedler/former director denied knowingly selling fakes. Evidence of warnings is not a criminal finding of their intent.
4. [DOJ SDNY arrest announcement, 2013-05-21](https://www.justice.gov/archive/usao/nys/pressreleases/May13/RosalesGlafiraArrestPR.php): tax return and undisclosed foreign account charges **at the time allegations**; September plea (source 1) establishes her subsequent admission.
5. [ArtNet news, 2017-01-31](https://news.artnet.com/art-world/glafira-rosales-knoedler-forgery-probation-840923): actual sentencing house arrest/probation (10m version only). Avoid repeating 2013 DOJ's then-future proposed sentence as completed.
6. [ArtNet 2012 contemporaneous gallery account](https://www.artnet.com/magazineus/features/grant/the-knoedler-mess-4-4-12.asp): Knoedler closed in **2011**; management disputed bad-faith allegations and asserted separate business motives. Do not invent a court ruling about closure causality.

**Factual event → next consequence:** invoice establishes claimed origin → 60 surprise works demand explanation → maker and aging mechanism undercut signatures → IFAR/material discrepancies undercut chronology → hidden proceeds make claimed owner testable → actual 2013 plea states owner never existed → civil settlement distinguishes responsibility from allegation.

No invented direct participant quotes, emotions, losses beyond sourced figures, secret meetings, exactly dated discovery beyond sources, or retrospective conviction of gallery management.
