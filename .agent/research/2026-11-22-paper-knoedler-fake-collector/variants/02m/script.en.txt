Could this really be a lost Rothko? A New York gallery helped turn that question into a sale worth millions.

The painting was supposed to come from a private collection. The owner, according to the dealer who brought it in, wanted to remain anonymous.

That made the work sound mysterious. It also made its history difficult to check.

From 1994 to 2009, dealer Glafira Rosales offered New York galleries more than sixty previously unknown works attributed to celebrated artists, including Mark Rothko and Jackson Pollock.

The galleries sold the works to buyers for more than eighty million dollars.

But the real painter was working in a home in Queens. Others added false signatures and treated canvases to make them look older.

And the collector in Switzerland? Federal investigators found that person never existed. Another supposed collector in Spain had never owned the paintings.

There were warning signs. Experts questioned the history of a supposed Pollock, and tests found materials inconsistent with the dates claimed for other works.

The long-established Knoedler gallery closed in 2011. Rosales pleaded guilty in 2013 to taking part in the fraud and to related financial crimes. The gallery and its former director denied knowingly selling fakes; later civil claims were settled.

So the trick wasn't simply to paint something convincing. It was to give that canvas an owner, a past, and a famous name.
