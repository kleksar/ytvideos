Do I carry this sock straight to the bedroom, or fetch a basket first?

It is on my living-room floor, and its drawer is in the bedroom.

Those are my two methods. A means carrying every object home, one at a time. B means collecting everything in one basket and visiting each destination once. Before choosing, I count only corridor steps. This is a model on a drawn floor plan, not a real cleaning-speed test.

The bedroom is eight steps from my living room. The basket sits four steps in the opposite direction. I start and finish in the living room, and I put the empty basket back.

Pick A or B.

For one sock, A is simple: eight steps to the bedroom, eight back. Sixteen.

With B, I first walk four steps to fetch the basket and four back. I still walk sixteen steps to deliver the sock. Then I return the basket: another eight steps. Thirty-two. The basket doubles my walking.

Now I find seven more misplaced things beside that sock. Four belong in the bedroom; four belong in the kitchen, five steps down the same hall. One method, same eight objects, same finishing condition.

A sends me on four kitchen round trips and four bedroom round trips: 104 corridor steps.

B fetches the basket, collects all eight, walks past the kitchen to the bedroom, returns, and puts the basket away. Thirty-two steps.

The method that lost with one sock now saves seventy-two steps.

That doesn't prove it saves time. Picking up a basket adds handling, and I haven't measured a second. But when the floor fills up, grouping trips matters. So before I carry the next thing, I check how many more are waiting.
