## Model: fully specified fixed initial state and scoring

- Forty physical socks, twenty exact pairs `P01` … `P20`; every ID appears **twice**. Within-pair mark is ID, not visible from color alone. P01–P05 red, P06–P10 blue, P11–P15 yellow, P16–P20 green.
- Both methods start from this same written left-to-right order; all symbols are just anonymous item labels, **not commercial brands or uploaded visual assets**:

```text
P20 P01 P14 P18 P10 P10 P17 P18 P08 P09 P03 P13 P15 P04 P16 P03 P16 P12 P14 P11
P05 P09 P07 P05 P06 P11 P04 P15 P12 P19 P07 P06 P01 P20 P02 P02 P13 P08 P17 P19
```

- **Measure: number of identity looks.** One look when examining the first sock to identify it; one per candidate partner inspected; grouping charges one look per sock to determine its color. This is an abstract count, not seconds, eye movements, skill or measured working time. Handling/repositioning separately excluded.
- **A (direct):** inspect leftmost sock and compare to following items left→right until finding its exact mate; remove both and preserve relative order of all remaining socks; repeat until empty.
- **B (color sort):** inspect all 40 once, move each into a color group preserving arrival order within each group, then run the same direct matching algorithm independently for the groups. Group order does not change total count. For first-pair comparison B begins matching with first-encountered group (green).
- Tie-breaking deterministic: no rescans/free comparison, no visual memory shortcuts, no intervention, no elapsed-time claim.
- Hand-verified with independent local Python function implementing these rules; mathematical author check only, not real-life experiment and not independent reviewer.

| Scenario and endpoint | A direct looks | B sorted looks | Winner, with reason |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| All 20 colorful pairs finished | 241 (=20 anchor +221 candidate) | 120 (=40 color +20 anchor +60 candidate) | B by 121 |
| First complete pair only | 34 | 48 (=40 group +8 first pair) | A by 14 |
| First 4 numbered pairs P01…P04, original filtered order | 13 | 21 | A by 8 |
| First 8 numbered pairs | 46 | 46 | tie |
| First 10 numbered pairs | 60 | 56 | B by 4 |
| All 20 pairs recolored black with identity marks intact | 241 | 281 (=40 color +241 pairing) | A by 40 |
| Twenty pairs split into 2 useful color groups | 241 | 171 | B by 70 |
| Twenty pairs into 20 pair-specific colors | 241 | 80 | B by 161 |
| Twenty pairs arranged adjacent in identity order (same 4 colors) | 40 | 80 | A by 40 |

For original colorful 4 groups, candidate comparisons red=14, blue=12, yellow=19, green=15, sum=60. For direct method first two pair searches compare 33 and 31 candidates, total **64** (plus 2 first socks), not 66.

**Sensitivity / different measurement:** B requires 40 extra placements into color buckets relative to A (the moves of finished pairs common to both). If each saved look were 0.5 hypothetical seconds, the 121-look saving is 60.5s; at 0.5s per extra placement B nets +40.5s; at 2s per extra placement B loses 19.5s. **Neither assumption was measured**. Showing a winner in look-count is not showing a winner in elapsed time.

## Cause → change → payoff and constraints

Same starting mixed pile → choose A/B before result; A repeatedly searches entire remainder (241) → B invests 40 upfront, then reduces candidates through useful color partitions (120) → goal shifts to one urgent pair (A wins) → color loses discriminatory power/all black (A wins full task) → final: break-even depends on task, starting layout, metric and group quality.

The color-to-single-black experiment is **a new controlled input/scenario**, not the same source condition silently changed mid-A/B; both A and B reset to the same modified start for that comparison. No real-life claim about skin, hygiene, jobs, missing socks or real washing outcome; no invented dialogue or physical experiment.
