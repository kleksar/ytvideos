Should I sort forty socks by color before pairing them, or just grab one and look for its match?

I have twenty different pairs on the bed. Five pairs are red, five blue, five yellow, and five green. Each pair has its own little mark. The socks start in exactly the same mixed order for both methods.

This isn't a filmed speed test. I'm using a repeatable model that counts each time I have to look at a sock to identify it.

Method A is the impatient one. I pick up the first sock and scan the remaining pile until I find its twin. Then I repeat. My first sock makes me check thirty-three others. The next makes me check thirty-one. Across the whole pile, including picking up each first sock, that is two hundred and forty-one looks.

Method B feels slower at first. I look at all forty socks to sort them into four color groups. Then I match the little marks inside each group. I count every sorting look and every matching look. Total: one hundred and twenty.

Sorting saves one hundred and twenty-one looks. But here's the catch.

If I need just one pair right now, A reaches the first match in thirty-four looks. B needs forty-eight. And if all forty socks are black, with the same twenty tiny pair marks, color sorting helps nothing. A stays at two hundred and forty-one; B rises to two hundred and eighty-one.

So this model doesn't say sorting is always faster. It says a useful color split can save a lot of searching when I have a whole pile. Real hands and real seconds still need testing.
