Can saying a message out loud actually take longer than typing it?

I have a sixty-word update to send before I leave. It includes a meeting place, a time, and a name. On the same phone, I can type every word, or dictate exactly the same message.

Pick A for my thumbs, B for the microphone. The score is not how fast words appear. It's how long until the message is correct and ready to send.

I'm using a model, not claiming I timed a real phone. In this example, my keyboard produces finished text at fifty words a minute. Sixty words take seventy-two seconds.

For B, I assume speech goes in at a hundred and fifty words a minute. That's only twenty-four seconds. Then I check and correct it for twenty more.

B is ready in forty-four seconds. The microphone wins by twenty-eight.

But here's the catch. Saying a draft isn't the same as sending a message.

I reset to the same sixty words, same phone and same typing speed. This time the dictated text takes fifty-five seconds to check and fix. B now takes seventy-nine seconds. A still takes seventy-two. Typing wins by seven.

Neither correction time is a measured result. They're two disclosed conditions in the model.

The dividing line is forty-eight seconds of correction. Below that, dictation wins this sixty-word race. Above it, typing wins.

And for a tiny twelve-word reply, even twenty seconds of checking is too much: typing takes about fourteen seconds; speaking and checking takes about twenty-five.

So the fastest way to enter words isn't always the fastest way to send them. The real result depends on message length and how much work the draft needs.
