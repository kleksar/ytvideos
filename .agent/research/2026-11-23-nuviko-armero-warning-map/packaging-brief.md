**Audience promise:** not a general “deadliest eruption” highlight. Explain why a scientifically anticipated mudflow killed a town far away, and what failed *between the map and the people*. Main question: how can a hazard map be right yet the evacuation fail?

## Honest title directions

1. **The Volcano Map Was Right. Armero Still Died.** — clear unsettling contradiction; “died” metaphor is emotionally heavy, not a claim that no one survived.
2. **The Warning That Didn't Save Armero (1985)** — short and concrete but could imply no warnings; prefer title 1 with nuance in video.
3. **How a Volcano Buried a Town Miles Away** — strongest physical mystery but omits map reversal.

Proposed winner: **The Volcano Map Was Right. Armero Still Died.** Do not suggest every resident died; actual description must clarify victims and survivors, not invent individual testimonies.

## Promise → opening → evidence → payoff

- **Text opening:** “What happens when a warning is scientifically right, but the people in its path still don't escape?” This asks a moral/causal question; voice must not imply a literal complete absence of warnings.
- **First visual events:** volcano distant beyond a winding valley, then a historically grounded hazard-map **recreation explicitly represented as a reconstruction**. Do not fake a contemporary map scan. The first scene must establish geography and the route before institutional process.
- **Mid-story mechanism:** hot material over glacier; water takes sediment and becomes lahar; guided by rivers; aerial-like reconstruction of a valley leading toward a lower settlement. Avoid gore, unsourced timing overlays and sensational victim reenactments.
- **Main payoff:** prior hazard map accurately indicated flow routes; the chain of understanding, decision, communication and evacuation failed. Must be stated before any outro.
