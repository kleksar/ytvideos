## Three actual finalists — competing promises

**Armero.** First spoken question: “How could a town be buried by volcanic mud when scientists had already mapped the danger?” After opening: months of unrest → incomplete warning chain → snow and ice melting → lahars follow valleys → Armero is hit → a map accurately predicted the route. Full payoff: scientific knowledge is insufficient without practiced decisions and public evacuation. Source backbone USGS 1993/2009, Pierson et al. 2014, Colombian Geological Service 2025. Why watch: the final revelation reframes the disaster. Why might it fail: very grim subject, risk of abstractions about bureaucratic mistakes and weak anglophone evidence; viewer deserves respectful human stakes, not outrage theatre.

**Loma Prieta / Cypress.** First spoken question: “How did a short earthquake collapse a double-deck freeway dozens of miles from its centre?” After opening: occupied freeway → upper deck crushes vehicles below → local responders search → engineers investigate shaking/structures → seismic retrofit. Payoff: one structure accounted for 42 of the area's 63 reported deaths; structural practices changed. Verified by [NIST overview](https://www.nist.gov/el/earthquake-loma-prieta-california-1989) and [USGS report](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/pp1552B); visual [USGS archive interview](https://www.usgs.gov/media/videos/initial-observations-cypress-structure-collapse-oakland-ca-1989). Strength: clear physical mechanism and eyewitness visuals. Objection: familiar earthquake disaster footage; technical collapse explanation may be too detailed at 10m. Note USGS public pages differ between 41 and 42, so use the original structural study if chosen.

**HMS Thetis.** First spoken question: “How did a submarine reach the surface, yet still leave ninety-nine people trapped?” Development: trials → flooded torpedo tube → stern lifted → escape attempts → broken salvage cable/atmosphere → four survivors; full payoff, why rescue failed. Direct [June 1939 UK Parliament statement](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/1939-06-05/debates/595afee3-d695-41dd-b1c5-91dcf79cb579/OralAnswersToQuestions), [Royal Museums Greenwich](https://www.rmg.co.uk/collections/objects/rmgc-object-67493), [US Navy analysis](https://www.history.navy.mil/research/library/online-reading-room/title-list-alphabetically/w/war-damage-reports/submarine-report-vol1-war-damage-report-no58.html). Objection: visual access and source complexity, many individuals and contradictory technical accounts; overlapping emotional territory with recent Nuviko submarine PR #373. Thetis is **not** rejected by owner.

**Why Armero over strongest alternative Thetis:** more meaningful reversal after hook — the hazard map was not disproved, it was not made actionable — as well as a directly understood general audience question. Loma Prieta has arguably stronger opening imagery and is the nearest alternative for click appeal. Most serious objection to Armero: tragic and painful, with no named safely verified personal narrative in this draft; it must carry engagement through observable mechanism and public decisions.

## Verified factual chain and constraints

1. **Before eruption:** activity from approx. 1984; instruments and monitoring, scientific hazard assessment, September 1985 minor eruption; maps published about one month before. [USGS Volcano Watch, 2009](https://www.usgs.gov/observatories/hvo/news/volcano-watch-lessons-learned-armero-colombia-tragedy), paragraphs beginning “The volcano first began…” and “A minor explosive eruption…”; [USGS 1993](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/preventing-volcanic-catastrophe-us-international-volcano-disaster-assistance-program), abstract sentences 2–4; [Pierson, Wood & Driedger (2014)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13617-014-0016-4), “Case study 5” (accurate hazard maps, only about one month to disseminate).
2. **Mechanism:** 13 Nov 1985 eruption under storm; pyroclastic material melted snow/glacier; water entrained debris, lahars routed along river valleys. The 1993 USGS article states 20 million m3 erupted material and possible valley flows 40m thick / 50km/h; use those values only with scope “in some valleys,” not at every point. On geography distinguish straight-line town–mountain 45 km [USGS 2009] from 74km route to crater [USGS 1993]; scripts say “tens of kilometres” to avoid mixing measures.
3. **Arrival:** HVO 2009 says ~2 hours; USGS 1993 says 2.5h. Narration uses “roughly two”/“two to two and a half” and does not claim a stopwatch-accurate countdown. Human evacuation is counterfactual uncertain; do **not** claim everyone would have survived. [USGS 1993](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/preventing-volcanic-catastrophe-us-international-volcano-disaster-assistance-program), final paragraph; [USGS 2009](https://www.usgs.gov/observatories/hvo/news/volcano-watch-lessons-learned-armero-colombia-tragedy).
4. **Warnings:** USGS 1993 confirms risk information reached emergency coordinators and at-risk locations had been identified; **not literally “no warning existed.”** Pierson 2014, “Case study 5,” identifies unclear decisions, late map dissemination, poor public risk communication, incomplete response plans; contextualize as cumulative failures rather than inventing an individual culprit or thoughts. No unsupported named dialogue.
5. **Casualties:** different estimates: Pierson 2014 approximately 21,000 in Armero; USGS 2009 about 23,000 in Armero and >25k total; [SGC news release 2025-11-12](https://www2.sgc.gov.co/Noticias/Paginas/El-SGC-estrena-el-documental-Armero-40-anos-con-ciencia-volcanica.aspx) approx. 25,000 overall. Chosen 05m says ~25,000 *across disaster*, long explicitly explains estimates; don't sum these counts.
6. **Geography beyond Armero:** SGC release 2025 describes Lahars down Azufrado, Gualí and Lagunillas and effects on Villamaría/Chinchiná via Nereidas, Claro and Chinchiná. Do not suggest only one valley/one town was affected.
7. **Rescue and aftermath:** scientific review Pierson 2014 “Hazard response and recovery planning” describes unstable muddy deposits, loss of roads/landmarks, slow extraction. SGC 2025 anniversary release describes modern monitoring and community-facing work; says it released a 52-minute documentary in Nov 2025. **Not used as image rights or an actual watched documentary.**
8. **General lahar warning practice:** Pierson 2014 review covers sensor detections, communication, emergency drills, and danger of treating infrastructure alone as an answer. Long contrasts methods without claiming they were actually installed at Armero.

## Causal beats for selected story

Mountainside snow + valleys → risk map and monitoring → inadequate decision/communication procedure → storm obscures eruption → hot debris melts ice → water picks up debris and runs down Lagunillas → Armero not broadly evacuated despite time window → lethal burial and difficult rescue → map's risk corridors were accurate → aftermath: monitoring **and public response** are inseparable. Where narration might lose attention: a second generic paragraph about communications before the event. Keep a concrete volcanic/mud action before repeating governance.

## 2026-11-23 scoped revision — verified new evidence for 10m only

New primary retrospective opened and read: [National Research Council, *The Eruption of Nevado Del Ruiz Volcano*, 1991, Chapter 4, pp. 49–53](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/1784/chapter/6) (accessed 2026-11-23). This is a post-event National Academies report, not a contemporary eyewitness diary. Evidence newly used only in revised 10m, EN/RU:

| Exact report location | Supported script claim | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Chapter 4, “Historical eruptions and experience,” pp.49–50 | A devastating 1845 lahar traversed what became the Armero site; a 1985 Colombian Civil Defense booklet did not include volcanic hazard | Does not prove any particular resident read or ignored the booklet |
| “Initial threat detection,” pp.50–51 | Unrest from late 1984; March 1985 specialist requested instruments, risk map, evacuation planning; first portable seismographs installed in July, daily quakes in August | Equipment presence is not a verified public warning or accurate eruption-time prediction |
| “Efforts to increase awareness” and subsequent map discussion, pp.51 onward | Preliminary risk map dated 7 October 1985; revised report/map dated 10 November; both indicated Armero at risk if a lahar occurred | Conditional hazard map does **not** specify date/time of the next eruption |
| “Initial threat detection,” p.51 | On 12 November summit visitors collecting gas samples observed no clear indications of imminent explosion | An observation with uncertainty, NOT a guarantee of safety; not a reason to blame those geologists |

Additional accessible corroboration: [USGS Volcano Watch, 2009](https://www.usgs.gov/observatories/hvo/news/volcano-watch-lessons-learned-armero-colombia-tragedy) on old deposits, the storm, delayed warning and approximately two hours; [Pierson et al., 2014, case study 5 / hazards](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13617-014-0016-4) on accurate maps with insufficient dissemination, deep unstable lahar deposits and communication limits. These sources were read as HTML; no PDF or audio was processed. Casualty denominators and travel time still have the old explicit ranges; no new invented quotation or victim motive.

**Before → after story map, 10m only:** originally instruments/map → storm → mud → destruction → map reveal → several rounds of generalized institutional lessons. Revised sequence: old 1845 mud in Armero's ground → 1985 everyday risk underrecognition → instruments and September eruption → **7 Oct map vs 10 Nov revised map (new concrete escalation)** → **12 Nov summit observation (new uncertainty obstacle)** → storm/eruption/ice melt → valley transport → **2–2.5 h without equivalent household warning** → destruction and materially difficult rescue → accurate-map reveal → one concise causal explanation of the incomplete warning chain. The final message no longer repeats hypothetical sensors, training and policy in separate essays. This material adds verified story decisions and timing uncertainty, not unrelated disaster facts. 02m and 05m untouched.
