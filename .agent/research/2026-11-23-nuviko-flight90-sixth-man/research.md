## Viewer reason and decision

Audience: English-language Nuviko History audience interested in verifiable human choices, technical causes and unexpected post-event evidence. Promise: why did a helicopter leave someone in the icy river, and why was that person's name still uncertain months later? A single crash statistic does not justify this video. The causal chain offers three changing situations: takeoff failure despite deicing → rescue from ice with one man missing → seemingly obvious identity overturned by eyewitness disagreements → combined evidence and official identification. The strongest objection is saturation of the Flight 90 story and risk of depicting disputed observations as a certainty. Avoid heroic thoughts, invented quoted dialogue, or a blanket claim that the man saved all five survivors unaided.

## Three finalist pitches, equal questions

**A — Air Florida Flight 90 (selected).** Opening: “Why did a rescue helicopter leave one passenger in the freezing Potomac?” First visual proposal: ice-covered river, Park Police helicopter above small section of wreckage; do not claim archival photographic recreation. After the intro: missed anti-icing, anomalous engine readings, bridge impact, six on surface/five survive, helicopter passes, Skutnik, eyewitness uncertainty, Coast Guard finding. Payoff: formal 1983 identification as Arland D. Williams Jr., with caution that some eyewitness descriptions disagreed. Biggest objection: well-covered tragedy and standard aviation-crash video saturation. This angle is about evidence and acts in the water, not just cockpit reenactment.

**B — I-35W bridge (strongest alternative).** Opening: “How could a bridge survive decades, then fail while repairs were underway?” NTSB source below states undersized gusset plates + increased structure weight + construction loads. Visible first frame: bridge midspan, heavy material staged, with no fictitious diagrams as historical footage. Progression: unseen design calculation problem → maintenance loads → collapse → NTSB isolates plates. Payoff: an old design error rather than a simple current-day construction mistake. Objection: technically dense bolt-and-plate explanation may outlast audience curiosity; another engineering documentary is already plausible. On independent review this may beat A for mechanisms; not owner-rejected engineering by category.

**C — Empress of Ireland.** Opening: “How could a large passenger ship sink in less than a quarter of an hour?” First visual: two hulls meeting in fog. Movement: navigational uncertainty → collision → rapid list → blocked evacuation → rescue and losses. Payoff: devastating speed and contrast with better-known Titanic. Objection: audience may know the outcome at title, and without close sourced individual choices the middle is mostly chronology. New coherent 10m require more primary survivor material than inspected here.

**Selection:** A has more independent changes to expectation and a documented identity inquiry after the primary disaster. B is competitive, not rejected; C needs more eyewitness documentation. The selection is an editorial experiment, not audience demand proven by numbers.

## Source-backed factual chain (opened/read, not just snippets)

1. **NTSB accident synopsis** — https://www.ntsb.gov/investigations/Pages/DCA82AA011.aspx (read 2026-11-23). “What happened”: 1982-01-13, 737-222, 74 passengers + five crew, collision with 14th Street Bridge at 16:01, four bridge deaths, four passenger + one crew surviving; “What we found”: no engine anti-ice, snow/ice on wing, continued takeoff after anomalous readings, prolonged post-deice ground delay, degraded wing leading-edge handling. Supports 79, 5, 4 bridge and cause. Do not confuse four on bridge with 74 plane deaths.
2. **FAA Transport Airplane Lessons Learned, N62AF** — https://www.faa.gov/lessons_learned/transport_airplane/accidents/N62AF (read 2026-11-23). Investigative narrative: engine pressure ratio sensing distorted by icing, misleading thrust, aerodynamic loss of lift, deicing delays, decision to continue and follow-up recommendations. Supports 5m/10m mechanism and why two separate degradations combined. FAA page's introductory crew-size wording is inconsistent with official NTSB 5; use NTSB count.
3. **Washington Post contemporary identification report**, 1983-06-06 (publication archive reads June 7 URL), https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/politics/1983/06/07/mystery-hero-of-air-crash-is-identified/d5cba0a5-e885-421b-9086-4df0405d7b97/ (read 2026-11-23). Contemporary account: six reached water, helicopter offered lines, five survivors, rescuer's disputed appearance, survivor Joseph Stiley differing recollection, Williams medical cause of death and Coast Guard conclusion, medal presented. Reports about five saved via various participants are not proof one man personally rescued five.
4. **Washington Post, Ken Ringle, Coast Guard methods**, 1983-06-06, https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/politics/1983/06/07/how-the-coast-guard-identified-williams-pinning-down-a-haunting-image/66c60ace-2d45-4542-a5de-2b3f411e98cb/ (read 2026-11-23). Coast Guard Commander D.C. Addison combined autopsies, recovery positions, photos, interviews; mother Virginia requested identification; official award determination. He stressed no single piece sufficient. Do not present as direct footage or all-witness consensus.
5. **UPI contemporary**, 1982-01-22, https://www.upi.com/Archives/1982/01/22/Medical-evidence-mounted-Friday-that-a-46-year-old-government-employee/3976380523600/ (read 2026-11-23). At the time of rescue, a helicopter observer could not positively identify photo with beard. Supports contemporary discrepancy, not present-day DNA proof.
6. **The Citadel Today / park rescue interview/photo**, https://today.citadel.edu/remembering-air-florida-flight-90-hero-arland-williams-jr-citadel-class-of-1957/ (read 2026-11-23). Late remembrance with Windsor account; secondary institutional corroboration of actions, not sole authoritative identity evidence.
7. **I-35W NTSB** — https://www.ntsb.gov/investigations/Pages/HWY07MH024.aspx (read 2026-11-23); 13 deceased, 145 injured; insufficient gusset plate capacity and concentrated construction loads.
8. **Canadian Museum of History** — https://www.historymuseum.ca/exhibitions/canadas-titanic-the-empress-of-ireland (read 2026-11-23); 1914 May 29 collision, sinking under 15 minutes, estimated 1,012 deceased.

### Cause/next question map

1. Snow delays and deicing → why is protection insufficient? [NTSB/FAA].
2. Engine anti-ice off, false thrust indication and iced wings → why continue? [NTSB/FAA].
3. Impact kills, six survivors to the surface → who will reach them? [NTSB/Post].
4. Helicopter lifeline passed to others, shore assistance; five saved → where is the sixth? [Post/UPI/Citadel].

6. Coast Guard compares medical, diver positions, witnesses → 1983 identification + medal. [1983 Post/UPI].
7. Aviation board publishes safety failures → separates preventable cause from post-event heroism, completes technical question. [NTSB/FAA].
