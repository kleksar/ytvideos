## Primary/source map for selected story

All source URLs were opened through public search/retrieval or their indexed full text; exact quoted report sections noted. Claims are **not** verified merely by a video title.

1. **US Navy War Damage Report No. 56**, `§2-4`, `§2-5`: https://www.history.navy.mil/research/library/online-reading-room/title-list-alphabetically/w/war-damage-reports/uss-franklin-cv-13-war-damage-report-no-56.html . It records two bomb hits 19 March 1945, 31 aircraft fueled/armed on flight deck and 22 in hangar, bombs and rockets fueling further explosions, fires for about ten hours, loss of firefighting systems and propulsion, tow before all ammunition stopped detonating, restoring power March 20. **Critical qualification**: `§2-5` says fire damage itself would not have sunk ship; watertight integrity and protected vital machinery survived. The report is primary institutional analysis, *not* omniscient testimony about personal thoughts.
2. **US Navy DANFS**, full chronology `Franklin III (CV-13)`: https://www.history.navy.mil/research/histories/ship-histories/danfs/f/franklin-v.html . Specific dates 19 March attack, Santa Fe rescue and transfer, Gary, O'Callahan, Pittsburgh tow to anchor chain, Japanese air attack during tow, steering and self-propulsion March 20, Ulithi March 24, New York April 28. The narrative describes earlier 30 October 1944 damage and later non-return to combat. Official DANFS says anchor chain connection about 10:04; the technical report says tow commenced about 14:00. **Do not conflate connection with beginning to move**.
3. **US Navy H-043-1**, historian Samuel Cox, 2020: https://www.history.navy.mil/about-us/leadership/director/directors-corner/h-grams/h-gram-043/h-043-1.html . Notes absence of torpedo strike below waterline and distinction from carriers sunk after torpedo damage. Death totals range widely across official or firsthand sources due to accounting. **Use “hundreds”**, not an invented single precise final number.
4. **Congressional Medal of Honor Society, Donald Gary**, original award citation: https://www.cmohs.org/recipients/donald-a-gary . Several hundred men in smoky mess room; Gary returns three times; firefighting; no. 3 fireroom raises boiler steam. Award dated 1946. This establishes actions, **not** their inner emotions.
5. **Congressional Medal of Honor Society, Joseph O'Callahan**, award citation: https://www.cmohs.org/recipients/joseph-t-ocallahan . Fire crews, care for wounded, ammunition disposal, flood magazine, cool armed bombs with hose; Medal of Honor 1946.
6. **US Navy historic image and summary collection**: https://www.history.navy.mil/browse-by-topic/ships/aircraft-carriers/uss-franklin.html . Ships and visual record, but source page availability does not establish rights to specific future image bytes.

### Evidence chain / story map

- Precondition: near enemy coast during active strike prep, aircraft fueled/armed `report §2-4, DANFS` → two enemy bombs at launch → ordnance explosion chain.
- Fire chain: fuel → secondary bombs/rockets → damaged water mains and smoke in machinery → propulsion stops `§2-4` → need evac + damage control simultaneously.
- Human action: Gary sees hundreds trapped → finds route → repeats three escorts → helps firefighting/steam `Gary citation`. O'Callahan cools live ordnance / coordinates magazine and hoses `O'Callahan citation`.
- Outside response: Santa Fe takes off survivors, destroyers rescue men from water `DANFS` → Pittsburgh tows by starboard anchor chain `DANFS, report` → air attack driven off → next-day steering and steam restored.
- Payoff: carrier proceeds under own power, Ulithi March 24, New York April 28 `DANFS` → no combat return; separate ship-survival and death-loss truths `DANFS, H-043-1`.
- Explanation: watertight integrity remained; distinction between fire disaster and torpedo damage, **NOT** “the ship was physically about to sink” `report §2-5`.

### Material uncertainties and corrections

- Death counts differ **even across Navy pages**: 724, 796, 807, more than 800, depending on inclusion methodology. Six scripts use “hundreds” and mourn losses, not a precise total. Do not place one definitive number on a cover.
- Explosions described as “over 120” in the Navy H-gram, but no exact count necessary for narration; avoid easy but disputed headline.
- Navy engineering report's statement “would not ... have caused the loss” applies to *damage alone* given hull integrity. It does **not** guarantee safe survival in the face of new attack or inability to recover power.
- “Voluntarily remained aboard” appears in institutional histories. It does not mean every survivor had an independent safe option to leave. Scripts avoid psychologizing.
- Specific historical images, archive footage, voices and rights have **not** been downloaded, reviewed or licensed. No real reference asset bytes supplied; no production rights PASS.
- The 10m EN adds October 1944 prior damage and explicit armor explanation, each sourced, rather than inventing unseen characters or dialogue. No period quotation used.

## 2026-11-23 revision: new narrated technical constraints
