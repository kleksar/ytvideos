### Three real proposals, considered on comparable terms

**A — USS Squalus / selected.** First spoken line: “A rescue chamber began climbing from a sunken submarine with eight survivors inside. Then its cable jammed.” First image: small steel rescue chamber surrounded by deep water; cable above is visibly stressed; not imagined archival footage. **Stop:** rescuer becomes trapped. **Stay:** 26 lost/33 alive; locate wreck; seal dry chamber; 7+9+9 safely lifted; final 8 jammed; second cable nearly fails; carefully adjust buoyancy. **Payoff:** all 33 surviving crew/civilian reach Falcon; 26 fatal losses openly acknowledged. Strong objection: demand not established; an already large competing documentary covers this event, and a 10-minute version can turn into an operating manual. Primary Navy account/log provide checkable actions.

**B — HMS Thetis.** Candidate exact opener: “Four men escaped a submarine that was close enough to shore for rescuers to see. Ninety-nine others never got out.” First image: exposed stern near surface and ships alongside, actual visual to be reference-verified. **Stay:** flood on test dive → air/escape mechanism → first exits → failed subsequent attempt → loss of remaining people; inquiry. **Payoff:** understanding why proximity to shore did not mean rescue, and how Thetis later became Thunderbolt. Strong objection: competing details of the failed escape and very sombre outcome can overwhelm the central mechanism. Evidence: Royal Museums Greenwich [HMS Thetis object/historical account](https://www.rmg.co.uk/collections/objects/rmgc-object-67493) and [Grove documentary metadata](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sjaXAad8h_A). Not scripted; would require full provenance check before scripting.

**C — Operation Cottage / Kiska.** Candidate exact opener: “The invasion force landed on an island after its defenders had already sailed away. Then the casualties began.” First image: Allied troops in Aleutian fog against an apparently empty shore. **Stay:** Japanese evacuation → Allied assumptions → landing in fog → friendly fire, mines/accidents → discovery of absence. **Payoff:** concrete consequences of acting on an outdated enemy estimate. Strong objection: casualty totals differ by inclusion of USS Abner Read incident; reveal of empty island too early can leave only grim incident listing; some online commentary trivializes losses. Sources: [US Navy Aleutians photo inventory](https://www.history.navy.mil/content/history/museums/nmusn/explore/photography/wwii/wwii-pacific/aleutian-islands-campaign/allied-invasion-kiska.html), [NDU historical analysis](https://ndupress.ndu.edu/Media/News/News-Article-View/Article/577595/operation-cottage-a-cautionary-tale-of-assumption-and-perceptual-bias/), Reddit commentary [2023 questions](https://www.reddit.com/r/HistoryMemes/comments/13qyhq2) (opinions, not verified history).

**Decision:** A is stronger than B on complete human outcome, and stronger than C on post-hook causal progression. The fourth ascent creates a genuine second emergency that does not depend on rhetoric or an invented psychological motive. C offers a more instant ironic opening but less safe and less satisfying ten-minute development. B has high stakes, but the verified outcome is overwhelmingly tragic. These are editorial judgments, **not audience measurements**.

## 4. Verified chronology and causality for production

23 May — intake valve fails / aft compartment floods / 33 in forward section / marker smoke → Sculpin discovers wreck (NHHC Sculpin).
24 May — Falcon positions chamber / divers prepare hatch / 1st trip 7 → 2nd 9 → 3rd 9; surviving remainder 8 (Navy diving log).
24 May evening — fourth chamber takes eight; downhaul jams; diver cuts the jammed cable; separate retrieving wire strands → chamber lowered (Navy rescue report §15).
25 May 00:04–00:25 — deliberate ballast blows / near-neutral buoyancy / final controlled retrieval → eight aboard; total rescued 33 (§17).
25 May — fifth check of flooded aft compartment, no additional survivors (§18).
13 Sep 1939 → 1940 — salvage to Portsmouth, rename Sailfish and return to service (Navy salvage excerpt).

**Expectation changes:** simple lift? → rescue requires repeated exact dockings; almost done? → jammed device becomes the trap; pull harder? → cable is nearly gone; add buoyancy? → too much also unsafe; measured adjustment returns final eight. This **causal** arc is the reason to watch and is not supplied by spectacle alone.
