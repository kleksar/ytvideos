## Comparable three finalist pitches

### Selected — Ankrom: a counterfeit road sign that became state-approved directions

- **Why stop:** “A man dressed as a road worker put two fake signs above a Los Angeles freeway.” Understandable without knowing his name.
- **Why stay after hook:** problem at the left-hand north I-5 exit → exacting materials and official-looking disguise → actual Caltrans employees nearby do not stop him → public 2002 reveal forces state response → original crushed in 2009 but correction retained on official replacement.
- **Payoff:** unauthorized metal vanished, but useful directions survived on the official sign. The government kept the information, not the original art or the unauthorized procedure.
- **Strongest objection:** authentic artist film and The Drive 2024 retelling already exist. Viewers complained a roughly nine-minute video stretched too little material. September 2026 Instagram revival suggests renewed attention but also short-form saturation; it does NOT validate a Paper Detour five-minute video. Safety optics: avoid celebrating illegal highway work as safe or instructions for imitation.
- **Genre:** surprising human-interest art/civic problem, not disaster or engineering-only procedural.
- **Evidence basis:** 2002 reporting, 2009 artist interview, 2021 firsthand retrospective; contrasted on YouTube as below.

### Strongest alternative — *Dreadnought* hoax

- **Cold open:** “The Royal Navy saluted four foreign princes. The visitors were British pranksters in borrowed robes.”
- **After hook:** fake telegram → elaborate disguise → speech in invented foreign words → dining invitation declined for fear face paint would smear → press photo exposes the visitors.
- **Payoff:** navy embarrassment, contemporary press response and questions about ceremonial security.
- **Sources:** [USNI Naval History, 2024-06](https://www.usni.org/magazines/naval-history-magazine/2024/june/dreadnought-hoax), [The Guardian, 2012-02-05 on organizer's 1910 letter](https://www.theguardian.com/books/2012/feb/05/bloomsbury-dreadnought-hoax-recalled-letter). Contemporary letter is participant self-report, not independent observation.
- **Strongest objection:** racial disguise and imperial-era context need careful narration; primary document has comic self-aggrandizement, and three independent lengths risk large context overhead. Strong image but less immediate relatable problem than Ankrom.

### Other serious finalist — 1987 Max Headroom signal intrusions

- **Cold open:** “Chicago's newscast vanished. A masked stranger replaced it. Two hours later, the stranger interrupted another channel.”
- **After hook:** first short interruption defeated by engineers → second interruption during *Doctor Who* remained on air longer → authorities investigate the signal path.
- **Payoff:** interruption ended; perpetrator remained unidentified, a materially less decisive revelation than Ankrom's official replacement.
- **Sources:** [Historic Mysteries, published 2026-11-02](https://www.historicmysteries.com/unexplained-mysteries/max-headroom-incident/40200/), [detailed evidence account](https://unsolvedfiles.com/the-max-headroom-signal-hijacking-incident/). The later account is not a substitute for primary FCC documents.
- **Strongest objection:** no known culprit or clear causal finale, large existing mystery-video ecosystem, and broadcast footage usage requires rights review. An open mystery has attention value but is a less satisfying Paper Detour payoff here.

**Decision:** Ankrom beats *Dreadnought* on instantly familiar missed-exit problem, clear multi-stage action and positive material/meaning inversion. Against Max Headroom it has a resolved, verifiable ending. Counter: The Drive/viral retellings make it crowded; selection is an *editorial hypothesis*, not measured channel demand.
