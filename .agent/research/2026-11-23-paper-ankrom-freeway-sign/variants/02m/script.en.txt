A man dressed as a road worker put two fake signs above a Los Angeles freeway. They stayed there for eight years.

In 2001, the exit from the 110 freeway to northbound Interstate 5 was easy to miss. It appeared on the left, just after a tunnel. Artist and professional sign painter Richard Ankrom had missed that turn himself.

So he decided to add the warning he thought the road needed. Without asking permission.

He didn't scribble an arrow on cardboard. He studied California's sign specifications, cut aluminum panels, matched the green paint, and found the old-fashioned reflectors used on the existing sign. He made an Interstate 5 shield and a panel saying NORTH.

On August fifth, he put on a hard hat and orange safety vest, brought a ladder and traffic cones, and climbed to the sign structure. Friends filmed him attaching his additions. Real road workers were nearby. Nobody stopped him.

For about nine months, drivers simply used the sign.

Then Ankrom showed the film, and the newspapers found out. So did the state transportation agency. It warned that the stunt had been dangerous. But the addition was accurate and useful, so officials left it in place.

That was only the first surprise.

In 2009, workers replaced the whole freeway sign. Ankrom wanted to save his signed original, but it had already gone to a recycler and been crushed with other metal.

The replacement was official. It still displayed his northbound I-5 directions.

The fake object disappeared. The correction survived.

And if you had driven under that sign in 2001, you would have seen what looked like a perfectly ordinary road crew at work.
