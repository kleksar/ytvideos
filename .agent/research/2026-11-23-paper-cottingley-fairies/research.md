## Three competing story proposals — equally serious objections

**A: Cottingley — selected, audience paper-stories/curious history.**
- Stop: "Five fairy photographs were real photos; the fairies were paper cutouts." Cold opening establishes tangible contradiction without unknown names.
- Stay: photos enter spiritualist circles -> photographic examiners fail to see darkroom manipulation -> creator of Sherlock Holmes backs the story -> *marked fresh plates* and three additional photos challenge skepticism -> 1983 correspondence explains the physical staging -> disagreement about photo five.
- Payoff: the camera truly recorded a scene; the *objects* were staged, so checking negative for darkroom alterations misses the trick. Physical hatpins and cutouts tie directly to Paper Detour's material language.
- Strong objection: 107-year-old, widely retold hoax (including a multi-million-view short); 10m can become an epistemology lecture and evidence does not prove this audience's demand. Additional issue: genuine historical plates/photographs have image-rights obligations and cannot be assumed licensed.
- First scene for 05m: paper-physical stream, photo plate, real girl versus silhouette; narrator asks how the examiners failed. Main hook after documentary introduction: expert examination appears to confirm undisturbed plates, but that test misses staged objects.

**B: Operation Bernhard — strongest alternative for human stakes.**
- Stop: prisoners were forced to forge British banknotes so convincing they threatened to undermine trust in real money.
- Stay: German wartime scheme -> prisoners' constrained technical work -> notes used -> aftermath and testimony. Payoff: identifying real currency becomes difficult while the people producing it were captives. No invented motives or escape.
- Objection: moral gravity and material human testimony must be treated fully, not reduced to an ingenious print trick; primaries not fully read in this run. Entry background: Bank of England Museum https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/museum/online-collections/blog/operation-bernhard and Sachsenhausen https://www.sachsenhausen-sbg.de/en/collection-and-research/objects-in-focus/a-genuine-forgery/ (candidate references, NOT complete factual QA).

**C: 1910 Dreadnought hoax — credible human comedy alternative.**
- Stop: a party of impostors boarded the Royal Navy's most famous battleship pretending to be foreign dignitaries.
- Stay: invented identities -> crew reception -> discovery -> press fallout.
- Payoff: authority, uniforms and expectations enabled the deception; not a technical breakthrough.
- Objection: blackface/racist performance is integral to the historical act, can be mishandled; verified lines and a distinct 10-minute consequence chain are lacking. Historical candidate references: https://www.usni.org/magazines/naval-history-magazine/2024/june/dreadnought-hoax ; https://www.theguardian.com/books/2012/feb/05/bloomsbury-dreadnought-hoax-recalled-letter.

**Decision:** Cottingley beats Bernhard for this channel's paper-built action with source-backed sequential escalation and a complete, visual payoff. Bernhard has much greater human stakes; this is a channel-fit decision, not a claim that it is weaker history. Dreadnought has a comic first act but a more fragile 5/10-minute documentary arc. Demand for Cottingley as Paper-style 05m is still **UNVERIFIED**. This decision would change if an approved upcoming video or duplication is found.

**Claim limits:** Do NOT say the girls used Photoshop, altered the negatives or were professionally filmed. Marked plates did not prove a supernatural event and were not continuous surveillance.
