## Three distinct honest Long title + thumbnail promises

1. **Main: “The Music CD That Hid Software on Your PC”**. Thumbnail: handmade cardboard paper CD case in a store shopping bag at left, 2005-era desktop monitor at right, translucent file icons visually missing from a paper folder on monitor. Distinct large Paper-branded lettering on physical paper label: **HIDDEN INSIDE**. Promise: this *particular kind* of disc carried concealed software; do NOT imply every Sony CD or Windows PC was affected. Early evidence: inserted disc launches XCP software, researcher finds hidden files. Final payoff: the company's copy-protection and uninstall approaches created serious security concerns; recall and consent rules.
2. **Alternate: “Sony Tried to Fix Its CD Software. The Fix Was Unsafe.”**. Thumbnail: two three-dimensional paper labels “INSTALL” and “REMOVE” on either side of a CD, each with a warning cutout. Paper title **THE FIX?**. Promise: documented vulnerability in removal process; not a claim that an identified individual was hacked.
3. **Alternate: “When a Music Album Changed Your Computer”**. Thumbnail: CD placed on an ordinary home desk; cabinet-like drawers in a paper computer file system move behind a closed door. Paper title **WHO'S IN CONTROL?**. Promise: consumer control/consent, rather than sensational hacker fiction.

Tradeoff: the main title is concrete and understandable immediately; #2 may appeal to security viewers but promises the later twist without the setup; #3 is broader, less immediately searchable.
