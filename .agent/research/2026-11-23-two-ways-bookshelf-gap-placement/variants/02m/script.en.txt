Should I keep four empty bookshelf slots together, or spread them out?

I have twelve books, labeled A through L, in alphabetical order. The shelf holds sixteen. I'm trying two layouts before four more books arrive.

In A, every empty slot is at the far right. In B, I leave one gap after C, F, I, and K. Same books. Same space. Neither shelf is fuller.

This is a model, not a timed experiment. Every book is one slot wide. Each time I slide an existing book by one slot, that's one move. Placing a new book isn't counted. Both methods keep the alphabet intact.

First, four new titles arrive. They belong just after A, E, H, and K. Which layout would you pick?

On shelf A, the first addition pushes eleven books to the right. The next pushes seven, then four, then one. Twenty-three moves.

On shelf B, I move two books, then one, then one. The last new book fits straight into a gap. Four moves.

The scattered gaps win by nineteen moves. But I shouldn't call them the winner yet.

I reset both shelves. This time the four new books belong after L, at the very end. On A, I simply place them in the open spaces: zero moves. On B, each new title needs space past L. Shifting the row takes one, then four, then eight, then twelve moves. Twenty-five.

So there's no universally faster shelf. Gaps near the next insertion points help. Gaps at the end help when the collection grows at the end. Real books aren't identical widths, and I haven't measured anyone's speed.
