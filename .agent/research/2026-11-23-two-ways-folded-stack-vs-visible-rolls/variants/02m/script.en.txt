I can fold these eight T-shirts faster than I can roll them. So why would I roll them for a trip?

I’m packing one suitcase. I’ll choose a shirt on three different mornings, without emptying the whole bag. Which method gets me through packing and those three searches faster: a flat stack or eight visible rolls? Pick one.

This is a timed model, not a filmed experiment. Both sides start with the same eight shirts. Folding takes five seconds per shirt. Rolling takes nine. The folded shirts form one stack; the rolls stand separately so I can see and lift any one.

Here is the catch: I won’t know which shirt I need until I open the bag. In this model, any remaining shirt is equally likely. Taking a shirt takes two seconds. With a stack, moving each shirt above it aside takes three seconds, and putting it back takes another three.

Packing is easy: forty seconds folded, seventy-two rolled. Folding leads by thirty-two.

But the first search through eight folded shirts means moving three and a half shirts on average. That costs twenty-one seconds, plus two to grab mine. On the next mornings there are seven, then six shirts left. Those searches average twenty and seventeen seconds.

Fold and search three times: one hundred seconds. Roll and search three times: seventy-eight. The slower packing method wins by twenty-two seconds.

Now change just one thing: I only need one shirt before unpacking everything. Folding takes sixty-three seconds in total; rolling takes seventy-four. Folding wins.
