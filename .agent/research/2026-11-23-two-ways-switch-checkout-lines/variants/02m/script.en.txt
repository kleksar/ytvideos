Should I switch checkout lines just because the one beside me moves first?

I have a small basket and two people ahead of me. The next register has four. I stay in the shorter line.

Now I run both choices through a made-up but fixed checkout schedule. This is a simulation, not a visit I measured.

At my register, the first customer needs ninety seconds. The second needs sixty. Scanning and paying for my basket takes forty-five.

Next door, each of the four customers needs forty seconds. My basket still takes forty-five.

One minute passes. Nobody in my line has finished. Next door, one customer has already left and another is halfway done. That looks like a sign to move.

I can either keep my place or walk across and join the end. I want to know when I actually get out, not which line looks busier.

If I stay, my two remaining customers need ninety more seconds altogether. Then I'm done forty-five seconds later. Three minutes and fifteen seconds from the start.

If I switch, the other line still has one hundred seconds of work ahead of me. Even after it moves first, I leave at three minutes and twenty-five seconds.

Switching makes me ten seconds later.

And the short walk across? The other cashier is still working during those five seconds. I can't add the walk on top of the whole queue.

Change just one thing: give the second customer in my original line a twenty-five-second price check. Now staying takes three minutes forty, while switching still takes three twenty-five. Switching wins by fifteen.

So the answer isn't always stay. It's that one completed customer doesn't tell me how much work is left. Next time the other line jumps forward, I won't call that a win. It is the moment I have to choose again.
