I have one cake and two friends. The cake has three flavors, and we don't all like the same ones. Should I cut three equal slices, or let the people who are eating decide where the knife goes? Pick A or B.

Here's my model. The cake has twelve equal sections: four chocolate, four vanilla and four strawberry. Each of us gives the whole cake a value of twenty-four points, but we distribute those points differently. My test is the smallest share anybody gets of their own twenty-four points. I want everybody to get at least eight.

First, I cut three identical-sized servings. Max takes chocolate. Lee takes vanilla. I'm left with strawberry. To me, that's only four points. I've got a third of the cake, but just a sixth of its value to me.

I put the cake back together in the model. This time, I propose the chocolate end because it's worth eight to me. Max thinks it's worth sixteen. So he trims it down until it's worth eight to him. Lee looks at the smaller piece. He wouldn't pay eight points for it, so he passes. Max gets that piece. The trimming goes back with the rest of the cake.

Now I divide what's left into two pieces I value equally. Lee picks the one with more chocolate, and I take the other. Our scores? Eight for Max, ten for Lee and ten for me. Nobody gets less than a third of their own total.
