A man traded away a year of free rent. Two deals later, he had a snow globe. Somehow, that detour helped him get a house.

In 2005, Kyle MacDonald put a red paperclip on the internet and asked what people would give him for it. Two women offered a pen shaped like a fish.

He exchanged the pen for a handmade doorknob, the knob for a camping stove, and the stove for a generator.

The generator was harder to get rid of. Firefighters even took it away during a trip to New York. After getting it back, Kyle swapped it for a neon beer sign, a keg and a promise to fill it.

That became a snowmobile, then a trip, a truck, a recording contract and finally a year of free rent in Arizona.

Instead of moving in, Kyle traded the rent for an afternoon with rock musician Alice Cooper. Then he exchanged that opportunity for a KISS snow globe. People following his blog thought he'd lost his way.

What they didn't know was that actor Corbin Bernsen had already offered Kyle a part in a movie. Bernsen collected snow globes. Kyle had found something the actor actually wanted.

The globe went to Bernsen, and Kyle got the movie role.

A small Canadian town, Kipling, wanted that role for a public talent contest and a chance to attract visitors. It offered Kyle a house. The town got its contest, and a local young man got to appear in the movie.

Fourteen trades. One year. The original paperclip wasn't worth a house. Each new owner had a reason to want what came next.

The most revealing trade looked like a terrible mistake.
