The only man in a Spanish village who hadn't won the lottery was the man filming the winners.

In December 2011, Sodeto was a farming community struggling with drought and debt. A local women's association raised money by selling small shares in Spain's Christmas lottery. They went from house to house with shares of the same number.

On December twenty-second, the children conducting the draw in Madrid sang out the winning number: 58268.

The mayor of Sodeto used the village loudspeakers to announce the news. People gathered in the square. The money wasn't going to one lucky family. Nearly every household had a claim to part of the prize.

Costis Mitsotakis, a Greek filmmaker who lived outside the village, heard that something was happening. He took his camera and joined the crowd.

His neighbors asked how many shares he'd bought. None. The sellers had missed his house, and he hadn't gone looking for a ticket.

He kept filming. Soon the outsiders arriving in Sodeto included people selling expensive cars. But farmers were paying off loans, improving their irrigation and buying machinery they actually needed.

Even Costis received an unexpected phone call. Land he'd been trying to sell finally attracted a buyer. Then another neighbor made an offer. He refused to turn it into a bidding war.

He continued working on a documentary about the village. In December 2025, it was still unfinished, he said.

By then the women's association was reserving a share for him, and he was paying for it.

But in 2011, when nearly every family in Sodeto had a winning ticket, who was holding a camera instead?
