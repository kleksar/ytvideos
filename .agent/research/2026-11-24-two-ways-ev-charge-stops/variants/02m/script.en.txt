One long charging stop or two short ones? Which gets me home sooner?

I'm at a fast charger with twenty percent left and a two-hundred-and-eighty-kilometer drive ahead. I want to arrive with twenty percent still in the battery. There's another charger on my route, after a hundred and sixty kilometers.

This is a simplified model, not a road test. I'm comparing the time from here to my destination. Pick A, one stop, or B, two stops.

With A, I need ninety percent before I leave. In my model the car charges from twenty to sixty in twenty minutes. Going from sixty to eighty takes another twenty. And the last ten, up to ninety? Another twenty minutes.

I spend an hour charging, plus five minutes getting in and out. I pass the second charger and arrive with twenty percent.

Now I reset the car and try B. I charge to sixty and leave after twenty minutes of charging. I reach the second charger with twenty percent left. I only need fifty percent to finish the trip, so I plug in for fifteen more minutes.

Two stops, ten minutes getting in and out, thirty-five minutes of charging. That's forty-five minutes instead of sixty-five. Same road, same driving time, same charge at the finish. I get home twenty minutes earlier by stopping twice.

But if I have to wait twenty-five minutes for that second charger, I lose. One stop is five minutes faster.

So I check the charging curve, the next station and my reserve before deciding. I don't just count the stops. Before plugging in on another trip, I ask the same question.
