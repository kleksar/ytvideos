I'm buying four tickets for the same flight. Should I book all four at once, or split them into two bookings?

I've made a model of a ticketing system, with two tickets at a hundred and thirty-five dollars and four more at two hundred and fifteen. All four of us are adults, and we want the same flight and the same travel conditions. I'm measuring the total price for all four.

Pick A or B before I open the checkout.

With A, I ask for four tickets. This model sells only one price level per booking. There aren't four tickets at the lower price, so all four come back at two hundred and fifteen. That's eight hundred and sixty dollars.

I reset everything and try B. I book the first pair for two hundred and seventy dollars. Then I buy the other two for four hundred and thirty. All four tickets together cost seven hundred. Same flight, a hundred and sixty dollars less.

But I've still got a problem. What if the price changes while I'm between bookings?

I reset again. This time the last two tickets rise to three hundred and ten dollars each after I buy the first pair. My total becomes eight hundred and ninety. If the original four-ticket offer was available when I could have paid, A would have saved thirty dollars.

That's why I compare the prices for one, two and four travelers before buying. I also check the baggage, seat options and rules for changes. Separate bookings can make traveling together more complicated.

I don't automatically split the tickets. I look for a saving large enough to justify the extra risk. And it starts before I enter any payment details.
