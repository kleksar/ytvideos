Can ten extra pages make my cheaper annual printer plan cost more than the monthly one? I'd like to know before I pick a subscription.

I'll model two offers listed by HP Instant Ink in the United States. Same printer, same pages, no trial or taxes. My score is the plan charges for the first twelve months, not the value of any printing I can do later.

A is ten pages a month for a dollar ninety-nine. B is a hundred and twenty pages for twenty dollars and ninety-nine cents, prepaid. Which would you choose?

I print ten pages every month for eleven months. If I print nothing in month twelve, A costs twenty-three eighty-eight. B costs twenty ninety-nine. The annual plan saves me two dollars and eighty-nine cents.

Now I change just the last month. I need twenty pages. On A, ten are included and the other ten fit in one two-dollar extra block. My total becomes twenty-five eighty-eight.

On B, the first ten finish my annual allowance. But HP's annual billing period can end as soon as the pages run out. With automatic renewal on, I buy another annual allowance at the assumed unchanged price.

Now B costs forty-one ninety-eight. I print the last ten pages from the new allowance and still have a hundred and ten left.

A costs sixteen dollars and ten cents less over my first year. That doesn't make the remaining pages worthless. If I keep printing, that second payment may cover much of next year.

I'd check my likely page count, the renewal setting and my actual offer before choosing. The annual price alone doesn't tell me what those last ten pages could cost.
