I need a printer for occasional paperwork at home. Paying for an entire year looks cheaper than twelve little monthly payments. But I want to know what happens when the printer counts the last page in that yearly allowance. Does the cheapest plan stay cheap?

I'll compare two offers from HP Instant Ink's United States price list. This is a model, not a test of two real accounts. I start after any trial, with the same eligible printer and no saved pages. I'll keep prices constant and leave both plans unchanged. My metric is the subscription charges attributable to the first twelve months, before the next normal anniversary renewal. I exclude tax, paper and hardware.

On side A, I get ten pages per month for a dollar ninety-nine. On side B, I buy a hundred and twenty pages up front for twenty dollars and ninety-nine cents. Twelve payments on A add up to twenty-three eighty-eight. B looks like an easy win. Pick one before I start printing.

I put ten pages through each printer in month one. Ten more in month two. I keep doing that through month eleven. Both plans have covered a hundred and ten pages. If my printer stays idle through the final month, the annual plan saves two dollars and eighty-nine cents. I have ten unused pages on B, and I don't get to carry them into another annual period.

But suppose a batch of forms arrives on the first day of month twelve. Instead of zero pages, I need twenty. I don't change the plan or the printer. I just put the same twenty pages through both sides.

On A, the month's allowance takes care of the first ten. There are no leftover pages from earlier months. HP lists extra blocks of ten to fifteen pages, depending on the plan, for two dollars each. Either block size covers my remaining ten pages. My first year's charges are now twenty-five dollars and eighty-eight cents.

On B, the annual balance falls from ten to zero. Here is the rule I would have missed if I'd only looked at the price: exhausting the annual allowance can end that billing period early. With auto-renewal enabled, another allowance starts. In this model the second payment is the same twenty ninety-nine, so I can print the remaining ten pages. My total is forty-one ninety-eight.

That makes A cheaper by sixteen dollars and ten cents within my first twelve months. But the second annual payment is not just an extra fee for ten pages. There are a hundred and ten pages left in the new period. I may need them.

I'll keep that last example running for another eleven months. Ten pages each month from month thirteen through twenty-two, and five in month twenty-three. The second annual period still has five pages unused. Before its next anniversary renewal, B has cost forty-one ninety-eight for two hundred and thirty-five pages. A has twenty-three monthly charges and the one extra block: forty-seven seventy-seven. B now costs five seventy-nine less. My original comparison was about this year's budget, not a lifetime verdict.

There is another way the monthly plan can surprise me. Imagine a different year: I need only a hundred and ten pages, but they're all for a project in the last month. I have kept A active. Its unused monthly allowance carries over, but only up to thirty saved pages. With ten new pages, I have forty available. The remaining seventy need five to seven extra blocks. That puts A between thirty-three eighty-eight and thirty-seven eighty-eight. B covers the entire project for twenty ninety-nine.

So I wouldn't decide from the annual discount or even the total number of pages alone. I'd make a calendar of when I actually need to print. I'd also check what happens if I stop auto-renewal, and whether I'll use the next allowance. Switching plans or using another ink option could change the result; I haven't priced those here.

For occasional steady printing, B can save a little. A can protect my first-year budget from the particular renewal in our twenty-page month. For a concentrated batch or continued printing, B can win again. The useful choice is the one that matches my printing dates and my budget, not just the smaller number on the sign-up screen.
