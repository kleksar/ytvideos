I can take the same train with one ticket for eighty pounds, or with two tickets that might cost less. Which would you choose?

I'm using a model of a British rail journey, not a live booking. I want to get from West to Harbour in the same seat class, on the same train. My score is the total price I pay.

A is easy. I buy a ticket from West all the way to Harbour. Eighty pounds.

For B, I look at the stops. My train calls at Junction and Park. I can buy a ticket to Junction for twenty-eight pounds and another from Junction to Harbour for twenty-seven. Together they cost fifty-five.

Both tickets cover my journey, and the train actually stops at Junction. I don't need to get off just because one ticket ends there, although I might have to change seats if the reservations differ. The same ride costs me twenty-five pounds less.

But suppose I choose an express that skips Junction and still stops at Park. I can't use that first pair of ordinary tickets on this train. I need to split at Park instead. The two valid tickets cost forty-six and nineteen pounds. That's sixty-five altogether. Still cheaper than the eighty-pound through ticket, but the saving is smaller.

And if I find a valid through fare for forty-nine pounds on my original train, the single ticket wins against fifty-five.

I check the stops, the restrictions, and the final price including any fees. Two tickets aren't automatically better. I'm looking for the cheapest valid way to buy the journey I already want.
