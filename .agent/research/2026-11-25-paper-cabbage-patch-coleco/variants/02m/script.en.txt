In 1983, about a thousand shoppers rushed into a Pennsylvania store for just two hundred and forty dolls. Half an hour later, there were none left.

The dolls were Cabbage Patch Kids. Each came with a name, a birth certificate and adoption papers. The idea started with handmade cloth dolls in Georgia. Toy company Coleco turned it into something factories could make by the million.

By the end of 1983, Coleco had shipped more than three million of them. You might expect the company to be celebrating. Instead, it finished the year in the red.

Coleco was also trying to sell a home computer called Adam. It was designed to give families a complete system for about six hundred dollars. Production fell behind, faulty machines came back, and the computer business swallowed money.

The following year, Cabbage Patch sales reached five hundred and forty million dollars. Coleco as a whole still lost almost eighty million.

So it abandoned Adam. In 1985, with doll sales still growing, the company made a record profit. The recovery was real.

Then the rush for dolls began to fade. Coleco had expanded into other toys and bought a board-game company. The new business didn't make up for the collapse in doll sales. Expenses and debts remained even as revenue fell.

In July 1988, Coleco filed for bankruptcy protection. A year later, Hasbro bought most of its remaining non-cash assets and arranged to make the dolls itself.

Cabbage Patch Kids had survived the failure of the company that made them a mass-market sensation. And before Coleco ran short of money, its customers faced a very different shortage.
