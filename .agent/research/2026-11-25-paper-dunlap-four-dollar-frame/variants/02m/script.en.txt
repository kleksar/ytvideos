A man paid four dollars for a painting he didn't want. He wanted the frame.

At home, he took a closer look at the picture, and a folded piece of paper fell out. He threw the painting away but kept the paper.

This was Pennsylvania in 1989. The sheet said that thirteen American colonies were declaring their independence from Britain. The collector didn't know if it was genuine. A friend persuaded him to have it examined.

The surprise was that this wasn't a souvenir of the Declaration of Independence. It was one of the copies printed to announce independence in 1776, before the famous parchment with all the signatures was made.

John Dunlap had printed about two hundred sheets in Philadelphia so the news could travel. By 2025, only twenty-six copies were known to survive.

Nobody knows how this one ended up behind a painting. But Sotheby's authenticated it, and in 1991 it sold for two million, four hundred and twenty thousand dollars.

Nine years later, it came up for sale again. This time television producer Norman Lear and his partner paid more than eight million dollars.

And then something unusual happened to this expensive piece of paper. Instead of staying in a private collection, it went on tour. The exhibition reached every American state. People could see one of the actual sheets that had been printed to bring the news of independence to the public.

More than two centuries later, the paper was doing its old job again.
