The first recording that solved the internet's most mysterious song wasn't the recording people had been looking for.

Back in the eighties, a German teenager named Darius taped a song off the radio. He kept the music, but he didn't know the band's name. In 2007, his sister Lydia asked for help online.

Years later, strangers across the internet were sharing clues. They had an entire song to work with, yet they couldn't find a record with the right name.

In 2024, a researcher named Marijn was going through old newspaper archives. He found an article about a band called FEX, recognized one of its members from another band, and got in touch with him.

Michael Hädrich sent him some old recordings. One was called Subways of Your Mind. It was the missing song, played a little differently. FEX had no idea people had spent years searching for them.

The musicians reunited. Then, over Christmas, Michael found a box containing more than two hundred cassettes. One had FEX written on it.

He adjusted the playback speeds and compared its recording with the familiar radio tape. They matched. His cassette even had two drum hits at the beginning that were missing from the radio copy.

The song finally had a name, a band, and a surviving copy of the performance that started the search. In 2025, both versions came out officially.

Even the old radio recording appeared on FEX's album. The teenager's tape had become part of the band's own discography.
