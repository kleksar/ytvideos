The original creator of Flappy Bird pulled the game from both major phone app stores while it was number one.

Dong Nguyen had made a game with one control: tap the screen to keep a little bird above the ground and steer it through gaps between pipes. By February 2014, it had reportedly been downloaded fifty million times. An interview put its advertising revenue at fifty thousand dollars a day, though Nguyen later declined to confirm the exact amount.

He gave players twenty-two hours' notice. Then the download disappeared. He said the game was taking up too much of people's lives, and the pressure had affected his own sleep.

Phones that still had the game on them turned up on eBay. But the site's rules required sellers to reset the devices, which would remove the very thing they were advertising. Listings were taken down.

Meanwhile, other developers released so many similar games that one publication counted about sixty new ones a day over four days.

Years later, the fight was over the name itself. In September 2023, a company opposed Nguyen's US trademark application. He defaulted in that proceeding, and in January the opposition succeeded.

A group announced a new Flappy Bird that September. Nguyen publicly said he hadn't sold anything and wasn't involved. In April 2025, a new game under the name appeared on Android.

The creator's original game and the new release were different products. The familiar name was back without him. What had happened the last time the original creator controlled its release?
