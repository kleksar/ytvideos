For Christmas in 1977, Kenner sold a cardboard stand and a certificate instead of Star Wars action figures.

The movie had become a hit in May. But the company making its toys could get puzzles and games into stores faster than it could develop a whole line of plastic characters. Christmas was coming, and the figures weren't ready.

So Kenner made something parents could put under the tree. The illustrated package had stickers, a fan club card, and a fold-out display for a collection of twelve characters. There wasn't a single figure inside.

The certificate was the important part. Send it to Kenner and four figures would arrive by mail in 1978: Luke, Leia, Chewbacca and R2-D2.

Some shops refused to sell the package. Others ran out of stock. People were already returning certificates before Christmas.

The company delivered the toys. The first four arrived in small white mailers with plastic pegs for standing the figures on the cardboard display. The rest of the original line followed.

Now look at three lots from a collector's auction in July 2025. A preserved certificate package sold for more than eighteen thousand dollars. A sealed set of the four actual toys went for over forty-five thousand.

And the cardboard store display that had held the certificate packages? With an example of the original package included, it sold for one hundred and twelve thousand, one hundred dollars.

The thing a toy store used to sell a promise had become more expensive than the toys in that particular auction. What was in the package Kenner put on sale that first Christmas?
