I compare a $151 New York CityPASS with individual tickets to the same five places. Which would you pick?

I'm planning a trip on paper. In my model I have nine days, and I want the same visits either way. I'm comparing admission costs, not time in line.

First, I want to see the American Museum of Natural History and one of its ticketed exhibitions. That's $43. The Intrepid museum costs $38, the Guggenheim $30, and the ferry to the Statue of Liberty and Ellis Island starts at $26.

I still want to visit the Whitney Museum. At 26, without any special discounts, I would pay another $30 during regular admission hours. My five separate tickets come to $167.

The pass lets me choose those five attractions for $151, including its fee. So the pass wins by $16.

Now I reset the trip. Same museums, same ferry, same exhibition. I change just one thing: I'm 25 instead of 26.

The Whitney admits visitors aged 25 and under for free. The other four visits still cost me $137. I can enter the Whitney for free separately, but the pass itself still costs $151.

This time, individual tickets win by $14.

Even at 26, I could get that result if I can reserve a free Friday evening visit at the Whitney.

My birthday doesn't change what I want to see. It changes what I need to pay. Before I book, I price the actual itinerary, including free admission I can use. How do I check whether the pass is worth buying in the first place?
