I can tap my bank card, or I can tap my phone with that very card in its wallet. The money comes out of the same bank account. Would it cost me the same to ride the subway all week?

I'm going to model twenty New York subway rides in seven days. The measure is simple: how much do I pay for exactly the same trips? I start with no previous fares on either payment method, and the regular fare is three dollars per ride.

In A, I use the plastic card every time. In B, I alternate between the card and my phone. Pick A or B before I reach the end of the week.

After eleven rides, I've spent thirty-three dollars either way. But on ride twelve, something changes. In A, my total reaches thirty-five dollars. That's the weekly fare cap. The rest of my rides in that period are free.

In B, I've made six trips with the card and six with the phone. Each has eighteen dollars toward its own cap. Neither has reached thirty-five.

By the twentieth ride, A still costs thirty-five dollars. B costs sixty: thirty on the card and thirty on the phone. The discount never kicked in on either one.

That's twenty-five dollars more for the same twenty rides, without going a single extra stop.

This is a calculation using the published fare rules, not a real payment test. It doesn't mean paying by phone is more expensive. If I use that same phone and wallet card throughout, I can earn the same cap.

The choice that changes my bill isn't which train I take. It's how I get through the gate.
