How can the same forty dollars in road tolls produce two very different rental car bills? Why might a seventy-dollar unlimited pass be cheaper than paying for the roads individually? And if I keep the car longer, does the charge for paying tolls through the rental company keep growing every day?

Imagine I'm collecting a rental car for a trip in Florida. I've already budgeted for the car, fuel and places to stay. At the counter, the remaining decision is how I'll pay for the toll roads. I have a rough itinerary in my phone. I know the road charges, but I haven't written down which days I'll encounter them. That missing piece is what I'm going to test.

I'll use the published Florida toll-service rules from Budget and compare two payment methods on a hypothetical rental. I won't pretend I've made a booking or received a bill. The unlimited prices and all the individual road charges in my examples are inputs to a calculation. I compare only the additional cost of covered road tolls and the rental company's toll service. The car, duration, roads and their prices are identical between A and B within each test. Taxes, parking, fuel and other charges are outside the comparison.

With option A, the standard service, I pay the tolls at the applicable non-discounted rates. The company also charges six dollars and ninety-five cents on each day I incur a toll through that service. That fee is not charged separately for every crossing. It's capped at thirty-four dollars and ninety-five cents over a rental period of no more than thirty days.

With option B, the unlimited service, I choose a quote in advance. The covered tolls are included. For my first test, I assume the five-day quote is seventy dollars. My planned road charges total forty dollars. The unlimited option costs seventy. Before I count the service fees, which would you choose?

I start with a five-day itinerary. On day one, I collect the car and drive through five toll points. At each point the non-discounted charge is eight dollars. There are no more tolls for the next four days.

With B, the same roads are included in the seventy-dollar price. I'm paying for the entire rental period whether I encounter a toll each day or not. A has saved me twenty-three dollars and five cents.

What happens if I rent the same car for a week? I need a new unlimited quote, not the old five-day price. For a separate model, suppose my seven-day quote is eighty-four dollars. This time I can see the standard service fee cap in action.

Five toll days create thirty-four dollars and seventy-five cents in service fees. On a sixth toll day the remaining room under the cap is just twenty cents. On the seventh, there is no room left for another service fee. I still pay every road charge.

If I have forty-five dollars in tolls spread across all seven days, the standard total is seventy-nine dollars and ninety-five cents. A wins even though I used the toll service every day. If the road charges on the same seven-day schedule are fifty-five dollars instead, A becomes eighty-nine ninety-five. B wins.

So the fee cap is not a cap on my whole toll bill. It limits only one part of it. The road tolls still matter. And the price that made the unlimited plan attractive for a five-day trip can't automatically tell me what to buy for a week.

Before signing, I'd check that my quote includes the actual roads on my itinerary. I would look at the non-discounted road rates rather than assume I qualify for a discounted transponder rate. The unlimited service must be chosen at reservation or pickup; I shouldn't plan to wait until the last day and retroactively pick the winning option.
