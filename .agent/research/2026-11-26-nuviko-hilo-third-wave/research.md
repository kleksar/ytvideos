## Primary and secondary claim ledger

A. USGS Circular 1187, https://pubs.usgs.gov/circ/c1187/c1187.pdf (1999, revised 2005): printed pp. 1–2 mechanism and travel times, p. 6 Brown's retrospective account and siren interpretation, p. 7 Eaton's retrospective account and water-level data, p. 8 Onagawa photographs/caption, p. 17 interview provenance and 1961 papers. Parsed full relevant text and chart/photo descriptions; screenshot attempts failed with 403, no actual image QA. Brown's encounter with policeman is attributed to her recollection, not proof of a formal citywide all-clear. Seven-foot radio report is not identified as same point or reference level as bridge's four feet. Household survival, outage and detached house supported. Bridge sequence: first rise after midnight +4 ft, 00:46 +9 ft, near 01:00 −7 ft relative to normal, visual front ~20 ft, flight uphill, later additional readings. Metric conversions rounded in RU. Names and locations from text. No claim that every resident evacuated or that one police officer caused all casualties.

B. https://www.usgs.gov/observatories/hvo/news/volcano-watch-golden-anniversary-chilean-earthquake-comes-early (USGS 2010), paragraphs 2–6: warning 18:47, sirens ~20:30, series >2 hours, highest bridge level ~14 ft; wave size differs from surfing wave; use modern 9.5 not older scales. 
C. https://doi.org/10.1785/BSSA0510020135 (Eaton, Richter, Ault, 1961; abstract accessed), range 3–17 ft on other coasts of island, Hilo third wave about 35 ft above sea level. Not a 35-ft vertical wall everywhere. Different observations not combined. 
D. https://dod.hawaii.gov/hiema/history/ (May 1960 entry): 61 deaths, 229 dwellings and 308 business/public buildings destroyed, eight waves. Timeline slightly differs from A/B (01:05 largest vs clock 01:04; first arrival entries not identical); clock time sourced separately, use approximate first arrival; no disputed injury figures. 
E. https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/news/day-1960-chilean-earthquake-and-tsunami (NOAA NCEI): 9.5 magnitude, basin-wide damage, 1965 warning system. 
F. https://seagrant.soest.hawaii.edu/like-the-whole-ocean-was-coming-at-you/ (Sea Grant, Hilo section): 1946 rebuilding, raised road, restricted buffer, 1960 clock recovered/refurbished, post-1960 Kaiko'o greenbelt and 1965 recreation area, revised warnings/education. Design intent not proof of complete protection. 
G. https://archives.starbulletin.com/2004/05/23/news/story7.html (AP 2004 anniversary report): Fusayo Ito unconscious, floating on screen door, Coast Guard rescue, later honored; clock 1:04. Retrospective; old magnitude in article not used. 
H. https://www.weather.gov/safety/tsunami-during (current NWS guidance): waves may continue, official/local instructions, remain outside hazard zone pending official clearance. Historical scientists' return not advice to imitate.

## Promise and answer
The first waves had arrived; why was returning not safe? One night in Hilo, two eyewitness viewpoints (A/B), a timestamp (F), a documented human consequence (A/D) and a mechanism that has practical relevance (H). 2m: household decision then bridge surprise; 5m: uncertainty, chronology, differing readings and warning interpretation; 10m: 1946 context, earthquake physics, Ito, Onagawa as same-wave counterpoint, rebuilding. All versions use same facts and resolved outcome. Actual archival visual assets and rights not reviewed; no fabricated reference bytes or media.
