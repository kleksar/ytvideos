How did a man answer every question correctly on television and still lose a million pounds? What made the producers stop his cheque?

On the British version of Who Wants to Be a Millionaire, Charles Ingram had ended his first night with four thousand pounds and just one lifeline left. When he returned the following night, in September 2001, his game looked very different.

On a question about a pop album, he said he hadn't heard of singer Craig David. Then he chose Craig David. Correct.

As Ingram climbed towards the jackpot, people working on the show became suspicious of the coughing in the studio. Ingram was thinking through his possible answers aloud, and there seemed to be a connection between the noises and his choices.

At the final question, Ingram had to name the number consisting of a one followed by a hundred zeroes. He chose a googol. The host, Chris Tarrant, handed him the cheque.

But the cheque was dated for the planned broadcast, more than a week later. The producers reviewed the recording, stopped the payment and contacted the police.

In court, the prosecution argued that another contestant, Tecwen Whittock, had deliberately coughed to signal correct answers, and that Ingram's wife Diana was involved. Whittock said he suffered from allergies. Ingram denied cheating. All three were convicted in 2003.

The Ingrams continued to dispute the verdict, and their lawyer later questioned the audio evidence. The programme itself became famous after the trial, but the prize was never paid.

Ingram had won the game on the screen. The cheque was stopped before the broadcast, and the jury later found that it had been obtained by deception.
