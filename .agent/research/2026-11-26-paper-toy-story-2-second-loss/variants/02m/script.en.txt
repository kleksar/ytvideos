A computer buckled into the back of a Volvo gave the makers of Toy Story 2 another chance to finish their movie.

At Pixar, staff were looking at Woody's files when they noticed them disappearing. Other characters were vanishing too. Oren Jacob, one of the technical directors, called the machine room and asked for the server to be shut down.

By then, the project had lost most of its data. Fortunately, Pixar made backups on tape. The team restored the files and rendered sample pictures from the movie. Everything seemed to work.

Then artists noticed problems in their scenes and found that some files were much older than they should have been. The backup itself was incomplete.

Galyn Susman, who supervised the technical team, had been working at home after having a baby. Her computer contained another copy of the project.

They brought it back to Pixar. Its files were about two weeks old, so the team spent the weekend comparing them with the tape restoration and material still on other computers.

Enough of the movie was recovered for production to continue.

But the next time Pixar faced a major problem with Toy Story 2, the computers were working. The filmmakers had watched the movie and decided it wasn't good enough.

They rewrote the story, abandoned much of the existing animation and worked against the deadline to make a new version. The finished film reached American cinemas in November 1999.

The first loss took away the artists' work. The second was their own decision. What had given them a chance to make that decision at all?
