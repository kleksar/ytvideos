I can put my fan one metre from the window or five metres back. Which position would cool my room more?

It's hot inside, but the evening air outside is down to twenty-two degrees Celsius. I have just one open window. Both fans in this comparison represent the same fan, pointing outwards at the same height.

I'm using a published computer simulation, not a test I've performed at home. My score is the average temperature of all the air in the room after one hour.

A is near the window. B is deeper inside the room. Pick a side.

Even without a fan, the room starts to cool. Cooler air can come through the lower part of the opening while warmer air escapes above it.

With B, air moves across the room towards the window. The surprising part is what happens at the opening. Some of that moving air turns around near the wall outside and comes back. Inside, the fan mixes the warm and cool air quite well.

A directs the moving air towards the opening from much closer. There is still room for cooler air to enter around the stream.

After an hour, B reaches twenty-six point one degrees. A reaches twenty-four point four. With the window alone, the room is still at twenty-six point seven.

So the position that mixes the room better doesn't necessarily cool it better. And I haven't had to change the fan at all.

That doesn't make one metre the perfect distance for every window. The wind, room shape and temperature outside matter, and blowing inwards wasn't tested.
