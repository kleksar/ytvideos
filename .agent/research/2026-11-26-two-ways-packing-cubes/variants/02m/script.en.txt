Can adding another bag help me fit more clothes in my carry-on?

I have two ways to pack. I can fold my clothes straight into the bag, or put them into compression cubes and close the extra zipper to squeeze them down. Pick A or B.

I'm reconstructing a published packing test, not doing a new experiment. My first score is the number of garments I can fit. Socks get their own score. Both methods start with the same clothes in the same order, and the outside of the bag has to stay within the test's size limit.

With A, the folded clothes go straight into a small soft travel bag. There's no extra fabric separating one bundle from another. Once the garments are in, I can fill any remaining little spaces with socks.

With B, the second zipper makes a bundle flatter. That sounds like a win. But when I arrange those bundles inside the bag, I still have to find somewhere to put the next garment. A gap that can take a pair of socks may be useless for a shirt.

Here's the surprising result. Folding fits twenty-two garments. Compression cubes fit nineteen.

Then I count the socks. Folding fits five pairs. The cubes fit eighteen!

If I need twenty garments and five pairs of socks, I'd start with A. If nineteen garments are enough and I need ten pairs, I'd try B. That's a comparison of the reported counts, not a guarantee for my own clothes.

For more garments, folding wins this test. Compression cubes may still help me stay organized, or work better with a different bag. Before I buy another organizer, I'll ask myself one very specific question.
