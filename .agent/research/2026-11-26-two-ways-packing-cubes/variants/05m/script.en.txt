I'm getting ready for a trip with one small carry-on. There's a pile of clothes on my bed and I don't want to bring a second suitcase.

I've seen bags with two zippers that are supposed to help. The first closes around my clothes. The second squeezes the bundle flatter. Do I actually get to take more clothes, or am I just making a neat little package that takes up space of its own?

I'll compare A, folding my clothes directly into my bag, with B, using compression packing cubes. Which would you choose?

I'm going to reconstruct a published test that compared both methods in four kinds of luggage. I haven't repeated the experiment myself. I'll use its reported counts rather than inventing a packing sequence. For each bag, the test started with the same clothing items in the same order, checked the outside size of the soft bags, and used socks to fill small leftover spaces. My main score is the number of garments, with pairs of socks counted separately.

I want to look at the small soft holdall first. Its flexible walls make it tempting to stuff in just one more thing. But the outside size limit still applies.

With A, I can fold a garment to suit the space I have. I can put a stack beside another stack and use a small gap for something else. There's no cube wall to work around. I keep packing until the reported test reaches twenty-two garments. Then five pairs of socks go into the remaining space.

With B, I get a different problem. The compression zipper makes the contents of the cube thinner, but the whole bundle has a shape. I have to fit that shape into the carry-on before I can take advantage of the compression. When I put the next bundle beside it, there may be a strip of space that isn't wide enough for another bundle. Even a bag with flexible sides can't grow without limit.

Now look at the reported result. Nineteen garments. Three fewer than A. If I'm buying the cubes because I want to take one more shirt, that is exactly the wrong result.

But there is another number on the scorecard. The space around the bundles isn't necessarily wasted. The cube method accommodated eighteen pairs of socks, thirteen more than folding. I can have a smaller garment count and more small items at the same time.

If my list calls for twenty garments and five pairs, A clears both counts and B misses the garment target. If my list calls for nineteen garments and ten pairs, B clears both and A misses the sock target. These are numerical targets applied to the test results, not proof that every specific outfit on my list will fit.

Is the small bag just an awkward shape? The other three bags give me a useful check. Folding also beat compression cubes on garment count in the larger hard case, the backpack and the tiny wheeled case. The margins weren't the same: one, five and two garments, respectively. Across all four separate comparisons, folding had an eleven-garment lead. That's not eleven extra clothes in any one bag.

There's an important reason not to turn this into a rule against compression cubes. A separate travel test took a fixed business-trip wardrobe, shoes and other accessories in a larger carry-on. With rolling, everything fit, but there wasn't much room left. With compression cubes, the tester had space left and could close the case easily. No spare-space measurement was given, and the test wasn't the same as my garment-count comparison. I can't combine the numbers. The result does show why I wouldn't dismiss a different packing list.

So what would I actually do before a trip? I'd write down the clothes I need first, pack them directly, and see which ones remain outside. If I use cubes, I'd try the very same set again, keeping the outside size and my packing priorities fixed. If both fit, I can choose whichever makes it easier to find my clothes. If one method leaves a shirt behind, I can judge whether the organization is worth that trade.

The compression zipper can make a package smaller. My real goal is to make the things I need fit inside the carry-on. For the small bag in this comparison, folding wins on garments; cubes win on socks. Now I know which result to look for before I spend any money.
