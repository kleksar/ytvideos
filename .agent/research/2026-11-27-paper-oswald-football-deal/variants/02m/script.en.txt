How did an American football commentator help Disney get a cartoon rabbit? And what did that rabbit have to do with Mickey Mouse?

In 1927, Walt Disney and animator Ub Iwerks created Oswald the Lucky Rabbit. Their studio made twenty-six cartoons for Universal. The rabbit was a hit.

A year later, Walt went to New York to negotiate a new contract. He wanted a higher fee for each film. Instead, he discovered that the distributor, Charles Mintz, had arranged to take much of his animation team. And Universal owned Oswald.

The studio had to finish its remaining rabbit cartoons while developing something of its own. Iwerks stayed with Walt. They made a mouse. After two silent cartoons struggled to find distribution, the sound cartoon Steamboat Willie became a success.

Now jump to 2006. Al Michaels, the longtime voice of Monday Night Football, wanted to leave ABC and ESPN to join John Madden at NBC. But he had a contract.

NBC belonged to the same group that held Oswald's rights. Disney owned ABC and ESPN. Suddenly, the two businesses had something to negotiate.

On February ninth, Disney announced the agreement. Michaels was released to join NBC. Disney acquired Oswald, while ESPN obtained golf broadcasts, Olympic highlight rights and other benefits.

It wasn't simply a man exchanged for a drawing.

In 2010, Oswald appeared alongside Mickey in the video game Epic Mickey. The game imagined them as brothers, with the older one living in the shadow of the younger.

The real rabbit finally had a place at the studio that created him. And the mouse that had replaced him no longer had to stand alone.
