The nearly seventy-three-million-dollar award was about Nerf, not the Super Soaker. Both toy lines owed something to the same engineer.

In 1982, Lonnie Johnson worked at NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory. At home, he was testing an idea for a more environmentally friendly cooling system. Water shot across his bathroom, and he saw the potential for a toy.

Johnson hoped a toy would earn him enough to work on his own inventions full time. But setting up a production line and making the first thousand toys would cost about two hundred thousand dollars. He couldn't afford it.

He looked for a manufacturing partner instead. In 1989, he got a meeting with Larami. He remembers opening his suitcase and sending a stream of water across the conference room.

The Power Drencher reached shops in 1990. A year later, it became the Super Soaker, generating over two hundred million dollars in sales.

Hasbro eventually acquired Larami. Johnson's patents also formed the basis of the Nerf N-Strike line.

Years later, Johnson's company and Hasbro disagreed about which toys qualified for royalty payments under two different licenses. In 2013, the Nerf dispute produced that enormous award. The Super Soaker dispute was a separate lawsuit.

Hasbro challenged the decision. In February 2014, it agreed to pay Johnson's company fifty-eight million and forty thousand dollars to settle both disputes.

Johnson continued developing energy technology. A toy had given him a route into independent invention. So which toy was at the heart of that famous award?
