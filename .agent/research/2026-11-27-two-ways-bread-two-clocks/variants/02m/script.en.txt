I can leave my bread on the counter, or put it in the fridge. Which would you choose?

I want two things: soft bread and less mold. In this illustrated comparison, I'm using the results of a published storage test, not a new experiment in my kitchen.

I start with matching wheat loaves from the same batch, in the same kind of plastic bag. A stays at about twenty-two degrees Celsius. B goes into a fridge at about eight degrees, the temperature used in that test.

I'm tracking two things separately: the day mold becomes visible, and the force needed to compress the bread.

By day four or five, the bread on the counter has visible mold. That loaf is out. In the fridge, no mold was detected during the ten days of the test, even in the laboratory checks.

But there's a second result. The refrigerated bread became firmer, faster. The two bags had almost the same humidity, so I can't explain this by saying the fridge simply dried the bread out.

Inside the crumb, starch gradually forms more ordered structures as bread ages. Refrigerator temperatures speed that process up. So I can slow mold down and still make my bread stale faster.

I wouldn't eat the moldy loaf, or assume the other one is safe just because ten days passed without visible mold in this test.

My decision depends on how soon I need the bread. For a few upcoming meals, the counter may suit me. If I need more time without a freezer, the fridge can delay mold. For later meals, I'll freeze portions while they're fresh.
