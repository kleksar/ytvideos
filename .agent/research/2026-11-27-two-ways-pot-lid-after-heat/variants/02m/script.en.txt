My pot lid makes the biggest difference after the water is already hot.

A: I leave my pot open. B: I cover it. Which would you pick? I'll compare heating time and electricity use. This is a replay of published experiments, not my own test.

In each version I start with about two liters of water in a small stainless steel pot, using the same induction setting. The modeled timer starts at twenty degrees Celsius and stops at ninety.

Without a lid, thirteen minutes and twenty-three seconds. With a lid, twelve minutes and seven seconds. I've saved just over a minute and about nine percent of the electricity.

Now I change the job. In a separate published test, I have a bigger pot with seven and a half liters of water. It's already at ninety degrees. I need to keep it there for forty-five minutes.

I can turn the heat much lower under the covered pot. Both versions stay near the target temperature. The open pot uses about two thirds of a kilowatt-hour. The covered one uses about one sixth.

That's nearly three quarters less energy for this stage. In the reported test, the open pot also loses almost a kilogram of water. The covered pot loses about eight grams.

Much less water escapes as vapor. When my covered soup gets hot enough, I'd lower the heat. If I'm reducing a sauce, though, I want water to escape.

These numbers apply to the tested pots and settings, not every dinner. But when I'm keeping something hot, when does my lid make the biggest difference?
