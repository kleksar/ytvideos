I can rinse rice ten times and still end up with sticky rice. Would you rinse it or put it straight into the cooker?

I want separate grains, so I'm comparing how much the cooked rice sticks, not how clean the water looks. I'm using a reconstruction of a published experiment, not my own measurements.

On side A, I leave the rice unwashed. On side B, I stir it in fresh water, drain it and repeat ten times. Both portions come from the same bag. They get the same final amount of cooking water and the same thirty minutes of steaming.

I start with jasmine rice. A small instrument presses the cooked grains and measures how strongly they hold on when it pulls away. The ten-rinse portion has a lower average reading. So far, washing looks like the better choice.

Then I repeat the comparison with sticky rice. This time, the washed portion actually has a higher average reading. Across three varieties, the experiment didn't establish a significant overall effect of washing on stickiness. The response depended on the variety.

Here's what I was missing. The cloudy water carries loose material from the outside of the raw grains. But as the rice cooks, starch from inside the grains comes out and coats them again. Washing the outside doesn't remove that supply.

If I want a different texture, I'll choose the right variety first, then follow its cooking and washing instructions. Rinsing can remove surface debris, but I won't keep rinsing just to chase a guaranteed separation of the grains.

Because even after I pour away the last bowl of cloudy water, what happens when the starch inside the rice comes out?
