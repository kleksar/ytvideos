## Fact ledger

All references are actual opened public source pages or readable archive excerpts. Use source locations by question heading / dated document; paraphrase in our own words.

- E1: NPS concept and design https://www.nps.gov/edis/learn/photosmultimedia/talking-doll-faq-concept-design-and-technical-development.htm — 1877-11-23 notebook NV17018; Jacques apparatus 1887-06-22 D8750AAA; Batchelor work NB011AAA and MBJ004 March 1888 diaphragms/return mechanisms; 1888 tin records, subsequent wax. Do not say Edison single-handedly invented or manufactured everything.
- E2: NPS cultural history https://www.nps.gov/edis/learn/photosmultimedia/a-cultural-history-of-the-edison-talking-doll-record.htm — paragraphs with notes 19–22: toy mechanism patents and earlier wax/tin experiments, commercial wax in 1890. Individual recordings and hired performers; no names/age assumptions for the particular recovered speaker. 
- E3: Smithsonian object and article https://www.americanhistory.si.edu/collections/object/nmah_1413900 and https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smithsonian-institution/epic-failure-thomas-edisons-talking-doll-180955442/ — object dimensions, porcelain, tin torso, openings, hand crank, horn. NPS sound recordings https://www.nps.gov/edis/learn/photosmultimedia/talking-doll-faq-sound-recordings.htm — duration 18–22 seconds, speed irregularity, poor sound even when new, individual recording work, investor Spalding 1890-03-06 D9060AAM. Physical acoustic mechanism explained by groove/needle/diaphragm, no reconstructed dialogue.
- E4: NPS industrial production https://www.nps.gov/edis/learn/photosmultimedia/talking-doll-faq-industrial-production.htm — D8964AAG 1889-02-21 order of 100,000 bodies, not deliveries; West Orange assembly; February to early May 1890; 1891 memorandum 10,060 accepted and approx. 7,500 still packed, not an audited retail sales count.
- E5: NPS economics https://www.nps.gov/edis/learn/photosmultimedia/talking-doll-faq-economics.htm — New York Tribune 1890-04-20, $10 undressed / $12–20 dressed. No contemporary inflation conversion. NPS marketing https://www.nps.gov/edis/learn/photosmultimedia/talking-doll-faq-marketing-and-sales.htm — 1888 reports, Scientific American 1890-04-26 cover, adult gift buyers probable.
- E6: NPS durability https://www.nps.gov/edis/learn/photosmultimedia/talking-doll-faq-product-durability.htm — retailer letter 1890-04-25 D9060ABD: several returns, loose works, silence, Salem voice faded during an hour, St Joseph return cost approx $1.60. Groove wear is consistent with this report but not a diagnosis. Prototype needle detachments and cracked wax, planned lining change; separate failure modes. July 18 letter D9060ACM requests 50 handmade mechanisms after model examination; not claim produced or commercial second run. End October SC90030 debt >$50k, no loan/funding. Stopping production E4.
- E7: NPS Early Talking Doll Recording Discovered https://www.nps.gov/edis/learn/photosmultimedia/early-talking-doll-recording-discovered.htm — May 11, 2011 recovery, Carl Haber and Earl Cornell, bent solid-metal cylinder from fall/winter 1888, unidentified woman's verse of Twinkle, Twinkle, Little Star, all except first syllable. 3D surface scanning and digital signal recovery; no claim every word sounded the same on original hardware. NPS archive overview https://home.nps.gov/edis/learn/photosmultimedia/edison-talking-doll-recordings-1888-1890.htm and catalog https://home.nps.gov/edis/learn/photosmultimedia/hear-edison-talking-doll-sound-recordings.htm — later wax/metal examples; eight examples publicly cataloged by 2015. Distinct artifact from the returned commercial doll. Audio bytes not retrieved/listened; exact record and image reuse rights not cleared.
- Do not assert haunted doll, customer fear, 100,000 sales, precise number of retail sales, cause of Salem failure, actual first consumer recording without qualification, or actual listening to original sound. No actual museum audio needed for the text payoff.
