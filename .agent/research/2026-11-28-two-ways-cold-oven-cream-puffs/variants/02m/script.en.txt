I have two ways to bake six cream puffs: put them in a cold oven, or wait ten minutes for the oven to heat. Which would you pick?

I want to use as little electricity as possible, but the puffs need to keep at least ninety percent of the size I'd get with preheating, and their tops need to look about the same.

I'm using a model based on published baking tests, not a new kitchen experiment. Both trays have six equal portions of dough. The oven, temperature setting and ten-minute rest in the switched-off oven are the same.

With A, I heat the oven for ten minutes, bake for eighteen, then let the puffs rest inside. With B, I start with a cold oven and give the dough twenty-one minutes of powered baking before that same rest.

A takes about thirty-eight minutes from a cold oven. B takes thirty-one. But have I made the puffs too small?

The average puff from A has a volume of about 135 milliliters. B gives me about 125. The tops are similar in color. B keeps about ninety-three percent of the volume and uses twenty-three percent less electricity.

So B wins under my rules. But if I need at least ninety-five percent of A's volume, even the other color-matched cold-start options in this comparison fall short. A wins that version of the test.

For this particular oven and dough, skipping the wait is a useful trade, not a free upgrade. When the next tray is ready, what are my two choices before baking?
