I can switch on a light that warns me when I've used twenty litres in the shower. But will my whole home actually use less water?

I have two identical shower heads. In A, the light stays off. In B, it changes colour as the water runs, and a little sign explains the colours. Nothing automatically shuts the water off.

Which would you pick? I'm going to judge both by the water meter for the entire home.

I'm using a model based on a real household trial in Singapore, not measurements from my own bathroom.

In that study, people initially thought a shower used about thirteen litres. The recorded average was about twenty-five. That's a lot of water they didn't realise they were using.

Once the warnings were switched on, the average saving was over five litres per shower.

Now I'll put that result into a daily household water budget, using the study's estimates. Both sides start with 496 litres a day: 163 for showers and 333 for everything else.

On side B, the shower category drops by 37 litres. If the rest of the house stays unchanged, I'd expect the meter to fall by 37 too.

But the reported total saving is smaller. Water use outside the shower category rises by an estimated 13.5 litres. The researchers couldn't establish exactly what people did differently.

That leaves a net saving of 23.5 litres a day. B still wins, but roughly a third of the shower saving has disappeared.

I wouldn't judge a shower gadget by its own reading. I'd check the whole home's water meter. And if I want to test the warning without changing the plumbing, what can I change?
