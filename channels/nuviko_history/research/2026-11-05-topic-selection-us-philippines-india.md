## Editorial hypothesis

Research Anandibai Joshee first (India, 1883–1887). This is a story-selection hypothesis based on a clear, individual goal and arc, primary archival material, an unambiguous but tragic closure, and low known overlap with the Nuviko History catalog. It is not based on the combined geography share. First test whether her own letters support the decisions and motivations needed for a fair narration. If they do not, move to the Pinatubo warning/evacuation story.

## Candidate 1 — Anandibai Joshee

**Angle.** Can a young woman who crosses an ocean to study medicine return home and begin treating women? Follow Joshee from her decision to pursue medical training, through study in Philadelphia and graduation in 1886, to an appointment at the Albert Edward Hospital’s women’s ward in Kolhapur. She returned to India ill and died on February 26, 1887, before beginning practice.

**Documentary base.** Drexel University’s Legacy Center identifies digitized admission and funding correspondence, faculty/minutes and matriculation records, 1886 commencement materials, and Joshee’s medical thesis. Its guide also documents the appointment and death. The Library of Congress provides an institutional historical profile. [Drexel Legacy Center archive guide](https://drexel.edu/legacy-center/the-collections/research-guides/joshee/); [Library of Congress profile](https://blogs.loc.gov/international-collections/2020/03/anandibai-joshee-the-first-indian-woman-to-earn-a-medical-degree-in-the-united-states/).

**Open question / boundary.** Biographies differ in their account of her husband’s role and her agency. Read Joshee’s letters before establishing the motive or sequence of her decisions; do not invent interior thoughts. A substantial part of the story happens in the United States. If a “first” is used, prefer the narrow documented wording “the first woman from India to receive a medical degree in the United States.”

## Candidate 2 — Mount Pinatubo warnings and evacuation

**Angle.** How did scientists and public officials persuade people to move while the timing and size of an eruption were still uncertain? Start with unrest on April 2, 1991; follow PHIVOLCS and USGS monitoring, hazard maps, alerts and evacuation decisions; end with the June 15 eruption and the human and community aftermath.

**Documentary base.** The USGS account records the work of Filipino and US scientists, the uncertainty in forecasting, instruments and hazard map, the evacuation from Clark and surrounding areas, the June 12 and June 15 eruptions, and later lahars. PHIVOLCS’s eruption history dates the Pinatubo eruption sequence June 9–September 4, 1991. A participant report by Punongbayan et al. provides the chronology of hazard assessments and warnings. [USGS, Mount Pinatubo 25-year retrospective](https://www.usgs.gov/news/featured-story/remembering-mount-pinatubo-25-years-ago-mitigating-a-crisis); [PHIVOLCS eruption history](https://vmepd.phivolcs.dost.gov.ph/volcan/erupt-history?page=2); [Punongbayan et al., warning assessments](https://pubs.usgs.gov/pinatubo/punong2/); [PHIVOLCS retrospective](https://www.phivolcs.dost.gov.ph/looking-back-and-moving-forward-the-stories-and-lessons-of-the-1991-pinatubo-volcano-eruption/).

**Open question / boundary.** The reported “at least 5,000 lives saved” is an estimate, not a count; keep it qualified. Give Filipino scientists, officials, residents and Aeta communities narrative agency, and account for displacement and long-running lahar impacts. The Nuviko registry has a legacy Galveston 1900 reference without a current context file, while Paper Detour has a separate complete Galveston episode. This is not an exact duplicate, but the natural-disaster framing could feel repetitive; the differentiating story is warning, evacuation and aftermath.

## Candidate 3 — 1925 Serum Run to Nome

**Angle.** How could urgently needed diphtheria antitoxin reach Nome when the port was frozen and aircraft could not reach it? Follow the town’s quarantine and the relay of medicine by rail to Nenana, then by dog teams to Nome. The Alaska State Archives states that the 674-mile relay delivered the serum in 127.5 hours and provides the 1925 governor’s correspondence files. NPS documents the outbreak, the decision to use multiple sled teams, and the difference between Togo’s and Balto’s legs. [Alaska State Archives — Serum Run of 1925](https://archives.alaska.gov/education/serum.html); [National Park Service — Togo](https://www.nps.gov/people/togo.htm); [Smithsonian — commemorative serum-run medal](https://avpreservation.si.edu/object/nmah_1445140).

**Open question / boundary.** Avoid retelling this as a Balto-only legend. Verify the relay stages and individual contributions; avoid claims about a precise number of people directly saved. There is an active project-chat story about the Boston flooding, so the US candidate is not Boston. The Serum Run is less tied to that work, but its familiar popular retellings make a fresh team-centered angle necessary.
