In Boston, a giant tank of molasses stood near people's homes. It had been leaking for so long that children collected the sweet drips in buckets.

On January fifteenth, nineteen nineteen, the tank burst. A wave of molasses rushed through the streets, destroying buildings. Twenty-one people died, and around one hundred and fifty were injured.

The company that owned the tank claimed someone had blown it up. But how could that explain a tank that had leaked for years?

After years of legal proceedings, the company was held responsible. The tank had been poorly built, and signs of danger had not been properly addressed. The warning had been visible before the disaster.
