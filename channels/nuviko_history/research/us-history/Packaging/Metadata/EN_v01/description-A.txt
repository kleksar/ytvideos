In 1803, two American negotiators went to France to buy New Orleans. Napoleon’s government offered them all of Louisiana. A revolution in what is now Haiti had upended France’s plans in the Americas, and war with Britain was looming.

The Louisiana Purchase transferred France’s claims to the United States. Indigenous nations still lived and governed on that land. As the country pushed west, the deal opened a struggle over where slavery could spread and who would have the power to decide.
