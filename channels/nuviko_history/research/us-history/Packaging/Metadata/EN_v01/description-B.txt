Congress drew a line across part of the Louisiana Purchase. It banned slavery north of that line, with Missouri as the exception. Congress paired Maine’s admission as a free state with Missouri’s admission as a slave state. The bargain preserved the balance in the Senate for a time. Why did it stop working?

As the United States reached the Pacific, the question of slavery in new territories returned with greater force. Follow the argument from Congress to violence in Kansas and the Supreme Court’s ruling in Dred Scott’s case, then see how the dispute led to secession and war.
