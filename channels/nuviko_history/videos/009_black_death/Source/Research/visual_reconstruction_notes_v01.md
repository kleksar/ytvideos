# Visual reconstruction notes v01

Research supports what may be shown; the retained narration remains unchanged.

- The London Museum identifies East Smithfield as an emergency burial ground near the Tower of London, says its burials were carefully laid in rows rather than thrown into pits, and records 181 coins found with one woman. The museum describes fear of infection while removing clothing only as a possible explanation; visuals must keep her motive unknown. Source: [The Great Pestilence in London](https://www.londonmuseum.org.uk/collections/london-stories/great-pestilence-london/).
- Historic England describes the 1348–50 East Smithfield burials as orderly internments in individual graves and trenches. Source: [Commemorative and funerary scheduled monuments](https://historicengland.org.uk/images-books/publications/dssg-commemorative-funerary/heag239-commemorative-and-funerary-ssg/).
- The State Archives in Dubrovnik cites the 27 July 1377 decision in the *Liber viridis*: arrivals from plague-affected places had to spend 30 days in isolation before entry. Temporary isolation sites predate the large surviving Lazareti complex. Source: [Državni arhiv u Dubrovniku, HR-DADU-107 inventory](https://dad.hr/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/HR-DADU-107-Sumarni-inventar-Uprava-Lazareta-u-Dubrovniku.pdf?lang=es).
- London Museum records plague reaching London by November 1348 and the emergency cemeteries at East and West Smithfield. Its overview is the visual chronology reference, not a substitute for the episode's research ledger.

Generated art is reconstruction, not documentary evidence. Do not draw exact grave layouts, clothing details, medical procedures, or specific individuals beyond what the narration and cited institutional records support. In particular, show no causal motive for the 181 coins and no invented “first patient.”
