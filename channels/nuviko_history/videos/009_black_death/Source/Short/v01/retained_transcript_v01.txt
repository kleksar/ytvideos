When the sick person was a stranger, you could step away. But what could you do when the person who fell ill lived with you? A home, where a person should have been cared for, could become a place of isolation. Not everyone had the means to leave. Waiting out the disaster in the countryside required money, shelter, and people to provide for everyday life. But if everyone leaves, who will bring water to the sick? Who will bake bread? Who will take away the dead? This was one of the epidemic's hardest contradictions. Staying away from the sick could reduce the danger to an individual, yet someone still had to care for those who could no longer help themselves.
