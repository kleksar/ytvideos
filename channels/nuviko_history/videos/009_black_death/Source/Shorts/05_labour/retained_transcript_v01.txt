After the Black Death, a worker's bargaining power could change, because so many workers had died. Across England, fields and workshops still needed labour, but the supply of people had fallen. Some survivors could seek better wages or another employer. Landowners faced higher costs. The government tried to hold wages down. An ordinance in 1349 and the Statute of Labourers in 1351 sought to control pay and compel work under set conditions. Those laws did not simply restore the old order, and change was uneven.
