# Marseille 1720 — production fact-check notes v01

Scope: check names, dates and the causal framing against Marseille's city history museum and the Bouches-du-Rhône departmental archives. The source narration and its story are retained as instructed. These notes guide subtitle spelling and visual choices; they do not authorize rewriting or re-voicing the source.

## Corroborated sequence

- The Grand Saint-Antoine returned to Marseille on 25 May 1720 with valuable cargo. Captain Jean-Baptiste Chataud filed a clean health patent despite multiple deaths during the voyage. The ship's surgeon was among the dead. [Musée d'Histoire de Marseille: arrival of the Grand Saint-Antoine](https://musee-histoire.marseille.fr/node/2295)
- The health register for 25 May 1720 records Chataud's declaration. The departmental archives summarize the decision to let passengers and goods reach the mainland Lazaret, while ships suspected of plague were often sent to Jarre. The path from textiles to contagion is presented there as the most common explanation, not a fully documented chain. [Archives départementales des Bouches-du-Rhône: declaration of 25 May 1720](https://www.archives13.fr/n/documents-a-dechiffrer/n%3A292)
- The Marseille museum places the first reported illness on rue Belle-Table on 20 June, Gairard's recognition of plague in porters on 7 July, and Jean-André Peyssonnel's diagnosis on 10 July. It also identifies his father Charles as dying in September 1720. [Musée d'Histoire de Marseille: the outbreak in the city](https://musee-histoire.marseille.fr/index.php/node/2308) and [spread of the plague](https://musee-histoire.marseille.fr/node/2306)
- The museum's account says the captain's cargo was valued at about 100,000 écus and connected to the Beaucaire fair. It describes the ship's arrival, the quarantine decisions and the spreading outbreak. [Musée d'Histoire de Marseille: saving the cargo](https://musee-histoire.marseille.fr/index.php/node/2309)
- The museum says the epidemic of 1720–1722 reached Provence and killed about half of Marseille's population. Wider Provence totals are uncertain; the narration's approximate 100,000 figure is not treated as an exact count. [Musée d'Histoire de Marseille: plague chronicle](https://musee-histoire.marseille.fr/pages/chroniques-de-la-peste-1720-1722)

## Subtitle orthography corrections

The alignment source follows the existing audio/transcript. For captions only, use the historically correct spellings below where the spoken word matches the name:

- `Chateau` → `Chataud`
- `Pessonel` → `Peyssonnel`
- `Gerard` → `Gairard`
- `Beaucer` → `Beaucaire`
- `Belle Table` → `Belle-Table`
- `Chateau d'If` → `Château d'If`
- `ecu` → `écus`
- `Echevin` → `échevin`

Keep the voice and timing intact. Record these as orthographic subtitle corrections, not spoken-word substitutions. Do not add source credits or other editorial text to the image; the only on-screen words remain narration subtitles.
