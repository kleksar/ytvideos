## Story brief and changes

- Central question: how did a reconstructed annual basket's cost in London labourer wage-days change across selected periods?
- Answer: approximately 333 before the Black Death, 176 in 1500–1549, 216 in 1600–1649 and 116 in 1850–1899. The early-modern reversal is explained by consumer prices growing faster than daily silver wages.
- Initial intuitive version: a much higher daily payment should make the same necessities easier to afford. Strongest alternative tested: prices may have risen still faster. Wage and CPI comparisons establish that alternative for the selected periods.
- Hook: concrete necessities and a modelled income shortfall. Model status is audible from the opening and explained once in the introduction.
- Development: shortfall → post-plague labour conflict in a primary legal text → later earnings above the model line → 176-wage-day benchmark → higher daily silver pay.
- Participation: predict whether the basket will need fewer or more wage-days after the daily wage more than doubles; immediate payoff in the next section.
- Climax: 216 rather than 176; the CPI grew to about 2.78 times its earlier value versus 2.22 for wages.
- Resolution: 116 later; distinguish greater basic purchasing power from observed household spending, guaranteed work or leisure.
- Ending: return to ordinary goods and the daily payment, without repeating all four values or opening another factual mystery.
- Thrinaxodon and clothing-history references influence presentation only: concrete opening, direct sequence, vivid conflict and limited repetition. No subject matter, Paper Detour aesthetics or Shorts loop imported.
