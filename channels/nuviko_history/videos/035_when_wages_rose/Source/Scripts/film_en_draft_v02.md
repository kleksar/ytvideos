The model adds a minimum rent markup of five percent, not an observed rent bill. [C6]

A welfare ratio of one means assumed annual earnings equal the basket's modeled cost. Above one, earnings exceed that cost; below one, they fall short. [C6]

We can turn the ratio around: how many days' worth of wages, at the modeled daily rate, equal the basket's cost? Divide 250 by the welfare ratio. [C8]

This gives us a way to compare periods. But these are model-implied wage-day equivalents, not recorded days at work. They do not tell us whether work was available, how many days someone actually worked, or what a particular family experienced. [C6][C8]
