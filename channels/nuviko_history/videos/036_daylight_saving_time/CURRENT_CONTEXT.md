## Story limits

- Germany was first to introduce a nationwide daylight-saving regime in 1916; proposals and local experiments preceded it. Do not say Germany invented the idea or was first to change clocks at any scale.
- Britain received royal assent on 17 May 1916; first application was 21 May.
- U.S. federal seasonal daylight saving began in 1918 as a wartime measure. In 1919 Congress repealed that provision over Wilson's veto after multiple objections, including documented agricultural complaints.
- Congressional records document particular complaints about dew delaying hired field work and shops closing early; do not generalize to every farm.
- U.S. year-round War Time ran from 9 February 1942 to 30 September 1945; its actual end was set by a separate act.
- The Uniform Time Act of 1966 set a common schedule while allowing states to opt out. The current U.S. dates took effect in 2007.
- Safe causal limit: war accelerated national adoption and spread of an existing idea. No single German-origin cause for modern worldwide practice is established.
