## Consulted recommendation

Dramatic engine: “War switched it on. Peace repealed it. A new war brought it back.” The main question is not who invented DST, but why the United States repealed it and later returned to it.

**Central question:** Why did the United States repeal daylight saving time despite Wilson's veto, then return to it?

**Causal chain:** existing proposals/local trials → Germany nationwide in 1916 and Britain in May → U.S. seasonal federal shift in 1918 for wartime resource economy → peacetime objections including documented agricultural complaints → 1919 repeal over Wilson's veto → year-round War Time in 1942, ended by separate law in 1945 → common schedule with state opt-out in 1966 and current dates in 2007.

**Six-beat allocation suggested:** hook 0–6s; intro 6–13s; development 13–26s; climax 26–41s; resolution 41–55s; ending 55–63s. Climax is the 1919 repeal, not Germany's first national use. Keep 1966/2007 as a compact coda.

**Participation point:** ask why more evening light did not make field work easier; immediately answer that clocks moved but the sun and dew did not. Congressional testimony should be framed as particular complaints, not universal farm experience.

**Proposed loop:** end with “...when” and begin “In 1916 Germany...”; the supplied English sample was a grammatical causal join. The Russian draft uses the equivalent “...когда в 1916 году...”.

## Editorial checks / limits

- “Бунт” in the consultant's sample hook overstates the evidence; the script should say opposition or conflict.
- The viewer question should not imply every farmer was affected; phrase it as asking why some farmers objected.
- The historical source attribution “Congressional records” describes a primary event/document, not researcher authority. Keep internal source IDs and omit bibliography from narration.
