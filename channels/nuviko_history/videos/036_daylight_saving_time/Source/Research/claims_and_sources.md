# Claims and sources — 036

Internal research record. Public narration and packaging should not cite research sources by default.

| Claim / limit | Source and exact location | What it supports / boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Germany's national regime began 1 May 1916 at 23:00 on 30 April; proposals and local experiments preceded it. | Reichsgesetzblatt 1916 No. 67, p. 243, order No. 5130 (6 Apr 1916); NARA, “Daylight Saving Time Begins, 1916” (26 Mar 2019), https://text-message.blogs.archives.gov/2019/03/26/daylight-saving-time-begins-1916/; Hansard Lords (16 May 1916), cols. 1039–1041, https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/lords/1916/may/16/summer-time-bill-1 | Supports first national regime and earlier parliamentary proposals. A local 1908 Port Arthur/Fort William trial is reported by Thunder Bay Public Library, https://www.tbpl.ca/blogs/post/the-origins-of-daylight-savings-time-tbpl-research-blog/; that page was not independently accessible in the audit. Avoid claiming a first at every scale or inventorship. |
| Britain first applied Summer Time on 21 May 1916; Royal Assent was 17 May. | Summer Time Act 1916, ch. 14, p. 1, §1(1)–(2), https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/Geo5/6-7/14/pdfs/ukpga_19160014_en.pdf; Hansard Commons (15 May 1916), cols. 1318–1324, https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/commons/1916/may/15/clause-1-local-time-in-summer-months | Distinguish enactment from first application. |
| U.S. federal seasonal daylight saving was introduced in 1918 and its provision repealed in 1919 over Wilson's veto. | Standard Time Act, 19 Mar 1918, 40 Stat. 451, §3, https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/STATUTE-40/pdf/STATUTE-40-Pg451.pdf; Public Law 66-40, 20 Aug 1919, 41 Stat. 280–281, https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/STATUTE-41/pdf/STATUTE-41-Pg280.pdf; Congressional Record (19 Aug 1919), pp. 3980–3982, https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GPO-CRECB-1919-pt4-v58/pdf/GPO-CRECB-1919-pt4-v58-19-1.pdf | The 1919 repeal removed §3, not time zones. The veto was overridden by House (19 Aug) and Senate (20 Aug). Agricultural objections were important but not the only objections. |
| The record includes complaints about dew delaying field work and shops closing early. | Congressional Record, House (18 Jun 1919), vol. 58, pp. 1305–1307, https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GPO-CRECB-1919-pt2-v58/pdf/GPO-CRECB-1919-pt2-v58-5.pdf; U.S. House History, “Time Travel: Daylight Saving Time and the House,” https://history.house.gov/Blog/2019/November/11-1-Daylight-Saving/ | Supports existence and content of specific testimony; not universal prevalence or measured national impact. |
| U.S. year-round War Time ran during WWII and ended on 30 Sep 1945 by separate legislation. | Public Law 77-403 (20 Jan 1942), 56 Stat. 9, §§1–2, https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/STATUTE-56/pdf/STATUTE-56-Pg9.pdf; Public Law 79-187 (25 Sep 1945), 59 Stat. 537, https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/STATUTE-59/pdf/STATUTE-59-Pg537.pdf; U.S. Naval Observatory, https://aa.usno.navy.mil/faq/daylight_time | Establishes the year-round national clock advance and its statutory end date. No continuity between 1919 and 1942 is implied. |
| Uniform Time Act standardized seasonal dates but permitted state exemption; current U.S. dates began in 2007. | Public Law 89-387 (13 Apr 1966), 80 Stat. 107–108, §§3, 6, https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/STATUTE-80/pdf/STATUTE-80-Pg107.pdf; 15 U.S.C. §260a, https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-121/t15/s260a/; NIST, https://www.nist.gov/pml/time-and-frequency-division/popular-links/daylight-saving-time-dst; U.S. DOT, https://www.transportation.gov/regulations/time-act | 1966 created the initial common schedule; current dates were introduced in 2007. NIST describes rules; DOT is federal regulator. |
| War accelerated adoption and national spread of an existing idea, but is not demonstrated as the singular cause of modern practice. | Hansard Lords (16 May 1916), cols. 1039–1041; Austrian State Archive, https://wk1.staatsarchiv.at/alltag-hinterland-verpflegung/einfuehrung-der-sommerzeit/; Congressional Record (18 Jun 1919), pp. 1306–1307; U.S. Naval Observatory, https://aa.usno.navy.mil/faq/daylight_time | Supports wartime conservation rationale and separate national choices. Do not infer that Germany's 1916 act was necessary or sufficient for modern worldwide observance. |
