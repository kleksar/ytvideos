# Daylight Saving Time: How War Shifted the Clock

**Status:** English production language adaptation of accepted Russian story v01. It preserves accepted events and meaning. No audio has been generated.

## HOOK

In 1916, Germany became the first country to adopt daylight saving time nationwide, during wartime.

America adopted it, repealed it despite a presidential veto, then restored it. Why?

## INTRO

Earlier proposals and local trials existed. War accelerated adoption.

## DEVELOPMENT

In 1918, America shifted clocks to save resources. After the war, savings were disputed.

## CLIMAX

Why did this trouble some farmers?

Clocks shifted. The sun and dew didn't.

At hearings, some complained: hired workers waited for dew to dry; stores in nearby towns closed an hour earlier. Other objections arose.

In 1919, Congress repealed federal seasonal clock changes, overriding Wilson's veto.

## RESOLUTION

In 1942, another war restored it: clocks advanced an hour year-round. Separate legislation ended it September 30, 1945.

## FINALE

1966 standardized scheduling with state opt-outs. Today's dates began in 2007.
