How do you fix a spacecraft nearly 24 billion kilometers away without ever reaching it? Launched in 1977 to explore Jupiter and Saturn, Voyager 1 kept traveling long after those encounters. In November 2023, its messages became unreadable. Its radio signal was still reaching Earth, and the spacecraft was still receiving commands, but its scientific measurements and reports on its own condition could no longer be interpreted.

Every attempt took patience: a command needed about 22.5 hours to arrive, and its reply took just as long. In March 2024, engineers decoded the onboard computer's memory and found damage affecting about three percent of it. The necessary program could not fit into any single free section. They divided the code, moved the pieces into undamaged memory, and updated the references to their new addresses.

The fix was sent on April 18. On April 20, readable status information returned for the first time in five months. By June, all four operating scientific instruments were sending data again. The damaged memory remained damaged.
