Almost 45 hours could pass before engineers knew whether a single attempt on Voyager 1 had worked. At roughly 24 billion kilometers from Earth, a radio command needed about 22.5 hours to reach the spacecraft, and its reply needed just as long to return.

Voyager 1 had launched in 1977 to explore Jupiter and Saturn. In November 2023, its messages became unreadable, although the radio signal continued and the spacecraft still received commands. For months, engineers worked without understandable scientific measurements or reports on its condition. In March 2024, they decoded the contents of its computer memory and discovered that about three percent was damaged. The chip could not be repaired, and the necessary program would not fit into one free memory section. Engineers split the code and updated the references to its new addresses.

The fix was transmitted on April 18. On April 20, the first readable status information in five months arrived. By June, all four still-operating scientific instruments were sending data again. The damaged memory was never physically repaired.
