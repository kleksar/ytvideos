## A — How Do You Fix Voyager 1 From 24 Billion Kilometers Away?

![Обложка A](/Users/diplomat/ytvideos/channels/nuviko_history/videos/037_voyager_1_repair/Packaging/Thumbnails/v01/thumbnail_A_v01.png)

[Полное описание A](/Users/diplomat/ytvideos/channels/nuviko_history/videos/037_voyager_1_repair/Packaging/Descriptions/v01/description_A_en_v01.txt)

How do you fix a spacecraft nearly 24 billion kilometers away without ever reaching it? Launched in 1977 to explore Jupiter and Saturn, Voyager 1 kept traveling long after those encounters. In November 2023, its messages became unreadable. Its radio signal was still reaching Earth, and the spacecraft was still receiving commands, but its scientific measurements and reports on its own condition could no longer be interpreted.

Every attempt took patience: a command needed about 22.5 hours to arrive, and its reply took just as long. In March 2024, engineers decoded the onboard computer's memory and found damage affecting about three percent of it. The necessary program could not fit into any single free section. They divided the code, moved the pieces into undamaged memory, and updated the references to their new addresses.

The fix was sent on April 18. On April 20, readable status information returned for the first time in five months. By June, all four operating scientific instruments were sending data again. The damaged memory remained damaged.

## B — They Couldn't Repair Voyager 1's Memory. So They Split the Code.

![Обложка B](/Users/diplomat/ytvideos/channels/nuviko_history/videos/037_voyager_1_repair/Packaging/Thumbnails/v01/thumbnail_B_v01.png)

[Полное описание B](/Users/diplomat/ytvideos/channels/nuviko_history/videos/037_voyager_1_repair/Packaging/Descriptions/v01/description_B_en_v01.txt)

Damaged memory had left Voyager 1 unable to prepare readable messages. The instructions it needed were stored in the faulty section. Launched in 1977 to explore Jupiter and Saturn, the spacecraft was roughly 24 billion kilometers away when its data became unreadable in November 2023. It could still receive commands, but Earth could no longer interpret its scientific measurements or system status.

In March 2024, engineers decoded a diagnostic memory readout. About three percent of the memory was damaged. Replacing the chip was impossible, and no single free section of undamaged memory was large enough to hold the necessary code. The team split the code into smaller pieces, placed them in different intact locations, and changed the references so the instructions could work together again.

With radio signals taking about 22.5 hours each way, testing required patience. A fix sent on April 18 brought readable status information on April 20, the first in five months. By June, data from all four operating scientific instruments had returned. The original damage remained.

## C — Almost 45 Hours Per Attempt: Voyager 1's First Readable Reply in Five Months

Almost 45 hours could pass before engineers knew whether a single attempt on Voyager 1 had worked. At roughly 24 billion kilometers from Earth, a radio command needed about 22.5 hours to reach the spacecraft, and its reply needed just as long to return.

Voyager 1 had launched in 1977 to explore Jupiter and Saturn. In November 2023, its messages became unreadable, although the radio signal continued and the spacecraft still received commands. For months, engineers worked without understandable scientific measurements or reports on its condition. In March 2024, they decoded the contents of its computer memory and discovered that about three percent was damaged. The chip could not be repaired, and the necessary program would not fit into one free memory section. Engineers split the code and updated the references to its new addresses.

The fix was transmitted on April 18. On April 20, the first readable status information in five months arrived. By June, all four still-operating scientific instruments were sending data again. The damaged memory was never physically repaired.
