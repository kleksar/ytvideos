On July 8, 1941, German commander Fritz Schlieper signed a military report that acknowledged the heavy cost of assaulting Brest Fortress.

The fighting had begun on June 22, when German artillery struck the fortress. Its separated sections became isolated battlefields, while Soviet defenders struggled with disrupted communications, dwindling supplies, scarce water, and wounded comrades.

By the end of June, the main organized defense had ended. Yet individual soldiers and small groups continued resisting into July. Major Pyotr Gavrilov was captured on July 23.

Schlieper's report recorded 453 killed or missing and 668 wounded in his division during the fighting for Brest-Litovsk as a whole, not exclusively within the fortress.

This film follows the battle and what the German commander's account reveals about the resistance his division encountered.
