The German commander who attacked Brest Fortress later acknowledged how costly it was to assault a determined defense. His own report offers a striking view from the other side of the battle.

On June 22, 1941, artillery struck the fortress as German forces advanced into the Soviet Union. Soviet troops fought from isolated positions, struggling with broken communications, wounded comrades and a desperate shortage of water.

By the end of June, organized defense had largely collapsed, but individuals and small groups continued resisting into July. This film follows the defenders’ struggle and examines what the commander’s report can—and cannot—tell us about it.
