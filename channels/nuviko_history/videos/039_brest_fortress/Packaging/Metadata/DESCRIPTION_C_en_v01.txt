“I am dying, but I will not surrender! Farewell, Motherland.”

These words were discovered on a wall inside Brest Fortress after the war. The inscription was dated July 20, 1941, but the identity of the person who wrote it has never been reliably established.

To understand the circumstances around those words, the film returns to June 22, when German artillery opened fire on the fortress. Soviet soldiers fought from separated positions as supplies ran low and access to water became increasingly dangerous.

The main organized defense ended by late June, but isolated resistance continued into July. Major Pyotr Gavrilov was captured on July 23, though he cannot be identified as the last defender.

The film also examines a July 8 report by German commander Fritz Schlieper, who acknowledged the heavy cost of the assault. One account came from the attacking commander; the other was left by someone whose name remains unknown.
