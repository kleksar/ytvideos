# Tu-160 — draft packaging concepts, edit v02

A — Strategic dilemma. Viewer question: why build a bomber for a nuclear confrontation if it need not fly over its target? Answer: stand-off cruise missiles, survivability and recall. Candidate title: The Soviet Bomber Built to Strike America From Far Away. Thumbnail situation: a Tu-160 far out over an ocean, with distant coast and an unmistakable separation between carrier and target; emphasis on distance, not an attack depicted as fact.

B — Engineering solution. Viewer question: how could such a huge machine handle slow long-range flight and supersonic speed? Answer: variable-sweep wings, four powerful engines and tradeoffs. Candidate title: How the World's Heaviest Combat Aircraft Flew Twice the Speed of Sound. Thumbnail situation: the same bomber shown with its wings spread and swept in a mechanical exhibit, guide indicating the change; emphasis on engineering.

C — The strategic role of a bomber. Viewer question: why keep a bomber when ballistic missiles could cross oceans in minutes? Answer: the bomber could take off and return if an order never came. Candidate title: Why Nuclear Powers Still Needed Bombers in the Missile Age. Thumbnail situation: bomber at the runway ready for departure next to a visibly inactive silo on the horizon, guide at the junction; emphasis on the choice to return.

Pairwise concept comparison before consultation and images:
A/B titles: A promises reach and stand-off role; B promises the physical engineering behind speed and size. A/B covers: A shows aircraft and far coast; B shows two wing positions as a mechanical exhibit.
A/C titles: A asks how the bomber could strike from a distance; C asks why a recallable aircraft remains useful alongside ballistic missiles. A/C covers: A shows separation across ocean; C shows an aircraft at the moment of departure beside a missile silo.
B/C titles: B asks how wings and engines reconcile conflicting flight demands; C asks what strategic option a bomber adds. B/C covers: B shows variable geometry; C shows a departure decision and silo.
All are provisional: title/thumbnail consultations and review have not been performed.
