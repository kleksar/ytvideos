The Tu-160 was built for a mission its enormous white airframe could not accomplish alone: carry long-range cruise missiles toward a launch area far from their targets.

In the final decades of the Cold War, the Soviet Union wanted an aircraft that could cross vast distances, carry heavy weapons and accelerate beyond twice the speed of sound. This film explores why the bomber remained part of nuclear strategy, how the Tu-160's variable-sweep wings and four engines met competing demands, and how its Kh-55 missiles shaped its role. It follows the aircraft from its first flight through the end of the Soviet Union and the return of production decades later.
