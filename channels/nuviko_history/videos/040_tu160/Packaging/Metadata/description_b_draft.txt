How do you make an aircraft enormous enough to carry fuel and heavy weapons, yet fast enough to fly at supersonic speed? The Tu-160 forced its designers to solve exactly that problem.

This film examines the engineering of the Soviet Union's largest strategic bomber: wings that sweep backward as speed rises, a massive central structure, and four NK-32 engines. It also explains the tradeoff between thrust and fuel consumption, the long-range cruise missiles the aircraft was designed to carry, and the Cold War strategy behind its creation. From the first flight in 1981 to the return of production decades later, this is the story of a machine built around competing demands.
