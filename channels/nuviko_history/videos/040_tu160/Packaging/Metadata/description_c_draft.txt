A ballistic missile, once launched, cannot be called back. A bomber offers another option: take off during a crisis, head toward a patrol area, and return if the order to use its weapons never comes.

This film follows the strategic logic behind the Soviet Tu-160 and the engineering required to make it real. Designed in the Cold War to carry long-range cruise missiles, the huge aircraft combined variable-sweep wings with four powerful engines. We explore how it could travel vast distances, why its speed came with a steep fuel cost, and how its role complemented other parts of the nuclear arsenal. The story follows its first flight, its entry into service and the return of production decades later.
