## Completed answer

### Recommended sound direction

Use one evolving original synth score, not a collection of cinematic cues. The emotional progression should move from confinement and uncertainty to endurance, exhaustion, temporary despair, and finally restrained relief.

The music should support the scale of the story without pretending to recreate historical sounds.

### 1. Musical progression

| Story beat | Musical treatment |
|---|---|
| Endurance trapped in ice | Low, slowly evolving sustained pad. Sparse minor harmony. A feeling of confinement rather than immediate danger. |
| Ship crushed | Gradually narrow the harmony and reduce musical movement. Brief musical dropout after the loss of the ship. No simulated cracking, impacts or metal sounds. |
| Men drifting on ice | Remove the low drone. Introduce a thin, exposed upper-register note. Slow, almost imperceptible harmonic movement. |
| Elephant Island | Minimal, fragile sustained harmony. A moment of temporary safety, not victory. |
| James Caird crossing | Introduce a quiet, repeating two-note synth pulse. Gradually increase harmonic tension and register. This is the first substantial musical build. |
| South Georgia crossing | Remove the pulse. Use isolated, slowly changing notes over a restrained low harmonic foundation. Emphasize physical exhaustion and uncertainty. |
| Failed rescue attempts | Return to the original motif, but repeatedly interrupt or leave its resolution unfinished. Each failure should make the arrangement thinner, not louder. |
| Yelcho rescue | A short near-silent passage before the rescue. Then introduce warmer harmony and allow the original motif to resolve. Restrained emotional release, without a heroic climax. |
| Ross Sea Party | Treat as a separate, sober coda. Remove the resolved Endurance motif. Use sparse, darker harmony to acknowledge the three deaths without undermining the survival of all 28 Endurance men. |

### 2. Where silence matters

Use musical silence selectively:

1. When the ship is finally lost: remove the score for a short moment.
2. At the transition from drifting ice to Elephant Island: allow the narration to carry the change.
3. Before the South Georgia crossing: a brief break can establish the scale of the challenge.
4. Between failed rescue attempts: occasional short dropouts are more effective than continuously increasing tension.
5. Immediately before Yelcho's arrival: the most important musical silence in the documentary.

Silence means removing the music, not cutting the narration or necessary ambience.

Avoid leaving every dramatic sentence with a musical accent. Some facts should simply land.

### 3. Reproducible synthesis setup

No external samples

Use the DAW's built-in subtractive synthesizer. Two main patches and one optional pulse are sufficient.

| Layer | Synthesis | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Main pad | Triangle + soft filtered saw, slow attack and release | Primary emotional bed |
| High texture | Sine/triangle, long release, low volume | Isolation and vulnerability |
| Pulse | Soft synthesized pluck, short envelope | Caird crossing and forward movement |

Recommended starting parameters:

- Tempo: 68 BPM.
- No conventional drum kit.
- No rhythmic impacts or simulated environmental sounds.
- One recurring three-note motif.
- Minor tonal centre, with occasional suspended harmonies.
- Slow filter automation rather than constant melodic activity.
- Keep the same core patches throughout the film.

The score should evolve primarily through changing density, register, harmony and note duration. Avoid introducing a completely new musical identity for every chapter.

### 4. Voice and mix strategy

The narration is the primary information channel. Music must never compete with consonants or important names.

Recommended starting mix:

| Parameter | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Narration | Front and centre, consistent level |
| Music under speech | Approximately 20–25 dB below perceived narration level |
| Music ducking | Additional 2–4 dB when narration becomes dense |
| Music EQ | Gentle reduction around 1.5–4 kHz |
| Low frequencies | High-pass music around 90–120 Hz |
| Stereo | Moderate width; narration remains centred |
| Compression | Light bus compression, no aggressive pumping |
| Master | Preserve transient and dynamic headroom |

Automate music manually at major narrative transitions. Sidechain ducking can provide additional protection, but should not create audible pumping.

### 5. Practical production workflow

1. Generate the three original synth patches and save them as presets.
2. Compose one 30–60-second musical motif.
3. Build the documentary score from variations of that motif.
4. Create separate automation lanes for volume, filter, harmony and stereo width.
5. Place the narration first and score around its actual pauses and emphasis.
6. Export music-only and narration-plus-music versions.
7. Listen to the complete mix on headphones, desktop speakers and a phone.
8. Check particularly the Yelcho transition and Ross Sea Party coda.

Main creative principle: the score should not tell viewers what to feel every second. The expedition's events already provide the drama. Music should make the scale, isolation and uncertainty perceptible, then give the eventual rescue room to matter.

## Production decision

Use a restrained original synthesized bed with silence at key transitions and a sober shift for the Ross Sea coda. The parameter values are starting points, not evidence of audible suitability. Check the actual mix and preserve narration in full. Avoid any suggestion that invented sound effects are historical recordings.
