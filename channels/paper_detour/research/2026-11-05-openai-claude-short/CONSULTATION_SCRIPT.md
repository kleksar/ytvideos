## EN — Spoken Script

Before Claude became a rival to ChatGPT, some of its creators worked at OpenAI.

And at one point, they chose not to release their own AI.

In December 2020, OpenAI announced that its vice president of research, Dario Amodei, was leaving. He had worked with teams developing GPT-2 and GPT-3—early steps on the road to the technology behind ChatGPT.

Dario didn't leave alone. In 2021, he, his sister Daniela, and other former colleagues founded a new company: Anthropic.

Its stated goal was to build powerful AI systems that people could better understand, direct, and rely on. That May, the company announced 124 million dollars in funding.

Then came a choice that shaped the story.

Anthropic had an early version of Claude. But instead of rushing it out, the team held it back for roughly six months. Dario later said they were uncertain about safety and didn't want to trigger a race.

On November 30, 2022, OpenAI released ChatGPT, built on GPT-3.5.

Anthropic kept working. One method taught Claude to critique and revise its answers against a set of written principles. That was a safety technique, not a promise of perfect behavior.

On March 14, 2023, after private testing, Anthropic introduced Claude through chat interfaces and an API.

Dario and his former OpenAI colleagues now had a competing AI of their own. And its arrival came after a deliberate decision to wait.
