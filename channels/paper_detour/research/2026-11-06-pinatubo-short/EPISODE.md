## Narration — черновик N001 v01

In 1991, roofs began collapsing miles from a volcano in the Philippines.

No lava had reached them. So what was crushing the buildings?

Mount Pinatubo had erupted, throwing volcanic ash high into the sky.

That same day, a typhoon swept across the region. Its winds spread the ash. Its rain soaked it.

Volcanic ash is made of tiny pieces of rock and glass. As the wet ash piled up on rooftops, the weight grew too much for some buildings.

At Clark Air Base, about twelve miles from the volcano, roofs buckled under the load. In nearby towns, buildings were damaged too.

The danger had travelled through the air, settled above people's heads, and turned the rain into an extra weight their roofs could not hold.
