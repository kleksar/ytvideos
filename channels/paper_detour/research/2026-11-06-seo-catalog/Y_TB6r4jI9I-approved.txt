In 1377, the trading city of Ragusa—now Dubrovnik—required people arriving from plague-affected places to wait 30 days before entering. Even residents returning home could be made to wait.

The city was trying to reduce the risk of plague without shutting down the trade it depended on. A later 40-day waiting period gave us the word quarantine.
