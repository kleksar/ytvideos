Uranus kept
turning up in
the wrong part
of the sky.
What if another
planet was pulling
it off its
expected path?
In 1846, French
mathematician
Urbain Le Verrier
used
those differences
to predict
where the unseen
planet might be.
He sent
his calculation
to an observatory
in Berlin.
The night
the letter arrived,
Johann Galle and
Heinrich d’Arrest
compared
their telescope
view with
a star chart.
A point of
light was missing
from the chart,
about one
degree from
where Le Verrier
had told them
to look.
By the next
night
it had shifted
against the stars.
They had found
Neptune.
