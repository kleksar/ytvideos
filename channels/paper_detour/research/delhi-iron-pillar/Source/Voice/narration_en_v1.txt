This iron pillar is about sixteen hundred years old. And something helped protect it: rust.

The pillar stands in Delhi, India. The part above ground has survived remarkably well. But that doesn't mean the iron never rusts.

A dense layer of corrosion products formed on its surface. It makes it harder for moisture to reach the metal and slows further damage. Phosphorus in the iron helps this protective layer form.

The surrounding conditions matter too. The pillar isn't invincible: below ground, it has rusted more heavily.

So rust doesn't always just destroy iron. Sometimes, it helps protect the metal underneath.
