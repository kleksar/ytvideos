# Paper Detour 038 — Carrington Event: factual basis v01

Status: DRAFT; scope 2 November 2026. This file separates the story's evidence and limits from its English narration. Topic selected as a Paper Detour history-of-science film. The Extra High consultation was waived by the user for this session; no model consultation is claimed.

## Story promise

A new telegraph line appears to work after its operators disconnect the batteries. The cause is an intense solar storm, whose light and electrical effects reached Earth in September 1859. The story follows the flash observed by Richard Carrington, the aurora and telegraph disruption, then closes on the connection the operators had not yet understood.

## Working sources

- NOAA NESDIS, [What Was the Carrington Event?](https://www.nesdis.noaa.gov/about/k-12-education/space-weather/what-was-the-carrington-event) — September 1 observation, approximate interval, aurora, telegraph effects and general mechanism.
- NOAA, [Five historically huge solar events](https://www.noaa.gov/heritage/stories/five-historically-huge-solar-events) — independent observations, telegraph shocks/fires and southern lights; used with station-level qualification.
- NASA NTRS, [Duration and Extent of the Great Auroral Storm of 1859](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/search.jsp?R=20050212156) — chronology and observations. The page identifies a public NASA Goddard technical preprint; do not portray its lower-latitude model limit as directly measured.
- NASA NTRS, [Carrington and Hodgson accounts / historical review](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20050210157/downloads/20050210157.pdf) — contemporary reports collated in a NASA-hosted review.
- NASA Marshall Space Flight Center, [Flows, Gusts, and Blasts from the Sun](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20180006106/downloads/20180006106.pdf) — reproduces the Washington newspaper's report of two hours' operation without batteries.
- NASA, [Sun Series: What is Space Weather?](https://www.nasa.gov/podcasts/curious-universe/sun-series-space-weather/) — background corroboration; the planned film does not reuse its dialogue or narration.

## Boundaries / fact-check pass

- Do not conflate the visible solar flare with the cloud of solar plasma. In the story, distinguish the brief bright flare from the later arrival of material/field disturbance; the ~17-hour historical estimate is attached to the resulting event, not presented as a directly watched object crossing space.
- Avoid the popular but unsupported exact claim that modern civilization would collapse, a precise economic cost, a precise recurrence period, or a guaranteed worldwide blackout.
- Do not make sparks/fire universal or say the telegraph operators knew the source. Their observed current is a result they experienced; the solar connection is the later physical explanation.
- The visual of the Sun-to-Earth path is a clear scientific diagram/diorama device, not a literal eyewitness view of invisible material or a scaled depiction.
