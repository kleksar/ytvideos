## Card copy

### 01

**ONE RADIO. TWO LANGUAGES.**  
Could a single radio broadcast deliver spoken Portuguese and readable Japanese at the same time?

### 02

**A SPECIAL BROADCAST — 1988**  
On July 10, Rádio USP FM in São Paulo aired *Clip Informátka Especial* for the city's Japanese community.

### 03

**TWO CHANNELS. TWO MESSAGES.**  
One FM stereo channel carried the host speaking Portuguese. On the other, sound signals carried a Japanese translation of the same text.

### 04

**THE COMPUTER READ THE RADIO**  
A connected computer decoded the second channel and displayed the Japanese message on its screen. The radio had carried a message that a computer could turn into readable text.

## Caption

In 1988, a radio broadcast in São Paulo reportedly used FM stereo to deliver spoken Portuguese through one channel and computer-readable Japanese through the other.

A connected computer could decode the message and display it on screen. One broadcast, two languages, two ways to receive the same information.

**If you could add one new capability to radio today, what would it be?**

## Source and factual limits

- Contemporary report: [Superinteressante, “Computador FM,” 31 August 1988](https://super.abril.com.br/comportamento/computador-fm/). It explicitly describes the second channel as the same text encoded in sound and translated into Japanese, then decoded for display by a connected computer.
- This project has not independently reproduced the transmission or decoding. Keep “reportedly” in the caption; do not present it as our experiment.
- The article separately mentions a computer version for people with hearing impairments from June. Do not merge that initiative with this 10 July Japanese-community broadcast.
- Do not conflate this broadcast with the film’s separate MSX Avenger story.
- Research and prior editorial checks: [derivative research note](../../../Research/DERIVATIVE_RESEARCH_FM_STEREO_JAPANESE_20261102.md).
