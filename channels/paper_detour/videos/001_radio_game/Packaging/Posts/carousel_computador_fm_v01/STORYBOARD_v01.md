## Card 01 — hook

**Text:** `ONE RADIO. TWO LANGUAGES.` / `Could a single radio broadcast deliver spoken Portuguese and readable Japanese at the same time?`

Generic radio receiver below center, facing slightly toward the viewer, against an abstract layered-paper acoustic background. Two paper sound paths emerge from the same radio: a smooth wave and a coded sequence of short/long geometric pulses. Their difference hints at the puzzle but does not yet explain it. Keep the title at the top and the question in the protected bottom band. No people, logos, technical controls, studio, pseudo-Japanese or Portuguese text.

## Card 02 — date and audience

## Card 03 — stereo split

**Text:** `TWO CHANNELS. TWO MESSAGES.` / `One FM stereo channel carried the host speaking Portuguese. On the other, sound signals carried a Japanese translation of the same text.`

The single radio remains the source at center. One signal path divides into two distinct parallel paper ribbons: one smooth wave for spoken Portuguese; one sequence of structured short/long pulses for the coded sound carrying the Japanese translation. Keep both attached to one source and visually parallel so they read as two channels of one broadcast. Title above; body copy below, away from the paths. No two radios, two broadcasts, speaker/microphone, invented modulation diagram, language glyphs or literal message.

## Card 04 — payoff

**Text:** `THE COMPUTER READ THE RADIO` / `A connected computer decoded the second channel and displayed the Japanese message on its screen. The radio had carried a message that a computer could turn into readable text.`

Bring the recurring radio to the left and a generic 1980s CRT computer to the right, joined by a clearly visible cable. The pulsed second ribbon enters the computer; a structured block of text-like line marks appears on the screen to signal decoded readable content. Use abstract line lengths and alignment only: no random Japanese-like glyphs and no invented quotation. This is a schematic screen reconstruction, not the broadcast’s recovered transcript or actual interface. Keep the heading top and both body sentences in a protected bottom band, leaving the cable and display visible.
