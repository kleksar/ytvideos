# Fact check — Rádio USP and the game in the radio signal

Research date: 29 October 2026. Scope: historical facts for an original illustrated episode. This is not a clearance for archive footage, broadcast audio, or game assets.

| Claim | Evidence | Safe wording / limits |
|---|---|---|
| Rádio USP carried a programme called Clip Informática. | [University of São Paulo, history of Rádio USP](https://www5.usp.br/noticias/institucional/nas-ondas-da-educacao-e-da-cultura/) | University identifies Clip Informática with producer Alexandre Fejes Neto; this page alone does not establish the episode date or game. |
| A Rádio USP programme called Clip Informática transmitted computer data as audio. | [Datassette archive, Clip Informática Especial no. 102](https://datassette.org/pt-br/softwares/diversos/audios/br-brasil/clip-informatica-especial-numero-102); [digitized broadcast as identified by uploader](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QSzMP64DPnI) | Datassette attributes its no. 102 recording to 1987, describes audio software for MSX, and credits Edson Kadoya for digitisation. The accessible video description describes radio software and cassette use but does not identify no. 102 or Avenger. The relationship between the archive copy and the video has not been independently established. |
| A later researcher says data from the recording matches an MSX game. | [Weber Kai, MSX tape decoder README](https://github.com/weberkai/msx-tape-read#the-big-challenge-r%C3%A1dio-usp---clip-inform%C3%A1tica---episode-102) | Kai identifies the linked video as episode 102, reports low and accelerated audio, says data starts around 25:00 in the video and produces six decoded blocks; he says the data matched an MSX game called Avenger. No hash, complete reference comparison, or evidence that the game was run is given. We have not independently decoded or tested the recording. The video time is a position in the recording, not the time of the broadcast. |
| An MSX game named Avenger existed in the period. | [MSX Computing, February/March 1987, scanned issue](https://download.file-hunter.com/magazines/EN/MSX%20Computing/MSX%20Computing%201987-02.pdf); [MAME MSX cassette catalog](https://sources.debian.org/src/mame/0.251%2Bdfsg.1-1/hash/msx1_cass.xml) | The researcher did not identify the comparison's exact release or publisher; do not invent either, or depict its gameplay as though verified. |
| Rádio USP broadcast a Portuguese programme with its text simultaneously encoded in sound and translated into Japanese on the other FM stereo channel. | [Superinteressante, “Computador FM,” 31 August 1988](https://super.abril.com.br/comportamento/computador-fm/) | The contemporary article reports that at 15:00 on 10 July 1988, Clip Informátka Especial, dedicated to São Paulo’s Japanese community, used two FM stereo channels: the presenter spoke in Portuguese on one; the other carried the same text as sound-coded Japanese translation. It says a radio connected to a computer decoded the message onto the screen. The article directly supports the translation wording; the event and successful decoding remain the article’s report, not an independently reproduced test in this project. It is separate from the 1987-attributed MSX recording/Avenger story. The article separately reports a computer version for people with hearing impairments from June; do not merge the two initiatives or imply they used the same programme/transmission. See [the 2026-11-02 carousel-angle research note](DERIVATIVE_RESEARCH_FM_STEREO_JAPANESE_20261102.md). |
| Listeners elsewhere in Brazil recorded programmes onto cassette. | [UFPB, Rádio: estudos contemporâneos, participant interviews](https://www.ccta.ufpb.br/editoraccta/contents/titulos/comunicacao/radio-estudos-contemporaneos/radio_estudoscontemporaneos.pdf#page=145) | Interviews recorded in 2013 refer to Rádio Universitária FM in João Pessoa, not Rádio USP. Use as supporting context for the general technique, not to assert what a specific USP listener did. |

## Not established

- Exact date or time of the episode 102 broadcast; audience size, identity or experiences of individual listeners; whether a specific listener successfully loaded Avenger.
- That Rádio USP or this programme invented radio software transmission or was first in the world; other radio experiments existed.
- Whether the preserved YouTube recording is identical to Datassette's file. Attribution to 1987 comes from the archive's catalogue and remains an attribution.
- Whether decoding produced a runnable or complete game; the researcher reports a data match, not a demonstrated successful launch. His recovery parameters apply to the available accelerated recording, not necessarily the original radio signal.
- Source music/audio, the broadcast recording, photographs and the game are not cleared for this project. Use original illustrations and a synthetic illustrative effect, not an actual archive signal or game screen, unless separately licensed. GPL on the decoder is not a license to the broadcast or game.

Do not imply files move through radio like modern packet internet, or that every cassette signal and every 1980s computer shared one format. This episode explains a simplified process and a specific reported recovery.
