# Visual plan — radio game

Production guide for `001_en_script.md`. Timing is provisional and must be derived from recorded speech. One language-neutral 16:9 master; any visible words or dates should be optional localizable layers, not baked into the background. The approved paper explorer is the visual reference, not a new character design. No mouth sync. The project does not yet have an approved reusable animation master.

1. **The signal:** a friendly paper explorer turns a radio dial; music-like visual waves snap into jagged tones. Hook holds briefly on a puzzled face. Use an original synthetic illustrative tone, clearly a reconstruction, not the archival audio.
2. **A hidden message:** the squiggle transforms into a cassette and a simple abstract game icon; avoid showing a specific game screen or implying the shown sound is the original. The paper explorer opens the cassette like a puzzle box.
3. **Tape mechanics:** simplified cutaway of tape moving past a read head, then a waveform moving through a cable. No need to simulate magnetic physics precisely; graphics are diagrams.
4. **Data as sound:** zeros and ones appear as an editable graphic layer; matching marks turn into two distinguishable sound patterns and then back into symbols. Clearly schematic rather than exact MSX timing.
5. **Transmission chain:** sequential arrows link cassette/player → radio transmitter → home radio → cassette → compatible computer. The explorer performs each connection. A red broken wave shows reception and compatibility can fail.
6. **São Paulo and the archive:** a map silhouette identifies Brazil and São Paulo; an illustrated radio studio fades into an archive box marked by separately localizable text: `1987 — archive attribution`. Avoid recreating a real presenter or studio without reference.
7. **The recovery:** timeline from old recording to a modern laptop; an abstract waveform is weak and compressed, then enlarged. The investigator is represented as an anonymous silhouette, not a portrait of Weber Kai. Display six blocks as a schematic counting device.
8. **The match and its limits:** six blocks align with a separate stack labelled `Avenger` as an editable layer; an outlined question mark remains over a monitor. Explicitly avoid a fake game boot, screenshot, or a visual claim of an independent byte-for-byte test.
9. **A different 1988 broadcast:** draw two diverging lanes from one radio: voice and text. Localizable caption `separate broadcast, 1988`; narrator explains the lanes belonged to a different reported experiment.
10. **Payoff:** reverse the chain: abstract code → sound → broadcast → sound → code. Character waves goodbye beside a radio. Leave the final image to breathe without an invented call to action.

Audio: narration, music and effects must be mixed for each language. Do not reuse original broadcast sound, game graphics, or music without rights clearance. A demonstration signal must be original and labelled as an illustration rather than an authentic or verified decodable MSX recording.
