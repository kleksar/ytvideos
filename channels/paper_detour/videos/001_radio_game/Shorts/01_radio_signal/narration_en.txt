A Brazilian radio show once broadcast computer software. Not an advertisement for software. The program was carried as sound.

In the nineteen eighties, Rádio USP aired a technology show called Clip Informática. An archive dates its copy of episode one hundred and two to nineteen eighty-seven, and says the audio near the end was software for MSX computers.

How could that work?

A radio could carry the tones. With suitable equipment, a listener could record them onto a cassette. Then a compatible computer could try to turn those patterns back into data.

To a person, it might sound like a squeal. To the right machine, it could be a message.
