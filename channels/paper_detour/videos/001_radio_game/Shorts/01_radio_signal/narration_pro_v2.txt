Pressing record could save a computer program.

In 1980s Brazil, Clip Informática sent software over the radio as sound.

Here's the principle. A radio receives the broadcast. A recorder saves the audio on cassette. A compatible computer can read the recorded patterns as data.

Datassette dates its copy of episode one hundred and two to nineteen eighty-seven, and identifies the software segment as intended for MSX computers.

Now look at the cassette. The very thing that lets it keep a song also lets it keep an encoded program.

A familiar record button had an unfamiliar job: catching software.
