A radio broadcast carried Portuguese for your ears, and Japanese for a screen.

A nineteen eighty-eight article describes a July tenth broadcast on Brazil's Rádio USP.

It used the two channels of stereo for different jobs. One carried the presenter's Portuguese speech. The other carried encoded sound representing the same text in Japanese.

A computer connected to the radio could decode the signal and display the text.

The Japanese text was already in the transmitted data. The computer's job was to read it.

So one broadcast offered two ways to receive the message: listen in Portuguese, or read in Japanese.
