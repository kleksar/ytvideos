## Narration

What if a radio show could be followed without hearing it?

In São Paulo in the late 1980s, Alexandre Fejes Neto began sending a computer version of Rádio USP’s Clip Informátka for people with hearing impairments.

Separately, a late-1980s Rádio USP programme showed text on a computer screen, much like subtitles.

Neither account specifies the technical setup or the number of users.

But the idea was striking: radio could offer words as well as sound.

And it leaves us with one question: what if a radio show could be followed without hearing it?

## Editorial intent and factual boundaries

- A standalone story about a late-1980s attempt to make a radio programme available as computer text.
- Superinteressante reports a June computer version of Clip Informátka for people with hearing impairments in São Paulo. Folha separately recalls a Rádio USP programme whose text appeared on a computer screen like subtitles. The sources do not establish that these were the same programme or implementation.
- Do not infer universal availability, audience reach, measured success, technical architecture, or a user count.
- Keep this separate from the 10 July 1988 Japanese-community FM-stereo broadcast and the parent film's MSX Avenger story.
- Sources: [Superinteressante (31 August 1988)](https://super.abril.com.br/comportamento/computador-fm/) and [Folha Online (10 November 2001)](https://www1.folha.uol.com.br/folha/pensata/ult514u40.shtml).
