When they buried Marguery McCall, an expensive ring was still on her finger.
Her husband had tried to take it off, but her finger was too swollen.
Marguery had died of a fever, and her family rushed the burial,
afraid the illness might spread.

They knew about the ring.
They opened the coffin and tried to pull it off, but it wouldn't budge.
So one of them pulled out a knife.
If he couldn't remove the ring, he would cut off her finger.
The blade touched her skin.

The thieves ran for their lives, while Marguery climbed out of the grave
and walked home in her burial clothes.
Her husband was sitting with their children when someone knocked at the door.
He froze and said,
If your mother were still alive, I'd swear that was her knock.
He opened the door.
There stood Marguery, in the clothes she'd been buried in, with blood on her hand.
When he saw his wife, he clutched his chest and dropped dead.
A few days later, they buried him in the very coffin Marguery had climbed out of.
She lived for many more years, married again, and had children.
And when her own time came, these words were carved on her headstone.
Lived once, buried twice.
