A friar had Maya books burned—then left behind a clue to reading what survived.

Diego de Landa matched Maya signs to Spanish letters. Read as individual letters, they couldn't produce coherent texts.

In 1952, Yuri Knorozov systematically tested syllabic readings against reproductions of surviving codices and Maya words. Eric Thompson challenged this phonetic approach, but later researchers built on its central insight.

De Landa's list preserved useful phonetic values. The clue wasn't a failed alphabet. It was a partial syllabary.
