## Initial editorial recommendation and fact corrections (later superseded)

Develop Minnie Freeman as the leading candidate. The 1888 University of Nebraska–Lincoln special-collections item includes an introduction reproducing a near-contemporary account attributed to Freeman. Its sequence is explicit: the door blew in, then windows; she remembered and confiscated twine, tied the pupils together, waited, and **then** the roof blew away; she carried the youngest and led them toward the nearest house through the storm. The source is a contemporary printed account reproduced in a song publication, not a modern transcript or independent verification of every quoted word. History Nebraska separately summarizes that 13 children were tied single-file and led to a farmhouse.

The ChatGPT script and first-beat description put the roof coming off before the children are linked. Preserve the flash-forward as a deliberate hook, but make the rewind clear and restore the source's chronology in the explanatory sequence. Avoid inventing dialogue, the children's reactions, a precise mapped route, or unsupported physical injuries. Keep the children linked by a visible but non-coercive rescue line; avoid imagery that reads as punishment or restraint. “All thirteen reached shelter” is supported in the contemporary account and History Nebraska's retrospective, but the account of the named twine is best attributed as the contemporary report rather than independently proven detail.

The weak old script ending on general advice is replaced in this candidate by returning to the confiscated string: the former classroom nuisance becomes the tool that keeps the group connected. This is a stronger dramatic proposition, not evidence that any particular plot will achieve high retention.
