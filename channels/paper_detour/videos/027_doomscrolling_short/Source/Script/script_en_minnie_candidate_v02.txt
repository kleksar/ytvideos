Minnie Freeman took away a boy’s ball of twine while he played in class. Nebraska, 1888: a blizzard struck her school.

The door blew in, then the windows. Minnie knew the building would not last. The nearest house was three-quarters of a mile away.

She remembered that twine, tied all thirteen pupils together, and waited. Only then did the roof blow away.

Carrying the youngest, she led the line through the blizzard. At one point, Freeman thought they might be lost.

All thirteen reached the farmhouse.
