# Minnie Freeman — scenes and layered storyboard v04

**State:** visual development draft for script candidate v03; not an approved final script, timed edit, or Palmier timeline.  
**Format:** English Paper Detour Short, vertical 9:16.  
**Estimated duration:** 38 seconds from the current 85-word VO estimate. All times are provisional until a real voice recording is timed. Seven principal shots; each is at least three seconds.

## Story aim

Open on the mismatch between a small classroom confiscation and thirteen pupils in a school threatened by a blizzard. The first image gives the question; the sequence answers it in causal order. Minnie remembers the twine, joins the pupils, and waits before the roof is lost. The group reaches the farmhouse before the loop returns to the confiscated ball. The end answers the rescue on the first view; the replay changes the ball from a classroom nuisance into the beginning of the visible line.

The pictures are handmade paper-diorama reconstructions, not photographs and not claims to reproduce the exact school, farmhouse, or people. Use the user-approved Paper Detour parallax reference at `channels/paper_detour/videos/004_last_video/Storyboard/user_reference_parallax_20261101.png` for cut-paper depth, physical occlusion, edge texture, soft contact shadows, and restrained warm/cold contrast. A National Park Service one-room school is a broad period reference only; it is not Minnie Freeman's school. Do not copy its distinctive details as fact.

The first view answers the rescue; replay gives the opening confiscation its later meaning.

At every full-group view, count **13 pupils**. In the travel and farmhouse shots the layout is **12 pupils on foot plus the youngest carried by Minnie**. Keep all figures whole and recognizable; do not hide a pupil behind a type accent, door edge, snow, or the crop when the script says all thirteen arrived.

## Historical basis and limits

- The order of door, windows, twine, waiting, roof, and journey comes from the contemporary 1888 printed retelling attributed to Minnie Freeman, reproduced with a song publication: <https://openspaces.unk.edu/spec-coll/10/>.
- History Nebraska independently summarizes the rescue of thirteen pupils in single file to a farmhouse: <https://history.nebraska.gov/looking-back-on-the-blizzard-of-1888/>.
- For only a broad schoolhouse visual reference, the National Park Service says its Freeman School has been restored to resemble a prairie school of the 1870s/1880s: <https://www.nps.gov/home/planyourvisit/freemanschool.htm>. That is not Minnie Freeman’s school.
- History Nebraska’s historical-marker page calls the school a sod schoolhouse and says Minnie was still in her teens, but the other sources do not establish its exact design or an exact portrait. Treat the images as a plainly stylized reconstruction; keep architecture, clothing and faces generic and do not attach unverified identifying details to Minnie.
