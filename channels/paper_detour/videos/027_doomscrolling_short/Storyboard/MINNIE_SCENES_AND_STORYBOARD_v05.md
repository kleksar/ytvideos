## Story aim

Open on a brief, unexplained consequence from the climax: a line of thirteen pupils is already moving through the blizzard, with no destination shown. The first image gives the question; the next shots go back to the school and rebuild the cause in order. Minnie remembers the twine, joins the pupils, and waits before the roof is lost. The group reaches the farmhouse before the loop returns to the line in the storm. The end answers the rescue on the first view; the replay changes the opening twine from a classroom nuisance into the link between all thirteen.

The pictures are handmade paper-diorama reconstructions, not photographs and not claims to reproduce the exact school, farmhouse, or people. Use the user-approved Paper Detour parallax reference at `channels/paper_detour/videos/004_last_video/Storyboard/user_reference_parallax_20261101.png` for cut-paper depth, physical occlusion, edge texture, soft contact shadows, and restrained warm/cold contrast. A National Park Service one-room school is a broad period reference only; it is not Minnie Freeman's school. Do not copy its distinctive details as fact. Add a soft, moving film-grain plane across all seven scenes and every transition; the generated stills do not contain that motion layer yet.

## Historical basis and limits

- The order of door, windows, twine, waiting, roof, and journey comes from the contemporary 1888 printed retelling attributed to Minnie Freeman, reproduced with a song publication: <https://openspaces.unk.edu/spec-coll/10/>.
- History Nebraska independently summarizes the rescue of thirteen pupils in single file to a farmhouse: <https://history.nebraska.gov/looking-back-on-the-blizzard-of-1888/>.
- For only a broad schoolhouse visual reference, the National Park Service says its Freeman School has been restored to resemble a prairie school of the 1870s/1880s: <https://www.nps.gov/home/planyourvisit/freemanschool.htm>. That is not Minnie Freeman’s school.
- History Nebraska’s historical-marker page calls the school a sod schoolhouse and says Minnie was still in her teens, but the other sources do not establish its exact design or an exact portrait. Treat the images as a plainly stylized reconstruction; keep architecture, clothing and faces generic and do not attach unverified identifying details to Minnie.
