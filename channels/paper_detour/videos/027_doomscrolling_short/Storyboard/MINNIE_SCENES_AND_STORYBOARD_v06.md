## Story aim

Open on a brief, unexplained consequence from the climax: a line of pupils is already moving through the blizzard, with the destination hidden. Make a strand of twine plainly readable between figures in the first second; without it, the line looks like an ordinary evacuation. Then mark the jump back to the school with a full-frame paper page-turn. Rebuild the cause in order: door, windows, Minnie remembers the twine, joins the pupils and waits, then the roof is lost. The group reaches the farmhouse before the same page-turn grammar returns the loop to the earlier storm crossing. This is an editorial time jump; never animate the rope itself backwards or show the group leaving the farmhouse.

The pictures are handmade paper-diorama reconstructions, not photographs and not claims to reproduce the exact school, farmhouse, or people. Use the user-approved Paper Detour parallax reference at `channels/paper_detour/videos/004_last_video/Storyboard/user_reference_parallax_20261101.png` for cut-paper depth, physical occlusion, edge texture, soft contact shadows, and restrained warm/cold contrast. A National Park Service one-room school is a broad period reference only; it is not Minnie Freeman's school. Do not copy its distinctive details as fact. Add a soft, moving film-grain plane across all seven scenes and every transition; the generated stills do not contain that motion layer yet.

## Loop and count check

At the last cut, the phrase “All thirteen reached the farmhouse, connected by—” joins the opening “Twine taken from one boy.” The completed sentence is: **“All thirteen reached the farmhouse, connected by twine taken from one boy.”** First hold the exterior arrival so the rescue is complete on the first view. Then match the line’s curve and use an opaque paper fold to mark the return to the earlier storm crossing. Because the opening group is moving through snow, do not carry its movement smoothly out of the farmhouse shot; that could suggest they reached shelter and set off again. The twine match is an editorial echo, not a literal rewind of the object. The replay gives the opening twine its later meaning.

At every full-group view, count **13 pupils**. In the cold open, travel and farmhouse shots the layout is **12 pupils on foot plus the youngest carried by Minnie**. At the final arrival, all thirteen are visible outside/at the farmhouse threshold; do not imply that they entered the house. Keep all figures whole and recognizable; do not hide a pupil behind a type accent, door edge, snow, or the crop when the script says all thirteen arrived.

## Historical basis and limits

- The order of door, windows, twine, waiting, roof, and journey comes from the contemporary 1888 printed retelling attributed to Minnie Freeman, reproduced with a song publication: <https://openspaces.unk.edu/spec-coll/10/>.
- History Nebraska independently summarizes the rescue of thirteen pupils in single file to a farmhouse: <https://history.nebraska.gov/looking-back-on-the-blizzard-of-1888/>.
- For only a broad schoolhouse visual reference, the National Park Service says its Freeman School has been restored to resemble a prairie school of the 1870s/1880s: <https://www.nps.gov/home/planyourvisit/freemanschool.htm>. That is not Minnie Freeman’s school.
- History Nebraska’s historical-marker page calls the school a sod schoolhouse and says Minnie was still in her teens, but the other sources do not establish its exact design or an exact portrait. Treat the images as a plainly stylized reconstruction; keep architecture, clothing and faces generic and do not attach unverified identifying details to Minnie.
