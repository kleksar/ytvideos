## Three title + thumbnail hypotheses

### A — Archaeological mystery

**Title:** We Found Their Tools. Where Are Their Clothes?  
**Thumbnail text:** WHAT'S MISSING?  
**Image:** [concept PNG](../Thumbnails/Concepts/a_tools_where_clothes_v02.png) · [upload JPG](../Thumbnails/Upload/a_tools_where_clothes_v02.jpg)

**Viewer question:** How can stone tools survive while we still cannot show exactly what their makers wore?  
**Payoff in the draft:** the first tools establish the evidence gap; a later surviving Neolithic esparto sandal makes one piece of footwear tangible through reconstruction.  
**Risk:** Do not imply that a known garment was stolen or that the film recovers an exact early outfit. The fading fibers are a visual metaphor, not a documented find.

### B — Visual spectacle

**Title:** When Dresses Became Architecture  
**Thumbnail text:** A DRESS THIS WIDE?  
**Image:** [superseded concept PNG](../Thumbnails/Archive/previous_round/b_dresses_architecture_v02.png) · [superseded upload JPG](../Thumbnails/Archive/previous_round/b_dresses_architecture_v02.jpg)

**Viewer question:** How dramatically could clothing reshape a human silhouette, and what came after the widest forms?  
**Payoff in the draft:** the contrast between 18th-century court dress, the mid-19th-century crinoline, and much straighter 1920s silhouettes.  
**Risk:** “Architecture” is a metaphor for visual volume; the draft does not explain dress engineering in depth. The thumbnail focuses on women's clothing within a film spanning a broader history.

### C — Journey through time

**Title:** The River Stayed. The Silhouettes Didn’t.  
**Thumbnail text:** none  
**Image:** [superseded concept PNG](../Thumbnails/Archive/previous_round/c_river_silhouettes_v03.png) · [superseded upload JPG](../Thumbnails/Archive/previous_round/c_river_silhouettes_v03.jpg)

**Viewer question:** How much can clothing change while the same visual landscape keeps returning?  
**Payoff in the draft:** the recurring fictional river links silhouettes from different periods and ends with varied 2020s clothing.  
**Risk:** The river is a continuity device, not a verified archaeological site. The figures represent separate times, not a historical gathering.

## Shared English description — draft

Some of the earliest stone tools found in Western Europe are more than a million years old. But what did their makers wear? The tools offer no clear answer.

Follow changing silhouettes from a reconstructed Neolithic sandal and Roman Britain through medieval Europe, Amsterdam in 1559, 18th-century court dress, the Victorian crinoline, the 1920s, and today's varied styles.

A recurring riverbank links the eras in this paper-diorama journey, with music that shifts to match each period. Which silhouette surprised you most?
