After the hurricane of 1900 devastated Galveston, rebuilding meant more than replacing houses. The Texas port built a seawall—and raised the ground beneath a living city.

This paper-diorama history follows the storm, the recovery, and the years of screw jacks, temporary boardwalks and pumped sand that reshaped Galveston. Saint Patrick's Church offers one striking example: hundreds of jacks working together to lift a massive building. The new defenses reduced the danger, but never made the island invulnerable.

The paper scenes are historical illustrations, not footage of the events. The archival photograph of Saint Patrick's Church is from the Galveston and Texas History Center, Rosenberg Library, accession G-59262 FF3-7:
