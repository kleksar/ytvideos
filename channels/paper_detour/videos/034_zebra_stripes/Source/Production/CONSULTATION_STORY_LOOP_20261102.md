## Visible result

**Concept: “The Missing Half.”** The central question is what changed when cows wore stripes: whether flies approached, or what happened when they got close.

**Opening image and line:** extreme close-up of a black-and-white striped cow, biting flies highlighted against its coat, image freezes. “Why did this striped cow have only half as many biting flies in the photograph?”

**Five beats:**

1. Six Japanese Black cows each experienced three conditions: black-and-white stripes, black stripes alone, and no paint. The same cows became their own controls.
2. After each 30-minute observation, researchers photographed the cows and counted biting flies visible in the images.
3. The black-and-white condition showed about 50% fewer visible biting flies than the two controls.
4. The photographs could not tell whether a fly landed, bit, or never appeared. A separate zebra study offers a clue: horseflies approached faster and touched more often, yet did not land.
5. These findings suggest that approaching and successfully landing are different events. The cow study does not prove that the stripes changed the final approach; the mechanism is unknown.

**Payoff:** the count is clear; the explanation is not. The measurable result and the unsolved mechanism coexist.

**Proposed loop:**

- Final line: “The count is clear. The mechanism isn’t. So look again:”
- Opening line: “Why did this striped cow have only half as many biting flies in the photograph?”
- Read together: “The count is clear. The mechanism isn’t. So look again: Why did this striped cow have only half as many biting flies in the photograph?”

The first viewing follows the surprising count. The zebra observation reframes the question around the difference between approaching and landing. Replaying returns to the photo and opens that question again without pretending to solve it.

## Coordinator note

This is a useful structural starting point, not an accepted script. In narration, keep method detail brief and dramatize the fly’s approach; retain the distinction between what the cow photos measured and what the separate zebra study observed. The opening wording needs revision so it does not imply one cow in one photograph had a controlled “half” result; the study compared visible counts across conditions.
