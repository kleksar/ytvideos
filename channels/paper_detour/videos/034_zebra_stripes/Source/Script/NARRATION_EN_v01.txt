Could painted stripes mean fewer biting flies on a cow?

Researchers tested six Japanese Black cows under three conditions: black-and-white stripes, black stripes alone, and no paint. Each cow experienced all three.

After each 30-minute observation, researchers photographed the cows and counted the biting flies visible in the images. With black-and-white stripes, the photographs showed about half as many visible flies as with no paint.

A separate study added a twist: horseflies approached zebras faster and touched them more often without landing.

That points to a possible weak point in the fly’s approach—but it does not prove why stripes work.

The vulnerable moment may be touchdown, yet the exact mechanism remains unknown.
