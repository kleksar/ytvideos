At first, the mantis shrimp’s striking club looks like one solid piece. What keeps a crack from splitting it after a blow?

The first clue is hidden inside. Micro-CT shows the club’s two segments. In cross-section, three regions appear: impact, periodic, and striated. No uniform block.

The outer impact surface is especially hard and mineral-rich. Deeper in, the material changes. But layers alone don’t answer the question.

Look at a damaged cross-section. Under the microscope, tiny cracks run between chitin fibres. Some cross several layers, then stop. X-rays reveal the fibres themselves are stacked in a spiral, each layer turned slightly from the one below.

A computer model adds a broader clue. It simulates the club striking steel and maps stress through its overall shape. But it leaves out microscopic fibres. We see those cracks only in damaged sections.

The answer isn’t that the club can’t crack. Its architecture can deflect and contain damage, helping resist a catastrophic break.

That explains something about how the club works today. The study doesn’t tell us how this structure first evolved.
