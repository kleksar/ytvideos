One hunting limb took two very different paths: it became a spear, or a hammer.

Mantis shrimps hunt with front limbs. In some species, those limbs spear prey; in others, they smash it. But the fossil record shows these weapons were not always so distinct. Some ancient forms had four pairs of similar raptorial limbs. In others, the front pair stood apart. A shared hunting toolkit was becoming more specialized.

But there was no single destination. In spearers, the front limb became a narrow, spined trap that shoots forward. In smashers, it became a compact, reinforced club built to deliver a blow. These are not two stages on one ladder. They are different specializations of the same kind of hunting limb.

The hammer wasn't a separate invention; it was an old raptorial limb repurposed as a biological club. One ancestral toolkit could lead to strikingly different weapons.
