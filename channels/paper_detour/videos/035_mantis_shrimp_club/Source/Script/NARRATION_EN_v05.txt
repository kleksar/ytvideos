One hunting limb. Two weapons: a spear or a hammer.

In living mantis shrimps, the front pair can be radically different. Some are narrow and spined, suited to spearing; others are thick, compact clubs built for impact. Yet both are versions of the same basic raptorial limb.

The fossils make that contrast more surprising. Some ancient forms had four pairs of hunting limbs that were still broadly alike. In other fossils, the front pair was already more distinct. This is not a neat ladder from spear to hammer, and the fossils do not reveal a direct chain of parents and descendants. They show something subtler: the front limbs were not locked into one shape. Across lineages, the same basic anatomy followed different evolutionary paths, with the front pair becoming specialized in different ways.

A spear and a hammer are not successive steps. They are separate outcomes from a shared anatomical starting point.
