A spear in one mantis shrimp. A hammer in another.

At first, the difference was not so dramatic. Early mantis shrimps had several pairs of hunting limbs that looked broadly alike. Over time, the front pair began to stand apart from the others.

Across different lineages, that front pair took two very different shapes. In spearers, it developed a slender, spined end for catching and piercing. In smashers, its striking end became a compact, thickened club, built to deliver a powerful blow.

As the front pair changed, it stopped being just another pair of limbs. It became the animal's defining weapon — a spear here, a hammer there.
