A predator and an injured amphibian lay side by side in the same burrow for 250 million years. How did they end up there?

A drought had struck southern Africa. Thrinaxodon, a small, sharp-toothed predator, retreated underground and fell into a deep sleep.

Then came Broomistega, a young amphibian with broken ribs, searching for shelter. It crawled into the burrow and found the sleeping predator. The owner didn't wake, and the amphibian stayed beside him.

Then a flood swept through the land, filling their hiding place with mud and burying them together. The burrow turned to stone, preserving both skeletons.
