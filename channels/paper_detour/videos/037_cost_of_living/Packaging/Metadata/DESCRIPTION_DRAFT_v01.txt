How much did a year of basic household needs cost in daily wages?

This Paper Detour compares a reconstructed London household basket with day wages across several centuries. A figure such as 333 wage-days means the annual basket cost the equivalent of 333 daily wages. It does not mean a worker was assumed to work 333 days in one year; the model separately uses 250 paid working days.

The comparison shows why a rising daily wage does not automatically make everyday needs easier to afford. The basket’s cost rises relative to pay in the early 1600s, then is much lower in the later 19th-century comparison. This is one reconstructed budget and wage series, not a complete account of every household’s income, employment, or bills.
