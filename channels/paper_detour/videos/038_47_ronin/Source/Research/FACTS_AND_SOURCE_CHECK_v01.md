# Paper Detour 038 — Akō Incident: factual basis v01

Status: DRAFT, 2 November 2026. Carrington material is preserved separately as an earlier, unaccepted topic draft.

## Story promise

After their lord is sentenced and his domain dissolved, a group of masterless retainers must choose between accepting the shogunate's ruling and taking revenge. Their raid succeeds; the state then faces a harder question: honor their loyalty, or punish an unauthorized killing to protect public order?

## Claims and limits

| ID | Claim | Evidence / boundary | Planned use |
|---|---|---|---|
| F01 | In 1701, Asano Naganori attacked Kira Yoshinaka in Edo Castle during preparations for receiving imperial envoys. | Bitō dates this to 21 April 1701 Gregorian, corresponding to the 14th day of the third month on the Japanese calendar. The eyewitness account records Asano's words about a past grudge, not its underlying cause. | Opening; do not invent an insult, bribe, or settled motive. |
| F02 | The shogunate ordered Asano to perform seppuku and confiscated Akō; his retainers became rōnin (masterless samurai). | Bitō and Akō Castle Digital Museum chronologies. Do not imply Kira was found guilty in a modern trial. | Turning point. |

## Sources

- Bitō Masahide, [“The Akō Incident, 1701–1703”](https://www.columbia.edu/~hds2/pdf/2003b_Bito_Ako_Incident.pdf), translated by Henry D. Smith II, *Monumenta Nipponica* 58:2. Bitō is professor emeritus of Japanese history at the University of Tokyo; the study draws on surviving contemporary accounts and correspondence. Used for cause uncertainty, chronology, strategic choices, calendar conversion, participant-count caveat, legal deliberation, and later mythmaking.
- Akō Castle Digital Museum, [“Akō: The Birthplace of Chūshingura”](https://ako-castle.jp/en/learn/the-birthplace-of-chushingura/), maintained by Akō City. Used for local context, event sequence, and later stage-play tradition. It uses Japanese-calendar dates; pair them with Bitō's Gregorian conversion.

## Exclusions

- Do not claim a specific etiquette insult or failed bribe caused Asano's attack; surviving evidence does not establish it.
- Do not say exactly 47 men took part in the raid; the group is remembered as the Forty-seven Rōnin, but Bitō records uncertainty about the last member's participation.
- Do not call the revenge legally sanctioned or say the rōnin were simply heroes. Their action was private retaliation after an official judgment; the shogunate faced an order-versus-loyalty conflict.
- Do not treat Chūshingura drama as an exact account of 1701–1703. Do not add graphic violence or unsupported inner thoughts.
