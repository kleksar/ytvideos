# Paper Detour 038 — Akō Incident: factual basis v03

Status: DRAFT, 2 November 2026. Revised after the second user-supplied story assessment. The July 1702 decision about Asano Daigaku is the mid-story turning point; it closes one concrete route to restoration without claiming it reveals Ōishi's private motive. The attack count follows Henry D. Smith's description of the prevailing historical view: 47 attacked, while 46 surrendered and were sentenced.

## Story promise

Ōishi guides the former retainers of Asano through a series of choices: surrender Akō Castle, seek restoration of the house, then attack Kira after the route through Asano Daigaku closes. They avenge their lord, surrender, and receive a sentence that recognizes samurai status while upholding shogunate law.

## Sources

- Bitō Masahide, [“The Akō Incident, 1701–1703”](https://www.columbia.edu/~hds2/pdf/2003b_Bito_Ako_Incident.pdf), translated by Henry D. Smith II, *Monumenta Nipponica* 58:2. See especially pp. 157–162 and 165–167 for choices, Daigaku, the July 1702 custody decision, the overlapping aims, the count caveat, and the legal dilemma.
- Henry D. Smith II, [“The Trouble with Terasaka: The Forty-Seventh Rōnin and the Chūshingura Imagination”](https://nichibun.repo.nii.ac.jp/records/252), *Japan Review* 16 (2004), pp. 3–65. Says most historians count 47 attackers, explains the 46 surrendered, surveys conflicting accounts of Terasaka, and records his death in 1747.
- Henry D. Smith II, [“Rethinking the Story of the 47 Rōnin”](https://www.columbia.edu/~hds2/47ronin/47ronin_rev.htm), Columbia University. Dates the debate at seven weeks and gives March 20, 1703 for the order/executions.
- Columbia University ExEAS, [“Law and Society: The Story of the 47 Rōnin”](https://afe.easia.columbia.edu/special/japan_1450_47ronin.htm). Confirms the Asano/Kira outcome and that 46 were sentenced to seppuku.
- Akō Castle Digital Museum, [“Akō: The Birthplace of Chūshingura”](https://ako-castle.jp/en/learn/the-birthplace-of-chushingura/), maintained by Akō City. Gives the July Daigaku decision, Maruyama meeting, 47-attack account, and later play tradition.

## Script boundaries

- Use 47 attackers and 46 surrendered/sentenced as the selected scholarly framing; retain the source disagreement about Terasaka.
- State that Terasaka lived until 1747. Keep uncertainty attached to his participation and separation, not his survival.
- The July 1702 decision closes the path to restoring Akō through Daigaku. Do not claim the date proves exactly when Ōishi's private intention changed; restoration and revenge could coexist.
- Avoid invented dialogue, private feelings, raid choreography, weather, disguises, passwords, or unsupported casualty counts.
- End on the documented legal dilemma, not on Terasaka's disputed biography.
