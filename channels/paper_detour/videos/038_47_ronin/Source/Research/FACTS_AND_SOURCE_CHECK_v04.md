# Paper Detour 038 — Akō Incident: factual basis v04

Status: DRAFT, 2 November 2026. The user approved v03 as storyboard basis with three final editorial adjustments. v04 applies those changes and clarifies the seven-week interval without claiming to know the contents of the shogunate's discussions.

## Story promise

Ōishi's former fellow retainers kill the man their lord first attacked. They lose their positions, pursue restoration, then carry out the revenge and surrender. The state weighs loyalty against the rule of law and sentences 46 men to seppuku.

## Claims and limits

| ID | Claim | Evidence / boundary | Planned use |
|---|---|---|---|
| F01 | Asano attacked Kira first in Edo Castle in 1701; Kira was lightly wounded. | Bitō and Columbia ExEAS. Cause of Asano's attack is unknown. Kira did not kill Asano; no invented precipitating insult or failed bribe. | Opening paradox. |
| F02 | Asano was sentenced to seppuku and Akō was confiscated; Kira was not punished. The retainers were released from service and became rōnin. | Bitō and Columbia ExEAS. Define rōnin as samurai without a lord; the loss of posts/service is the supported human consequence. Do not imply a modern trial. | Stakes and immediate consequence. |
| F03 | Ōishi chose peaceful surrender of Akō, partly to avoid bringing further trouble on Asano Daigaku, and pursued restoration of the Asano house. | Bitō, pp. 157–160. Other retainers considered siege, junshi, petitions, and revenge. Do not assign one motive to all. | Character arc. |
| F04 | Horibe and two companions favored a prompt attack, abandoned it after reports that Uesugi might protect Kira, then went to Akō and urged a siege; Ōishi maintained his decision to surrender. | Bitō, pp. 159–160; Horibe's account is the basis for the early attack story. The shortened narration must retain the distinction between the abandoned attack and later siege proposal. | Brief strategic contrast. |
| F05 | In the seventh month of 1702, Daigaku was released from house arrest and placed in custody of the main Asano house in Hiroshima, making restoration through him moot. | Bitō, p. 160; Akō Castle Digital Museum also describes the July decision as ending restoration hopes. This closes a concrete route, not a provable moment in Ōishi's private mind. | Midpoint. |
| F06 | After that decision, retainers met in Kyoto and moved toward the attack. | Akō Castle Digital Museum; Bitō's caution on overlapping aims and unknowable private expectations remains. | Turn leading to revenge. |
| F07 | Forty-seven retainers attacked Kira; Terasaka separated from the other forty-six before they surrendered. | Henry D. Smith, “The Trouble with Terasaka,” says most historians count 47 attackers. Bitō's account follows a 46-attacker count; preserve this disagreement in notes, while narration follows the selected scholarly majority. | Raid. |
| F08 | Terasaka lived until 1747; the uncertainty concerns his role and separation. | Smith, “The Trouble with Terasaka,” pp. 4–7, 23. | Brief clarification; do not make it the film's final question. |
| F09 | Seven weeks passed between the surrender and the shogunate's order sentencing the 46 men to seppuku. | Henry D. Smith, “Rethinking the Story of the 47 Rōnin,” dates the decision/execution to March 20, 1703. Bitō confirms that the decision was not reached until the second month of the following year, but cautions that what was debated is not fully known. Use “seven weeks pass before a decision,” not “officials debated for seven weeks.” | Hook and resolution. |
| F10 | The ruling weighed loyalty/samurai standing against state law and public order. | Bitō, pp. 165–167; Columbia ExEAS describes seppuku as the honorable form of death granted to the men. Phrase final narration as synthesis, not as a verbatim historical quotation. | Final moral statement. |
| F11 | The vendetta later became the Chūshingura stage story. | Akō Castle Digital Museum and Bitō. | Brief sentence before the final thought. |

## Sources

- Henry D. Smith II, [“The Trouble with Terasaka: The Forty-Seventh Rōnin and the Chūshingura Imagination”](https://nichibun.repo.nii.ac.jp/records/252), *Japan Review* 16 (2004), pp. 3–65.
- Henry D. Smith II, [“Rethinking the Story of the 47 Rōnin”](https://www.columbia.edu/~hds2/47ronin/47ronin_rev.htm).

## Script boundaries

- Narrate 47 attackers and 46 surrendering/sentenced as the selected reading; retain the 46-attacker variant in this ledger.
- Show the initial attack's direction clearly: Asano attacked Kira; Kira did not kill Asano.
- Ground human stakes in loss of service/posts after the confiscation. Do not invent individual family members, poverty, dialogue, or feelings.
- The July 1702 order closes the route to restoring the house through Daigaku. Do not call it the precise moment Ōishi privately decided on revenge.
- No invented raid choreography, disguises, passwords, weather, or casualty counts.
- End with the documented law/loyalty conflict; keep Terasaka's biography before that ending.
