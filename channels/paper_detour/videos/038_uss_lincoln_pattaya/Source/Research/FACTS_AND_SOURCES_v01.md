# Facts and source review v01 — USS Abraham Lincoln / Pattaya

**Purpose:** Establish usable factual limits for the Paper Detour Short.

## Findings for narration

- **Ship and setting:** USS Abraham Lincoln reached Laem Chabang, near Pattaya, on September 2, 2026. Pattaya is the shore-leave destination; the carrier did not dock in Pattaya itself.
- **Duration:** The carrier's full deployment lasted 286 days. AP reported 264 days at sea before arrival; Stars and Stripes reported a brief Oman port stop where some sailors could leave the ship only within a secure compound and counted 208 continuous days at sea in its account. Because definitions and timelines vary, v02 uses the well-supported description “first full port visit” / “nine months without a full port visit,” not “264 days without stopping” or “nearly a year trapped.”
- **People:** Approximately 5,000 sailors and Marines were aboard the carrier. Shore leave was staggered; do not imply that all 5,000 were ashore together.
- **Shipboard conditions:** Families reported food shortages, broken toilets and other problems. The Navy acknowledged supply/logistics problems while disputing broader reports of poor conditions. Do not assert that sewage flooded living decks or corridors; no source reviewed here substantiates that claim for the Abraham Lincoln. Separate reports of sewage overflow on USS Gerald R. Ford must not be transferred to this ship.
- **Police operation:** Khaosod English reported that 25 people were detained in an August 29 operation targeting suspected public solicitation. Other reports described separate or broader operations and different totals; v02 uses the specific reported 25-person operation and “detained,” not “arrested.” Pattaya’s mayor said the operation was not specifically connected to the U.S. visit. Thai authorities also stepped up patrols for the port visit.
- **Source distinction, checked November 3, 2026:** Khaosod’s report identifies 25 people detained on August 29 and describes alleged loitering, soliciting or approaching tourists for prostitution in public areas. The Nation separately reports the mayor’s denial of a specific link and describes 25 women arrested for solicitation about a week before the port call. Keep the 25-person count tied to Khaosod and the mayor’s statement attributed to him; do not use the different reports to add gender or claim a single precisely described operation beyond what each source says.
- **Military restrictions:** The U.S. Navy set rules for its own personnel ashore. Pattaya officials said the city was not imposing a general area ban; avoid implying that Thai police blocked sailors from sex-work districts.
- **First-night outcome:** The Washington Post quoted one anonymous go-go bar owner saying she saw no apparent Abraham Lincoln customers at her venue on the first night and that the hype was bigger than reality. This is one venue’s report, not evidence that sailors did not visit other businesses or that the city was empty.
- **Unsupported viral details:** No reliable source reviewed here confirms that sex workers took rare vacations, that the 5,000 sailors “overwhelmed” them, or that sewage flooded residential decks and hallways.
