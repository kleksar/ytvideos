## Editorial arc

Unexpected Iranian-born volunteer / U.S. shore leave → carrier and Iran-war context → long deployment and port arrival → reported shipboard strain → separate Pattaya enforcement and leave rules → Peseshk’s local role and words → identical opening composition, now understood differently.

The opening answers the premise immediately. Peseshk’s quote resolves the emotional contrast before the final line; the final unfinished clause leads grammatically into the hook. The last frame is an exact reuse of the first still.

## Shared image and text rules

- Build each scene as one complete layered paper diorama with visible cut edges, matte paper/card, paper fibers, vellum for windows or light, and soft physical shadows. FG/MG/BG describe depth inside one still; they do not imply separate animation layers.
- Keep character and object poses still. No camera motion, parallax, or independent object/character animation. Use direct cuts with clear changes of shot size or angle.
- Keep the lower subtitle-safe area clear: approximately `x=100–980`, `y=1520–1780`. Keep faces and important objects outside it. Reserve the upper editorial-accent placement area, approximately `y=280–550`, with a suitable scene background.
- Do not paint lettering into scene-image PNGs. The two editorial accents are separate static caption assets owned by the Paper captions stage; narration subtitles are also composed separately.
- Keep arrests, the mayor’s denial, stepped-up patrols, and U.S. shore-leave rules visually separate. Do not imply a causal link between the August operation and the Lincoln visit.
- Peseshk is an Iranian-born Pattaya resident who participated in volunteer street patrols. Do not give him unverified citizenship, formal police powers, a personal assignment to monitor U.S. sailors or sex workers, or a personal role in arrests.
- Walking Street remained accessible. Do not depict city police cordons, a closed street, sailors being turned away, a mass simultaneous shore-leave crowd, sewage flooding, or every sailor as hungry.

## Accents and loop

1. **`IRAN-BORN` — S01 only.** Cream-colored, prominent but not dominant. It describes Peseshk’s origin, not his current citizenship. Captions produces it as a separate static overlay, appearing after the spoken phrase “Iranian-born” and ending by “ashore.” Use the one planned alternate anchor `(0.285, 0.16)` in the upper-left area, keeping the whole phrase and its shadow fully left of the fold and inside safe margins. S17 uses the clean master PNG without an accent so the repeated composition can carry the loop without repeating a fact the voice does not say there.
2. **`WE ARE ALL BROTHERS` — S16.** The only emotional quote accent; captions produces it as two centered lines (`WE ARE ALL` / `BROTHERS`) at primary anchor `(0.66, 0.16)`, separate from lower narration subtitles and clear of the foliage.

No other editorial accents are recommended after a scene-by-scene audit: S02–S05 form a continuous war-to-arrival-to-shore-leave visual account; S06 is a short context beat; S07–S09 keep distinct reports and visit patrols visually separate; S10–S12 form one map-and-rules block; S13–S15 lower visual density before the quote resolves; S17 intentionally returns to the clean loop image. All other visible writing is functional labeling on maps or objects. Keep scene-image stills free of overlay lettering; do not animate the editorial accents.

Loop read-through: “For Peseshk, everyone in Pattaya was a brother—even on the night when an Iranian-born volunteer patrolled Pattaya as U.S. sailors came ashore during a port call by a carrier strike group that had spent months supporting strikes on Iran.” The S17→S01 image cut is frame-matched by reusing one master PNG.

## Explicit visual exclusions

- Peseshk beside detainees, checking sailors’ papers, blocking a street entrance, or acting as a named officer’s partner.
- The detainees and the carrier in one scene, a visual arrow linking the carrier’s arrival to the arrests, or a mayor directing the depicted raid.
- Police cordons on Walking Street, closed gates, or sailors being turned away.
- Sewage flooding ship decks, every sailor shown hungry, all 5,000 leaving at once, or a staged encounter between Peseshk and U.S. sailors.
