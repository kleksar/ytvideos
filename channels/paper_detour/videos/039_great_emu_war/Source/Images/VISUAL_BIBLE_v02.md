# Visual Bible v02 — The Great Emu War 039

**Status:** production guide derived from the locked narration v04 and storyboard v01. It does not add story events.  
**Format:** landscape 16:9, one complete opaque full-bleed handmade paper/card diorama per scene. Request 3840×2160 where supported; record each returned file's actual dimensions separately. Direct cuts between stills.

## Paper world

- Use the actual accepted Paper Detour004 reference at `channels/paper_detour/Shared/References/Paper/paper004_user_38.png` for material, cut edges, folds, volume, tangible fibers, physical light and shadow. Its room, furniture, characters, portrait crop and exact palette are not part of this story.
- Build scenes from layered matte paper/card with visible fibers, hand-cut edges and believable physical contact shadows. People and animals remain paper constructions, never photographic subjects, flat vector shapes or smooth digital 3D renders.
- Keep an earthy Western Australian farming landscape: dry ochre ground, restrained wheat gold, muted brown-green scrub, weathered timber and wire, a pale dusty sky, and muted water at the dam. Change time-of-day light when the narration requires it; retain the same constructed paper material.
- Use foreground, middle ground and background to create a coherent handmade paper composition. Each scene remains one finished still; the whole image may receive a restrained pan/zoom in FFmpeg, with no independently moving depth layers or parallax. No split panels, collage, diagram, text, labels, borders or watermark.
- Use landscape 16:9 throughout, with the full image reaching all four edges. Do not leave bars or blank margins. Preserve action, faces, bird silhouettes and scene-specific text regions against the actual motion/crop envelope, not a guessed universal safe percentage.

## Continuity keys

- **Recurring farmer:** an anonymous paper farm worker in a broad-brimmed hat and plain light work shirt. Keep the same silhouette and clothing when returning to him; do not assign a personal name, family backstory or invented emotion.
- **Soldiers:** one small recurring Australian army unit in restrained period khaki and simple field gear. Keep weapons, vehicle, positions and unit continuity aligned to the storyboard. Do not add a named soldier. Major Meredith is represented as a distinct senior officer within that unit; use a modest paper portrait, not an invented photo likeness or unsupported personal details.
- **Cameraman:** a separate civilian paper figure accompanying the unit with a period camera on a tripod. Avoid legible film labels or invented dialogue.
- **Emus:** large, flightless, dark gray-brown paper birds with layered feather shapes and long running legs. Preserve recognizable whole-body proportions, direction and count/spacing specified by each shot. Fallen birds are shown without blood or graphic detail.
- **Farm environment:** one coherent dam, dirt road, fence language, wheat crop, modest farm buildings and dry plain recur where the storyboard calls them back. Damage persists until the accepted sequence changes it; returning soldiers do not reset the damage.
- **Truck:** one consistent period farm/army transport carrying the same mounted Lewis gun across S39–S45, then only as directed by later scenes. Preserve its paper silhouette, wheel placement and gun mount between views.
- **Canberra:** one consistent paper parliamentary chamber for S48–S49. It is a separate setting from the farm district.

## Movement and text

- Animate the whole accepted still in FFmpeg. Choose pan and/or zoom from the actual image and spoken beat; record focus, start/end framing and reason in the edit plan. No parallax or separate layer movement. Verify composition and protected details across the full path before import.
- End-screen scenes S71–S72 keep important subjects outside the reserved native YouTube element areas for their full motion path. The PNGs contain only the finished paper background; YouTube UI, recommendation cards and interface text remain native overlays.
- Narration subtitles and meaning accents are separate screen-space layers. Do not reserve a universal blank subtitle band or bake narration text into the image. Follow the scene-specific accepted cue sheet when it is prepared.
