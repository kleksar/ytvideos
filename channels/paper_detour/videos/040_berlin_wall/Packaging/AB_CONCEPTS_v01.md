# Paper Detour 040 — A/B/C concepts

Film: 040_berlin_wall_review_v02.mp4; packaging status: proposal, not approved. Audience language: English.

## A — the answer

Title: “Immediately. Without Delay.” The Berlin Wall Press Conference

Thumbnail lettering: EFFECTIVE WHEN?

Viewer question: how did the answer to a journalist's question change the evening? Image: an official searches a document at a microphone while a journalist asks a question.

Description:
In East Berlin, a question at the end of a press conference called for a simple answer: when would the new travel rules take effect?

The answer went out on television. Within hours, people were gathering at Berlin's checkpoints, while the guards faced a situation their instructions had not prepared them for. This is the story of the announcement, the growing crowds, and the night the crossing opened.

## B — the checkpoint

Title: The Night Berlin’s Border Guards Had No Clear Orders

Thumbnail lettering: OPEN THE GATE?

Viewer question: what could the guards do when the public expected the border to be open? Image: a guard at a closed barrier faces waiting citizens.

Description:
Television said the border was open. At the checkpoint, the barrier was still closed.

As more East Berliners arrived, border guards had to decide what to do with a crowd that believed it had permission to cross. Follow the tense hours at Bornholmer Straße, from confused instructions to the moment the checks stopped.

## C — the people

Title: Would You Go to the Berlin Wall That Night?

Thumbnail lettering: WOULD YOU GO?

Viewer question: why did ordinary East Berliners head to the border and stay there? Image: a resident walks toward the illuminated checkpoint behind neighbours.

Description:
Imagine hearing on television that you could finally travel to the other side of Berlin. Would you go to the border that night?

On November 9, 1989, people did exactly that. They gathered at the checkpoints, asked to cross, and waited as the guards tried to understand their orders. Their determination helped turn an announcement into an open passage.

## Pairwise distinction

A/B titles: A asks about the press conference answer; B focuses on the guards' lack of instructions. Images: microphones and document / guard and barrier.
A/C titles: A asks about the announcement; C asks about a resident's choice. Images: press desk / resident approaching the checkpoint.
B/C titles: B focuses on the guards' dilemma; C on the public's decision to go. Images: a guard at the barrier / a resident approaching the crossing.
