How did an African arrow end up in a stork’s neck in Germany?

In autumn, swallows and storks vanished from parts of Europe. In spring, they returned. Where had they been?

In the seventeenth century, English scholar Charles Morton proposed an extraordinary answer: swallows and storks spent winter on the Moon.

Then, on May twenty-first, eighteen twenty-two, a white stork was shot near Klütz, in northern Germany.

In its neck was an arrow about eighty centimeters long, from Central Africa.

The bird had carried it across an enormous distance. The arrow stork became the first physical evidence of long-distance migration by European birds.

The specimen is still preserved in the University of Rostock’s zoological collection.

The answer was not the Moon. Some of those birds crossed continents to spend the winter.
