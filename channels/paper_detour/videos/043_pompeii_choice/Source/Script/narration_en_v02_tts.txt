Would you run through falling stones—or stay beneath a roof that might collapse?

In AD 79, Mount Vesuvius erupted. For hours, pumice fell on Pompeii, piling up in streets and on rooftops. As the stones accumulated, getting out became harder.

Some residents escaped while routes were still open. Others stayed inside. But shelter could become a trap: some people died when roofs and walls collapsed.

Leaving early gave some a way out. Remaining indoors did not guarantee survival.

And outside, the stones kept falling.
