What if returning home meant waiting 30 days outside the city? In 1377, decades after the first wave of the Black Death, the trading city of Ragusa, now Dubrovnik, introduced a rule for arrivals from plague-affected places. Before entering, they had to spend 30 days apart in designated locations. The rule could apply to residents returning home, too. Waiting meant people needed food and support while contact with the city was restricted. The authorities did not know the bacterium that caused plague, but they were trying to reduce risk without ending the trade on which the port depended. Later, a 40-day period gave us the word quarantine.
