“We are holding our own.”

That was the last known radio reply from the Edmund Fitzgerald. Soon afterward, the massive freighter disappeared from radar during a violent storm on Lake Superior. No distress call was heard, and all 29 people aboard were lost.

On November 10, 1975, the Fitzgerald was heading toward Whitefish Bay, with the Arthur M. Anderson following behind. The crew had reported damage, a list, and failed radars. Yet when the Anderson contacted them that evening, the Fitzgerald was still responding.

The wreck was later discovered about 160 meters underwater, in two main sections. The U.S. Coast Guard and the National Transportation Safety Board offered different explanations involving water entering the cargo hold and the hatch covers.

But neither investigation could fully reconstruct the ship's final minutes.

What happened between that last known radio reply and the Fitzgerald's disappearance remains an unanswered question.
