The Arthur M. Anderson had reached sheltered waters when it turned around and headed back into a violent storm on Lake Superior.

Another freighter, the Edmund Fitzgerald, had disappeared.

On November 10, 1975, the two ships had been navigating worsening weather while trying to reach Whitefish Bay. The Fitzgerald reported damage, a list, and failed radars. The Anderson helped by radio, providing information about the shore and nearby vessels.

Then came the Fitzgerald's last known reply: “We are holding our own.”

Soon afterward, its radar echo vanished. Its lights were no longer visible, and repeated radio calls went unanswered.

The Anderson reported the disappearance to the Coast Guard before returning into the storm to help search. Lifeboats and debris were found, but there were no survivors. All 29 people aboard the Fitzgerald had died.

Later investigations offered different explanations for the disaster, but the exact events of the ship's final minutes remain unknown.
