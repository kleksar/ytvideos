About 160 meters beneath Lake Superior lies the wreck of the Edmund Fitzgerald, divided into two main sections. Its bow rests upright. Its stern lies upside down.

But even the discovery of the wreck did not settle what happened during the ship's final minutes.

On November 10, 1975, the massive freighter disappeared during a violent storm. Its last known radio reply was “We are holding our own.” No distress call was heard. All 29 people aboard were lost.

The United States Coast Guard concluded that water had probably been entering the cargo hold gradually through hatch covers that were not sufficiently watertight, causing the ship to settle lower in the water.

The National Transportation Safety Board proposed a different explanation for the final stage. Water may also have been accumulating inside the ship, but then one or more hatch covers gave way under water pressure, causing the cargo hold to flood rapidly.

These are investigation conclusions, not a record of what happened aboard.

Two explanations. No definitive reconstruction of the final minutes.
