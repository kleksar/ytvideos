After the Fitzgerald disappeared, the Arthur M. Anderson left sheltered waters and turned back into the storm to help search.

The Edmund Fitzgerald left Superior on November 9, 1975, bound for the Detroit area with 29 people aboard and more than 26,000 tons of iron ore. The 200-meter freighter was making another crossing of the Great Lakes when a powerful storm closed in on Lake Superior.

The Fitzgerald and the Arthur M. Anderson changed course toward the Canadian shore, then tried to reach the more protected waters of Whitefish Bay. The Fitzgerald reported damage, a list and pumps running. Later its radars failed, and the Anderson helped by radio with the ship’s position.

The last known radio reply was: “We are holding our own.” Soon afterward, the Fitzgerald disappeared. No distress call was heard. The Anderson reported the loss and turned back into the storm to help search. All 29 people aboard died.

Searchers later found the wreck about 160 meters below the surface, in two main sections. The U.S. Coast Guard concluded that water probably entered gradually through hatch covers that were not sufficiently watertight. The National Transportation Safety Board proposed a different final sequence: one or more hatch covers may have failed under the water’s pressure, flooding the hold rapidly. These are investigation conclusions; the final minutes were never fully reconstructed.
