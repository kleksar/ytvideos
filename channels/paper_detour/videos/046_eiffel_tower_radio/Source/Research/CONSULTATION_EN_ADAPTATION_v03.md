The Eiffel Tower, about 300 metres tall, opened in 1889 for the World's Fair. In 1898, engineer Eugène Ducretet sent a wireless Morse code signal from the tower to the Panthéon in Paris. In 1899, military engineer Gustave Ferrié watched Italian inventor Guglielmo Marconi demonstrate wireless telegraphy. Ferrié was then assigned to develop radio for the French military. Transmitters and receivers were tested at military facilities and lighthouses. Some within the military doubted that the new technology would be useful.

In 1903, Gustave Eiffel, the man behind the tower, let Ferrié use it for experiments and helped fund the research. The military engineering department gave permission to install antennas. Ferrié's team worked in a wooden room near the tower's southern leg.

Now the tall structure in Paris was sending signals to fortresses in eastern France, exactly where the army needed communication. Later, signals from the tower reached a military base in Bizerte, Tunisia.

In 1909, a permanent military radio station was installed beneath the Champ de Mars. The equipment was placed underground, while the antennas were mounted on the Eiffel Tower.
