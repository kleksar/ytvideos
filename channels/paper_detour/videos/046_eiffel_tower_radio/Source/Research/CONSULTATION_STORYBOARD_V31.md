The French army needed radio communication between Paris and the fortresses in eastern France. At the fortresses, antennas were lifted by tethered balloons, but this depended on the weather. In Paris, the army needed a permanent, tall structure to support an antenna. A tower built for an exhibition, not for the military, could serve that purpose.

The Eiffel Tower, about 300 metres tall, opened in 1889 for the World's Fair. In 1898, engineer Eugène Ducretet sent a wireless Morse code signal from the tower to the Panthéon in Paris. In 1899, military engineer Gustave Ferrié watched Italian inventor Guglielmo Marconi demonstrate wireless telegraphy. Ferrié was then assigned to develop radio for the French military.
