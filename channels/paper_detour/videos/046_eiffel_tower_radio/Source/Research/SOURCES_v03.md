# Additional evidence for story revisions v17–v20

Research supplement, 2026-11-05. This is not a complete fact-check. Earlier limits in `SOURCES_v01.md` and later research in `SOURCES_v02.md` remain in force.

| Claim considered for narration | Evidence | Limit / editorial action |
|---|---|---|
| In 1899 Ferrié attended a presentation by Marconi, became interested in wireless telegraphy and was appointed by the Minister of War to a committee studying its military applications. | French Ministry of Armed Forces, [Gustave Auguste Ferrié](https://www.defense.gouv.fr/chemins-memoire/histoire-memoires/personnalites/personnalites-lettre-e-lettre-m/gustave-auguste-ferrie). | Use the presentation and committee appointment as separate documented events. Do not claim the commission assigned him to the Eiffel Tower. |
| One of Ferrié's military objectives was to improve communication between Army General Headquarters in Paris and French fortresses on the eastern frontiers; work required higher antennas, more powerful transmitters and more sensitive receivers. At frontier forts, antennas were carried aloft by tethered balloons. | URSI Bulletin No. 223 (1982), [historical account of wireless telegraphy](https://www.ursi.org/content/RSB/RSB_223_1982_12.pdf), section “The XX Century”. | State the objective without saying the whole chain of fortresses was connected. The article includes specific range figures and dates that remain excluded. Its captive-balloon description is used without the reported altitude. |
| Eiffel offered the Tower and financial support for Ferrié's military research; the Department of Military Engineering authorised antennas; Ferrié worked in a wooden shelter by the south pillar; a permanent underground military station was installed in 1909. | Official Eiffel Tower site, [The Eiffel Tower and science](https://www.toureiffel.paris/en/the-monument/eiffel-tower-and-science); [How radio saved the Eiffel Tower](https://www.toureiffel.paris/en/news/130-years/why-was-eiffel-tower-kept). | Keep the 1903 decision to retain the Tower separate from later radio results. Do not use the site's disputed 70-year/1910 concession claim. |
| The 1898 Tower–Panthéon wireless trial is described as 4 km by the official Eiffel Tower history and National Archives material, but as just over 400 m in the URSI account. | Official Eiffel Tower history and National Archives evidence already recorded in `SOURCES_v01.md`; URSI Bulletin No. 223 above. | Avoid an exact distance in narration until the discrepancy is resolved against stronger primary evidence. Drafts v01–v18 stated 4 km; v19 onward omit it. Do not confuse this discrepancy with the separately excluded 400 km military range. |

The 1908 time-signal proposal in `SOURCES_v02.md` is not used: the requested story ends at the permanent military station in 1909. The disputed concession duration, 1910 legal ending, 400 km / 6,000 km range figures, disputed dates of later trials, and an invented last-minute demolition threat remain excluded.
