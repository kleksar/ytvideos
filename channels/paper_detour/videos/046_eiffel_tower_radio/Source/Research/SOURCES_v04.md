# Ferrié's military wireless work — evidence supplement

Research supplement, 2026-11-05. Use with the boundaries in `SOURCES_v01.md` and `SOURCES_v03.md`. This adds documented events that develop Ferrié's military work; it does not supersede the disputed distance, concession, or demolition limits.

| Claim considered | Evidence | Safe narration boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Ferrié represented the French War Ministry at Marconi wireless demonstrations in 1899. The Minister of War then asked whether France could develop military uses without relying on a foreign company; Ferrié was tasked with developing French military wireless. | Paul Brenot, “Le Général Ferrié,” *Revue historique des Armées* 23-1 (1967), [Persée](https://www.persee.fr/doc/rharm_0035-3299_1967_num_23_1_6049?pageId=T1_24), lines 38–48; Michel Amoudry, “Le Général Ferrié (1868–1932),” *Bulletin de la SABIX* 48 (2011), [OpenEdition PDF](https://journals.openedition.org/sabix/pdf/1003), lines 138–160; French Ministry of Armed Forces, [Gustave Auguste Ferrié](https://www.defense.gouv.fr/chemins-memoire/histoire-memoires/personnalites/personnalites-lettre-e-lettre-m/gustave-auguste-ferrie). | State the commission and assignment. Do not invent a spoken answer or claim the commission assigned the Eiffel Tower. The foreign-company decision is government context, not a personal rivalry. |
| Ferrié's team tested wireless equipment at military sites; contemporary military institutions still questioned its value, and later lighthouse tests confirmed results. | Brenot, *Revue historique des Armées*, [Persée](https://www.persee.fr/doc/rharm_0035-3299_1967_num_23_1_6049?pageId=T1_24), lines 50–61; Amoudry, *Bulletin de la SABIX* 48, [OpenEdition PDF](https://journals.openedition.org/sabix/pdf/1003), lines 156–184. | Describe tests and institutional skepticism without invented individual conflict, failed attempts, or exact disputed ranges. No need to name every test site. |
| The 1902 Mount Pelée eruption broke the telegraph cable between Martinique and Guadeloupe. Ferrié was sent with improvised equipment; wireless posts on the islands exchanged a first message on 4 December 1902, and the link continued until the cable was restored in late 1903. | Robert Bourgeois, “Discours sur Gustave Ferrié,” *Académie des sciences* (1932), [notice PDF](https://www.academie-sciences.fr/pdf/eloges/ferrie_notice.pdf), lines 4688–4728; Amoudry, *Bulletin de la SABIX* 48, [OpenEdition PDF](https://journals.openedition.org/sabix/pdf/1003), lines 192–198; Brenot, *Revue historique des Armées*, [Persée](https://www.persee.fr/doc/rharm_0035-3299_1967_num_23_1_6049?pageId=T1_24), lines 62–64; École Polytechnique, [Ferrié biography](https://www.polytechnique.edu/bibliotheque/actualites/ferrie-gustave-x1887), lines 61–64. | This is a distinct, documented operational use that demonstrates wireless could carry messages when a cable was unavailable. Do not say the event caused the City of Paris's 1903 decision or Eiffel's offer; do not add casualty figures or exact distance. |
| Balloon-borne antennas served eastern forts but depended on weather; Ferrié sought a fixed high antenna support in Paris, and Eiffel's Tower supplied it. | Bourgeois, *Académie des sciences* (1932), [notice PDF](https://www.academie-sciences.fr/pdf/eloges/ferrie_notice.pdf), lines 4742–4787; Amoudry, *Bulletin de la SABIX* 48, [OpenEdition PDF](https://journals.openedition.org/sabix/pdf/1003), lines 200–218. | It is safe to state that balloon-supported antenna use depended on weather and that Ferrié wanted a fixed, high Paris support. Avoid technical claims about storms or precise effects on signal quality. |
| Eiffel made the Tower available and helped fund military wireless tests; the Department of Military Engineering authorised antennas; equipment was placed in wooden shelters near the south pillar; a permanent underground military station was installed beneath the Champ de Mars in 1909. | Official Eiffel Tower site, [The Eiffel Tower and science](https://www.toureiffel.paris/en/the-monument/eiffel-tower-and-science); [How radio saved the Eiffel Tower](https://www.toureiffel.paris/en/news/130-years/why-was-eiffel-tower-kept); Amoudry, *Bulletin de la SABIX* 48, [OpenEdition PDF](https://journals.openedition.org/sabix/pdf/1003), lines 205–227. | Keep the City's 1903 retention decision distinct from subsequent military results. Do not claim radio alone saved the Tower, use a fictitious last-minute demolition threat, or include disputed concession terms. |
