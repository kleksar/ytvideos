- Prompt scope: develop a coherent visual approach and four representative static Paper diorama briefs from the approved English narration summary. No story rewrite or fact-check.

## Decision

Visual concept: “The landscape held the evidence.” Organize progression as uncertainty → testimony and geography → targeted searching → physical discovery. Let folded coastlines, sculpted ice, documentary objects, exposed shore fragments, water surfaces and submerged wooden structures carry the visual world; avoid repeating generic maps or sonar screens. Every frame is one complete static 16:9 diorama, 4–6 seconds, changed only by hard cuts. Use consistent paper thickness, cut edges, sculptural relief and directional studio light. Restrained palette: deep slate water, muted blue ice, ivory documents, mineral-grey shorelines, weathered timber. One focal idea and at most two supporting elements per frame. Match shapes across cuts without implying literal time travel or exact routes. Narration carries dates and qualifications; images stay concrete and avoid conjectured meetings, coordinates or unknowable moments.

## Representative staging decisions

1. **Opening, Terror Bay (2016):** a small research vessel enters darker sheltered water; empty seabed, no wreck silhouette, no Kogvik depicted aboard, no invented discussion, route or survey equipment. Editorial base tab: “TERROR BAY”. Curved bay water can cut to a nineteenth-century ship’s bow as a shape match.
2. **1848 note:** close view of an aged document on a stepped paper surface; distant broken ice and open water, no wrecks. Editorial year tab “1848”; no invented quotation or scene of writing/discovery. This marks the gap between abandonment and final wreck locations.
3. **Inuit knowledge:** coastal relief dominates; separate record slips name William Uligpak, Takulittuk, Ipiivik, Inukpudjijuk, with later distinct slips for David Woodman and Louie Kamookak. Do not depict one generic Inuit figure, invented portraits, simultaneous meetings, shared process or invented coordinates.
4. **2014 shore artifacts to Erebus:** conceptually separate shore and ocean cutaway areas on one diorama base. Unspecified weathered fragments on shore; small research boat and separate submerged wreck in the ocean cutaway; vellum fan suggests sonar survey. Label “EREBUS” on editorial base. No exact artifact placement/track, finder, instantaneous scan, or literal map arrow.

The consultation also proposed a final single diorama with two physically distinct underwater recesses and separate “EREBUS” / “TERROR” labels; the composition must not imply adjacent wreck sites.
