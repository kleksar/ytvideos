In early nineteenth-century France, food prepared by Nicolas Appert’s method spent three months in sealed vessels at the anchorage near Brest. When the Board of Health inspected the samples, it gave a favorable assessment. How could food survive that long, and could others reproduce the result?

Follow the pastry chef’s experiments with glass vessels, corks and heat, the committee’s examination of his food, and the conditions that led to his method being published.
