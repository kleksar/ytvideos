In 1878, Anandibai Joshee's infant son died after just ten days.

She believed better medical care might have saved him. His death helped shape a decision that would take her thousands of miles from India: she wanted to study medicine in the United States and become a doctor.

Joshee had a particular purpose. Some women in India would not accept treatment from male physicians. She wanted to be able to treat those women herself.

On February 24, 1883, Joshee publicly explained her plan in Serampore, India. She addressed concerns about women studying medicine and about her intention to travel overseas.

She acknowledged that some medical instruction was available in India, but considered it inadequate for the thorough training she wanted. She also hoped to return with medical knowledge and establish instruction for women.

Travel raised another concern. Going overseas could lead to exclusion from her religious and social community. Joshee intended to remain Hindu, keep her customs, and return to India.

Her husband supported her education and expected to send money. By late June, Joshee had reached Roselle, New Jersey. But crossing the ocean had not yet secured her a place at a medical college.

On June 28, 1883, she wrote to the Woman's Medical College of Pennsylvania in Philadelphia, seeking admission on terms she could afford. She had seventy dollars and expected twenty dollars a month from her husband, less the cost of transferring the money.

By October, she was enrolled in Philadelphia.

In 1884, Joshee suffered diphtheria and struggled to obtain familiar food. Rachel Bodley, the dean of the medical college, helped her with housing and familiar food. Joshee lived in Bodley's home for the remainder of her stay in Philadelphia.

Joshee continued her studies and completed five terms.

In March 1886, she graduated from the Woman's Medical College of Pennsylvania. Her thesis concerned obstetrics, the branch of medicine dealing with pregnancy and childbirth.

She became the first woman from India to earn a degree in Western medicine in the United States.

After graduation, she pursued further training, beginning a six-month internship at New England Hospital. Illness forced her to leave before she could complete it.

She then accepted an appointment in India as physician in charge of the women's ward at Albert Edward Hospital in Kolhapur.

In October 1886, Joshee sailed for India. She arrived in November, seriously ill.

On February 26, 1887, Anandibai Joshee died in Pune.

She had earned her medical degree, accepted a hospital appointment, and returned to the country where she hoped to serve. But she died before she could begin practicing medicine there.
