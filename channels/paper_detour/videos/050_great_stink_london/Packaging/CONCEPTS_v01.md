# Paper 050 — A/B/C concept cards v01

Source: accepted narration v01; full candidate `Deliverables/review_en_v01.mp4` remains under QA. Packaging is proposed, not uploaded or tested. English product language.

## A — The crisis reaches Parliament
- Viewer question: Why did London finally have to confront its polluted river?
- Film payoff: 1858 heat and exposed riverbanks made the smell unbearable; the stench disrupted lawmakers in Westminster.
- Title: **The Summer London's Stench Reached Parliament**
- Image situation: clearly distressed Victorian lawmakers by a window beside the Thames; foul river visibly intrudes into the seat of government. Exact generated lettering: **THE GREAT STINK**. The image expresses the crisis; the title locates the political pressure.

## B — Sewers did not clean the waste
- Viewer question: What did the impressive new system actually do with sewage?
- Film payoff: Crossness pumped sewage to a covered reservoir and discharged it into the Thames on the ebb; the original system did not clean it.
- Title: **London Built New Sewers. Why Did the Waste Still Reach the Thames?**
- Image situation: a dramatic cross-section of a covered reservoir and outfall meeting the river; a dark stream visibly joins the Thames. Exact lettering: **STILL IN THE THAMES**. No implication of modern treatment or a verified precise outlet design.

## C — The political choice of outlet
- Viewer question: Why was deciding where to discharge sewage so difficult?
- Film payoff: Parliament debated Barking Creek versus the longer Sea Reach route, while tides made the shorter route uncertain.
- Title: **The Sewage Question Parliament Couldn't Answer**
- Image situation: two opposing hands point to visibly different destinations on a tactile Thames model; reverse tidal current creates uncertainty. Exact lettering: **WHERE TO DUMP IT?** A schematic conceptual decision, not a historical route diagram.

## Pairwise pre-generation distinction
- A vs B titles: pressure on Parliament versus the limitation of the engineering solution. Images: lawmakers reacting to stench versus reservoir/outfall discharging into the river.
- A vs C titles: a crisis reaching government versus an unresolved policy choice. Images: bodily reaction to the river versus a contested map with two destinations.
- B vs C titles: why the new system still discharged waste versus where the disputed outlet should be. Images: physical flow into the river versus decision between routes.

All three concepts make different promises that the accepted story supports. Exact spelling, 320×180 readability, factual interpretation and actual pairwise distinctness must be checked on generated PNGs. No claim is made about CTR.
