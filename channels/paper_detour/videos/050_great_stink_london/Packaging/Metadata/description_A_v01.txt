In the summer of 1858, the smell of the polluted Thames reached the people making Britain’s laws.

London had long struggled with sewage entering its tidal river. When heat and low water made the stench impossible to ignore, Parliament faced a question the smell could not answer: where should the city send its waste? This is the story of the Great Stink, the dispute over the outfalls, and the system that followed.
