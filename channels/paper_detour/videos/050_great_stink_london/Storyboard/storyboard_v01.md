Оптимальная структура — 50 самостоятельных композиций примерно по 4,93 секунды. Это около 246,7 секунды при 30 fps, практически совпадает с длительностью дикторского текста 246,6 секунды. Это плановая сетка, а не монтажные границы: окончательные cut points нужно выставить по word-level audio alignment, не разрезая слова.

Главный визуальный принцип: история должна ощущаться не как последовательность исторических карт, а как инженерная и политическая проблема, которую зритель постепенно понимает через реальные механизмы, городскую среду, человеческие реакции и физические модели.

# Storyboard — London Sewers: The Great Stink and the Thames

# Production direction

- 50 unique full-bleed compositions, approximately 4.9 seconds each.
- Every shot is a complete handcrafted layered-paper diorama, not a collection of disconnected foreground and background elements.
- Tactile paper surfaces, visible cut edges, dimensional shadows, substantial physical depth and deliberate cinematic lighting.
- No decorative outer frame.
- No recurring presenter. Anonymous historical figures appear only when their actions or reactions explain the story.
- One composition per shot. Slow whole-image movement only; no parallax or independently animated paper layers.
- Keep important faces, labels and focal mechanisms inside the central safe area. Reserve the bottom 18% for subtitles. Allow approximately 8% additional edge clearance for later image movement.
- In-world labels should be large, sparse and physically integrated into the diorama. Do not reproduce fictional historical documents or invent inscriptions.
- Historical building and machinery silhouettes must be reference-locked before image generation.

# Act I — The machine and the river

## 01. A machine begins operating

**Narration:** “On April 4, 1865, an unusual machine began operating on the eastern edge of London.”

**Period / location:** 1865, Crossness, eastern London.

**Narrative job:** Open with a distinctive historical place and establish that something substantial has begun operating.

**Visible situation / focal idea:** Crossness pumping station dominates the composition. Its monumental Victorian brick architecture rises above the Thames-side landscape. The building should feel more like an industrial machine disguised as architecture than an ordinary public building.

**Camera / light:** Low three-quarter angle, looking upward. Strong early daylight catches the masonry while the lower structure remains in shadow.

**Meaningful details:** Repeated windows, actual roofline, architectural ornament and visible industrial openings, all taken from historical references. The building must be recognizable as Crossness rather than a generic Victorian factory.

**Scene text:** `CROSSNESS` / `4 APRIL 1865`. Editorial paper typography, not a fabricated historical plaque.

**Transition:** Direct cut from the monumental exterior to the machinery inside.

**Status:** Factual location and date; exterior reconstruction strictly reference-based.

## 02. The pumps lift the sewage

**Narration:** “At Crossness pumping station, steam-powered pumps lifted the city's sewage…”

**Period / location:** 1865, Crossness pumping station.

**Narrative job:** Reveal what the unusual machine actually does.

**Visible situation / focal idea:** A large steam-powered pumping assembly occupies almost the entire image. A dark column of sewage is being raised through the pumping system. The visual emphasis is on the vertical movement of heavy liquid against the enormous mechanical structure.

**Camera / light:** Dramatic low interior angle. Warm light catches the metal mechanism; deep shadows establish scale.

**Meaningful details:** Steam-powered engine, pump assembly, connecting mechanisms and water path must follow photographs or drawings of the actual Crossness machinery. No invented pistons, valves, connecting rods or pipe arrangements.

**Scene text:** None.

**Transition:** Follow the rising water visually into the reservoir.

**Status:** Factual function; mechanical reconstruction requires archival and surviving-engine references.

## 03. The covered reservoir

**Narration:** “…into a covered reservoir.”

**Period / location:** Crossness, 1865.

**Narrative job:** Explain where the lifted sewage goes.

**Visible situation / focal idea:** A large sectional view of the covered reservoir. Its roof is clearly visible, but a cutaway reveals the dark water stored underneath. The incoming flow occupies one side of the composition.

**Camera / light:** Isometric architectural section, viewed slightly from above. Bright light on the roof, dark interior beneath it.

**Meaningful details:** Roof structure, walls, dimensions and reservoir configuration must follow historical drawings. The stored sewage should be visibly contained rather than flowing through an open pond.

**Scene text:** `COVERED RESERVOIR`, on a small physical section label.

**Transition:** Hold on the stationary water surface.

**Status:** Factual structure and function; sectional reconstruction requires a historical plan or photograph.

## 04. It does not leave immediately

**Narration:** “But it wasn't released straight away.”

**Period / location:** Crossness reservoir, 1865.

**Narrative job:** Introduce the important distinction between pumping sewage and discharging it.

**Visible situation / focal idea:** Close sectional view of the reservoir. The dark water has accumulated beneath its roof. The composition emphasizes storage and stillness, not movement toward the river.

**Camera / light:** Tight horizontal composition. The waterline is the dominant visual element; the surrounding structure falls into darkness.

**Meaningful details:** The incoming water path remains visible. Do not invent a particular gate, valve or holding mechanism. The scene communicates the pause through the stored water itself.

**Scene text:** None.

**Transition:** Direct cut from the still water to the moving Thames.

**Status:** Factual process; interpretive sectional reconstruction.

## 05. Waiting for the outgoing tide

**Narration:** “…until the Thames began to flow out with the tide.”

**Period / location:** Thames beside Crossness, 1865.

**Narrative job:** Explain the tidal condition that controlled the timing.

**Visible situation / focal idea:** A low, close view of the Thames. The water visibly travels downstream. Layered paper current ribbons run in one clear direction, with exposed muddy banks helping establish the river's tidal character.

**Camera / light:** Low water-level perspective, long horizontal composition. Cool reflections contrast with warm exposed mud.

**Meaningful details:** Small ripples, sediment layers and the tidal riverbank. The direction of flow must communicate ebb tide, not an arbitrary river current.

**Scene text:** `EBB TIDE`, attached to a restrained directional diagram element.

**Transition:** Match the direction of the current in the next shot.

**Status:** Factual tidal process; simplified visual reconstruction.

## 06. Sewage enters the Thames

**Narration:** “Only then was it discharged into the river.”

**Period / location:** Crossness outfall, 1865.

**Narrative job:** Complete the opening mechanical sequence.

**Visible situation / focal idea:** The Crossness discharge point meets the moving Thames. A visible dark stream enters the river and follows the outgoing current.

**Camera / light:** Close oblique view just above the water. The meeting point of the two flows is the strongest contrast.

**Meaningful details:** Actual historical outfall location and configuration are required. Do not invent gates, a modern discharge pipe, concrete channels or an unsupported outlet structure.

**Scene text:** None.

**Transition:** Pull back conceptually into a much wider view of the sewer system.

**Status:** Factual discharge; outfall geometry requires historical reference.

## 07. Two ends of one system

**Narration:** “This was the southern end of London's new sewer system. Across the river, the northern branch led to Barking Creek.”

**Period / location:** Greater London, system context, 1865–1875.

**Narrative job:** Establish the two-branch structure before rewinding.

**Visible situation / focal idea:** A substantial three-dimensional paper model of the Thames and surrounding city. Two distinct sewer branches leave the London area: one northward toward Barking Creek, the other southward toward Crossness Point.

**Camera / light:** Elevated oblique view, not a flat map. The river forms a strong diagonal separating the composition into two geographic zones.

**Meaningful details:** Two clearly distinguishable branches and two separate destinations. Use correct relative geography from a historical map. Do not invent exact sewer alignments or distances.

**Scene text:** `NORTH — BARKING CREEK` and `SOUTH — CROSSNESS POINT`.

**Transition:** The model folds back visually, revealing the earlier city beneath it.

**Status:** Factual system relationship; schematic geography, not an engineering route plan.

**Meaningful details:** Dense city buildings, the sewer route and the receiving river. Do not show a treatment plant or an invented safe distance.

**Scene text:** None.

**Transition:** Push toward the outlet itself.

**Status:** Conceptual engineering diagram.

## 13. Where should it enter again?

## Visual variety audit

The 50 shots deliberately rotate between different visual languages:

- Monumental architectural establishing shots.
- Extreme mechanical close-ups.
- Covered-reservoir sectional views.
- Water-level river landscapes.
- Underwater cutaways.
- Tidal-flow diagrams.
- Human-scale riverbank reactions.
- Westminster architecture and generic parliamentary interiors.
- Engineering tables and physical route models.
- Geographic destination landscapes.
- Construction trenches and underground infrastructure.
- Completed pumping-station exteriors.
- Large final system diagrams.

Maps are limited to situations where geographic relationships genuinely matter. The rest of the film uses physical places, engineering details, people and sectional models.

**Most important production priority:** Crossness must feel like a real, extraordinary piece of Victorian engineering, not a generic pumping station. The Great Stink must feel like a human and political crisis, not just a sequence of dated parliamentary decisions. And the final image must make the limitation unmistakable: London moved untreated sewage farther downstream; it did not clean it.

## Что особенно важно перед генерацией PNG

Я бы не начинал генерацию всех 50 сцен одновременно. Сначала нужно зафиксировать пять визуальных эталонов:

1. Crossness exterior — чтобы архитектура оставалась узнаваемой.
2. Crossness pumping machinery — главный инженерный визуальный объект фильма.
3. Covered reservoir — без выдуманной конструкции.
4. Victorian interceptor cross-section — чтобы подземная система выглядела исторически убедительно.
5. Abbey Mills exterior — второй архитектурный ориентир, визуально отличный от Crossness.

После этого можно генерировать сцены пакетами, проверяя, что один и тот же объект не меняет архитектуру, механику и пропорции от кадра к кадру.

Особенно важно не делать из фильма 50 красивых, но взаимозаменяемых бумажных иллюстраций. Здесь должны запоминаться конкретные вещи: огромная машина Crossness, река с меняющимся течением, парламентский спор о двух направлениях, два разных насосных комплекса и финальный выход необработанных стоков в ту же Темзу.
