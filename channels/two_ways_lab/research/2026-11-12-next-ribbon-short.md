Current chat and local records were checked; all other project chats and the full public catalogue were not, so novelty is not established.

Candidate: three gifts need continuous ribbon pieces 4 m, 3 m and 3 m, inclusive of the bows. Rolls contain exactly 6 m and 4 m. Pieces cannot be joined. Both runs use the same gifts and rolls. First run: cut 4 m from the 6 m roll (2 m left), then 3 m from the 4 m roll (1 m left): two gifts finished; neither remaining piece is long enough for the third. Second: cut 4 m from the 4 m roll, two 3 m pieces from the 6 m roll: three gifts finished, no ribbon left. The first choice is an example, not a claim that every long-roll-first rule fails. Benefit: plan which lengths fit together before cutting. Explicitly reveal assumptions and count complete gifts; no claims about real wrapping measurements.

The mathematical family is the cutting stock problem, see https://www.matem.unam.mx/~omar/math340/cutting-stock.html . The numbers are an invented demonstrative model, not claims drawn from that source.
