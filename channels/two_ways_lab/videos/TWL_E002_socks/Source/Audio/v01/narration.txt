Eight socks. Four matching pairs. Would you match them one by one, or sort the colors first?

Let's try the same pile both ways. Every comparison or sorting move counts as one action.

First, you grab a sock and check the others until you find its match. Then you do it again. Four pairs, ten comparisons.

Now reset the pile. This time, drop every sock into its color group. Eight moves, and the pairs are already together.

Sorting wins this test by two actions. But a move and a comparison don't always take the same time, and a different pile can change the result.
