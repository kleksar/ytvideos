# Two Ways Lab E006 — parking near or far

User accepted the full Russian first-person story and requested a complete English Short with Mike. The narration below is its faithful production translation. Controlled scenario; compare identical starting point at parking-lot entrance to reaching shop doors. Immediate parking and exit 30 s + walk 90 s = 120 s; closer place after 120 s searching + 30 s parking and exit + 30 s walking = 180 s. The difference is 60 s. No general assertion that one strategy always wins; if nearer place is found sooner the result can differ.

<!-- narration:en:start -->
I pull into the store parking lot and spot an empty space right away. It's far from the entrance. Should I park here or look for a closer one?

I decide to compare both. I start the clock at the parking lot entrance and stop it at the store doors.

First, I take that open space. Parking and getting out take thirty seconds. The walk takes another minute and a half.

I reach the entrance in two minutes.

Now I go back to the start and try to find a closer spot.

I drive past one row. Full. Another row. Full too. Finally, after two minutes, I see an empty spot right by the door!

I park, get out and reach the entrance after a thirty-second walk. That's definitely closer.

But when I check the clock, three minutes have passed since I entered the lot.

In this test, parking right away saved me a minute. But if I'd found that closer spot sooner, the result could have been different.

Next time, I'll consider the search time as well as the walk.

And once again...
