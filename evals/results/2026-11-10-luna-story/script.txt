Boston once had a warning sign that looked like a treat.

In 1919, a giant tank in the North End held more than two million gallons of molasses. It leaked. Neighbors could see the dark syrup slipping through its seams; children even brought pails to catch it.

Then, on a mild January afternoon, the tank burst.

A fast, heavy wave swept through the streets, wrecking buildings and trapping people in the sticky flood. Twenty-one people died. A hundred and fifty were injured.

Afterward, the company blamed a possible bomb. But a court-appointed auditor found the tank structurally weak and held its owners responsible.

The tank failed in a moment. The court fight over responsibility lasted six years.
