Apollo 12 was hit by lightning—and Mission Control lost telemetry.

Thirty-six seconds after launch, lightning struck Apollo 12. A second bolt hit at 52 seconds. The strikes disrupted telemetry—the data sent back to Earth—and warning lights came on inside the spacecraft.

In Houston, flight controller John Aaron recognized the pattern from an earlier Apollo pad test where telemetry had failed. He suggested a switch. Mission Control called up: “Try SCE to auxiliary.”

The SCE was the spacecraft’s signal-conditioning system. AUX switched in an alternate signal path, restoring telemetry. Astronaut Alan Bean found the switch and flipped it. The data returned.

Apollo 12 continued to orbit, then went on to land on the Moon and return safely to Earth.

With the data back, Apollo 12 kept climbing.
