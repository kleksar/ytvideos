He lost his Olympic travel money in a gambling game—then hitchhiked to the race.

In 1904, Cuban runner Félix Carvajal had raised money by running across Cuba. But in New Orleans, he lost it gambling. To reach St. Louis, he had to hitchhike.

On race day, he arrived in heavy street clothes. Fellow runner Martin Sheridan trimmed his trousers and shirt. Then Carvajal stopped during the Olympic marathon to eat green apples. They gave him stomach cramps.

Still, he kept running—and finished fourth.

That race also saw a runner disqualified after taking a car ride, while eventual winner Thomas Hicks was given strychnine to keep going.

Carvajal’s marathon began before the starting gun.
