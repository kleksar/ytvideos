NUVIKO HISTORY SHORT — SCRIPT PACKAGE
Working title: The Escape Boat Built for Six
Topic: The Emden landing party’s escape from Direction Island, November 1914
Audience promise: A stranded German shore party escapes the destruction of its warship in a tiny schooner, then undertakes a long return journey.
Estimated spoken length: about 60–70 seconds at a measured narration pace; not audio-timed.

ENGLISH NARRATION

Their escape boat was built for about six people. Nearly fifty German sailors climbed aboard.

On November 9, 1914, a landing party from the German cruiser Emden was cutting telegraph cables on Direction Island in the Indian Ocean. Then the Australian cruiser HMAS Sydney arrived. Emden fought back, but after a fierce battle it was wrecked on a nearby reef.

The shore party watched its ship vanish. No rescue was coming. Their commander, Hellmuth von Mücke, took the island’s schooner, Ayesha. The men crowded aboard and slipped away before Sydney’s crew reached them.

They made it to neutral Padang, where they left Ayesha for a German ship. The escape continued across the sea and overland through Arabia. In May 1915, the survivors reached Constantinople; they later returned to Germany.

Ayesha was meant for a handful of sailors. Instead, it carried nearly fifty away from a burning warship—and into a nine-month odyssey.

РУССКИЙ ТЕКСТ ДЛЯ ЧТЕНИЯ

Их лодка для побега была рассчитана примерно на шестерых. На борт поднялись почти пятьдесят немецких моряков.

9 ноября 1914 года отряд с немецкого крейсера «Эмден» перерезал телеграфные кабели на острове Дирекшн в Индийском океане. И тут появился австралийский крейсер «Сидней». «Эмден» открыл ответный огонь, но после ожесточённого боя оказался разбит на соседнем рифе.

Береговой отряд увидел, как их корабль исчезает. Спасения ждать было неоткуда. Командир Хельмут фон Мюкке захватил островную шхуну «Айеша». Моряки набились на борт и успели уйти, пока команда «Сиднея» не добралась до острова.

Они добрались до нейтрального Паданга, где пересели с «Айеши» на немецкий корабль. Побег продолжился морем, а затем по суше через Аравию. В мае 1915 года уцелевшие достигли Константинополя, а позже вернулись в Германию.

«Айеша» предназначалась для нескольких моряков. Но она увезла почти пятьдесят человек от горящего крейсера — и отправила их в девятимесячное странствие.

RESEARCH NOTES

Selection: Chose the Emden–Ayesha escape for the sharp visual contrast (a roughly six-person schooner carrying a force of nearly fifty), the clear causal sequence (cable raid → Sydney responds → Emden destroyed → shore party stranded → commandeered escape), and a complete payoff that can loop back to the boat. It is less familiar to a broad English-language audience than the Christmas Truce; the truce offers less of a distinct escape mechanism. Ayesha therefore has the stronger short-form viewer promise. The underlying figures and route are supported by the cited records; “built for about six” is a secondary account’s description, so the narration says “about.”

Fact checks and boundaries:
- On 9 November 1914, Emden’s landing party raided the Direction Island wireless/cable station; HMAS Sydney fought Emden, which was beached on North Keeling Island. Australian War Memorial (AWM), “The Sydney-Emden Engagement”: https://www.awm.gov.au/navy/ch03/s02/
- AWM describes the German party leaving in the commandeered schooner Ayesha and arriving in Germany via Turkey in mid-1915: same AWM account above; contemporaneous sailor letter held by AWM: https://transcribe.awm.gov.au/transcription/item/rcdig0001018
- AWM collection record identifies the Ayesha as the schooner taken by the party after Emden was destroyed: https://www.awm.gov.au/collection/C997512
- German Federal Archives gives the transfer from Ayesha to a German steamer at Padang on 14 December 1914 and arrival in Constantinople on 23 May 1915 after travel through Arabia: https://www.bundesarchiv.de/themen-entdecken/online-entdecken/geschichtsgalerien/vorschlaege-zur-kriegsfuehrung-im-roten-meer-von-kapitaenleutnant-hellmuth-von-muecke-1916/
- The approximately six-person design/fit description comes from the Naval Historical Society of Australia’s account, “Last Voyage of the Ayesha”: https://navyhistory.au/last-voyage-of-the-ayesha/; used with qualification (“about six”). A contemporary first-person account by von Mücke, digitized at Project Gutenberg, describes the detachment and escape but is not independent of its commander: https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/45878/pg45878-images.html
- Do not imply that Ayesha took the party all the way to Constantinople: it was abandoned at Padang, and the party transferred to another vessel before the overland leg.

Editorial check: The opening states the scale contradiction; the middle explains why the sailors are stranded and how they escape; the ending completes the initial promise and returns to the boat. No invented dialogue or private emotions. The Russian text is a spoken translation, not a word-for-word back-translation.
